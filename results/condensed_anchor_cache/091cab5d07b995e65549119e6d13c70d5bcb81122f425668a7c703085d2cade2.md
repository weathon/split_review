## Merged Review

### Summary
VP-OR is a two-stage reoptimization framework for MILP with dynamic parameters. Stage 1 uses a GNN to predict binary variable values and ranges for general integer/continuous variables from the base instance’s solving process. Stage 2 iteratively fixes a sampled subset of variables guided by Thompson Sampling. Evaluated on nine reoptimization datasets (from the MIP 2023 competition), VP-OR is compared against SCIP, the predict-and-search (PS) approach of Han et al. (2023), ND, and Re_Tuning. Under a strict 10‑second time limit it mostly outperforms baselines in solution quality.

### Strengths

- **Originality and clear contributions** – The framework is novel for general MILP reoptimization. Key innovations include (i) predicting ranges for non‑binary variables, going beyond PS which only handles binary, and (ii) replacing traditional LNS with Thompson Sampling, which leverages real‑time solution information and encourages exploration. Both ideas complement each other and are likely useful beyond this work.
- **Sound methodology** – The GNN adaptation for binary, integer, and continuous variables is well‑motivated and includes necessary engineering details. The use of Beta distributions for variable‑fixing decisions is principled.
- **Strong empirical performance under strict time limits** – In the 10‑second setting VP-OR consistently yields higher‑quality feasible solutions than SCIP, PS, ND, and Re_Tuning across nine datasets. The results are credible due to the breadth of modifications represented in the datasets.
- **Interesting analysis in Section 4.1** – Observations on mispredicted variables are used to justify the iterative refinement process, adding depth to the design choices.
- **Clarity** – The paper is well‑structured and easy to follow. Each component (variable prediction, Thompson Sampling, iterative refinement) is explained in detail.

### Weaknesses

- **Inadequate baselines and missing warm‑start comparison** – The natural reoptimization baseline of warm‑starting SCIP with the base solution is not included. Because SCIP is the strongest contender in many experiments, this omission may inflate VP‑OR’s advantages. Similarly, the main competitor Re_Tuning performs anomalously poorly, raising concerns about its implementation. The paper does not include recent SOTA works such as Ye et al. (2023, GNN&GBDT‑guided large‑scale integer programming), Ye et al. (Light‑MILPopt), and Nair & Alizadeh (Neural LNS) as baselines.
- **Unclear adaptation of prediction‑based baselines for non‑binary variables** – PS was originally designed for binary variables only. The paper does not specify how PS was modified to handle general integer and continuous variables in the experiments, casting doubt on the fairness of the comparison. The same concern applies to ND, whose prediction framework is claimed to be similar but differences are not delineated.
- **Limited problem motivation and instance difficulty** – The paper does not clearly motivate why the reoptimization setting matters or how it differs from settings in ND/PS/follow‑ups. Moreover, the test instances are relatively easy (SCIP can solve most to near‑optimality in 60–80 seconds). Previous works used larger and harder instances; the choice of easy instances is not justified.
- **Potential issues with variable fixing** – The framework fixes a fixed percentage of variables based on GNN predictions without dynamic adjustment for confidence. In early iterations this can propagate prediction errors, leading to suboptimal or infeasible solutions and premature convergence. The paper does not analyze how the fixed percentage affects solution quality.
- **No convergence or feasibility guarantees** – There is no theoretical analysis of the iterative refinement process (e.g., convergence to high‑quality solutions) or sufficient discussion of how the relaxation mechanism ensures feasibility. Only empirical evidence is provided.
- **Insufficient ablation and justification for Thompson Sampling** – The choice of Thompson Sampling over traditional LNS is not clearly justified. An ablation study comparing Thompson Sampling against LNS strategies (e.g., those in PS, Neural LNS, or GNN‑guided LNS) is missing. Algorithm 1 (the core iterative procedure) should appear in the main body.
- **Limited statistical evidence** – Only 5 test cases per dataset are used. This small sample size raises concerns about the statistical significance of the reported performance.
- **Scalability analysis absent** – The paper does not analyze how runtime scales with the number of variables, constraints, or instance density. The GNN component is computationally expensive, and the method’s applicability to very large problems is unclear.
- **Overly optimistic claim and presentation issues** – The statement that VP‑OR “rapidly closes the primal gap” is contradicted by Fig. 2 (middle plot), where VP‑OR never closes the gap while other methods do. Also: the symbol `x%` is confusing because `x` also denotes decision variables; Fig. 2 is too small with illegible legends and fonts; Appendix D3 is empty.

*Minority reviewer note:* One reviewer (score 8) regarded the results as “fairly strong” and the contributions as original, while the other three (scores 5,5,6) raised more fundamental concerns about the baseline setup, instance difficulty, and missing comparisons. This contrast is preserved in the above points.
