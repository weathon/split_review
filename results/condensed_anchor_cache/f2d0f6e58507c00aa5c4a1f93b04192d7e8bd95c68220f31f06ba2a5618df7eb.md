## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a NAS method (MAE-NAS) that integrates DARTS with a masked autoencoder (MAE) objective to enable unsupervised architecture search. A hierarchical decoder (HD) and adjustment of the mask ratio are introduced to mitigate performance collapse in the unsupervised DARTS setting. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and NAS-Bench-201 are reported.

### Strengths
- The idea is straightforward and easy to understand (R1, R3, R4). The exploration of reconstruction targets as guidance for NAS is a novel integration (R2).
- The paper is clearly presented with straightforward figures and tables (R2, R3). Experiments are conducted comprehensively across common CNN search spaces and datasets, with multi-run results (R1, R2).
- An interesting observation is made about the correlation between task difficulty and performance collapse, which motivates the hierarchical decoder (R1).
- Addressing performance collapse in differentiable NAS under an unsupervised setting is an important and under-explored problem (R3).
- The synergy between unsupervised MAE and NAS is interesting to understand (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and Significance**: The core idea of applying MAE to NAS is a direct combination of existing techniques (UnNAS, MAE) and lacks a substantial methodological or conceptual leap (R1, R3). Two reviewers (R1, R3) consider the novelty insufficient for ICLR. The performance gains over existing methods (Tables 1–3) are incremental and could be attributed to minor training variations (R1). Performance on large-scale datasets like ImageNet and standard benchmarks appears saturated, and the method does not leverage MAE’s strengths in efficiency (training speed) or scalability (e.g., training ViT-Huge) to provide fresh insights such as addressing overfitting in NAS (R2).
- **Motivation and Synergy**: The necessity and advantage of using MAE over other unsupervised methods for NAS are unclear (R1, R3, R4). The paper lacks discussion of why MAE is particularly suitable (R4); the benefit is hard to discern from the unsupervised NAS comparisons (R4). The fundamental motivation for “MAE+DARTS” is not convincingly justified given the marginal technical novelty and improvements (R2).
- **Performance Collapse Analysis**: The claim that adjusting mask ratio mitigates training collapse is not adequately substantiated. Table 7 does not clearly demonstrate collapse; when mask ratio is 0.1 or 0.3, Top‑1 Errors are 2.79 and 2.77, while at 0.7 the error is 2.80—conflicting with the claim that collapse probability is high for mask ratios <0.5 (R3). Table 8 further shows that low mask ratios do not cause serious performance degradation (R3). The reason why collapse appears in the unsupervised setting is not explained (R3). Claims that the encoder learns more powerful architectures with larger mask ratios and that the unsupervised proxy acts as regularization lack empirical or theoretical evidence (R3).
- **Hierarchical Decoder (HD)**: The effectiveness of the proposed HD is limited. Ablation studies show minimal impact on performance (R2) and the improvement is only evident at high mask ratios, which are eventually adopted (R4). The design logic behind HD is not clearly justified (R2, R4); why this particular decoder works best is not explained (R4).
- **Outdated Search Spaces and Baselines**: The method is combined only with older differentiable NAS methods (DARTS, P‑DARTS, PC‑DARTS). It is unclear why it was not integrated with more recent differentiable NAS methods to demonstrate state‑of‑the‑art performance (R3). A clear baseline that justifies the method’s novelty is missing (R2).
- **Domain Limitations**: The method is confined to convolutional search spaces, failing to leverage MAE’s primary strength for transformers (R1). The resulting convolutional architecture cannot be pre‑trained with MAE, further undermining the motivation for using MAE as a proxy task (R1). The paper does not explore applicability to other domains such as 3D networks or medical imaging, where data is more limited and best architectures are less known, which could better demonstrate the method’s strength (R1).
- **Presentation Issues**: Section 3 contains only a single subsection (3.1) (R1). Citation format should be revised (R2, R3). Punctuation errors exist (e.g., “(i.e. DARTS)” should be “(i.e., DARTS)”) (R3).
