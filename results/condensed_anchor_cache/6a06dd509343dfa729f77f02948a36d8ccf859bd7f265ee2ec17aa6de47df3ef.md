## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the phenomenon previously called the “modality gap” in contrastive models like CLIP. The authors demonstrate that the gap also appears when using the *same modality* under contrastive loss, thus renaming it the “contrastive gap.” They show the gap is exacerbated by small batch sizes in high-dimensional spaces and propose mitigating it by adding uniformity and alignment terms to the CLIP loss. Experiments on zero-shot image classification, retrieval, and multi-modal arithmetic evaluate the approach. Reviewers note the work is clearly written and the central observation is interesting, but raise major concerns about conceptual clarity, experimental validity, missing baselines, and limited novelty. Confidence varies: one reviewer is more positive (score 6, conf 5), two are moderate (5, conf 4 and 3), and one is strongly critical (3, conf 4).

### Strengths
- **Clear demonstration of the contrastive gap**: The controlled experiment showing the gap arises even within the same modality is well-designed and significant (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The analysis of how contrastive loss induces the gap is clear and reasonable (Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive empirical analysis**: The study covers multiple gap metrics, downstream tasks (zero-shot classification, retrieval, multi-modal arithmetic), and dimensions/batch sizes (Reviewers 1, 3). The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Simplicity of proposed solution**: Adding uniformity and alignment terms is straightforward to implement (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear definition and conceptual issues**: The “contrastive gap” is never formally defined (Reviewer 1). The demonstration in Table 1 and Section 3.2 confuses the gap with a margin between samples (Eq. 2) (Reviewer 1). Insufficient justification for renaming “modality gap” to “contrastive gap” (Reviewer 3). The paper misinterprets Wang & Isola (2020) by claiming uniformity and alignment are “desirable properties” without proper context (Reviewer 3). A reviewer asks for a formal definition and a theoretical guarantee that closing the contrastive gap bridges the modality gap (Reviewer 1).
- **Fundamental misunderstanding of CLIP loss**: One reviewer points out that the paper incorrectly attributes CLIP’s loss to SimCLR’s NT-Xent loss, when CLIP actually uses multi-class N-pair loss, undermining the theoretical foundation (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental validity and scalability concerns**: All experiments are conducted in a small-scale fine-tuning setup, not large-scale pre-training; batch sizes and dimensionalities are also small, limiting real-world applicability (Reviewers 2, 3). Missing crucial baselines: no comparison with untuned CLIP or from-scratch training using the proposed loss (Reviewer 3). Generalization from 3D toy demonstrations to high-dimensional real spaces lacks proper justification (Reviewer 3).
- **Performance degradation in retrieval**: All fine-tuning methods seem to degrade the original CLIP’s retrieval performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The examples in Figure 9 appear to be false negatives, a known issue in MS-COCO; evaluation using ECCV-caption and MaP metric is suggested (Reviewer 2). Reviewers note contradictory results between improved zero-shot classification and worsened retrieval, with insufficient explanation (Reviewers 3, 4). One reviewer asks whether using categories as text for retrieval on datasets like CIFAR-10 would give conclusions similar to Figure 5 (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited methodological novelty**: The proposed mitigation (adding uniformity and alignment) closely resembles existing work (e.g., Al-jaff, 2023); the only difference is the inclusion of cross-modal uniformity (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Section 4’s innovation is limited (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental design choices**: Using identical images as positive pairs in the controlled experiment is not properly justified; a reviewer questions whether this provides meaningful insight for genuinely different modalities (Reviewer 3). The paper arbitrarily selects only the first caption per image from MS COCO (Reviewer 3). Missing comparison with prior modality gap solutions (e.g., Liang et al. 2022) (Reviewer 3).
- **Other reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**:
    - How does closing the contrastive gap differ from a margin? (Reviewer 1)
    - Can the proposed loss improve other tasks like image captioning or text-to-image retrieval? (Reviewer 1)
    - Why does accuracy in Figure 1 stay at 0.1 for ~150 epochs and then jump to 0.87? (Reviewer 4)
    - Why does increasing batch size or *s_max* reduce the contrast gap? (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient causal and trade-off analysis**: Lack of causal analysis linking gap reduction to performance improvement (Reviewer 3). Given that Liang et al. (2022) showed the optimal gap size is task-dependent, a reviewer questions the claim that uniformly reducing the gap is beneficial (Reviewer 3). Trade-offs introduced by the proposed loss terms are not discussed (Reviewer 3).
