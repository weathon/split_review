## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a causal concept faithfulness metric to evaluate whether LLM-generated explanations truthfully represent which input concepts influenced the model’s prediction. Faithfulness is defined as alignment between the explanation-implied effect (EE) and causal effect (CE) of each concept, where CE is estimated by counterfactually perturbing concepts via an auxiliary LLM and measuring KL divergence in output distributions. A hierarchical Bayesian model pools information across examples. Experiments on a social bias task and a medical QA task (30 examples each, 50 generations per dataset) reveal patterns of unfaithfulness: safety alignment can mask gender bias (shallow alignment), GPT-3.5 produces more faithful explanations at dataset-level than GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-sonnet, and different intervention types expose brittleness of safety guards. The exact explanation generation method (CoT, prompting style, ordering of explanation vs. prediction) is not specified; one reviewer noted this as a gap. Another reviewer found the validation insufficient, especially the lack of a controlled setting with known faithful explanations.

### Strengths
- Proposes a precise, causally inspired definition of faithfulness (causal concept faithfulness) and a principled counterfactual-based method to measure it.
- Enables fine-grained inspection of specific biases and unfaithfulness patterns in explanations, going beyond aggregate metrics.
- Empirically validated on two distinct QA datasets (social bias and medical QA), revealing interesting findings:
  - GPT-3.5 (older model) yields more faithful dataset-level explanations than GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-sonnet.
  - Safety alignment can cause models to hide true reasons (e.g., gender bias) in explanations, indicating shallow alignment.
  - Different intervention types (removing vs. swapping concept values) expose the brittleness of safety guards in GPT-3.5 and GPT-4o.
- The paper is well-written, information-dense, and addresses a highly relevant topic in AI explainability.
- The proposed metric is useful for the explainability community and offers a concrete, measurable way to evaluate explanation honesty.

### Weaknesses
- **Explanation generation details omitted**: It is unclear how explanations are generated (zero-shot CoT, few-shot prompting, generated before or after prediction?). The paper does not analyze how different prompting methods might change faithfulness. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient validation of the approach**: No controlled experiment verifies that the method correctly identifies unfaithful explanations (e.g., on a dataset with known faithful explanations). Without such validation, the method may falsely flag faithful explanations as unfaithful. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing reproducibility details**: The number of counterfactual perturbations per example, number of generations to estimate P(Y|X), decoding hyperparameters (temperature, max tokens), and how model responses are parsed (nuanced, semantically equivalent texts) are not reported. (Reviewer 2)
- **Small evaluation set**: Only 30 examples per dataset (with 50 generations each). It is unclear how robust the faithfulness scores are; the authors should show how the score varies as the number of datapoints increases to demonstrate stability and statistical power. (Reviewer 2)
- **Univariate counterfactuals ignore feature correlations**: Perturbing one concept at a time fails to account for correlations between features (e.g., school names and socio-demographics), which could confound causal effect estimation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited model coverage**: Only closed-source models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-sonnet) are evaluated. Including open-source models (e.g., Llama 3.1) would improve generalizability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Auxiliary LLM (GPT-4o) not validated independently**: The selection of GPT-4o for concept extraction and counterfactual generation is not motivated, and no human study or LLM-as-judge verification confirms that extracted concepts, alternative values, or counterfactual minimality are correct. The prompts should be included in the appendix. (Reviewer 2)
- **Methodological questions about the metric**:
  - For dataset-level faithfulness, why average per-example PCC instead of directly computing PCC over all examples? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Is per-concept faithfulness possible? In Figure 1, the correlation might be lower if computed per concept and then averaged. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Pearson correlation measures how well points lie on *a* line, not necessarily the line y=x; is this appropriate? Could non-linear metrics be better? (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear generalizability**: It is uncertain whether biases affect all reasoning tasks uniformly or if the findings extend beyond classification to other generative tasks (e.g., long-form generation). (Reviewer 4)
- **Typographical errors**:
  - Line 152: “distinct” should be “disentangled”. (Reviewer 1)
  - Line 194: x in the equation should be in vector form. (Reviewer 1)
  - Line 321: color mismatch — should be “orange for behavior, red for identity”. (Reviewers 2 and 4)
- **Conceptual questions that reflect missing analysis**:
  - Can a faithful explanation be biased? E.g., if both CE and EE are high for gender and the model predicts gender-biased outcomes, is it still faithful according to Definition 2.3? (Reviewer 4)
  - How should results be interpreted if models memorized benchmark datasets? Does this imply reasoning is vulnerable to bias overriding memorized correct answers? (Reviewer 4)
