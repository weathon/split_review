## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Unified Multi-Speaker Encoder (UME) that jointly learns representations for speaker diarization, speech separation, and multi-speaker ASR from a shared pre-trained speech foundation model (OWSM v3.1). It uses a weighted sum of hidden representations from multiple layers of the encoder as input to each task-specific head. Experiments on simulated two-speaker mixtures (Libri2Mix, LibriSpeech2Mix) report improvements over single-task baselines: DER reduced from 3.24 to 2.19 on Libri2Mix for diarization, and cpWER reduced from 11.9 to 9.2 on LibriSpeech2Mix for multi-speaker ASR. Reviewers agree the research direction (unifying multi-talker tasks) is important, but raise substantial concerns about novelty, evaluation fairness, missing comparisons, and insufficient experimental validation.

### Strengths
- Tackles the inherently difficult and practically important problem of unifying speaker diarization, speech separation, and multi-speaker ASR into a single model, replacing a pipeline of multiple systems.
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow.
- Experimental results confirm that joint multi-task training enhances performance over the single-task baselines, demonstrating mutual benefit among the three tasks.
- The use of a pre-trained foundational speech encoder (OWSM) and multi-layer feature fusion is a sensible approach to leverage existing representations.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** Multi-layer feature fusion via weighted sums (e.g., in SUPERB, Whisper-AT, hierarchical feature fusion works) and joint training of multiple speech tasks are well-established. The main contribution is simply applying these existing techniques to a new combination of three tasks, without introducing a novel architectural component or training strategy. [Reviewers 1,3,4]
- **Overclaimed state-of-the-art performance:**
  - For speaker diarization, only compared to ConvTasNet (2019) and EEND. No comparison to more recent strong methods such as Mossformer or Mossformer2. The claim of SOTA is not justified.
  - For multi-speaker ASR, the proposed method achieves 9.2% WER on LibriSpeech2Mix, whereas the reported SOTA (Whisper-based joint multi-talker system) is 3.43%. The paper does not cite or compare with this better result.
  - For diarization, only E2E methods were compared; performance with non-E2E diarization methods is not explored.
- **Unfair or incomplete evaluation protocol:**
  - The speaker diarization test set uses 100% overlapped speech (both speakers active throughout). This means the model can trivially predict both speakers speaking all the time, so low DER may simply reflect noise in ground-truth labels rather than meaningful diarization ability. The SUPERB benchmark does not use 100% overlapped speech. [Reviewer 2]
  - All experiments are on simulated two-speaker mixtures (LibriMix, LibriSpeechMix). No evaluation on real-world datasets (e.g., AMI, ICSI, LibriCSS) or with more than two speakers, severely limiting conclusiveness about real-world applicability. [Reviewers 1,2,3]
- **Missing training and reproduction details:** Epochs/steps, GPU type, VRAM usage, training time, batch size, sampling strategy, min/max/average utterance durations, and the specific model size of OWSM v3.1 (base? small? medium?) are not reported, making reproduction difficult. [Reviewer 2]
- **Task weight selection not justified:** Equal task weights (Eq. 16) are used without systematic exploration or justification. It is unclear if this is optimal or if alternative weighting schemes would yield better performance. Furthermore, the learned layer weights are nearly identical across tasks (Figs. 3-4), suggesting that task-specific weights may not be necessary and that unified weights might perform similarly. The authors did not explore this or compare to single-task weight patterns. [Reviewers 2,4]
- **Discrepancy in figures:** Figure 1 shows a single weight for each task, but Equation (3) introduces different weights per layer for different tasks, which is misleading. [Reviewer 1]
- **Typos and unclear sentences:** Line 37-38 (missing verb), line 40 (vague wording), line 48 ("so much"), line 154 ("gorund truth"), line 156 (incomplete sentence), line 298 (metric vs metrics), line 367 (hard-to-parse sentence comparing WavLM and OWSM), line 369 ("We explain" should be "We hypothesize"). [Reviewer 2]
- **Real-data training unclear:** It is not explained how joint training would work on real datasets where ASR and speech separation ground truths are typically disjoint (e.g., ASR datasets lack separation labels). [Reviewer 1]
