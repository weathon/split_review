## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FAACL, a Federated Adaptive Asymmetric Clustered Learning method that relaxes three constraints: fixed number of clusters, each device belonging to a single cluster, and reciprocal model sharing. It introduces asymmetric clustering where a high-quality-data device can help train a low-quality-data device’s model but not vice versa. The reviewers acknowledge the underexplored direction but raise significant concerns about implementation, comparison, writing, and experimental validity. One reviewer gave a score of 5 (moderate contribution, confidence 2), while two gave 3 (low confidence 3 and 5), reflecting disagreement on overall quality.

### Strengths
- Introduces the novel concept of asymmetric clustering in Federated Learning, a fresh perspective on an underexplored problem.
- Provides theoretical analysis of the proposed clustering strategy.
- Experimental results are comprehensive (MNIST, CIFAR10; reviewer 2).
- Source code is shared for reproducibility (reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing background and context**: The paper lacks an introduction to standard FL and CFL training processes and objectives; a BACKGROUND section is needed to clarify the baseline (reviewer 2). The expression in lines 186–196 reads like a list of contributions/steps rather than a description of components; a figure of the framework and workflow is also missing (reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons to related methods**:  
  - No comparison to the earlier CFL method by Sattler et al. (2020) or the distributed concept drift method by Jothimurugesan et al. (2023) (reviewer 1).  
  - No comparison to state-of-the-art traditional FL optimization methods (reviewer 3 cites [1–4] but does not list them; the paper should include such baselines).  
  - No ablation study separating the effect of asymmetric contribution from cluster merging (reviewer 1, Q5).  
  - No evaluation against an oracle or ground-truth clustering (reviewer 1, Q4).
- **Weak experimental performance and limited datasets**:  
  - On CIFAR10 the method is not effective and is even inferior to the baseline (reviewer 3).  
  - Experiments rely heavily on MNIST and its variants; evaluation on a more complex dataset like ImageNet is needed (reviewer 3).  
  - No details on how the data is partitioned or the rationale for the data division; different splits affect final performance (reviewer 2).
- **Practical implementation and resource concerns**:  
  - Determining whether cluster C2 supports cluster C1 in a real-world FL setting is challenging because devices cannot see each other’s data; this would require multiple training/testing rounds, leading to resource inefficiency (reviewer 2).  
  - How does the method handle a newly added device? Checking against all clusters or only prepared clusters may cause a performance hit (reviewer 1, W2).  
  - The main operations (Algorithms 5 and 6) are placed in the appendix but should be described in the main text for reader understanding (reviewer 2).
- **Lack of privacy analysis and secure aggregation adaptation**:  
  - The method cannot adapt to standard secure aggregation; the paper does not analyze whether privacy guarantees are equivalent to those of Sattler et al. (2020) (reviewer 1, Q2; reviewer 3).  
- **Unclear writing and omissions**:  
  - The statement on line 053 (“all existing techniques assume that each device contributes to a single cluster”) is ambiguous; the paper does not clarify how the proposed method addresses this (reviewer 1, Q1).  
  - The paper does not analyze computational complexity relative to other algorithms (reviewer 1, Q3).  
- **Incremental contribution**: The work appears to be an incremental extension of CFL with limited novelty (reviewer 3).
