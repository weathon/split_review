## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Inner Loop Feedback (ILF), a method to accelerate diffusion model inference by training a lightweight feedback module that predicts future features in the denoising process. ILF exploits the similarity of outputs from adjacent blocks/timesteps, using a block from the diffusion backbone itself as the feedback module. Training uses distillation losses while freezing the backbone, with a learnable scaling factor initialized to zero for stability. Additional innovations include Fast Approximate Distillation and Feedback-aware Inference Scheduling. The method is evaluated on DiT, PixArt-alpha, and PixArt-sigma for class-to-image and text-to-image generation, reporting 1.7x-1.8x speedups with FID, CLIP score, CLIP-IQA, and ImageReward.

### Strengths

- Novel approach that accelerates diffusion models while maintaining high image quality, focusing on matching best-case (20-step) results rather than only extreme few-step generation.
- Uses inner feature maps during distillation, which could encapsulate more information than standard distillation that does not observe intermediate activations.
- Flexible and adaptable across multiple architectures (DiT, PixArt-alpha, PixArt-sigma) and tasks (class-to-image, text-to-image).
- Evaluation includes visual and numerical comparisons, with strong performance on human-aligned quality metrics (CLIP-IQA, ImageReward).
- Lightweight feedback module (a copied block from the backbone) with minimal additional parameters and no need to store an entire separate model.
- Effective balance between speed and quality, outperforming caching-based methods.
- Fast Approximate Distillation and Feedback-aware Inference Scheduling contribute to training efficiency and inference improvement.
- Results are demonstrated on state-of-the-art text-to-image models.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited architectural scope:** The method is only evaluated on transformer-based models (DiT, PixArt). Whether it applies to UNet-based diffusion models (common in prior distillation/caching works) is not shown; a reviewer asks for such an analysis.
- **Insufficient comparison to step-distillation methods:** ILF is a self-distillation approach, but comparisons with existing step-distillation baselines (e.g., InstaFlow, Adversarial Diffusion Distillation, guided distillation) are missing. The only distillation comparison is a brief qualitative ablation (Fig. 11) with three samples, poorly documented and without numerical runtime or quality metrics. Including these baselines in the main quantitative results is considered essential.
- **Marginal improvement over simpler baselines:** In Table 1, ILF offers only minor improvement over a baseline that simply uses fewer denoising steps (e.g., steps=2) on prompt-aware metrics and FID, yet ILF requires ~100 H100 GPU hours of training. This baseline is also missing from qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7). Multiple reviewers note this makes ILF a less favorable option.
- **Inconsistency across experimental settings:** The main quantitative results (Table 1) use a different acceleration rate than the main qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7) and the ablation (Table 3). This inconsistency makes interpretation difficult and should be harmonized.
- **Writing quality issues:** Multiple reviewers cite poor writing, including verbal/informal expressions ("we already know that", "this is clearly not optimal"), unclear long sentences, statements without supporting explanation, and ambiguous phrasing. Specific problematic sections: introduction (hard to follow), lines 264 (typo), 350-360, 469-472. Reviewer 4 notes missing rationale for "redundancy" motivation (L127) and unclear contrast between "acceptable image quality" and "matching best case result" in the abstract.
- **Lack of comparison to consistency models:** The method can be viewed as a consistency model or self-distillation approach, yet no comparison to state-of-the-art methods from these classes is provided.
- **Missing standard benchmarks:** The paper does not report results on ImageNet-64x64 or MS COCO-30k, which are widely used in the literature for evaluating diffusion acceleration. Reviewer suggests using MS COCO-30k for better alignment.
- **No ablation on key design choices:** The choice of adding noise at step t/2 in Fast Approximate Distillation is not ablated; reviewers ask for analysis. The derivation and rationale for Eq. (5) (including factor i×(n/m)×(t/1000) and max with 10) are not explained.
- **Behavior not fully explained:** Figure 8 shows a drop in ImageReward after a certain time for different feedback schedules; reviewers ask why. Also, it is unclear whether the method can be extended to further reduce steps beyond the demonstrated range.
- **Contrast in reviewer opinion:** While one reviewer (score 5) considers the method novel and solid, the other three reviewers (scores 1, 3, 3) raise substantial concerns about insufficient baselines, unclear advantages over simple baselines, writing quality, and missing comparisons, leading to a low average rating.
