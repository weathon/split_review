## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ActSeq, a data augmentation strategy for action anticipation. To mitigate ordering bias in datasets, it induces a context-free grammar over observed action sequences and uses a parser to generate plausible next-action candidates. To handle noisy input, it randomly deletes or replaces actions. The method is evaluated on 50Salads, EGTEA Gaze+, and Epic-Kitchens-100, showing improvements across multiple baseline models. (Note: Reviewer 2 rated 8, considerably more positive about the contribution than Reviewers 1,3,4 who each gave 6.)

### Strengths
- Addresses concrete challenges: ordering bias, combinatorial action space, interleaved goals (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- Grammar induction captures hierarchical/compositional structure and can transfer to other domains (e.g., robotics, action recommenders) where grammars are normally handwritten (Reviewer 2).
- The action goal is specifically modeled and interpretable (Reviewer 4).
- Exhaustive evaluation on three standard datasets (50Salads, EGTEA Gaze+, Epic-Kitchens-100) across a variety of baseline methods (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Easy to follow, technically sound, and provides an interesting study on ordering bias (Reviewer 3).
- Practical temporary solution to the problem of intertwined action sequences (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Previous works have explored grammar for activity prediction and data augmentation for temporal bias. The paper should more clearly delineate its novel contributions (Reviewer 1). 
- **Missing Top-1 results**: Tables 8 and 9 only report Top-5. Top-1 accuracy might even degrade because augmented sequences include plausible but unlikely futures – at minimum, Top-1 should be shown (Reviewer 2).
- **Table 1 clarity**: No caption, negative values unexplained. This figure is vital for understanding the construction of Figure 3e (Reviewer 2).
- **Qualitative visualizations**: Lacking a figure or tree visualization for one of the datasets (Reviewer 2).
- **Alternative augmentation sources**: Could an LLM be used instead of the grammar? A qualitative comparison would help justify the grammar-based approach (Reviewer 2, missing experiment/comparison).
- **Missing related work**: The paper “Therbligs in Action” (Dessalene et al., CVPR 2023) imposes commonsense grammar rules for action anticipation and should be cited (Reviewer 2).
- **Claim of data efficiency not tested**: Section 3 claims the method reduces the need for many action sequences, but no low-data experiment is provided (e.g., compare model with/without augmentations under reduced data) (Reviewer 2, missing experiment).
- **Use of all plausible sequences**: Is there an augmentation strategy that uses the entire set of grammar-compliant sequences rather than a fixed multiple? (Reviewer 2, missing ablation).
- **MAOB study incomplete**: The multi-actor ordering bias analysis is only on 50Salads; it should be extended to EGTEA Gaze+ and Epic-Kitchens-100 to check correlation between bias reduction and performance gain (Reviewer 3, missing experiment).
- **Scalability of augmentation factor**: Tables 3 & 4 show that gains do not scale proportionally with the augmentation multiple (3×, 5×) – the hyperparameter X is critical and needs discussion on trade-offs (Reviewer 3).
- **Efficiency concerns**: The method requires large augmentations (e.g., 12×) for small improvements (e.g., >1% recall), raising cost issues for large datasets. Computational overhead should be reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation on other datasets**: Ablation studies like Tables 3 & 4 are only done on EGTEA Gaze+; similar analyses on 50Salads and Epic-Kitchens-100 are needed to show generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing issues**: Inconsistent dataset names (EGTEA Gaze+, EGTEA Gaze, EGTEA); Table 7-9 captions are sentences missing periods; no explanation of evaluated verb/noun/action items for Epic-Kitchens – paper should be self-contained (Reviewer 3, minor).
- **Missing reference**: “On Diverse Asynchronous Activity Anticipation” (ECCV 2020) not cited (Reviewer 3, minor).
- **Grammar limitation (Equation 1)**: The count-based equation may be inaccurate when the same atomic action appears multiple times in a video (Reviewer 4).
- **Only pairwise dependencies modeled**: High-order dependencies among more than two prerequisite actions not considered (Reviewer 4).
- **Frequency bias in AGI**: Frequent atomic actions may dominate the induced grammar, biasing generations (Reviewer 4).
- **Few augmentation comparisons**: Only random processing is compared (Table 6); simple augmentations like rotation or noise should also be compared to demonstrate superiority (Reviewer 4, missing comparison).
- **Missing implementation details**: How are augmented videos manipulated? Are they realistic? (Reviewer 4).
- **Inconsistent behavior of augmentation factor**: In Tables 3-5, why is 5× augmentation sometimes worse than 3×? (Reviewer 4, missing explanation).
- **Prediction of multiple future actions**: The method is only tested for single next-action prediction; its effectiveness for predicting many (e.g., 20) future actions is unexplored (Reviewer 1, missing experiment).
