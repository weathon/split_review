## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AgentStudio, a trinity of environments, tools, and benchmarks for building general virtual agents. It provides a lightweight interactive environment supporting multimodal observations (text, images, videos) and action types (GUI, APIs), tools for benchmark creation and data annotation, and a benchmark suite of 205 real-world tasks across applications such as VS Code, Google Workspace, and Office suites. Three datasets are introduced: GroundUI (GUI grounding), IDMBench (action labeling from videos), and CriticBench (success detection). Experiments evaluate current LLM/VLM agents (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Qwen-VL-Chat) and reveal shortcomings, especially on compositional tasks. The paper aims to address limitations of existing domain-specific environments and provide fundamental capability evaluations.

### Strengths
- Lightweight, interactive environment with highly generic observation and action spaces (video observations, GUI/API actions) that expand the task space to a massively open domain and real-world tasks. Supports diverse input modalities (text, images, videos) and action types, making it versatile for training agents (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Integrated tools for creating and validating benchmark tasks, annotating GUI elements, and labeling actions in videos, essential for customizing and validating tasks in real-world settings (Reviewer 1). Dataset curation process makes sense (Reviewer 2).
- Online interactions enable learning through trial and error, providing language feedback on failure reasons, crucial for open-ended learning and self-improvement of LLM-based agents (Reviewer 1). Also supports interactivity, data/tasks/tools, and language feedback (Reviewer 2).
- Introduces three datasets (GroundUI, IDMBench, CriticBench) targeting UI grounding, action labeling from videos, and success detection—providing a structured approach to evaluating and improving fundamental agent capabilities (Reviewer 1). This highlights effort to evaluate core skills (Reviewer 4).
- Benchmark suite of 205 real-world tasks across various applications (VS Code, Google Workspace, Office suites) (Reviewer 3). Adequate experiments showing advantages over previous works (Reviewer 2).
- Evaluation of current LLM-based agents (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Qwen-VL-Chat) on real-world software interaction tasks provides good analysis of failure modes and limitations, e.g., models do well on single API tasks but poorly on compositional tasks; specialized models (SeeClick) outperform general models (Gemini, Claude) on GroundUI (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Lightweight design, compatibility with various OS and Docker environments improve accessibility and ease of use (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Technical novelty is limited. The environment appears to be largely an integration of existing components without significant new technical contributions. Components are basic (e.g., annotation tool characterized as “minimalism”) (Reviewer 3). The main contribution is integration rather than fundamental technical advances. This limits impact (Reviewer 3, post-rebuttal).
- Datasets are small in scale: IDMBench (345 trajectories), CriticBench (350 trajectories). Small scale limits impact and raises concerns about representativeness. Plans to scale up are not discussed (Reviewer 3). Also, number of tasks (205) may not be enough; continued growth would be beneficial (Reviewer 2).
- Insufficient technical details about implementation: only cursory mention of VNC and Docker; no discussion of performance, latency, scalability, or reliability considerations (Reviewer 3). No discussion of whether AgentStudio can enable large-scale data collection, agent evaluation, multi-threading for online RL training (Reviewer 3).
- Claims somewhat overstated: AgentStudio focuses on 2D GUIs, but a general virtual agent should also interact with 3D virtual environments (e.g., metaverse, 3D video games). Current focus limits generalizability and applicability to more complex real-world scenarios (Reviewer 1). The benchmarks and tasks are simulated in controlled environments, which may not capture real-world complexities (variability in interfaces, network latency, unexpected system errors), potentially overestimating agent performance (Reviewer 4). Simulation may not adequately model variability and unexpected errors (Reviewer 4).
- Evaluation metrics (success rates and accuracy) are insufficient. They do not capture partial progress, efficiency, number of steps, or resource consumption. Need metrics assessing quality of interaction (Reviewer 4). In IDM-Multiple, accuracy based on exact match of action sequences is too restrictive; multiple optimal or suboptimal but successful sequences could demonstrate capabilities (Reviewer 3).
- Lack of detailed documentation and step-by-step instructions for setting up the environment, running examples, customizing tasks. This hinders adoption and usability (Reviewer 1). The usefulness of AgentStudio is further questioned: it is not clear college students are typical users, and whether there are any quality control processes; the 51% figure calls into question the labeling process (Reviewer 3, post-rebuttal).
- Software selection appears random; improvement could come from usage statistics to include top software (e.g., Photoshop) (Reviewer 2). Also unclear how failure examples are created (e.g., extracting first steps of successful trajectories) (Reviewer 2).
- How does AgentStudio handle user interactions and learning from user feedback? Feasibility of human evaluation of user experience? (Reviewer 1). Will AgentStudio be open sourced? (Reviewer 2). What are key technical differences from existing environments like VisualWebArena? (Reviewer 3).

*Minority perspective: Reviewer 3 is more skeptical, noting borderline technical novelty and small scale, and post-rebuttal questions about basic components and labeling quality. Reviewer 4 is more positive (score 8), praising the holistic toolkit and lightweight design, but still raises concerns about simulation and metrics. Reviewer 1 also criticizes overstated scope. The overall balance is a mix of strong support for the integrative toolkit and significant reservations about depth, scale, and evaluation.*
