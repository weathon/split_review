## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes MoDiff, a protein diffusion model built upon VFN-Diff that addresses the multi-motif scaffolding problem when relative positions among motifs are unknown. It introduces an Implicit Matching Module (IMM) and a motif reconstruction loss to automatically assign motifs to the protein backbone during diffusion. The model shows some success in generating diverse scaffolds with multiple motifs, but the evaluation is incomplete and key methodological details are missing.

### Strengths

- The paper clearly explains the importance of the multi-motif scaffolding problem and the limitations of existing inpainting methods, particularly the need for unknown relative spatial and sequential positions (R1, R3).  
- The multi-motif scaffolding task with unknown relative poses/positions is a novel and potentially impactful formulation for antibody and enzyme design (R1, R3).  
- The Implicit Matching Module (IMM) is an interesting, neat, and intuitive extension of cross attention that addresses the particular challenges of conditioning on multiple motifs; it appears to improve success rates (R1, R2, R3).  
- MoDiff leverages prior knowledge from data distributions without requiring expert knowledge (R2).  
- The paper provides a new modeling perspective for multi-motif scaffolding, adding cross-attention that integrates motif information into the SE(3) diffusion framework, and verifies feasibility on a benchmark dataset (R4).  
- The breakdown of success rates for design tasks on \(M^\text{exist}\) is informative (R3).  
- Two reviewers (R1, R3) found the paper well presented; R3 also noted the novelty of IMM and the clear explanation of limitations.

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient comparisons with existing methods:** The paper does not compare MoDiff on single-motif scaffolding against RFDiffusion (R1, R4), nor on multi-motif scaffolding against Genie 2 (R1) or FADiff (R2, R4). Such comparisons are essential to understand relative performance and whether the added flexibility helps or hurts.  
- **Ablation of IMM is unconvincing:** The central claim that cross attention is insufficient and IMM is necessary is not properly tested. Section 5.4 does not clarify whether the ablation replaces IMM with cross attention or removes conditioning entirely – a much easier baseline (R3). The ablation should directly replace IMM with cross attention (R3). Additionally, IMM computes attention separately for each motif rather than jointly considering motif-motif relationships (R2).  
- **Missing key algorithmic details:** The paper omits: how multi-motifs extracted from a known system maintain their relative features during generation (R4); the definition and physical meaning of label \(\mathbf{C}\) used in cross-attention supervision (R4); a summary of the VFN architecture and its key differences from other frame-based architectures like IPA (R1); the architecture of SeqTrans (R1); how residue index and motif mask are encoded (R4); and how “prior knowledge from data distribution” is concretely leveraged (R2). A latex error “??” appears on p5 (R2). Code is not released (R2).  
- **Metric choices and test set details not justified:** The rationale for using scTM > 0.5 instead of the more stringent scRMSD < 2 (common in RFDiffusion) is not given (R1). Criteria for filtering out PROSITE motifs present in training data are not described; test set sizes (\(M^\text{exist}\)=20, \(M^\text{unknown}\)=6) are small and their variation is unclear (R1). Novelty assessment does not state whether PDB structures from \(M^\text{exist}\) and \(M^\text{unknown}\) are included in the database (R1).  
- **Limited multi-motif evaluation and scope:** Qualitative results for 3+ motifs are shown, but the model is not benchmarked on tasks with more than two motifs (R3). In-depth analysis (e.g., success rate breakdown) is not repeated for ablated methods (R3). It is unclear whether motifs can consist of multiple discontinuous segments (R1) and whether motif sequences are fixed and input to the model (R1, R4). The claim of being the “first method” for more than two motifs needs rephrasing because FADiff already addresses this problem (R2).  
- **Presentation issues:** Although two reviewers found the paper clear, another reviewer found presentation mediocre, with insufficient description of VFN and key features (R4). One reviewer noted limited explanation of existing methods and why cross attention is inefficient (R2). A reference to a non-existent section appears (line 239) (R3).  
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Scores are split (5,5 vs 3,3). The two lower-scoring reviewers emphasize the lack of comparisons, insufficient ablation, and missing details as major weaknesses. One reviewer (R3) specifically recommended rejection based on the IMM ablation weakness, despite giving a score of 5.
