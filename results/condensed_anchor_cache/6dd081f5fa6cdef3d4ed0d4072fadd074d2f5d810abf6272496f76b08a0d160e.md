## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UIEG+, a semi-supervised underwater object detection method that uses attribute-based data distribution (color and scale) to guide image enhancement, aiming to reduce distribution differences between labeled and unlabeled images. It integrates with existing SSOD frameworks. Experiments on two datasets (including URPC from Zhangzi Island) show results that are at least on par with state-of-the-art. The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2, R4). However, multiple reviewers expressed concerns about limited novelty, insufficient experiments, lack of statistical significance, and potential overfitting.

### Strengths
1. [R1] Novel underwater image enhancement method (UIEG+) that reduces distributional differences between enhanced and unlabeled images by analyzing color and scale attributes via 3D color memory and 1D scale memory.
2. [R1, R3] Compatible with existing semi-supervised object detection (SSOD) frameworks, effectively addressing challenges in semi-supervised underwater object detection.
3. [R2] Achieves good results using a simple enhancement procedure.
4. [R2, R4] The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
5. [R3] Semi-supervised learning is important for underwater imaging due to high annotation cost; UIEG+ introduces a unique method by considering both color and scale distributions.

### Weaknesses
1. [R1, R2, R3, R4] Limited methodological novelty. The enhancement approach is simple: calculating average color/scale parameters from unlabeled images and using them to augment training data, effectively selecting the mode of the distribution (R2). The whole framework follows an established paradigm (R2). The solution may overfit the dataset, especially the URPC data from Zhangzi Island which is heavily biased (R4). No proof of generalization to diverse real underwater scenes with objects in different colors and scales (R4).
2. [R1] Experiment insufficient: recent related work from 2024 not compared. [R1] Also, need to compare with SOTA image enhancement methods to show the effectiveness of the proposed enhancement part (R1). [R3] Provide more detailed comparison with semi-supervised or self-supervised object detection methods.
3. [R1, R3] Ablation studies inadequate. [R1] Only CTransfor and STransfor components tested on URPC. [R3] Would like to see mAP with only color transform. [R3] Also include analysis of other attributes (edge/shape/frequency) that could improve performance.
4. [R2, R3] Results lack confidence intervals or standard deviations, making it hard to assess statistical significance of AP/mAP differences, especially since improvements are small (e.g., in Tab.1, Ours (PseCo) only improves baseline by 0.4%) (R4).
5. [R2, R4] The performance is not consistently strong: mAP does not show constant improvement over existing methods (R3); the gain over baseline is marginal in some cases (R4). [R4] Why not integrate with the best baseline Consistent-Teacher?
6. [R2] The claim that the approach takes into account appearance distribution of unlabeled images while others don’t is questionable: state-of-the-art approaches that process all images uniformly will mostly represent the main mode of the distribution, achieving the same result as explicitly selecting the mode.
7. [R3] The 3D color memory and 1D scale memory approach may introduce additional computational overhead; include time complexity information.
8. [R3] It is not clear whether an image enhancement framework is needed for underwater object detection; an end-to-end method might be better.
9. [R2, R3, R4] Missing details and design questions: [R2] How is weak augmentation performed? [R2] Color and scale augmentations seem to always choose the most frequent bin; why not employ weighted sampling? [R4] The authors should prove generalization and address overfitting (e.g., evaluate on more diverse underwater scenes).
