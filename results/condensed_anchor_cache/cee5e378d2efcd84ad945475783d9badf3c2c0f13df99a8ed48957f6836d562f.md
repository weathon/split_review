## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DiffSim, a blind docking method that integrates a diffusion model with molecular dynamics (MD) simulation. The approach aligns reverse diffusion sampling with MD simulation trajectories using a novel loss function, employs active learning to selectively train on MD data, and includes theoretical analysis showing mathematical equivalence between the reverse diffusion process and Langevin dynamics. Empirical results on PDBBind demonstrate improvements over prior methods on RMSD and centroid distance metrics. Reviewer opinions differ: one reviewer (rating 5) finds the hybrid idea promising and the improvements satisfying, while three reviewers (rating 3) consider the theoretical foundation weak, benchmarks insufficient, and the empirical gains marginal.

### Strengths
- Novel idea to seamlessly combine a generative diffusion model with MD simulation for blind docking.
- Theoretical analysis provides insights into the connection between diffusion models and MD (Lemma 3.1 showing the same differential form as Langevin dynamics).
- Active learning selectively uses expensive MD simulation, reducing computational burden.
- Generation of an MD dataset is a valuable contribution to the community.
- Improved performance on RMSD and Top-1 centroid distance metrics (though significance is debated).
- The introduction of a “bias and variance” tradeoff between MD and reverse diffusion is interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Weak theoretical foundation**: The claimed equivalence (Lemma 3.1) has been known since 2020. The step-wise bounding between reverse diffusion and MD trajectories is not solid because per-step marginal distributions differ fundamentally: Langevin dynamics sample from a Boltzmann distribution under a fixed energy, while intermediate reverse diffusion steps sample from a Gaussian that changes as `t` evolves. This introduces a variable “energy” (log probability) in reverse diffusion. Deeper discussions from existing works [1][2][3] are missing.
- **Insufficient benchmarking**: Only PDBBind is used; testing on more diverse complexes is needed. Missing comparisons with recent methods: E3Bind, FABind, and all-atom diffusion models like GeoDiff. Several published works on denoising diffusion for MD simulation [1,2,3] are not cited or compared.
- **Marginal empirical gains**: Results are only slightly better than DiffDock. The only significant improvement is on the Centroid Distance metric for TOP-1. Given the computational cost, this raises doubts about effectiveness.
- **Missing ablations and analysis**: No ablation isolating the benefits of active learning. No analysis of different alignment loss function forms. The “bias and variance” tradeoff hypothesis is not supported by any recorded metrics.
- **Lack of visualization**: Vital for MD analysis; no consistent dynamics of reverse diffusion are shown to justify that trajectories are correctly learned. No visualization of protein changes (e.g., side chain alterations) during docking.
- **Choice of backbone model**: Using DiffDock as backbone may conflict with MD philosophy due to its limited degrees of freedom (rotational/translational noise vs all-atom flexibility). An all-atom diffusion method (e.g., GeoDiff) might be more compatible with MD.
- **Missing computational efficiency comparison**: MD is expensive, and diffusion models with many steps are also costly; efficiency vs. alternatives (traditional docking, other deep learning methods) is not reported.
- **Active learning details**: No exploration of different RMSD thresholds for selecting training samples. The impact of the threshold on results is unclear.
- **No pocket RMSD or holo-protein baseline**: It would be informative to report pocket RMSD or results using the holo protein/pocket as initial structure.
- **Time efficiency of MD**: Discussion on the time cost of running MD simulations (especially for protein MD) is absent.
