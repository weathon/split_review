## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Flow-Based Dynamical Alignment (FDA), a flow-matching method to obtain consistent neural embeddings across recording sessions for BCI decoders. It uses attractor-like ensemble dynamics and source-free alignment via likelihood maximization. Numerical experiments on multi-day motor cortex datasets show improved decoding performance and stability over baselines. The method is theoretically argued to be dynamically stable.

### Strengths
- The method based on flow matching is original and tackles an important long-standing issue in BCI (Reviewer 2). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to apply flow-matching to BCI signal alignment (Reviewer 4).
- Problem formulation makes sense; quantitative results are quite thorough with comparison using several baselines and datasets (Reviewer 2). Extensive benchmarking was conducted against state-of-the-art models (Reviewer 4).
- The proposed FDA method does perform better than baselines on decoding cursor velocity, and the learned latent space has more stability (Reviewer 3).
- The method has relatively better computational efficiency although may need more parameters (Reviewer 3).
- The authors did multiple ablation studies on main components (Reviewer 3).
- The paper uses source-free alignment via likelihood maximization (FDA-MLA) and uses pre-training and fine-tuning to achieve consistent neural embeddings from non-stationary neural signals. The setup of latent extraction via conditional feature extraction based on neural dynamics was noted (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well written for the most part; figures are clear and well presented (Reviewer 2). Writing is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of comparison with a cited related work** [1] that uses a similar seq-VAE architecture. The absence of this comparison leaves a gap in understanding relative performance (Reviewer 2). 
2. **Concerns about evaluation of NoMAD and Cycle-GAN**: In a highly relevant companion submission (https://openreview.net/forum?id=LNp7KW33Cg), the R² of NoMAD and Cycle-GAN is nearly six times better than reported here. This discrepancy raises questions about baseline implementation and tuning (Reviewer 4).
3. **Discrepancy with Cycle-GAN paper results**: The same dataset was used in the Cycle-GAN paper (eLife, 2023, Figure 3A) where average R² is above 50%, but in this paper the evaluation shows average R² below 10% (Table 1). The source of this variation needs to be addressed (Reviewer 4).
4. **Selective reporting of results**: Main Table 1 and Supplementary Table 8 are nearly identical, but the authors included nine sessions per monkey in Table 1 while omitting two sessions with worst performance (Day 22 and 25 in CO-M; Day 40 and 77 in RT-M). This selective reporting undermines credibility (Reviewer 4).
5. **The term "attractor-like ensemble dynamics"** is heavily used in the Introduction but never referred to again in Methodology or Experiments & Results. How is the proposed method using and how is it relevant? (Reviewer 2). Additional explanation of what is meant by "attractor-like" is needed; the cited Gonzalez et al. 2019 paper mentions it only once regarding persistence of representations (Reviewer 4).
6. **The dynamical stability verification** is not clearly explained for the theoretical support (Reviewer 1).
7. **Some results are puzzling**: (Reviewer 1)
   - In Table 1, why are the results of ERDiff (NeurIPS 2023) extremely different and worse than others?
   - In Table 1, why does R² decrease to 23.79 (FDA-MLA) and 45.23 (FDA-MMD) at Day 8, but then increase to 50.15 and 55.9? In Cycle-GAN paper R² decreases continuously. Alignment should be worse with longer time-gap relative to Day 0.
   - Why is R² of CEBRA much better in RT-M dataset than CO-M dataset? (Reviewer 1).
   - For each method, the worst R² occurs on different days (e.g., in CO-M, LSTM worst on Day 29, but FDA-MLA worst on Day 8). Explanation needed (Reviewer 3).
8. **Results on Monkey C are very different from Monkey M** – explanation needed (Reviewer 3).
9. **Lack of cross-subject validation** (e.g., between Monkey C and M) is noted as a weakness (Reviewer 3).
10. **Several methodological clarifications requested**: (Reviewer 2)
    - How was the signal window sampled? How many windows per trial? Do they overlap?
    - Line 125: Why is behavior label y_i taken only at the w-th timestep? Does this mean decoding downsampled behavior, making the task easier than for baselines?
    - Figure 1: In the first block, shouldn’t c^S be at the top and c^T at the bottom? Also, x^T and z^T should not be used during Pre-training phase?
    - Line 185: Does the transformer utilize positional embeddings? If so, what kind?
    - Line 206: How was η pre-defined? Is η kept the same across days?
    - Figure 2c: Is each point the average across test sessions or across samples with the same ratio r? Please provide errorbars.
    - Figure 2c: Will other baselines reach FDA performance if r increases? At how many trials do they become comparable?
    - Figure 2d: Why 11 rows/columns when Table 1 shows 9 days?
11. **Additional clarifications**: (Reviewer 3)
    - How many trials are in the source domain?
    - What is the average across for average MLE? What are all the individual MLEs?
    - Is fine tuning the reason FDA performs better in few-trial scenarios?
    - How were hyperparameters chosen, especially latent dimension? Do all compared models have the same latent dimension?
12. **Part 3.2.2**: Explain why maximum mean discrepancy is used and why it is better than other metrics (Reviewer 1).
13. **Citation error**: Azabou et al., 2023a and Azabou et al., 2023b are the same paper (Reviewer 4).
