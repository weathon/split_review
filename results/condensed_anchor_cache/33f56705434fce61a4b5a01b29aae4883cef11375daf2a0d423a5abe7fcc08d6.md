## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers studied a paper that reinterprets “dying neurons” as a resource for structured pruning, introducing DemP (Demon’s Pruning) to remove dead neurons during training. The paper provides mathematical analysis (Brownian motion model) and experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, claiming DemP outperforms existing structured pruning methods and matches unstructured ones. Reviewer scores ranged from 5 to 6; two reviewers (6) were more favorable about originality and clarity, while two (5) were more critical of novelty, motivation, and missing experiments. All agreed the method is simple and the investigation of hyperparameter influence on neuron death is interesting, but raised several concerns about practical relevance, experimental rigor, and theoretical grounding.

### Strengths
- **Originality and insight**: The idea of using dying neurons for structured pruning is original (R1) and provides a new perspective on learning a structured sparse network during training (R3). The exploration of hyperparameter effects (batch size, regularization, learning rate) on neuron mortality is insightful (R1, R4) and fills a gap in structured pruning literature (R3).
- **Simplicity and applicability**: DemP is simple, computationally efficient, and straightforward to implement (R1, R2, R4). It is broadly applicable (R4).
- **Mathematical analysis**: The paper provides a mathematical analysis (Section 3.2) that offers intuition about neuron death and its relationship to hyperparameters, which is generally helpful (R1).
- **Experimental design**: The experiments (neuron death study and DemP comparisons) are well designed and clear, with thoughtfully chosen baselines (R1). Extensive empirical validation across multiple benchmarks (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) with comparative analysis (R4).
- **Performance**: DemP yields higher accuracy and stronger speedups compared to other structured pruning algorithms (R1). It outperforms current structured pruning techniques (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work**: Several reviewers questioned novelty. The concept of pruning by removing inactive neurons has been explored in earlier work (R2, citing [1,2,3] for activation-based pruning). The method may be a structured version of existing sparse training methods (e.g., RigL) without clear differentiation (R3). Existing works already prune dead neurons without requiring active encouragement (R4, citing [1,2] for structured/unstructured magnitude pruning). The technical contribution is not immediately clear (R3).
- **Practical relevance and speedup evaluation**: The paper lacks real inference speedup evaluation compared to the dense model (R3). This is a critical missing experiment for a pruning method (R1, R3, R4). Only one reviewer (R1) requested further speedup results, but even the basic speedup measurements are absent (R3). The practical relevance of DemP in realistic training setups (e.g., modern training recipes, transformers, language models) is unclear (R1, Q4). Specifically, testing on ViTs or GPT-2 Small would strengthen the paper (R1, Q4).
- **CIFAR-10 accuracy gap**: ResNet18 on CIFAR-10 achieves ~95% accuracy in typical reports, but the paper reports lower accuracy. The authors should clarify if this is due to the regularization schedule used to encourage sparsity (R1, Q2). If so, this is a limitation of DemP that must be stated (R1). This concern also relates to the motivation to accelerate neuron death, which may harm expressivity (R4).
- **Comparison baselines and experimental setup**: 
  - **Regularization**: It is unclear whether baseline methods (e.g., EarlyCroP) use the same regularization schedule required by DemP (R1, Q3; R2, Q). If they do not, comparisons may be unfair (R1).
  - **Unstructured pruning comparison**: Unstructured pruning baselines are not well-tuned (R1). For example, well-tuned magnitude pruning on ResNet18/CIFAR-10 achieves 95% accuracy at 95% sparsity, contradicting the paper's claim to match unstructured methods (R1, Q5). The benefit of DemP relative to unstructured pruning should be framed around speedup, not accuracy parity (R1). Why compare structured to unstructured methods at all? (R3, Q1)
  - **ImageNet results**: Table 1 lacks detail on the accuracy-efficiency frontier for DemP (R1, Q6). The authors should train the dense baseline themselves to control for code differences (R1, Q7). More DemP sparsity levels should be shown (R1).
- **Theory/analysis oversimplification**: The analysis in Section 3.2 (Brownian motion model of weights) oversimplifies the problem and is not clearly connected to DemP (R2, R3, R4). The Brownian motion focuses on individual weight behavior, not activation patterns; assumptions of constant noise may not hold in practice (R2). The theory seems redundant and not directly useful (R3). The role of noise in the experiments appears more like an amplifier than a key driver, and details on noisy updating are insufficient (R4). The analysis would benefit from explaining how the model links to activation-based pruning and DemP (R2, R3).
- **Definition and evidence of dead neurons**: The paper lacks a clear definition of "dead neuron" in convolutional neural networks (e.g., all elements of a feature map being zero) and does not discuss batch processing implications (R2). There is no direct evidence that dead neurons remain inactive throughout training (R4, citing [3] on overlap coefficient). The unit of "Dead Neurons Variation" in Fig. 3 is unclear (R4, minor).
- **Missing experiments and details**: 
  - No speedup evaluation (already noted).
  - No experiments on modern architectures beyond CNNs (R1).
  - No explanation of how DemP differs from dynamic sparse training (e.g., RigL) except at the neuron vs. weight level (R3, Q3).
  - Theory section should be adjusted to explain relevance to DemP (R3, Q4).
  - Need for more details on unstructured algorithm baselines (R1, Q5).
  - Should compare with more structured pruning baselines (R3, Q1).
- **Presentation and minor issues**: 
  - Figures should be placed near where they are discussed (e.g., Fig. 4 is two pages away) (R1).
  - Second paragraph of Section 3.1 unclear: where do the 3904 neurons come from? (R1)
  - Maxwell's demon analogy is confusing and should be cleaned up (R1).
  - Typo: "simplified version of Equation 4" should be "Equation 3" (R1).
  - Equation 5: unclear whether η is in denominator or numerator of exponent (typesetting) (R1).
  - Missing "=0" in last sentence of third-to-last paragraph on page 5 (R1).
  - Figure 7 caption incorrect (R1).
  - Reference to "Table 5" on page 8 should be "Table 1" (R1).
