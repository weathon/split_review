## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a comprehensive evaluation suite for text-to-image (T2I) model alignment, with over 100,000 annotations across four human annotation templates (Likert scale, pairwise comparisons, DSG-type scoring, etc.). Key contributions include: (1) a curated 2000-prompt set (Gecko2K) mixing 1k prompts from existing DSG and 1k new prompts; (2) a statistically grounded method for comparing T2I models; (3) a framework for systematic metric evaluation under three tasks: model ordering, pair-wise instance scoring, and point-wise instance scoring. The authors find that metrics vary across templates and settings, and propose a new auto-evaluation metric (Gecko) that uses QA/VQA with NLI-based hallucination removal and NLL-based confidence normalization. The metric is reported to correlate better with human ratings across templates and on TIFA160. Experiments are conducted using four T2I models (SD1.5, SDXL, Imagen Vermeer, Muse).

### Strengths
- Proper comparison with previous works and thorough evaluation of the proposed metric and dataset (R1).
- New benchmark dataset (Gecko2K) that highlights the issue of evaluation consistency across multiple annotation templates (R1).
- New metric for evaluating QA/VQA models that is interpretable; the approach of increasing coverage, removing hallucinations via NLI, and normalizing VLM scores is novel and effective (R1, R2).
- Rigorous and exhaustive experimental methodology, likely one of the most statistically sound evaluations of T2I models, supporting all claims (R2, R3).
- Insights and takeaways are highly useful for advancing research in both modeling and evaluating generative models (R2).
- The Gecko-2K prompt set is carefully designed; a lot of thought and analysis went into its construction, and the framework is extendable to other modality generation models (R2).
- The evaluation data (assuming public release) will be useful for future evaluation of reward models/metrics (R3).
- Well-written and easy to follow (R1).  *(Note: R2 disagrees; see Weaknesses.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Validity of the proposed metric (R1):** (a) The metric relies on NLI to detect hallucinations, so errors in the NLI model could undermine reliability. (b) Equation 2 uses NLL scores from a VQA model as a direct measure of confidence, without accounting for overconfidence (low NLL for incorrect answers) or under-confidence (high NLL for correct answers). No calibration step or adjustment is included. This could artificially inflate or deflate alignment scores.  
- **Readability and terminology (R2):** The paper is hard to read and requires multiple passes. Terms like "skills" and "reliable prompts" are used without proper introduction (first mentioned L044, not clarified until much later, e.g., Table 8 in supplementary; column names differ). The authors should define these terms in the introduction.  
- **Figure 2 is very difficult to parse (R2):** Too many rows make it hard to read. Suggestion: break prompt sets into separate figures or move reliable prompt sets to supplementary. The color scheme (red/less, green/greater, purple/equal) could be repurposed to show disagreement between templates instead.  
- **Differentiation between pairwise instance scoring and model ordering is unclear (R2):** It is not obvious how pairwise scoring (good on side-by-side comparisons) differs from predicting model ordering. If a metric ranks pairs well, wouldn’t it automatically give a total order? The paper should elaborate – e.g., whether pairwise gives a partial ordering and model ordering requires a total order, and how a metric can produce total order without exhaustive comparisons.  
- **Limited model and prompt scale (R3):** The evaluation covers only 4 T2I models on 2000 prompts. It is unclear whether scaling prompts 10× would change the conclusions or whether the current scale is already sufficient with diminishing returns.  
- **Data release status (R3):** It is not explicitly stated whether all evaluation data (annotations, images, etc.) will be publicly released. The contribution depends significantly on this release.  
- **Missing acknowledgment of concurrent work (R3):** A concurrent work on similar lines [1] should be acknowledged.  
- **Interpretation of "=" in Figure 2 (R3):** It should be clarified that "=" in a grid cell means the difference between models is not statistically significant for that evaluation protocol, even if absolute scores differ.  
- **Contrast on presentation:** While R1 found the paper well-written, R2 found it difficult to read and the terminology confusing.
