## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces an interpretable, unsupervised zero-reference framework for joint denoising and low-light enhancement, grounded in physical imaging principles and Retinex theory. The method derives a training strategy from paired sub-images with varying illumination and noise levels, uses Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) for frequency-domain decomposition in sRGB space, and employs an implicit-guided hybrid representation strategy to separate complex degradations. A retinal decomposition network guided by implicit degradation representations is proposed. Experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate effectiveness.

### Strengths
- One reviewer (rating 8) was notably more positive than the others (rating 6). That reviewer particularly praised the technical soundness of incorporating the Neighbor2Neighbor denoiser into unsupervised LLIE and appreciated the provision of code in supplementary materials.  
- The combination of Retinex theory and data-driven methods is well executed, building on the first effort of RetinexNet (Reviewer 1).  
- The frequency-domain decomposition using DCT for explicit multi-band separation is a well-used and effective technique that facilitates decomposition into illumination and reflectance maps (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- The unsupervised, zero-reference paradigm is a promising direction for real-world applications, crucial because it operates without paired or unpaired images (Reviewers 1, 5).  
- The self-supervised denoising method based on neighboring pixel masking is well-aligned with zero-reference challenges, and the creation of paired images with varying brightness enables self-supervised decomposition (Reviewers 1, 5).  
- The paper provides mathematical proofs for each network module, lending strong logical rigor (Reviewer 3).  
- Experiments are sufficient and demonstrate on multiple datasets that the proposed framework outperforms existing zero-reference methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).  
- Language adheres to English writing standards and is fluent (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing evaluations on standard independent test sets:** Reviewers 1 and 4 request results on LIME, NPE, MEF, DICM, VV (no ground truth) to fairly justify effectiveness and generalization. Reviewer 2 adds that a qualitative comparison on an independent test set is a key experiment to distinguish the method’s generalization advantage.  
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** Reviewer 2 points out that latest methods such as RetinexFormer and Retinex-Diffusion are not compared. Also, Reviewer 1 notes a missing citation of “Enhancing Visibility in Nighttime Haze Images Using Guided APSF and Gradient Adaptive Convolution.”  
- **Lack of statistical reliability indicators:** Reviewer 2 notes that Tables 1 and 2 do not provide standard deviation or confidence intervals, making the reliability and stability of results unclear.  
- **Unclear theoretical justification for DCT decomposition:** Reviewer 3 questions the basis for claiming DCT separates chromatic, semantic, edge, and noise information into distinct frequency bands (lines 175–180). While DCT separates low/high frequencies, mapping to semantic information is not standard and requires rigorous justification (correlation vs. causality). Reviewer 2 also asks what special characteristics the method brings compared to other frequency-disentanglement-based methods (citing “Unveiling Advanced Frequency Disentanglement Paradigm for Low-Light Image Enhancement,” ECCV 2024). Would different decomposition methods (e.g., wavelets) affect performance (Reviewer 3)?  
- **Inadequate loss-function analysis and missing term explanations:** Reviewer 3 notes the loss function (lines 322–333) lacks in-depth exploration – rationale for weights, sensitivity to changes, and impact of each term are not discussed. Reviewer 4 adds that several terms in equations are unexplained: term \(P_1\) in Eq. (8) is introduced without prior explanation (shape, learning, constraints, visualization); Eq. (14) contains an unexplained \(L_{reg}\) term; Eq. (15) needs clarification on the difference between first and third terms, and decomposed illumination/reflection maps should be shown to demonstrate that constraints are sufficient to learn reasonable Retinex decomposition.  
- **Over-smoothing concerns and residual noise:** Reviewer 5 notes the neighboring pixel masking strategy draws from Neighbor2Neighbor and MAE, known for over-smoothed results; differences and improvements to avoid over-smoothing need clarification. Reviewer 4 points out that in Figure 7, the method still leaves noticeable noise not seen in Neighbor2Neighbor comparative experiments, raising a potential training issue. Ablation studies on mask size, shape, and trade-off between noise reduction and detail preservation are needed (Reviewer 5).  
- **Generalization ability not explored:** Reviewer 2 requests experiments when training on low-light and testing on other exposure scenes. Reviewer 5 adds that LOL dataset has slight noise; denoising should be validated on datasets with significant noise (e.g., LSRW or other denoising datasets) with comparative analysis against state-of-the-art denoising methods.  
- **Exposure loss limitations:** Reviewer 5 notes the pre-defined exposure level (similar to Zero-DCE) may cause overexposure/color distortion in non-uniform or overexposed scenarios; an adaptive exposure adjustment strategy is recommended.  
- **Handling of edge cases:** Reviewer 1 asks how the method handles extreme noise or heavy color distortions that deviate from typical low-light patterns.  
- **Computational complexity and runtime efficiency not discussed:** Reviewer 1 finds this lacking.  
- **Presentation issues:** Reviewer 4 notes Figures 1, 2, 3, and 5 are not explained in the main text, and Figure 4 is explained but incorrectly referenced. The reference year for noise2noise (Line 150) is incorrect (should be 2018, not 1803).  
- **Visual quality comparison unclear:** Reviewer 4 observes that in Figure 6 the method shows no clear advantage over SCI, and the authors avoid explanation. Reviewer 2 states there are too few visual results overall; more real-world scenes should be provided.  
- **Insufficient mechanistic explanation:** Reviewer 2 notes the detailed mechanism of why the method works and why it outperforms previous methods is not adequately presented.
