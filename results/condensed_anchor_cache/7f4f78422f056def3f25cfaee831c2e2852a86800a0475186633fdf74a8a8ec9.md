## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Value Bonuses with Ensemble Errors (VBE), an exploration method for RL that maintains an ensemble of random action-value functions (RQFs). The key idea is to design rewards for these RQFs so that the value bonus provides first-visit optimism and can decrease to zero. The method is compared to Bootstrapped DQN, RND, and ACB on classic and Atari environments. Reviewers agree the core idea is interesting but have mixed assessments: one reviewer (score 6) is positive, citing good sample efficiency and addressing RND’s collapse; another (score 3) finds the presentation convoluted and contribution marginal; two reviewers (score 5) see promise but fault insufficient experimental validation and unclear differentiation from prior work.

### Strengths

- **Clear motivation and positioning**: The paper clearly motivates the need for value bonuses that provide first-visit optimism, discusses the relationship with Bootstrapped DQN, and correctly identifies that RND only rewards infrequent states and can collapse (reviewers 1, 3, 4). The literature discussion is sound (reviewer 3).
- **Novel core idea**: The use of ensemble errors from random action-value functions to define consistent rewards for deep exploration is interesting and novel (reviewers 1, 2, 4). The approach is a departure from conventional ensemble-based methods and worth further exploration (reviewer 2).
- **Promising empirical results**: VBE outperforms the compared baselines on several classic environments (Mountain Car, etc.) and shows faster learning in several Atari games (reviewers 1, 3). Early exploration behavior is informative (reviewer 3). The method is sample-efficient in the tested settings (reviewer 3).
- **Addresses a real limitation**: The paper correctly notes that RND requires 2 billion frames in the original paper; such sample inefficiency is undesirable for moving out of simulation (reviewer 3). VBE’s ability to allocate optimistic value estimates to unvisited state-action pairs is a key strength that reward-bonus methods lack (reviewer 4).
- **Implementation simplicity**: The method offers a straightforward implementation on top of DQN variants and can replace undirected exploration like epsilon-greedy (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper describes a simple idea in a convoluted manner (reviewer 2). The distinction between Equation 2 and the actual bonus used in Algorithm 1 is not clearly demarcated (reviewer 2). The generation and properties of Random Action-value Functions (RQFs) are not clearly explained, nor how their predictions combine to produce the final bonus (reviewer 2). Algorithm 1 does not define objectives for \(q_w\) and \(f_{w_i}\), and it is unclear whether the original reward is used when not in pure-exploration setting (reviewer 3). Minor issues: “baring the issue” → “barring the issue”, “w ereport” → “we report” (reviewer 3). The paper would benefit from intuitive explanations accompanying the math (reviewer 2).
- **Novelty and differentiation**: The contribution is marginal given existing methods (BDQN, RND, ICM, UCB-style analysis) (reviewer 1). VBE appears to be essentially a variant of classical UCB (replacing count bonus with ensemble error bonus) and a variant of RND (using ensembles) (reviewer 2). The paper does not adequately address how VBE differs from Bootstrapped DQN, which already encourages first-visit optimism (reviewer 2). Using ensemble error as an exploration bonus is not novel in itself (reviewer 2). The core issue: the bonus is still a function of visit frequency, similar to count-based methods, and it is unclear why learning a bonus as a value function provides advantage over a two-step process (reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical justification**: The claim that the bonus “goes to zero” is not unique—other UCB-style work and BDQN also have bonuses that go to zero with sufficient exploration (reviewer 1). The paper lacks a clear theoretical framework or compelling justification for why this particular bonus leads to better exploration (reviewer 2). The theory section in the main text lacks specific conclusions (reviewer 3).
- **Experimental validation is insufficient**: Experiments are too small-scale (only up to ~400k environment steps in Atari) (reviewer 3). No full-run experiments on larger Atari domains are shown in the main text; the Appendix Figure 7 does not include baselines for comparison (reviewer 3). The empirical evaluation does not sufficiently demonstrate advantage—environments are simple and gains not substantial (reviewer 1). There is no showcase on challenging sparse-reward tasks like Montezuma’s Revenge or Antmaze (reviewer 3). The worse performance on Pitfall and Gravitar in Figure 4 is not discussed (reviewer 3). The failure of RND in Mountain Car (Figure 3) is not explained, possibly due to state space simplicity causing early bonus collapse (reviewer 3). No ablation study is provided to investigate the impact of different components (reviewer 1). Experimental settings are non-standard: comparing DDQN (VBE baseline) against PPO (RND/ACB baseline) with different allowed environment steps is unfair, as DDQN is known to be more sample-efficient (reviewer 4). Concerns that the baseline implementation (RND, ACB) may not replicate original results, as the codebase is not official or widely validated (reviewer 4). Running 30 seeds instead of longer runs is questionable; more frames per run would be more informative (reviewer 4). The paper would benefit from evaluation on more challenging tasks like 3D navigation or procedurally generated environments (reviewer 4).
- **Missing details**: No explicit objective for \(q_w\) in Algorithm 1; also unclear if original reward is used when not in pure-exploration setting (reviewer 3). Multiple reviewers requested clearer algorithmic descriptions (reviewers 2, 3).
