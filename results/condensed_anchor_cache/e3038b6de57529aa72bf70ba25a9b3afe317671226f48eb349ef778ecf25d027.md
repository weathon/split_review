- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 6, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PPO+, a variant of PPO that incorporates three modifications: off-policy value function learning via a replay buffer and Q-function critic, entropy regularization, and discounting of the state distribution. The modifications are motivated to reduce hyperparameter sensitivity and align with theoretical principles. The method is evaluated on MuJoCo control tasks, claiming state-of-the-art performance among on-policy methods. Reviewers generally found the paper clearly written, but raised major concerns about novelty (all modifications are well-known), experimental scope (only 6 MuJoCo domains), missing comparisons (to off-policy methods like SAC/TD3 and to other on-policy methods like VMPO/SPO), insufficient ablation, and the lack of evidence for reduced hyperparameter sensitivity.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and explains the methodology thoroughly, with pseudocode and hyperparameter details (R1, R3, R5).
- The combination of modifications, while individually known, is practically beneficial and easy to implement (R2, R5).
- Ablation studies are well-constructed and provide insights into individual components (R4, R5).
- The paper identifies key limitations of PPO and motivates the need for a more principled approach (R4).
- It reviews relevant literature and places the approach within existing work (R4).
- One reviewer (the most positive, score 6, confidence 2) highlighted that PPO+ presents theoretically grounded modifications and achieves consistent improvements over PPO on MuJoCo, and that the ablation studies strengthen the claims.
- Another reviewer (score 5, confidence 5) noted that bridging the gap between on- and off-policy methods fills a void in the literature for fair empirical evaluations, and that stability of PPO is of high practical relevance.
- The paper’s focus on reducing implementation-level tricks and adherence to theoretical foundations is appreciated (R5).
- The paper is easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and limited contribution**: The proposed modifications are well-known in the literature and have been studied extensively; entropy regularization is already standard in most PPO implementations, discounting has been considered multiple times and not consistently proven effective, and using a Q-function critic is a natural extension (R1, R2, R3, R5). Merely combining them is not considered a significant contribution (R1). One reviewer noted that two out of three modifications are already routinely used in the literature/implementations, leaving the main contribution as the off-policy critic, which is not properly ablated (R2).
- **Insufficient experimental scope**: Evaluation is limited to only 6 MuJoCo control domains, with only two (Ant and Humanoid) considered high-dimensional by 2024 standards (R2, R4, R5). This environment diversity is insufficient to demonstrate generalizability or to claim state-of-the-art performance (R5). There is concern of overfitting to MuJoCo and potential over-tuning to this specific domain (R5). Extensions to discrete action spaces, grid-based tasks, or other benchmarks are needed (R5).
- **Missing comparisons**: Several important comparisons are absent:  
  - No comparison to state-of-the-art off-policy algorithms (SAC, TD3, MPO) even though the paper introduces off-policy components like a replay buffer and Q-learning (R1, R3). The authors themselves note that PPO+ underperforms these methods, which raises the question of why one should use the proposed combination (R1, R3).  
  - No comparison to other on-policy algorithms beyond PPO, such as VMPO (https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12238) or SPO (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07963); claims of state-of-the-art among on-policy methods are unjustified with only PPO as a baseline (R2, R5).  
  - Missing ablation of the separate backbone architecture: PPO+ uses separate networks for actor and critic, while PPO uses a shared backbone. Without an ablation comparing shared vs. separate backbones in PPO+, performance gains could stem from architectural differences rather than the proposed modifications (R5). Prior work (Andrychowicz et al., 2021) suggests separate networks generally perform better.  
  - Missing ablation of the CrossQ modifications; their effect is unclear and not controlled for (R5).  
  - Missing ablation of individual modifications in isolation, e.g., PPO with a Q-function only, PPO without discounting, etc. (R2). The current ablation (Figure 3) does not answer which modification drives improvements (R2, R3). For example, it is unclear how standard PPO with a Q-function alone performs, and the combination of on-policy and no discounting is not tested (R2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity claims unsupported**: While reducing sensitivity is a core motivation, the paper does not provide any empirical evidence that PPO+ is less sensitive to hyperparameters than PPO. The introduction of new hyperparameters (entropy coefficient, replay buffer size, number of critics) may even increase tuning difficulty (R5). The authors note that optimizing for the discounted objective increases sensitivity to the discount factor, but only present results for two discount factors; further investigation and mitigation strategies are needed (R4).
- **Contradiction with simplicity claim**: Learning a Q-function instead of a state-value function adds complexity, which contradicts the claim of increased simplicity (R3). The algorithm effectively shifts toward off-policy RL, requiring a replay buffer and Q-learning (R1, R3). Learning an action-value critic can be problematic for high-dimensional actions or large models, e.g., in RLHF or vision-based tasks (R2).
- **Experiment presentation and analysis issues**:  
  - Figures 1 and 2 show PPO failing on high-dimensional domains (Ant, Humanoid), which does not inspire confidence in the baseline implementation. It is unclear whether the asymptotic performance of PPO would be similar with longer runs (R2).  
  - The ablation study (Figure 3) lacks insight into whether PPO without discounting could be fine on-policy (the combination of on-policy and no discounting is missing) (R2).  
  - Some implementation details are missing, e.g., the specific baseline PPO implementation used, the exact benchmark environments (R1). Presentation issues include messed citations (bottom of page 3) and an unclear paragraph starting at line 294 (R1).  
- **Conclusiveness**: The experimental results are not conclusive; in some domains PPO performs better than PPO+ (R3). The method does not outperform SAC/TD3 despite incorporating similar components, and the core weakness is insufficient evidence to trust the proposed improvements (R2, R3). The paper does not demonstrate that the specific algorithmic combination is particularly useful or interesting beyond being a bag of tricks (R1).
- **Specific missing analyses that reviewer questions point to**:  
  - How does PPO+ perform across a range of hyperparameter settings compared to PPO? Does entropy regularization, number of critics, and replay buffer size make tuning harder? (R5).  
  - What is the effect of the CrossQ modifications? Is there an ablation of PPO+ without them? (R5).  
  - Could an ablation study comparing separate backbone (PPO+) vs. shared backbone be added to verify that performance gains are not due to architectural differences? (R5).  
  - Could the authors elaborate on the performance gap with off-policy methods like SAC/TD3 and discuss challenges and opportunities for bridging it within the on-policy framework? (R4).  
  - How does training time compare to PPO, especially as task dimensionality increases? (R4).  
  - How does the use of entropy bonus here differ from standard PPO implementations? (R3).  
  - In which scenarios would discounting the state distribution be beneficial? (R3).  
  - Why was only MuJoCo used? Would the authors extend tests to discrete action environments or grid-based tasks to validate generalizability? (R5).  
  - Consider using evaluation methodology like [rliable](https://github.com/google-research/rliable) for statistically robust results (suggestion from R5).  
- **Reviewer disagreement**: While one reviewer (the most positive, score 6) found the paper strong with theoretically grounded modifications and good ablation studies, and another (score 5) saw value in bridging on- and off-policy methods for fair comparisons, the majority (three reviewers with score 3) criticized the lack of novelty, limited experiments, missing comparisons, and insufficient contributions, and considered the experiments inconclusive. The paper’s core claims are not fully supported even by the more positive reviewer, who also noted the limited environment scope and missing off-policy comparisons.
