## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Ref-L4, a large-scale referring expression comprehension (REC) benchmark generated using GPT-4V, and includes an analysis of errors in existing RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg datasets. The authors reevaluate several LMMs on a cleaned version of those datasets and then benchmark 24 models on Ref-L4. The proposed dataset features 45,341 annotations, 365 object categories, long referring expressions (avg. 24.2 words), and a vocabulary of 22,813 unique words. The reviewers are in partial agreement but diverge on the overall significance. One reviewer (score 5) found the benchmark addresses the lack of sufficiently detailed referring expressions and values the comprehensive comparison. Two reviewers (scores 3) considered the contribution incremental and the connection to LMMs not strong, with concerns about dataset novelty and validation.

### Strengths
- **Large-scale dataset with LLM-based generation pipeline**: The paper proposes a sizeable REC dataset and describes the use of an LLM (GPT-4V) to generate referring expressions, which is noted as a useful contribution (R1, R3).
- **Error analysis of existing benchmarks**: The manual examination revealing 14% errors in RefCOCO, 24% in RefCOCO+, and 5% in RefCOCOg is a valuable observation (R1).
- **Extensive evaluation of many models**: The paper compiles and evaluates 24 large multimodal models on the proposed benchmark, which requires significant experimental effort and provides a useful reference for the community (R1, R2, R3).
- **Clear presentation and comparisons**: The writing is well-structured, and comparisons such as those in Table 3 help illustrate differences with existing works (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution relative to existing benchmarks**. The task form remains traditional referring expression comprehension, and many advanced datasets already exist (e.g., gRefCOCO for generalized referring segmentation, LISA for reasoning, PhraseCut for sub-object detection). The improvements over RefCOCO variants (larger size, longer descriptions, broader vocabulary) are modest—roughly twice the scale of prior work—and not considered significant enough to warrant a new benchmark (R1, R2). One reviewer (R3) did not raise this concern, but the other two reviewers found the novelty limited.
- **Weak connection to LMMs despite the title**. The core contribution is a cleaner, larger REC dataset, which is not inherently tied to “the era of LMMs.” The evaluation in Section 4 lists results without providing deeper insights about how LMMs differ from non-LLM models on this task, and the four claimed features (large scale, diverse categories, long expressions, large vocabulary) are not exclusive to LMMs; non-LLM methods can also address them. The title thus appears misleading (R1, R2).
- **Longer referring expressions are not necessarily better and may lack practical relevance**. Because GPT-4V is instructed to describe many attributes (color, size, position, context), the resulting long descriptions may actually make the task easier rather than harder (by providing more clues). Moreover, real-world referring expressions tend to be short and direct, so the dataset may not reflect performance under concise prompts (R1, R3).
- **Insufficient documentation of manual review and verification process**. Details are missing about how the LLM-generated expressions were manually validated and how the quality of annotations was assured. Figure 1 contains an example (“It is placed on the floor”) that one reviewer found imprecise, raising doubts about overall accuracy (R1, R3). The process for checking the 14%/24%/5% labeling errors is also unclear—why only test/val sets were examined and not the training set (R1).
- **Questionable statistics and comparisons**. The increase in category count (to 365) largely comes from the underlying Object365 dataset, not from the proposed dataset’s inherent design. “Instance area” is reported in absolute pixels, which is less informative than relative area compared to the image size (R1). Table 2, which shows accuracy improvement after removing erroneous samples, draws an expected conclusion and adds little insight (R1).
- **Missing discussion of related benchmarks with longer expressions**. Recent works such as Omnilabel (ICCV 2023) and Described Object Detection (NeurIPS 2023) also propose evaluation benchmarks with more instances and longer expressions. Without comparison to these, the claim that Ref-L4’s scale or description length is a differentiator appears invalid (R2).
- **Need for more examples and analysis of description length impact**. The current version includes relatively few samples to illustrate typical expression structures. A deeper discussion of how expression length influences performance—and whether longer descriptions are always preferable—would help readers assess the dataset’s utility (R3).
