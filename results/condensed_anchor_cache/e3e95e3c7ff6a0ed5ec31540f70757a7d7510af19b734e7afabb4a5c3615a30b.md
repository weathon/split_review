## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates whether LLMs can automatically discover novel and valid chemistry hypotheses given only a research background (question and/or survey). It proposes the MOOSE-Chem framework, which breaks the task into three stages: (1) retrieving inspiration papers from a large corpus, (2) generating hypotheses by combining background and inspirations, and (3) ranking hypotheses by quality. A benchmark of 51 high-impact chemistry papers (Nature/Science, 2024) is constructed, where each paper is split into background, inspirations, and hypothesis. Experiments with GPT-4o show promising results in rediscovering hypotheses, with evaluations by both experts and GPT-4o. The framework uses beam search and an evolutionary algorithm (mutation, refinement, recombination) to improve hypothesis quality.

### Strengths

- **First work** to investigate LLMs for hypothesis generation specifically in chemistry (R1). The three-step decomposition (inspiration retrieval, hypothesis generation, ranking) is novel (R1, R2).
- **Novel “mutate and recombine” trick** and evolutionary algorithm to foster diversity in hypothesis generation (R1, R2). Beam search systematic exploration is simple and powerful (R4).
- **Well-designed experiments** for each of the three research questions, with extensive comparative and ablation studies (R1, R2). Every claim on performance is backed by experiments (R3). Ablations test corpus size, window size, background survey, etc. (R1, R3).
- **Consistency** between expert evaluation and automated evaluation (MS scores) enhances reliability (R1).
- **No expensive training or fine-tuning** required (R4).
- **Clear and well-written** paper overall, guiding reader through motivation and derivation of method (R3). Some reviewers found the presentation lacking, particularly in introduction and methodology (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Small benchmark size**: Only 51 papers, insufficient to fully assess effectiveness. More samples from diverse chemical fields (Analytical, Organic, Inorganic, Environmental, Medicinal, Nuclear, interdisciplinary areas like Bioinorganic, etc.) are needed (R1). This also limits the technical contribution (R4).
- **Data and code not released**: No access to benchmark or source code; hampers reproducibility and future comparison (R3). Prompts used should be provided for reproducibility (R3).
- **Lack of formal definitions** for evolutionary operations: mutation, refinement, and recombination mechanisms are not clearly described. How are transformations applied? How is feedback used? How are hypotheses combined? (R3). Also, the probabilistic inference details in the methodology may be unnecessary (R4).
- **Evaluation bias from using same LLM**: Using GPT-4o to evaluate its own generated results may introduce bias. Recommend using different LLMs (LLaMa, Claude, Gemini) or comparing human-generated hypotheses with LLM-generated ones (R1).
- **Subjectivity of human evaluation**: Only chemistry PhD students were used; more experts should be invited and inter-rater consistency analyzed (R1).
- **Missing experiments and analysis**:
  - Ablation on mutation settings: what proportion of high-quality hypotheses are obtained without mutation? (R1 Q1)
  - Upper bound of rediscovery: original hypothesis should be ranked at top; experiment on ranking original vs generated, and analysis of cases where original is not top, is missing (R2).
  - Why smaller window size leads to better inspiration retrieval – requires explanation (R1 Q2).
  - Whether GPT-4o was used for all comparison methods – specify (R1 Q3).
  - Helpfulness of adding new significance dimension – quantify (R1 Q4).
  - Effect of corpus size on Hit ratio: appears minimal; need elaboration (R3 Q6).
  - Background survey has almost no effect on MS in Table 7; counterintuitive, needs explanation (R3 Q8).
  - Include baseline of random paper selection for inspiration retrieval (R3 Q7).
  - Clarify experimental setting in Section 5.1: what are “ground truth inspiration papers”? How are they related to the 51 benchmark papers? Why NAs in Tables 3 and 4? (R3 Q5, Q10).
  - Screen window size rounds: clarify paper count per round (Table 1); format Table 4 left-to-right (R3 Q7, Q9).
  - Formal description of P(i|b), P(h|b,i), R(h) inputs/outputs desired (R3 Q4).
  - Number of chemistry PhD students who constructed the dataset (line 134) (R3 Q11).
  - Use countermeasures for hallucination (e.g., multiple sampling with majority voting) and report variability (R3 Q12).
- **Unclear trustworthiness** of generated hypotheses: not made up from baseless hallucination? (R2). The assumption that LLMs have sufficient implicit domain knowledge is strong and may not hold for smaller models or niche areas; deeper analysis across other LLMs needed (R4). The assumption that LLMs can connect novel scientific relations without domain-specific tuning is overly bold (R4).
- **Limited distinction from prior work**: Framework resembles existing multi-agent scientific discovery systems; need clear differentiation (R4 Q1). Some reviewers (e.g., Reviewer 3) considered it a great novel contribution, while others (R2, R4) noted the technical contribution is limited given the scale.
- **Missing discussion** of limitations and future work (R3). Also, discussion of connection to Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and self-consistency methods would be beneficial (R2, R3).
- **Selection process** of the 51 papers and corresponding inspiration papers unclear: need explanation of how relevance, significance, and novelty are ensured (R4). Also, the constraint that inspiration should not be related to background is mentioned but not enforced or verified (R3 Q1).
- **Only titles and abstracts** used for retrieval; full text (especially Conclusion/Discussion) may contain important inspirations missed (R1).
- **Other papers** besides ground truth inspiration may be helpful; further evaluation (case studies on high-ranking non-I papers) needed (R1).
- **No practical validation**: Have any generated hypotheses been tested or align with ongoing research? (R4 Q2, Q3). How would this framework support a chemist beyond rediscovery? (R4 Q2).
