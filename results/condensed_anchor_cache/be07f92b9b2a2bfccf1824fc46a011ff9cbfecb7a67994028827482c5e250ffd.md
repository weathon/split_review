## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Pointwise Sliced Mutual Information (PSMI), computed from a partially trained model (after 95% loss reduction), to detect which training samples will be memorized during fine-tuning of LLMs (Mistral 7B, Llama 2 7B, Gemma 7B) on classification benchmarks (MMLU, ETHICS, ARC). The method requires only forward passes of an early checkpoint and is evaluated by FPR at TPR=0.75. Reviewers agree the work is well written and includes thorough experiments and ablations. However, there is significant disagreement: two reviewers rate the paper 5, praising its efficiency and empirical results, while two rate it 3, arguing that PSMI offers only marginal gains over simpler baselines (loss, logit gap) and that the classification-only setting limits relevance to generative LLM memorization. The higher-scoring reviewer noted the paper is effectively about classifiers, not LLM memorization.

### Strengths
- The method is simple, intuitive, and computationally cheap—only forward passes of an early checkpoint are needed (R1, R2).
- The paper is well written, with clear exposition, proper baseline comparisons, and extensive related work (R1, R3, R4).
- The ablation study effectively justifies the 95% loss-drop stopping criterion and the choice of layer for PSMI (R1, R4).
- Theoretical results support the connection between low PSMI and memorization (R2).
- The argument for evaluating memorization detectors via FPR at a fixed high TPR is convincing (R2, R4).
- The appendices are informative and thorough (R4).
- One reviewer increased their score from 3 to 5 after reading the revised manuscript, acknowledging the paper’s clarity and completeness, but noted that the focus is actually on classifiers, not LLMs (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Narrow scope for LLM memorization**: The method only applies to classification, not to generative fine-tuning or token-sequence memorization (e.g., regurgitation of private data). The title and abstract overstate the relevance to LLMs; the paper is actually about classifier memorization (R2, R3).
- **Marginal improvement over baselines**: PSMI does not significantly outperform the much simpler loss or logit gap baselines. At TPR=0.75 the gain in FPR is small, and at higher TPRs (e.g., 0.99) PSMI may be worse. The added complexity of PSMI (fitting Gaussians, sampling random projections, selecting a layer and stopping point) is not justified (R1, R3, R4).
- **Ground truth memorization measure**: Using LiRA (a membership inference attack) as ground truth is questionable. Standard memorization measures (e.g., kl-memorization from Biderman et al., edit-distance from Duan et al.) would be more appropriate and directly applicable (R1, R3).
- **Finetuning details unclear**: The paper does not specify the exact fine-tuning loss (e.g., classification head vs. language modeling loss on answers) or whether the partial model has already seen the at-risk samples, which would invalidate the early detection claim (R1, R2, R3).
- **Limited to LoRA fine-tuning**: Only LoRA is evaluated. Full fine-tuning and other PEFT methods (bitfit, soft prompts) could behave differently, as LoRA’s low-rank updates constrain representation changes. Experiments on these settings are needed to claim broad applicability (R1, R2).
- **Unrealistic training duration**: Fine-tuning for 10 epochs is atypical (many recipes use ≤1 epoch). Test loss begins increasing after epoch 1, suggesting early stopping would occur before memorization studied here becomes relevant (R3).
- **Speculative analysis in Section 3.1**: The three-phase memorization dynamics (pattern initialization, complexification, degeneration) lack evidence. Phrases like “superposition of all patterns” are vague, and the claim that hard samples are identified by non-monotonic loss is untested. The analysis is circular because “hard” is defined by low PSMI at the cut-off (R2, R3, R4).
- **Evaluation metric choice**: FPR@TPR=0.75 is arbitrary and context-dependent. The full ROC or AUC should be reported. The choice of TPR=0.75 appears to favor PSMI over baselines, raising concerns about cherry-picking (R1, R4).
- **Unfair comparison with Mahalanobis distance**: Mahalanobis distance uses activations from layer 13 while PSMI uses layer 29. The apparent superiority may be due to layer choice; a fair comparison using the same layer is required (R4).
- **Hyperparameter tuning of PSMI**: PSMI requires choosing a stopping epoch (currently 95% loss reduction) and a layer, whereas loss and logit gap require no tuning. The theoretical justification for PSMI’s zero-threshold is not practically important—practitioners can filter by amount of data to remove (R3, R4).
- **Sample ordering issue**: If a sample at risk of memorization has already been seen by the partial model used for PSMI computation, the detection may be confounded (R1).
- **Minor issues**: Language errors (e.g., tense mixing, “mutual information is null”) (R2); mischaracterization of Zhang et al. (they showed random-label memorization fails to generalize, not that it can achieve state-of-the-art generalization) (R1); Figure 4b is mostly whitespace (R3); missing reference to additional results in the appendix (R2).
