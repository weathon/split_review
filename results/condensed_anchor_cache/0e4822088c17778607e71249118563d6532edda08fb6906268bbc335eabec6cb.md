## Merged Review

### Summary
ASVD is a training-free method for LLM compression that applies SVD to weight matrices transformed using activation distribution (via a whitening matrix based on activation magnitude or Cholesky decomposition of activation covariance). It also introduces a sensitivity-based iterative calibration to allocate non-uniform ranks across layers. The method is extended to KV cache compression by reducing the channel dimension of KV activations. Empirical comparisons are made against SVD, FWSVD, and SVD-LLM, with results mainly on the LLAMA-2 family and MMLU.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and presents extensive empirical results demonstrating ASVD’s superiority over pure SVD and FWSVD. [R1, R3, R4]
- The combination of ASVD with quantization is a desirable practical direction. [R1]
- ASVD introduces a pioneering approach in SVD pruning by accounting for activation distribution, inspiring subsequent works like SVD-LLM and Palu. [R2]
- The sensitivity-based truncation rank search is an innovative method for dynamic, non-uniform rank allocation across layers. [R2]
- The method is training-free and uses activation values to rescale the weight matrix, emphasizing weight vectors with larger activation norms. [R4]
- ASVD further reduces the KV cache via low-rank decomposition. [R4]
- A minority reviewer (R3, score 8) finds the main idea clear and the experiments sufficient to show gains over pure SVD, also highlighting the added value of KV-cache compression and quantization.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to SVD-LLM and existing activation-based pruning:** R1 notes that ASVD’s whitening via Cholesky decomposition (ASVD+) is essentially the same as SVD-LLM; the layer-wise rank search, while relatively novel, is already used in other training-free LLM compression works [1,2]. R2 observes that activation-aware pruning is not new in the LLM pruning field (e.g., Wanda, RIA) and that ASVD’s concept of using the product of weights and activations diminishes novelty. [R1, R2]
- **Insufficient evaluation scope and missing benchmarks:** Only MMLU (on LLAMA-2 family) is presented as a solid dataset; other zero-shot tasks (e.g., GSM, Wino, PIQA, MathQA) are missing, despite being reported in SVD-LLM and other baselines. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Missing experiments on actual memory footprint and computation speedup:** The paper does not provide real measurements of memory savings or inference speedup, leaving efficiency claims unsubstantiated. [R2]
- **KV cache compression rationale unclear and potentially limited:** R1 questions the treatment of positional embeddings; in practice, peak running memory may not decrease, only storage. A comparison with EigenAttn [3] is requested, along with an explanation of how including the weight matrix in constructing the low-rank projection affects results. R4 notes that recomputing KV using low-rank representations may be needed. [R1, R4]
- **Compression rate lower than quantization or unstructured/semi-structured pruning:** This limits practical deployment on resource-constrained devices; the paper should acknowledge this limitation. [R2]
- **High computational cost of the sensitivity-based rank search:** Calculating perplexity for each layer at varying truncation ratios is time-consuming and may not scale to extremely large models; a detailed analysis of cost and scalability is missing. [R2]
- **Full-rank SVD doubling parameters:** R4 points out that SVD inherently stores two matrices, so pruning to 90% parameters actually removes 55% of ranks; better efficiency could be achieved on layers without activation functions (e.g., value matrix, output transformation) where 50% rank reduction gives 50% parameter reduction [1]. [R4]
- **Missing related works on SVD-based LLM compression:** R4 notes that prior work [2] learns ranks per weight matrix, which is highly relevant to Section 3.4. [R4]
- **Technical concerns about parameter counting and decomposition details:** R3 asks whether the S^{-1} matrix is counted in total parameters and whether setting S = L (Cholesky factor) is sensible for storage. R3 also requests plots of normalized singular values per layer (as in Gusak et al.) to provide further insight. [R3]
- **Inferior performance at low parameter ratios:** When the retained parameter ratio is less than 0.7, ASVD (and ASVD+) underperforms SVD-LLM, even though low ratios are more relevant for compression. [R1]
- **Minority positive view contrasts with majority concerns:** While R3 (score 8) considers the experiments sufficient, R1 (score 5) and R4 (score 6) find the contribution minor, and R2 (score 6) raises novelty and practical impact issues. This disagreement over significance is noted. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
