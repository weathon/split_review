## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Chemlactica-125M, Chemlactica-1.3B, and Chemma-2B, language models fine-tuned on a novel corpus of 110M molecules from PubChem (40B tokens). The authors introduce a molecular optimization algorithm combining genetic algorithms, rejection sampling, and prompt optimization. The models achieve state-of-the-art results on PMO (8% improvement) and other benchmarks. The training corpus, models, and algorithm are publicly released.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive dataset**: 110M molecules from PubChem with computed properties (40B tokens) (R1, R3).
- **Strong empirical performance**: SOTA on PMO with 8% improvement; property prediction experiments show strong results; Table 1 demonstrates transfer learning benefit (R1, R3, R4).
- **Thorough benchmarking**: Experiments on PMO and docking-based multi-property objectives with comparisons to strong baselines (R3, R4).
- **Innovative optimization framework**: Hybrid of LLMs, genetic algorithms, prompt optimization, and rejection sampling; token system that emulates genetic algorithms with Chain of Thought reasoning (R1, R2, R4).
- **Versatility**: High performance even with minimal fine-tuning data, showing adaptability to limited datasets (R1).
- **Open access**: Training corpus, model checkpoints, and optimization algorithm released to the community (R1, R3).
- **Detailed appendix**: Transparency around hyperparameter tuning and floating point precision (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Benefit from pre-training data vs. architecture unclear**: Reviewer 3 questions how much of the PMO performance gain comes from the larger PubChem dataset versus the specific Galactica fine-tuning. The PMO benchmark was designed with ZINC 250k pre-training; all compared models used that. Existing literature shows changing from ChEMBL to PubChem pre-training alone improves performance considerably. Ablations (fine-tuning Galactica with only ZINC 250k/ChEMBL, or pre-training comparison models with PubChem) are missing (R3).
- **Data leakage risk in benchmarks**: Reviewer 4 notes that PMO and docking benchmarks aim to rediscover known drugs, which may already exist in the PubChem training data. This could inflate performance metrics. Validation on a benchmark explicitly excluding known PubChem molecules is missing (R4).
- **Limited task selection and generalizability concerns**: Only 5 of 23 PMO tasks and 3 of 17 MoleculeNet tasks are reported in the main text (R4). ADMET prediction results in the appendix were less successful, raising questions about consistency (R4).
- **Missing comparison with most similar method**: Reviewer 4 requests direct comparison with Wang et al. 2024 ("Efficient Evolutionary Search over Chemical Space with Large Language Models"), which also applies genetic algorithms with LLMs but without fine-tuning. This would clarify the advantage of the proposed approach (R4).
- **High oracle evaluations and computational efficiency not compared**: The optimization algorithm relies on many oracle evaluations (R1). Reviewer 2 notes no comparison of overall processing times or inference cost across methods. Questions on time and memory for deployment and inference remain unanswered (R2, R3). Reviewer 3 asks for statistics on how many times fine-tuning was performed and how long it took (R3).
- **No exploration of efficient training methods**: Reviewer 2 suggests investigating freezing layers, layer skipping, or parameter-efficient fine-tuning instead of full model fine-tuning (R2).
- **Limited multi-property optimization (MPO) experiments**: Reviewer 2 requests additional experiments demonstrating LLM capabilities for MPO, and asks how performance changes if target properties are enumerated rather than produced as a product (R2).
- **No 3D conformation considered**: The model only uses SMILES representation; explicit 3D conformation is lacking (R1).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The performance of the models appears sensitive to hyperparameter choices, especially during optimization (R1). Table 9 shows high variability and unpredictable behavior when tuning for conditional generation on molecular weight; it is unclear if this behavior occurs for other properties (R3).
- **High variance in docking scores**: Figure 11 shows high variance in DRD2 docking scores even at the end of the run. Reviewer 3 expected variance to decrease as the model is fine-tuned with only the best molecules, and asks if this variability is present on other docking targets (R3).
- **Lack of novelty claimed by one reviewer**: Reviewer 1 states the molecule optimization algorithm lacks novelty (R1).
- **Missing references to recent studies**: Reviewer 1 notes missing references (R1).
- **Missing diversity metric from GEAM**: Reviewer 3 notes GEAM reports diversity with #Circles; the paper does not include this metric (R3).
- **Typo**: "10e60" noted as a mistake in number of valid candidate molecules (R3).
- **Model selection not justified**: Reviewer 4 questions why Galactica and Gemma were chosen over more widely benchmarked models like Llama or GPT, and suggests comparing performance with these (R4).
- **No experimental validation**: The paper lacks real-world experimental validation such as biological assays for generated molecules (R1).
