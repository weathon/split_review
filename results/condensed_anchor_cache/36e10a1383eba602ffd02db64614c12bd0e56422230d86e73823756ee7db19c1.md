## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GRABLI, a pre-training method that aligns a text language model (LM) with the UMLS knowledge graph via a separate GNN encoder. Biomedical mentions in PubMed abstracts are linked to UMLS, and local KG subgraphs are used as cross-modal positive samples for contrastive alignment. Evaluations are performed on QA, entity linking, and relation extraction tasks, using PubMedBERT, BioLinkBERT, and other backbones. All four reviewers note the method is clearly presented and the idea has some novelty, but the critical reviews (scores 3,3) find the empirical evidence insufficient and the gains over baselines modest or insignificant, while the more positive reviews (score 6,5) acknowledge the approach’s potential but also highlight missing analyses and reproducibility concerns.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and approach**: The specific combination of a GNN encoder and LM with contrastive alignment on local UMLS subgraphs is novel and distinct from prior works (e.g., ERNIE, DRAGON, JAKET). The local subgraph sampling reduces graph size and makes joint training tractable (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and Figure 1 is clear and informative (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Experimental coverage**: Multiple downstream tasks (QA, entity linking, relation extraction) and backbone models are tested. Several ablation studies are provided, including comparison of GNN methods (Table 3) and hyperparameter sensitivity (Table 4) (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Simplicity**: The resulting model is not task-specific and is easy to use (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority positive view**: Reviewer 1 (score 6) highlights robustness of the evaluation and the significance of integrating domain-specific knowledge, while Reviewer 2 (score 5) finds the method straightforward and sensible.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient empirical gains**: The improvements over baselines are often small and not statistically significant. For QA (Table 1), GRABLI underperforms DRAGON models, and gains over PubMedBERT/BioLinkBERT rarely exceed 1× standard deviation (needing at least 2×). The improvement on BioASQ is large for base models but negligible for BioLinkBERT-large, and standard deviations are large. For entity linking (Table 2), GRABLI does not exceed SapBERT, and SapBERT’s performance drops after GRABLI training. For relation extraction (Table 6), no strong baseline is provided, and gains are again not significant (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Missing comparisons with recent works**: The paper omits strong contemporary baselines such as BioLORD-2023, CODER, and MedCPT, and does not compare with state-of-the-art models for the evaluated tasks. SapBERT is no longer SOTA (Reviewer 3,4).
- **Complexity not justified**: The added GNN complexity does not clearly outperform a linearized graph embedding with the LM (Table 3). It is also unclear whether two separate encoders are needed (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 requests details on the number of trainable parameters added by the GNN to assess whether gains stem from larger model capacity.
- **Pretraining data and quality issues**: Only 1.5M sentences and 600K nodes are used – limitations are not discussed. The reliance on BERN2 for concept linking may introduce errors; sensitivity to linking quality is not analyzed. No discussion of data leakage between pretraining and downstream evaluation datasets (e.g., PubMedQA, NCBI) (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Reproducibility**: Code, datasets, and pretrained models are not currently available. Details on pretraining data construction and tools (BERN2) are insufficient. The method to avoid data leakage is not described (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Missing evaluation of entity representations**: The paper focuses on downstream tasks but does not evaluate the quality of the learned entity embeddings directly (e.g., link prediction) (Reviewer 3).
- **Inconsistent effectiveness**: GRABLI does not consistently improve all backbones; it hurts SapBERT and GEBERT performance, which already incorporate external knowledge (Reviewers 2,4). The MLM training loss is shown to be more important than the alignment loss (Table 4) (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational cost**: No analysis of FLOPs or training/inference cost relative to simpler alternatives like SapBERT, CODER, or BioLORD is provided (Reviewers 1,3). Scaling to larger KGs is not discussed.
- **Significance of results**: Standard deviations from multiple runs are high, and the paper does not demonstrate that improvements are statistically robust. Reducing variance would be needed to make claims convincing (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Typo**: Line 421 should say “entity linking task” instead of “QA task” (Reviewer 2).
