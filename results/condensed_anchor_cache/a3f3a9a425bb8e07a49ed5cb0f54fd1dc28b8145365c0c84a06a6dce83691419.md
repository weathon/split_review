## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ToM-agent, a framework that equips LLM-based generative agents with Theory of Mind (ToM) capabilities for open-domain conversational interactions. The agent tracks counterparts’ beliefs, desires, and intentions (BDIs) while disentangling confidence from mental states, and uses a counterfactual reflection mechanism to update inferred BDIs by comparing predicted and actual responses. Experiments on empathetic and persuasive dialogue datasets (using GPT-4 and GPT-3.5) evaluate first- and second-order ToM inference and downstream task success. Reviewers overall find the topic highly relevant and the approach promising, but raise concerns about methodological novelty, scalability, evaluation transparency, and experimental design limitations. One reviewer (score 3) is notably more critical, citing limited innovation and marginal gains relative to resource cost, while others (scores 5, 5, 6) are more positive, emphasizing originality in open-domain ToM integration and the counterfactual reflection mechanism.

### Strengths
- First work to integrate ToM reasoning into open-domain conversation, moving beyond narrow task-based settings (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Explicit modules for both first- and second-order ToM inference and counterfactual reflection to improve predicted BDIs (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Disentangling confidence from BDI representation enables dynamic updates of mental states as conversations progress (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Counterfactual reflection is innovative: it shifts from indirect mental state estimation to direct prediction through conversational utterances, enhancing transparency (Reviewer 4).
- Solid experimental results on two dialogue tasks (empathetic and persuasive) with ablation studies highlighting the role of counterfactual reflection (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Paper is well-written, well-structured, and uses clear visual aids to explain complex concepts (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Using open-domain empathetic and persuasive dialogue datasets showcases practical implementation potential (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Methodological innovation limited**: Approach largely depends on prompting LLMs to assess BDIs and confidence levels, raising scalability concerns as agent numbers and conversation length grow (Reviewer 2). Minority reviewer (score 3) highlights that the performance gains appear marginal relative to the resource-intensive approach.
- **Lack of baselines and model comparisons**: No comparison with simpler baseline updating only beliefs (instead of full BDI framework) to justify added complexity (Reviewer 2). No comparison with a wider range of ToM-capable models, including open-source LLMs such as LLaMA, limiting generalizability (Reviewers 1, 2). Experiments rely exclusively on GPT-series models (Reviewer 2).
- **Human evaluation lacks transparency**: No inter-rater agreement reported for human evaluations of first- and second-order ToM (Reviewers 1, 3). Details on annotator qualifications and specific assessment criteria are missing (Reviewer 2). Rating calibration not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Confidence and BDI usage questions**: Why is it important to disentangle confidence level from BDI if only the top BDI is selected for utterance generation? (Reviewer 3). In Figure 2, the 2nd-order ToM judgment module does not receive input from the “BDI tracking with CR” module – is this intentional? (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited depth in failure analysis**: The paper mentions suboptimal confidence in “bad” dialogue examples but does not analyze why they failed or how to improve, leaving model limitations unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental design simplifications**: Only one agent tracks the other agent’s BDIs, while the other only has self-BDI; this limits conversation types (e.g., cannot make the counterpart empathetic) (Reviewer 3). What would be the result of equipping both agents with both self- and other-BDI tracking? (Reviewer 4).
- **Evaluation metric concerns**: Human annotators evaluate similarity between predicted and true BDIs instead of using text embeddings; no inter-rater agreement reported, adding variance (Reviewer 3).
- **Capability requirements**: Counterfactual reflection shows similar performance to simple reflection in GPT-3.5 and only improves with GPT-4, suggesting the method requires strong LLM reasoning capability; unclear if open-source LLMs could benefit (Reviewer 3).
- **Generalizability to open-domain**: Application to open-domain conversations where BDIs are not explicitly defined or impractical to enumerate all possible BDI combinations for response ranking raises questions (Reviewer 4). The paper should provide a qualitative example of BDI annotations in the empathetic and persuasion datasets (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing qualitative demonstrations**: Figures 3 and 4 are ambiguous about which dialogue scenario (empathetic vs. persuasive) they represent; confidence curves per condition would provide more insight (Reviewer 4). Adding transcripts of interactions between humans and the agent would better demonstrate practical effectiveness (Reviewer 4).
