## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the paper’s attempt to improve high‑dimensional Bayesian optimization via derivative‑enhanced trust regions. Several note the novelty of using a global GP’s gradients and Hessians for local quadratic models (R2, R7). The convergence analysis is considered a positive contribution by some (R1, R3, R4, R7), but others question its practical significance, clarity, and validity of assumptions (R2, R5, R6, R7). Experimental results show strong performance with small budgets, but concerns include limited evaluation length (R4), missing comparisons (R4, R7), insufficient implementation details (R2, R5, R7), and a potential contradiction in results (R5). Clarity issues are widely noted, especially in the theory section and method description (R5, R6, R7). The paper’s soundness ratings range from 1 to 4, presentation from 1 to 3, and contributions from 1 to 3.

### Strengths
- The paper is overall clear and well‑written, with a well‑stated contribution and necessary background (R1, R4, R6; though R5, R6, R7 note clarity issues).
- The proposed method, TuRBO‑D, has a clear and good motivation: using derivatives from a global GP to build local quadratic models, which is novel and computationally beneficial compared to injecting expensive objective derivatives (R1, R2, R7).
- Theoretical analysis of convergence to a stationary point is provided; this is helpful and enhances the method’s robustness (R1, R3, R4, R7). *Note: some reviewers sharply criticize the theory (see Weaknesses).*
- Experimental results demonstrate that TuRBO‑D outperforms existing BO methods on tasks with up to 300 dimensions, including on real‑world applications, indicating better sample efficiency with a small budget (R1, R2, R3, R6; but see Weaknesses for limitations).
- The paper addresses the relevant problem of high‑dimensional BO (R6).

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical Analysis**
- The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) is weak: it guarantees only that the infimum of the gradient norm over the solution sequence is near zero, meaning only a subsequence converges; there is no guarantee that the solution at any large iteration has small gradient. Even at a basic level, the result does not seem meaningful (R2). 
- The theoretical analysis assumes noise‑less function evaluations (Algorithm 1, Eq. (2)), but the experimental evaluation allows noisy evaluations; this gap is not discussed (R7).
- The trust‑region update strategy in Algorithm 2 (theoretical) differs from the practical implementation described (Lines 190‑191); the algorithm’s performance when consistent with theory is not explored (R4, R7).
- The random subset selection of dimensions (Lines 213‑226) is not considered in the theorems (R2, R7).
- The validity of Assumptions 2, 3, and 4 under the GP prior (Assumption 1, e.g., SE or Matérn kernel) is not discussed; specifically, Assumption 3 (bounded sample path on $\mathbb{R}^D$) is not valid even with probabilistic arguments; Assumptions 2 and 4 (bounded gradient and Hessian) are not known to hold (R7).
- The theory section (Section 5) is disorganized: theorems and lemmas are presented without motivation or discussion, and proofs should be placed in the appendix (R6). The results are presented without explanation or intuition (R6). The theoretical content in the main paper lacks supporting text to help interpretation (R5).

**Methodological Gaps and Missing Justifications**
- The paper does not explain why TuRBO suffers from sample inefficiency, nor why using gradients and Hessians should improve sample efficiency (R5). 
- The choice of $g_k = \nabla \mu_k(x_k)$ and $B_k = \nabla^2 \mu_k(x_k) + \lambda \nabla^2 \sigma_k(x_k)$ (Line 177) lacks rigorous or intuitive justification; it is unclear how to tune hyperparameter $\lambda$ in practice and how it affects the convergence analysis (R1, R2).
- Using a global GP fit forgoes the heterogeneous modeling advantage of local GPs in TuRBO; if the global GP is poor, derivatives are badly informed, and the method may work poorly in high dimensions (R2). The claimed gain in sampling efficiency may be less important than maintaining heterogeneity (R2).
- The algorithm description is insufficient: no explanation of how the final point is chosen (Algorithm 1 returns a point for each trust region; the output of BO is typically a single point) (R5). 
- Trust region initialization (center points, radii) is not explained, and different strategies’ impacts are not discussed (R6). The trust region update strategy is barely described: the exact criteria for “good” or “bad” agreement, how radii are adjusted, and how parameters $\beta_1, \beta_2, \eta_1, \eta_2$ (Algorithm 2) are selected are not provided (R6).
- The decision to use second‑order derivatives (Hessians) is not justified relative to first‑order gradient‑only methods; an ablation study separating these effects is missing (R3).
- The impact of second‑order gradients on algorithm complexity is not discussed (R3). By globally fitting the GP, the method incurs a computational load as high as standard BO, which should be compared against TuRBO’s local fitting (R2). The runtime comparison with TuRBO is missing (R5). 
- The paper does not address the vanishing derivative issue of GP in high dimensions (Line 214); it is unclear whether the subset‑of‑dimensions approach is known or novel (R2).

**Experimental Limitations**
- The experiments use only a few hundred evaluations, which is far from convergence; more evaluations (e.g., a few thousand as in the original TuRBO paper) are needed to assess long‑term behavior and whether TuRBO‑D truly outperforms existing methods given sufficient budget (R4).
- An important related work on convergence of local BO using GP gradients (Wu et al., 2023) is not discussed (R7). Another strong baseline for small budgets, SAASBO (Eriksson & Jankowiak, 2021), is not compared (R4).
- The implementation details of the main benchmark TuRBO are not provided, making it unclear if the comparison is fair: how hyperparameters are set for both TuRBO and TuRBO‑D is missing (R2). Experimental settings (kernel choice, hyperparameter treatment, local optimization parameters) are inadequately described to allow reproduction (R7).
- Baselines and experiment functions (e.g., ALEBO, Griewank, Ackley) are not explained (R5).
- The bottom row of Figure 1 and Figure 2 shows that “TRBO‑B” (likely a typo for TuRBO‑D) sometimes obtains better final values than TuRBO; one reviewer (R5) interprets this as potentially invalidating the paper’s claim of sample efficiency, though with uncertainty (“maybe I am wrong”).
- The code was not provided (R3). 
- Hyperparameter $\lambda$ (controlling uncertainty in Hessian) and $M$ (number of trust regions) are not studied via ablation, making it hard to understand their contributions (R4). Sensitivity to predefined parameters is not examined (R3).
- Comparison against TuRBO applications (Eriksson et al., 2019) would be helpful (R1).

**Presentation and Clarity Issues (Multiple Reviewers)**
- The paper suffers from a lack of rigor and clarity; key concepts are insufficiently explained, notation is unclear, and justifications are missing (R5).
- The theory section is disorganized and disconnected from the rest of the paper (R6). All proofs should be moved to the appendix (R6).
- The method sections and algorithm pseudocode are stated without supporting explanation (R5).
- The description of the trust region strategy is insufficient (R6). The experimental evaluation section lacks explanation of baselines and functions (R5).
