## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents Δ-AI (Δ-amortized inference), a new algorithm for amortized inference in sparse probabilistic graphical models (PGMs). The key idea is to leverage PGM sparsity to derive a local constraint—matching the conditional distribution of a variable given its Markov blanket—that serves as a local loss for generative flow networks (GFlowNets). This enables off-policy training without instantiating all variables per update, reducing memory and time. The trained sampler recovers marginals and conditional distributions and supports inference over partial variable subsets. Experiments on synthetic PGMs and a latent variable model with MNIST show that Δ-AI outperforms vanilla GFlowNets and other amortized inference baselines. The three reviewers gave scores of 6, 6, and 8, indicating a split in overall assessment.

### Strengths

- **Novel use of local constraints in GFlowNets:** The paper introduces a principled way to leverage the Markov blanket structure of sparse PGMs for local credit assignment in amortized inference (R1, R2, R3).
- **Substantial efficiency gains:** Each training step only instantiates a single variable and its Markov blanket, resulting in negligible sampling costs, very low memory requirements, and substantially faster training compared to regular GFlowNets and other amortized methods (R1, R2, R3).
- **Sound performance in experiments:** Δ-AI performs well on synthetic PGMs and an MNIST-based latent variable model, outperforming vanilla GFlowNets and other amortized inference baselines (R1, R3).
- **Flexible inference:** The method amortizes multiple potential sampling orders into a single sampler by learning Bayesian networks with multiple Markov-equivalent structures (R3).
- **Clear writing and thorough related work:** One reviewer (R3) found the paper well written, the motivation clear, the discussion of prior work thorough, and the future directions promising.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited experimental scope:** Experiments are confined to synthetic PGMs and MNIST. One reviewer (R1) questioned how the method scales to natural images and suggested using Δ-AI within PixelVAE to test on real image data, considering the current settings too weak to show the algorithm’s full power.
- **Lack of clarity in exposition:** The core idea of local credit assignment becomes obfuscated as the paper progresses (R1). The paper is difficult to read (R2), and the connection between the Markov blanket and the local updates is not made explicit (R2). There is insufficient explanation of GFlowNets for readers unfamiliar with them (R2). One reviewer (R1) suggested rewriting portions of Section 3 to improve clarity.
- **Insufficient comparison to other methods:** The paper does not clearly compare Δ-AI to other amortized inference techniques in terms of computational cost and approximation quality, and it is unclear under what conditions Δ-AI is expected to be superior or worse (R2).
- **Missing explanation of model setup:** It is unclear why two separate representations (the factorized Markov network *p* and the inference network *q*) are used and whether *p* is provided with known parameters or if both *p* and *q* are learned (R2). This is a fundamental assumption that needs clarification.
- **Stringent assumption of known factors:** The algorithm assumes that the factors φₖ of the distribution are known, which is a strong condition; extending the framework to unknown factors is worth exploring (R3).
- **Simultaneous amortization over multiple DAG structures:** The paper claims this capability but provides insufficient explanation of how it is achieved (R3).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 8) found no significant weaknesses beyond the two points above, while the other two reviewers (scores 6) raised more critical issues—including limited experiments, lack of clarity, missing comparisons, and missing explanations—and expressed lower confidence due to unfamiliarity with parts of the work (R1, R2).
