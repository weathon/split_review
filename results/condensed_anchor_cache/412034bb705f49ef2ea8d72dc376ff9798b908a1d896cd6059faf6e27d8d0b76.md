## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a spectral contrastive regression method for improving generalization in regression tasks. It introduces two loss terms: a standard deviation loss that enforces the proportionality between feature distances and label distances (metric learning assumption), and a spectral norm loss that aligns the maximum singular values of feature matrices across domains to enable out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. Experiments are conducted on eight benchmark datasets for domain generalization in regression, including MPI3D, and show improvements over baselines.

### Strengths
- The manuscript is clear and easy to follow. (R1)
- Experiments show good results and are conducted on multiple different datasets. (R1)
- The idea is technically sound as a neat combination of different ideas to improve regression generalization. (R1)
- This work addresses an emerging issue: OOD generalization in regression tasks, which has been studied in classification but not in depth for regression. (R2)
- The proposed method measures feature-label distance proportion via a mapping function and aligns real and synthesized distributions by minimizing the difference between spectral norms of their feature representations. (R2)
- The method is quite novel, and the empirical results are promising. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern:** The proposed objective is a combination of several existing ideas, limiting technical novelty (R1). However, R3 finds the method quite novel – this disagreement is preserved.
- **Missing related works:** The paper does not cite or compare with: Yang et al. (ICML 2021) – deep imbalanced regression; Gong et al. (ICML 2022) – RankSim (which used t‑SNE to show proportional feature‑label distances); and Zha et al. (NeurIPS 2023) – Rank‑N‑Contrast. These papers also align feature‑label distances and evaluate OOD/zero‑shot generalization. The authors should avoid claiming they introduce the contrastive interdependence and should discuss differences/similarities. (R1, R3)
- **Ablation studies needed on Eq.4:** (1) Does $\mathcal{L}_{std}$ include augmented samples? (2) Since $\mathcal{L}_{mse}$ includes augmented samples, an ablation study is needed on how much improvement is introduced by using augmented samples in the MSE loss term. (R1)
- **Effect of $\beta$:** In Fig.2, $\beta$ has little to no effect on metrics across its entire range. The single svd loss can provide significant improvement and sometimes performs best, but when combined with std it shows no significant effect. The authors should provide speculation/analysis. (R1)
- **t‑SNE visualization:** Only provided for the full method. Visualization for only the spectral loss term and for the sum of spectral + std loss terms would give a fuller picture. (R1)
- **Abbreviation:** ‘FT’ (fine‑tuning) should be explained before use. (R1, R3)
- **Organization and clarity:** The paper attempts to cover too much ground; the abstract contains unnecessary details that obscure the main OOD focus. (R2)
- **Title “Spectral Contrastive Regression”:** Unclear – it does not mention OOD generalization (the main focus), and “spectral” does not directly relate to the contrastive loss used in the paper. This gives an impression of avoiding the core content. (R2)
- **Weak emphasis on contrastive learning:** The term “contrastive” appears only five times in the main text and not in the abstract. The writing feels disjointed; even after reading the std. loss, it is surprising to see the section titled “Relational Contrastive Learning.” (R2)
- **Unclear loss formulation:** In Eq.4, the third term (spectral norm alignment) – it is unclear which distribution it refers to (only real or both real and synthesized?). Equation 2 appears to be a general constraint without specifying the source of i and j from each distribution. (R2)
- **Related work section is perfunctory:** The three paragraphs lack informative content. (R2)
- **Performance pattern:** In the experiments, FT+L_std+L_svd seldom achieves the best results over all FT methods; an explanation is requested. (R3)
- **Method naming:** It would be better to give the method a descriptive name instead of “FT+L_std+L_svd”. (R3)
