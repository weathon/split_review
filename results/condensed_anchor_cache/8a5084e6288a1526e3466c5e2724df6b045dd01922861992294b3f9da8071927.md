## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies the batched best-arm identification (BAI) problem in both multi-armed bandit and linear bandit settings, aiming to minimize both sample and batch complexity. The authors propose algorithms IS‑SE and IS‑RAGE, which use adaptive grids and instance‑sensitive allocations. They achieve near‑optimal sample complexity \(\tilde{O}(\sum_i 1/\Delta_i^2)\) and break the \(\log(1/\Delta_2)\) barrier for batch complexity. Experiments compare to SE on simple instances; no linear bandit experiments are provided.

### Strengths

- The problem is practical and impactful for industry (e.g., parameter tuning) (Reviewer 1).
- Algorithms achieve sample complexity equivalent to the best non‑batched BAI (ignoring log factors) and are the first to break the \(\log(1/\Delta_2)\) batch‑complexity barrier (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Theoretical results are solid, demonstrating near‑optimal sample complexity while breaking the batch‑complexity barrier (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The introduction of the instance‑sensitive quantity \(R_I\) is novel (Reviewer 3).
- The algorithm uses precise batch‑size allocations to keep both sample and batch complexity low, offering novelty (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well‑written and easy to understand (Reviewer 2); another reviewer found parts difficult (see Weaknesses).
- The method improves over existing batched BAI results in both MAB and linear bandit settings (Reviewer 4).
- Experiments provide a comprehensive comparison with the SE algorithm (Reviewer 4); another reviewer found the experiments limited (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- The paper lacks sufficient empirical support: experiments are limited in scale, cover only simple problem instances, show roughly a 2x gain (not impressive), and no experiments are provided for the linear bandit setting (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Novelty and why the proposed algorithm is better than existing methods are not clearly stated (Reviewer 4). It is unclear whether IS‑SE offers any advantage over the original SE when the number of near‑optimal arms is limited (Reviewer 3).
- The claim of breaking the \(\log(1/\Delta_2)\) barrier is conditional: it depends on the number of arms with nearly optimal means being limited relative to the total number of arms; allocation may rapidly focus on only a few arms (Reviewer 3). Examples claim fewer batches than \(\log(1/\Delta_2)\), but it is unclear whether other algorithms also achieve that or whether it truly implies O(1) batches (Reviewer 4).
- The issue of a small gap \(\Delta_2\) is more serious in the fixed‑budget setting and has been observed in Bayesian BAI (e.g., “Exploration for Fixed‑Budget Bayesian Best Arm Identification,” arXiv:2408.04869); more related work should be considered (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is difficult to read in parts: Definition 1.1 for \(R_1\) is unclear across the three examples in Figure 1; \(\Delta_2\) is used in the abstract without definition (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The contribution reduces only a logarithmic factor in batch complexity by transferring it to sample complexity; it is hard to see an absolute impact on the field (Reviewer 2).
- No study is provided on the lower bound of sample complexity given a lower bound on batch complexity (Reviewer 1).
- The paper should compare with more baselines beyond SE (Reviewer 4).
- A question about reproducing RAGE results is raised, since RAGE has publicly available code that could be used to demonstrate improvements (Reviewer 2).
- Writing suggestions: define \(\Delta_2\) in the abstract, present more details about related work in the CL area, and use more natural language before heavy math notation in the introduction (Reviewer 1).
