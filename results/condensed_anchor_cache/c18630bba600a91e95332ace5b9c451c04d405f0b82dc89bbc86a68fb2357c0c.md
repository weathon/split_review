## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MMFNet, a model for multivariate long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) that employs multi-scale frequency decomposition using DCT and a learnable masking mechanism. Input series are segmented into three scales (e.g., segments of length 2, 24, and 720), each transformed via DCT, masked adaptively, interpolated to the desired length, inverse DCT applied, and summed. The model aims to capture both short-term fluctuations and long-term trends by filtering irrelevant frequency components at each scale. Experiments on ETT, Weather, Traffic, and Electricity datasets claim up to 6.0% MSE reduction over state-of-the-art models.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, with clear figures, a logical flow, and step-by-step explanation of the methodology (R1, R2, R3, R6).
- The motivation section correctly identifies limitations of methods like FITS (single-scale frequency decomposition) in handling non-stationary data and short-term fluctuations (R1, R2).
- Experiments cover a range of popular LTSF datasets (ETTh1, Weather, Traffic, Electricity), including both high-channel and low-channel settings (R1, R6).
- Ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of multi-scale frequency decomposition and the learnable mask (R3, R5, R6).
- The idea of decomposing time series into multi-frequency components is reasonable and empirically effective (R3, R6).
- One reviewer (R5, score 6, confidence 5) found the model design **simple, surpasses existing mainstream models**, and validated excellent long-term prediction capabilities through extended-horizon experiments. (Minority contrast: other reviewers were more cautious, scores 3–5.)
- The model shows some degree of innovation in applying multi-scale frequency decomposition in the frequency domain for LTSF (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and justification are weak**:
  - The paper lacks empirical evidence or concrete examples showing that single-scale frequency decomposition (e.g., FITS) fails to capture both short- and long-term dependencies simultaneously (R1, R2, R4). Why cannot single-scale localize specific frequency components? This claim is not substantiated (R4).
  - The advantages of multi-scale in the frequency domain over time-domain multi-scale methods (e.g., TimeMixer, PITS, FITS itself with multi-scale) are not explained. No comparison to time-domain multi-scale feature fusion is provided (R2, R5).
  - The choice of DCT over other transforms (e.g., FFT, STFT) is not justified. Missing discussion on computational complexity and potential information loss (R5). The ability of DCT to capture short-term fluctuations is not demonstrated with intuitive results or visualizations at different scales (R5).

- **Novelty is limited**:
  - Multiple reviewers consider MMFNet an incremental improvement over FITS (R3, R6). Both multi-scale analysis and frequency masking have been explored previously; the specific combination is not clearly differentiated from existing works (R2, R3).
  - One reviewer (R6) argues that directly splitting the frequency domain after FFT of the original input (without segmenting the time series) could achieve the same result, questioning the novelty of the segmentation approach. Also, a simple learnable mask with frequency interpolation may suffice, making the multi-scale decomposition unnecessary (R6).

- **Methodological concerns and missing details**:
  - The learnable mask is not truly “adaptive” because it is shared across all time series in a dataset (trained once), so different series are masked identically (R3). The paper does not clarify how the mask parameters are optimized or demonstrate consistent effectiveness across datasets (R1).
  - Missing formulas for interpolating DCT of various-length segments and merging them back (R3).
  - Specific segment numbers (2, 24, 720) are chosen arbitrarily; no ablation on sensitivity to these choices or alternative segment sizes (e.g., 4, 48) (R5). The model’s performance sensitivity to scale selection is unexamined.
  - The mask output figures in the appendix show lengths inconsistent with the described segments (mask length appears 720 even for fine-scale with segment length 2) – requires clarification (R5).
  - No explanation for ordering of operations: inverse DCT then sum vs. sum then inverse DCT (the latter would reduce complexity); this design choice needs justification (R5).

- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
  - **Missing baselines**: Lack of comparisons with TimeMixer, iTransformer, Client, and other recent models (R2, R4). Missing baseline [2] as cited in the paper (R1). No results on the National Illness dataset (R2) or the M4 dataset (which contains complex temporal variations and challenges linear models) (R6).
  - **Metric limitations**: Only MSE is reported; MAE and other standard metrics are missing (R4).
  - **Missing comparisons**: No evaluation of computational cost (GPU memory, parameter size, running time) against other models (R1, R2, R4, R6). Each segment has its own learnable mask and linear layer, so parameter count could grow significantly with more segments (R3). Training and inference times are not reported (R4).
  - **Missing ablation and analysis**: Impact of different segment number combinations on performance is not explored (R3). No comparison of “mask” vs. “low-pass filter” (R3). No results on different look-back window lengths (R3). No visualizations of predictions comparing different models (R3, R4), or features at each scale to show what information is captured (R5). No comparative figures between single-scale and multi-scale frequency transformations to illustrate the claimed benefits (R4).
  - **Robustness unclear**: The datasets used (ETTh1, Electricity, Weather) are mostly seasonal, not clearly non-stationary. Experiments on non-stationary or more complex datasets are needed to support claims (R1). The model uses a shared mask across all series, which may not adapt to non-stationarity at the individual series level (R3). The paper does not demonstrate how multi-scale decomposition inherently handles non-stationarity (R2, R4).
  - **Performance gains modest and inconsistent**: The absolute improvements over baselines are small, and standard deviations or confidence intervals are not provided (R1, R6).
  - **Univariate/multivariate confusion**: The model appears to treat each channel independently (similar to PatchTST’s channel-independent training), so it does not model inter-series correlations. If so, it is not truly “multivariate” modeling; experiments on univariate datasets (e.g., M4) are needed to verify effectiveness (R6). Explicit discussion of channel-strategy is missing (R6).

- **Other weaknesses**:
  - The paper's claim that Transformer-based models are limited primarily by computational workload is incomplete; overfitting is a more significant issue (R4).
  - The model’s applicability to anomaly detection (as FITS can) is not discussed, even though masking and interpolation might suit reconstruction tasks (R3).
