## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dynamic Diffusion Transformer (DyDiT), which dynamically adjusts computation along timestep and spatial dimensions during diffusion generation. Two core components are introduced: Timestep-wise Dynamic Width (TDW) adapts model width per timestep, and Spatial-wise Dynamic Token (SDT) reduces computation at unnecessary spatial locations. DyDiT initializes from pre-trained DiT weights and uses routers to decide which attention heads, groups, and tokens to process. Experiments on ImageNet, Food, Artbench, Cars, Birds, and text-to-image (PixArt-α) show DyDiT reduces FLOPs of DiT-XL by 51%, accelerates generation by 1.73×, and achieves FID 2.07 on ImageNet with <3% additional fine-tuning.

### Strengths
- The motivation is sound and clearly presented, supported by a well-designed teaser figure and interesting observations about loss curves and patch-wise difficulty.
- The paper is easy to follow, well-written, and includes many figures to illustrate the idea and results.
- The proposed TDW and SDT mechanisms allow dynamic adjustment of model modules, and the FLOPs-constrained loss effectively controls desired FLOPs.
- Extensive experiments and thorough ablation studies are conducted on multiple datasets (ImageNet, Food, Artbench, Cars, Birds) and DiT variants (DiT-S, DiT-XL). Comparisons include many state-of-the-art diffusion backbones.
- The method achieves significant FLOPs reduction (51%) and speed-up (1.73×) while maintaining competitive visual quality.
- The text-to-image experiments on PixArt-α demonstrate the plug-and-play nature of SDT and TDW.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty / insufficient baseline comparisons**: The model is somewhat incremental—it trains multiple routers to selectively mask MHSA heads and MLP blocks. Several reviewers note missing comparisons with state-of-the-art dynamic methods or architectural innovations: reviewers recommend including DiffiT, SiT, DiMR (architectural innovations to DiT) in Table 1, as well as dynamic methods like ITOP, SViTE, and S2ViTE. The comparison in Fig. 3 and Appendix A3 with other pruning methods is not considered convincing.
- **Hyperparameter λ sensitivity**: The hyperparameter λ (FLOPs constraint) is not chosen consistently across tables (e.g., 0.4 in Table 9, 0.5 in Table 10, 0.9 in Table 14), indicating sensitivity. A minority reviewer requests image samples for different λ values to show the FLOPs-quality trade-off, and suggests providing guidance on selecting λ.
- **Step / application limitations**: The method is built on a well-pretrained DiT model, and it is unclear if it is applicable for training from scratch. The adaptation to efficient samplers (e.g., varying sampling steps) is unclear: because the activation mask depends solely on timestep t, different samplers could use the same mask for the same t even though t represents different denoising stages. Combining DyDiT with LCM (a 4-step model) is potentially contradictory to the paper’s motivation (inspired by 250-step models); a reviewer speculates SDT plays the dominant role and the method may have weak influence on 1-/2-/4-step models. The acceleration on PixArt-α is inferior to class-to-image generation—a more in-depth analysis and sample images are requested. Also, the 512×512 results should be moved from the supplement to the main table (with other methods), as training speed is more critical at larger scales.
- **Ablation / analysis gaps**: Several questions remain unanswered: (a) Why is there a significant performance drop when using SDT only (configuration II)? (b) What is the result of using layer-skip only (i.e., removing TDW) in Table 3 III? (c) How would a baseline that incorporates the same gating module (multiplying output with original input) compare, keeping the same number of parameters? (d) In Figure 5, the first head is always activated across timesteps—is this manually set or learned? (e) Why does the paper use 100 DDPM steps in Table 12 instead of 250 like DiT, and what results would be obtained with 250 DDPM? (f) How are the results in Figure 6 obtained? (g) The authors demonstrate DyDiT’s effectiveness combined with LCM, but LCM’s 4-step regime may undermine TDW’s motivation (based on 250-step observations); further analysis is needed.
- **Implementation and presentation details missing**: Provide more implementation details, including training precision. A minor issue: Line954 “Our” should be capitalized. The authors are also asked to clarify how the “pre-define” masks in L214 benefit sampling—if masks are precomputed, they save only on attention routers, which seems a marginal saving.
- **Missing comparisons on λ range**: For DiT-XL, results for λ >0.7 are not provided.
