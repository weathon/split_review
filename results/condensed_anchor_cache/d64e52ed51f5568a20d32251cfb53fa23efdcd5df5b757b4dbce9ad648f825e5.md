## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper aims to understand why group robustness methods (group DRO, reweighting) succeed against spurious correlations, compared to ERM. Through empirical and theoretical analysis, it finds that both GRMs and ERM retain spurious information in representations, but GRMs learn a classifier more orthogonal to spurious features and produce less axis-aligned, more distributed representations. Based on these insights, a new baseline method called Group Distributionally Robust Feature Reweighting (GDRFR) is proposed, which uses group labels only during finetuning.

### Strengths
- The problem of understanding how GRMs achieve robustness is important and currently not fully understood. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4, 5)
- Formalizes and quantifies some findings of prior work (Kirichenko et al. ICLR 2023) using DCI metrics (disentanglement, completeness, informativeness), and introduces a novel perspective on disentanglement differences between ERM and GRMs. (Reviewer 1, 3, 5)
- Both empirical and theoretical analysis are provided; the logical chain is clear and systematic, with several possible mechanisms examined by comparing ERM and GRMs. (Reviewer 4 – minority higher rating)
- Experiments are executed well; Table 1 offers a worthwhile systematic comparison of methods across datasets. (Reviewer 2, 5)
- Figure 2 nicely demonstrates the difference in classifier behavior between ERM and GRMs. (Reviewer 5)
- The result in Section 4.1 (cosine similarity gradient larger for ERM) is surprising and interesting. (Reviewer 5)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and overlap with prior work** (severe, noted by four reviewers): The central observations—that GRMs primarily improve feature weighting rather than representations, that spurious features are retained in embeddings, and that GRMs yield classifiers more orthogonal to spurious features—have been largely shown by Kirichenko et al. (ICLR 2023) and Menon et al. (ICLR 2021). The paper does not sufficiently differentiate or elaborate on what is new beyond these works. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 5)
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Only two group robustness methods (reweighting and GDRO) and three datasets (CelebA, Waterbirds, MNIST-CIFAR) are considered. Findings are inconsistent across datasets (e.g., completeness differences are only ~3-5% and smaller on MNIST-CIFAR), and the setting is narrower than in the closely related work of Kirichenko et al. (Reviewer 1, 2, 5)
- **Proposed method (GDRFR) is not explained**: The paper barely describes how GDRFR works, providing only the sentence “Therefore, finetuning a classifier on features derived from ERM combining SUBG-FT and GDRO.” The method is missing a clear explanation, making the contribution hard to evaluate. It only shows gains on Waterbirds, not on other datasets. (Reviewer 1, 4, 5)
- **Formal/theoretical results are limited and not deep**: Theorem 4.1 is a straightforward consequence of reweighting the loss; it does not say whether the weight on spurious features becomes small relative to the weight on core features (which is what matters for accuracy and robustness). The theory does not address overfitting effects that are crucial to GRM success in practice (e.g., large models, ℓ₂ regularization, early stopping, or two-stage methods like deep feature reweighting). The statement of Theorem 4.1 is non‑standard—it appears to define quantities that are actually derived earlier (lines 268, 274-282). (Reviewer 2, 4)
- **Unclear or problematic definitions and notation** (multiple reviewers):
  - Groups {s0, s1, n0, n1} are abstract; no concrete example is given (e.g., waterbird example is only inferred). (Reviewer 4)
  - The “spurious vector” definition assumes that for all x in s0/n1, λ > 0, which is overly strong; a definition based on expectation would be more reasonable. (Reviewer 5)
  - The definition of GDRO (line 190) does not match the min‑max formulation of Sagawa et al. (Reviewer 5)
  - Notation confusion: L190 (ℒ_i undefined), L192 (𝕏_g undefined), L194 (FT in SUBG-FT not defined), L268 (gradient w.r.t. W^t, but t is discrete), L282 (p indexed by i but then by g). (Reviewer 3, 5)
  - Incomplete sentence on L013, “optimize to maximize” on L047. (Reviewer 3)
- **Questions that highlight methodological concerns** (included as weaknesses):
  - In Figure 1, why take SVD of representations and predict from each vector rather than directly predicting the spurious feature from the representation? (Reviewer 2, 5)
  - The condition in Remark 4.1 that the majority group’s loss is below the total average loss is not always true—it depends on whether it is as easy to achieve low loss on that group. (Reviewer 2)
  - Is a ~3-5% difference in completeness meaningful, and why is the difference smaller on MNIST-CIFAR when spurious correlations are weaker? (Reviewer 2, 5)
  - Why is the result that the last layer encodes the spurious feature described as surprising? What in the training dynamics causes that? (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Missing references**: Several relevant works are omitted, e.g., Deng et al. (NeurIPS 2023), Vasudeva et al. (TMLR 2024), Nam et al. (NeurIPS 2020), Setlur et al. (2023). (Reviewer 1)
- **Presentation issues**: Inconsistent capitalization of technical terms (“Empirical Risk Minimization” in middle of sentence), equations use excessive spacing, notation is dense and hard to follow. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
- **Minor errors/typos**: L322 may have inequality in wrong direction; CelebA data is placed in the 0.9 column in Table 1 without explanation; SUBG is never defined; the label in MNIST-CIFAR task is unclear. (Reviewer 5)

*Contrast note:* While one reviewer (Reviewer 4, rating 6) found the analysis solid and the logical chain clear, the majority of reviewers (1,2,3,5, ratings 3) rated the paper substantially lower, citing lack of novelty, limited scope, and insufficient explanation of the proposed method.
