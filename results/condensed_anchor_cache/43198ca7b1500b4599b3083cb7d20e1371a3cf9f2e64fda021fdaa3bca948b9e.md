- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 5, 5, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a Universal Speech Codec (USC) for privacy-preserving speech representation learning. USC disentangles speech into semantic tokens (content + paralinguistics, speaker-invariant) and residual acoustic tokens (speaker identity), using a DAC-based encoder-decoder with gradient reversal, semantic distillation (HuBERT), and local differential privacy (LDP). The authors evaluate reconstruction quality (STOI, MCD, PESQ, extended STOI) and privacy (linkability, singling out, k-anonymity, perceptual A/B/X tests) and compare against several codecs. They also demonstrate a voice conversion downstream application. While the topic and privacy evaluation framework are valued, all reviewers identify critical weaknesses in baselines, evaluation completeness, metric adequacy, and clarity.

### Strengths

- Addresses an important problem: privacy in speech data used for LLM training; strong motivation and good framing of disentanglement for anonymization.
- Detailed model description (architecture, losses, training) enhances reproducibility.
- Privacy evaluation metrics are novel and well-designed: linkability, singling out, k-anonymity, and perceptual A/B/X tests (especially Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- The proposed k-anonymity metric is noted as beneficial for privacy protection research.
- Background and related work review is thorough (Reviewer 5).
- The idea of separating semantic and acoustic information in a single tokenizer for anonymization is interesting (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

**Missing Baselines and Related Work**  
- No citation of any speaker anonymization study; ignores the VoicePrivacy Challenge (VPC) baselines and evaluation framework, which is the standard in the field (Reviewer 1, confidence 4).  
- FACodec and SpeechTokenizer are mentioned in related work but are not included as baselines despite being the most relevant disentangled codecs (Reviewer 2, 3, 4; reviewer 1 notes this limits novelty).  
- The claim that USC achieves “state-of-the-art speech reconstruction” is unsupported because FACodec and other SOTA codecs are not compared (Reviewer 1, 3).  
- The claim that USC is “the lowest bit-rate neural speech codec” is incorrect; WavTokenizer achieves a lower bit rate but is not cited (Reviewer 1).  
- Reviewer 2 notes that utility over SpeechTokenizer is limited; no test demonstrates retention of sentiment or prosody compared to SpeechTokenizer.

**Incomplete and Unconvincing Evaluations**  
- No subjective listening tests for reconstruction quality; claims of high-fidelity reconstruction are not acceptable without such tests (Reviewer 5, confidence 5; Reviewer 4 agrees). Objective metrics used (STOI, MCD, PESQ) are old, poorly correlated with perceived quality, and saturated for high-fidelity signals; VISQOL is the current preferred metric (Reviewer 5).  
- Statistical significance is not reported for any metric (Reviewer 5).  
- Voice conversion evaluation is incomplete: only a single qualitative example in Appendix A without audio; no quantitative metrics (e.g., speaker similarity, content accuracy) and no listening test results, even though one was mentioned (Reviewer 3, 4).  
- ABX privacy tests are missing for all baselines (e.g., SpeechTokenizer, FACodec), making the evaluation inconsistent and not directly comparable (Reviewer 3).  
- Varying speaker identity across the same utterance (as the method generates) is likely a give-away that the audio is generated; this practical flaw is not addressed (Reviewer 4).  
- No ablation of speaker reversal or semantic distillation; the paper relies on prior work ablations, but the specific combination may behave differently (Reviewer 4). Only LDP is ablated.

**Unclear or Imprecise Descriptions**  
- Section 3.2: the relationship between z^0_q and F_i (batch item index) is unclear; it should use speaker id, not batch index (Reviewer 2).  
- Section 3.4: the gradient stopping mechanism is not explained; it is unclear if it is automatically achieved by the losses (Reviewer 2).  
- Section 3.5 (differential privacy): the noise addition and projection steps are not described in the main paper; Appendix only explains W_in. The term “differential privacy” is misapplied—DP normally adds noise to gradients, not to embeddings—so the method may need renaming (Reviewer 2).  
- Equation (4) has many loss weights (hyperparameters); tuning across datasets is unclear and likely difficult (Reviewer 2).  
- The SIM metric (a key privacy measure) is described too briefly; in contrast, sections 3.5 and 4.3 are given too much space (Reviewer 5).  
- Privacy metrics (linkability, singling out) are not easy to understand at first glance; a visual demonstration would improve clarity (Reviewer 3).  
- Minor: simplify Section 3.2 (GRL is widely used); clarify why HuBERT L9 (layer 9) has no speaker-identifiable traits; include PESQ in Table 1 explicitly; fix typos (e.g., “i.e” to “i.e.” in Sec. 3.4) (Reviewer 1, 3).

**Other Weaknesses**  
- Bit rate comparison with DAC is unfair because DAC models 44.1 kHz audio while USC models 24 kHz (Reviewer 4).  
- Claims of privacy preservation should be limited to specific attributes; privacy is not fully guaranteed (Reviewer 5).  
- Some in-house datasets are used without collection details (Reviewer 3).

**Reviewer Disagreement**  
One reviewer (score 6, confidence 5) finds the problem importance and model description strong, but all five reviewers agree on the critical weaknesses in evaluation thoroughness (missing baselines, no subjective tests, incomplete metrics). Reviewer 1 (score 3) is the most negative, emphasizing the lack of novelty and ignored prior work; the other reviewers (scores 5–6) are more positive on the idea but still demand major additions.
