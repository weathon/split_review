## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DiFSD, an ego-centric fully sparse paradigm for end-to-end self-driving consisting of sparse perception, hierarchical interaction (with geometric prior via CIPV/CIPS selection), and iterative motion planner with position-level diffusion and trajectory-level denoising for uncertainty modeling. Evaluated on nuScenes open-loop benchmark, DiFSD achieves superior planning performance (e.g., 66% lower L2 error, 77% lower collision rate, 8.2× faster than UniAD) and efficiency over prior sparse methods. The method builds upon SparseDrive with optimized planning modules. Only open-loop experiments are conducted.

### Strengths
- The iterative motion planner idea makes sense, similar to TCP (Wu et al. 2022), iteratively adjusting attention with trajectory guidance (R1).
- Drawing inspiration from human drivers, the model learns to focus on the closest in-path target; Intention-Guided Geometric Attention is interesting (R2).
- The solution is solid in theory; the geometric prior through intention-guided attention is novel, and excluding dense scene representation helps speed up computation (R3).
- Ablation study and supplementary material are helpful, and experiments are extensive and detailed, supporting the effectiveness of proposed modules (R2, R3).
- Analysis and discussion guide deeper thinking about model design and performance (R3).
- The paradigm adopts an ego-centric design and sparse representation, showcasing originality (R4).
- On nuScenes, DiFSD outperforms SparseDrive especially in L2 error (R1, R4).
- The paper is well-structured and logically coherent (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Overall novelty is limited; the method combines existing modules (sparse perception from SparseDrive, etc.) with limited in-depth theoretical analysis (R1, R4).
- The claimed sparsity contribution is confused: the dense setting (DiFSD(Dense)) achieves faster inference speed (14.8 FPS vs. 10.7 FPS), suggesting dense representation obtains a better balance, and the dense results are used to calculate improvements in the abstract, undermining the sparsity argument (R1).
- Comparison in Table 1 is unfair: UniAD and VAD use different evaluation protocols as noted in PARA-Drive (Weng et al. 2024); the paper follows VAD’s protocol without transforming UniAD results, giving lower L2 error and collision rate for DiFSD. Also, the reported collision rate for SparseDrive shows a large gap from its original paper, requiring re-check (R1).
- Only open-loop nuScenes evaluation is provided; close-loop simulation is needed to prove effectiveness and avoid short-cut learning (Li et al. 2024) (R1, R2).
- The framework is built on SparseDrive with optimized planning modules, yet the worst L2 in the ablation study already surpasses SparseDrive by a large margin; the source of this gain is unclear (R2).
- Presentation issues: Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 are missing essential captions (R4); symbols on figures are not marked, making it hard to associate text with figures (R3); reference format is inconsistent (R4); the paper contains only two formulas (interactive score fusion and loss) – important sections like Uncertainty Denoising lack formulaic descriptions (R4); the paper does not sufficiently demonstrate how the method works or how much time it saves (R1).
- Missing experiments and analyses:
    - No failure case analysis, especially when predictions are opposite to the navigation command (R3).
    - No performance evaluation when the driving command is unseen during training (e.g., turning left/right or empty) (R3).
    - No latency comparison with and without interactive query selection to illustrate that module’s efficiency (R1).
    - No qualitative visualization of CIPV/CIPS selection across iterations (R1).
- Technical concerns and unresolved questions:
    - What is the relationship between agent features F_a, map features F_m, and surrounding features F_o? Are map queries involved in interactive query selection? (R1)
    - Will the path of other objects be used to calculate the geometric prior? Objects far from the ego path but closest to it might be incorrectly selected, while objects close during lane changing (far from the path) should receive more attention (R1).
    - In Figure 4, why is the ego query updated by weighted object queries? Does this mean map queries do not affect the ego query? (R1)
    - Why is batch size = 1 on A100 GPUs; is model training memory heavy? (R2)
