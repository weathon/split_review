## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Q-shaping, a framework that uses an LLM to generate heuristic domain knowledge (good/bad state-action pairs and heuristic Q-value estimates) to initialize the Q-function and policy before standard RL training. This is intended as an alternative to reward shaping for improving sample efficiency. Experiments across 20 continuous control environments report a 16.87% improvement over the best baseline and a 253.80% improvement over LLM-based reward shaping methods.

### Strengths
1. **Original and Intuitive Idea:** The approach is novel, providing a fresh perspective on incorporating LLM domain knowledge into RL by directly shaping Q-values rather than rewards. The method is simple to understand and generally applicable if structured information can be elicited from LLMs.
2. **Comprehensive Empirical Results:** The paper evaluates Q-shaping across 20 environments, reporting significant sample efficiency gains (16.87% over best baseline, 253.80% over LLM-based reward shaping). Reviewer 3 notes the experiments are well-executed.
3. **Effective LLM Utilization:** The paper demonstrates a new way to harness LLMs for guiding agent exploration, revealing new potentials for LLMs in RL applications. The number of citations and coverage of existing work is appropriate (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical Completeness and Proofs:**
   - **Theorem 1 (Contraction):** The proof appears to assume the conclusion; it provides an update formula for \(\hat{Q}\) and immediately references the Bellman optimal operator without demonstrating that the update aligns with it (Reviewer 1). It is unclear how the contraction property differs from the regular Bellman operator. The proof in Appendix B.2 (line 757) states optimality is only guaranteed if the addition of heuristic values is stopped (Reviewer 3).
   - **Theorem 2 (Lower Bound):** A lower bound is provided rather than an upper bound. The paper does not clarify sample complexity relative to other works or whether this bound is more favorable than established results (Reviewer 1).
   - **Notation Issues:** The symbol \(A^{\pi}\) (line 155) is not defined (Reviewer 3). Equation (1) uses \(D_g\) without defining it; is it \(D_{LLM} = \{G_{LLM}, B_{LLM}\}\)? (Reviewer 3). The Q-function update rule (line 181) appears to be missing a \((1-\alpha)\) coefficient and brackets, inconsistent with the appendix (line 744) (Reviewer 3).

2. **Comparison to Existing Works and Baselines:**
   - **LLM-Based Reward Shaping (Eureka, text2reward):** The paper does not clarify why the challenges of reward shaping cannot be addressed by these methods. The proposed approach appears to use the LLM to design regularization for RL (Reviewer 1).
   - **Baseline Implementation Concerns:** In 3 of 4 plots, Eureka and text2reward baselines remain flat and do not improve, which is atypical given their reported performance on similar robotic tasks. Setting the evolution round to 1 may be partially responsible, making the comparison unfair (Reviewer 2).
   - **LLM/Backbone Consistency:** The paper uses GPT-4o as the LLM and TD3 as the RL backbone for LLM-TD3. It is unclear which LLM (GPT-4, GPT-3.5?) and backbone (TD3?) were used for Eureka and text2reward, making the comparison non-uniform (Reviewer 1).
   - **High-Performance Agent Selection:** It is unclear whether the "high-performance agent selection" was applied to all baselines or only to Q-shaping. If only to Q-shaping, this introduces significant bias: the average of the best 10 agents for Q-shaping is reported while using overall averages for baselines. Additionally, the 150K extra steps (10 agents × 15K steps) taken during selection are not accounted for in sample efficiency, making the comparison unfair (Reviewer 2).
   - **Related Work Gaps:** Some LLM-assisted RL studies focusing on Q-function or value function design (e.g., “How Can LLM Guide RL? A Value-Based Approach”) are not discussed (Reviewer 1). The distinction between Q-shaping and standard Q-value initialization is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3).

3. **Experimental Design and Reporting:**
   - **Number of Seeds:** The number of runs used to generate learning curves is not specified, making confidence intervals and statistical significance unclear (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 notes curves oscillate heavily, raising doubts about conclusions.
   - **Evaluation Criteria:** The evaluation of LLM correctness (e.g., 100% correctness in assigning Q-values) is not clearly explained – how is correctness measured? (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also asks how heuristic functions are judged.
   - **Ablation Study:** The individual contributions of Q-shaping and policy-shaping components are not disentangled (Reviewer 2).
   - **LLM Prompting Details:** The environment description provided to the LLM is not included. The prompt example in the Appendix is too brief; a more comprehensive example including the output Q-function and policy function would greatly aid understanding (Reviewer 1). The number of LLM prompts per task and how outputs were filtered are missing (Reviewer 2).
   - **Generalization Assumptions:** The paper does not specify what the state representation is (e.g., images vs. low-dimensional). The method assumes clean environment code (e.g., continuous states with known structure), which may not be available for real robots or image-based tasks (Reviewer 2).
   - **Units for Figures:** Figures 4 and 6 do not specify the units for the x-axis steps (presumably millions) (Reviewer 1).

4. **Writing and Presentation:**
   - The overall writing is informal and lacking important experimental details (evaluations criteria not explained, flow not smooth) (Reviewer 2). The presentation of the idea is somewhat long-winded with inconsistent notation (Reviewer 3). Only one page is dedicated to result discussion, which is too limited for an empirical paper; some theory and notation could be deferred to the appendix (Reviewer 2).
   - **Minor Issues:** Lines 32-36 lack citations for the literature review on RL training efficiency (Reviewer 1). The term NPBRS (non-potential based reward shaping) in line 53 is introduced without citation or explanation (Reviewer 1).

5. **Significance and Generalization:**
   - In 6–7 out of 20 tasks, Q-shaping performs worse than the best baseline. While multiple factors could cause this, it raises concerns about the method’s generalization capability (Reviewer 2).
