## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Goal2FlowNets, a method that uses Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) to learn exploratory goal-conditioned policies that discover multiple nearly optimal paths to reach goals. Experiments on MiniGrid and BabyAI environments with sparse rewards and partial observations show improvements in sample efficiency, zero-shot generalization, and robustness to goal-position changes compared to PPO and SAC. Reviewers found the approach original and well-motivated, addressing the important problem of diversity in exploration. However, they identified significant weaknesses in writing clarity, missing algorithmic details, insufficient scalability evidence, overlooked related work, and a lack of theoretical and ablation analyses.

### Strengths
- **Sample efficiency**: Goal2FlowNets significantly improve sample efficiency in learning goal achievement in complex grid-world environments with partial observations and sparse rewards (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Robustness to goal changes**: The method maintains higher success rates than PPO under varying goal positions (Reviewer 1).
- **Zero-shot generalization**: Stronger zero-shot generalization to major changes in environment distributions without additional training (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Originality and motivation**: The approach is well-motivated, simple, novel, and interesting (Reviewer 2). It tackles the fundamental problem of diversity in exploration (Reviewer 1). Using GFlowNets for exploratory goal-conditioned policies is original and enables learning multiple nearly optimal paths (Reviewer 3).
- **Visualizations**: Experimental results and visualizations illustrate good properties of the method (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and clarity**: The paper is not clearly written and needs full revision. Notation is confusing (e.g., Section 4 states GFlowNet models *p(s′|s)* but the figure shows a policy *p(a|s)*; terms *P_B* and *F* are undefined). The algorithm’s training and inference processes are unclear, and pseudocode is missing (Reviewer 2, 3). Reviewer 1 also notes unanswered questions about the method’s relation to distributional RL, RL as inference, and sequence/diffusion models.
- **Missing related work**: The claim of being the first to use GFlowNets as policies in RL is incorrect. At least two prior studies apply GFlowNet to RL: *CFlowNets: Continuous Control with Generative Flow Networks* (Li et al., ICLR 2023) and *Diverse Policy Optimization for Structured Action Space* (Li et al., AAMAS 2023). This gap weakens the contribution claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Scalability and generality**: Experiments are limited to simple grid-world tasks (MiniGrid, BabyAI). It is unclear how Goal2FlowNets would scale to larger or more complex environments with richer sensory information (e.g., image-based observations, continuous action spaces). The improvements may not generalize beyond the tested domains (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). A discussion of scalability and broader applicability is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation studies**: The paper lacks ablation studies to understand the contribution of specific components, such as the role of timestep-augmentation in avoiding cycles in the underlying MDP (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical foundation**: The paper lacks a proper theoretical foundation for decision making, making it hard to contextualize within the existing RL literature (Reviewer 1). The exploration-exploitation trade-off in high-dimensional or continuous spaces is not addressed (Reviewer 1). The relationship to Max-Ent RL is not discussed; in grid worlds with equal-length trajectories, Max-Ent RL might achieve similar performance to GFlowNet (Reviewer 3, citing Bengio et al., JMLR 2023, Section 7.2).
- **Computational cost**: The computational cost of Goal2FlowNets compared to traditional RL or deep RL methods is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Reward structure**: The method is evaluated only with sparse rewards (“trajectories are rewarded only when the task is completed; no positive reward otherwise”). It is unclear how it performs with dense or shaped reward structures, which seems a significant limitation (Reviewer 1).
- **Baseline performance**: SAC performs poorly in the experiments, but theoretical links between GFlowNet and Max-Ent RL suggest that Max-Ent methods should have similar performance in grid worlds with fixed trajectory lengths. This raises questions about the experimental setup (Reviewer 3).
