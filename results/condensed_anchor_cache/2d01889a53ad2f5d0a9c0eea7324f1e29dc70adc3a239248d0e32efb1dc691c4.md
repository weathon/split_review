## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a unified high-resolution ODE (GM²-ODE) to model the dynamics of momentum-based optimization methods, uses integral quadratic constraints to derive a Lyapunov function for convergence analysis, and shows that many popular algorithms can be seen as discretizations of this ODE. It claims improved convergence guarantees for the triple momentum method (continuous-time) and the quasi-hyperbolic momentum algorithm (discrete-time) in the deterministic, smooth, strongly convex setting. Reviewer opinions are split: three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 1) find the contributions incremental and insufficient, while one reviewer (score 8, low confidence) is more positive.

### Strengths
- The unifying ODE framework (GM²-ODE) is interesting and brings together several existing HR-ODEs from the literature (R1, R4).
- The analysis improves convergence rates for some accelerated methods in the strongly convex regime, specifically for the triple momentum method and QHM (R1, R4).
- The paper is well written and the presentation is clear (R3, R4).
- The Lyapunov function design is intuitive, and the discrete-time rates derived from the continuous Lyapunov analysis recover (and in some cases enhance) known optimal rates of accelerated gradient methods (R3).
- The framework applies to many previous ODEs for accelerated methods (R3).
- The theoretical analysis is solid (R4).
- *Note:* One reviewer (R2) found no strengths at all.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty and limited impact:** The contributions are seen as incremental. The techniques (Lyapunov analysis, Grönwall lemma) are standard, and the analysis follows well-trodden paths (R1, R2, R3, R4). The improved factor for NAG is only a constant 2, and the improvement for the triple momentum method (a method not widely used) is marginal (R1). Similar minor improvements (e.g., constant from 1/2 to 2/3) are considered not significant (R3, R4). The GM²-ODE is very similar to the existing GM-ODE of Zhang et al. (2021), and deriving it from known ODEs is straightforward (R1). The paper provides no new insight on acceleration and does not lead to any novel algorithms with better practical performance (R3, R4).
- **Limited setting:** The analysis is restricted to deterministic, smooth, strongly convex functions. No consideration of non-convexity, stochasticity, or even general convex objectives (R1). The benefit of the modified ODE over Zhang et al. (2021) may be specific to the strongly convex setup (R1, Q4).
- **Writing and presentation issues:** The abstract is too general, omits the setting and precise improvements (e.g., factor, constants), and overly emphasizes generic statements (R1). The introduction lacks focus on the actual contributions and motivation for continuous-time analysis (R1). The paper is not concise; it uses space on textbook-level content rather than clearly differentiating the contributions (R1).
- **Missing assumptions and missing definitions:** Theorem 3.1 states only strong convexity but the proof relies on smoothness (L-smoothness) from Fazlyab et al. (2018); this assumption is missing (R1). The proof in Appendix B.2 uses undefined σ (R1). The curly F notation in the main text and Theorem A.1 is not introduced (R1). The constant C_QHM in Corollary 4.1.1 is not defined (R1).
- **Lack of justification and missing comparisons:** The paper does not adequately justify why the SIE discretization is preferred over others, nor does it demonstrate that the GM²-ODE is more consistent or leads to better practical outcomes than existing ODEs (R1, Q1). The claim that the Lyapunov function for GM-ODE does not achieve optimal rates needs more detailed explanation and comparison (R1). The "phase space representation" claim about NAG recovery (page 7) is ambiguous as to which discretization is assumed (R1, Q2). The paper does not explain whether the upper bound in Theorem 4.1 matches known constants for that setting (R1, Q5).
- **No discussion of generalizability or practical implications:** There is no discussion of whether the framework can yield better rates in convex (non-strongly convex) settings or other setups (R1, Q4). The understanding does not lead to new approaches with stronger practical performance, and the observed faster convergence of NAG over QHM (Figure 2) is not explained by the ODE viewpoint (R4, Q2, Q3).
- **Negativity from a minority reviewer:** One reviewer (R2, score 1, high confidence) found the paper to have essentially no novelty and questioned where the new parts beyond current research are, implying the whole work is derivative. This stands in contrast to the more positive reviewer (R3, score 8, low confidence) who nonetheless still found the contributions insufficiently significant.
