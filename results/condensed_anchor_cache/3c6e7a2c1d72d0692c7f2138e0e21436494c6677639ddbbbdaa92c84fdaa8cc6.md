## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses reporting bias in vision-language datasets by proposing BiAug, a bimodal augmentation framework that decouples object-attribute associations. BiAug uses an LLM, a grounding object detector (GroundingDINO), and an inpainting model (Stable Diffusion inpainting) to generate synthetic image-text pairs and hard negatives. Experiments compare CLIP and CLIP-ft baselines on object-attribute understanding benchmarks (ARO) and zero-shot retrieval (MSCOCO, Flickr30K). Reviewers’ scores range from 5 to 8, with one reviewer (R3) strongly positive (8, confidence 3) and three others moderately negative to neutral (5–6). The main disagreement concerns clarity, completeness, and experimental rigor.

### Strengths

- Reporting bias is recognized as an important, timely problem that affects VLM understanding and has implications for AI fairness. (R1, R4)
- BiAug’s idea of using several foundation models to generate synthetic and negative samples for object-attribute decoupling is non-trivial and sound in pipeline design. (R1, R2, R4)
- Experiments on the ARO dataset demonstrate effectiveness over two CLIP baselines, with a clear improvement trend as dataset size increases, indicating scalability. (R2)
- The paper’s motivation is significant; the rationale for employing large models (LLM, GroundingDINO, inpainter) is nuanced and supports the innovation. (R3)
- One reviewer (R3) praised the writing and organization as excellent and highly accessible, contrasting with two other reviewers (R1, R4) who rated presentation poor/fair. (minority positive view)
- BiAug may help reduce cultural and linguistic biases in VLMs, contributing to AI fairness. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- The framework is overly complicated and built on fragile assumptions: e.g., can the grounding detector correctly identify objects when color or shape changes? Are detected objects large enough for the inpainting model to process? This complexity may hinder generalization and reliability at scale. (R1)
- Many key concepts (e.g., “grounding object”) are not clearly defined. The paper is hard to read, especially the abstract and introduction, which use “reporting bias” without establishing it as a technical term. (R1)
- No numerical or intuitive quality measurement of the foundation models’ outputs: e.g., box quality in step 2 of Figure 2, or the quality of guessed potential objects in step 1. (R2)
- The claimed improvement on general retrieval benchmarks (MSCOCO, Flickr30K) is not solid: in Figure 5 tables (c) and (d), BiAug and CLIP-ft perform very closely on the 40k subset, and adding more examples shows no clear improvement over either baseline. (R2)
- Only two baselines are compared (CLIP and CLIP-ft). It is unclear whether BiAug can benefit other VL models like NegCLIP (Yuksekgonul et al., 2022), which is designed for hard negatives. (R2)
- The method currently modifies only one attribute of one object per augmentation. Should consider modifying multiple attributes or multiple objects simultaneously. (R3)
- Has the author explored more detailed attributes beyond conventional ones (color, shape, material), e.g., as in the reference [1] provided by LLMs? (R3)
- Broader experiments and more visualization results are advisable, especially on the Attribute Recognition Games (ARG) task. (R3)
- The meaning of the abscissa in Figure 5 is unclear; the subcaption needs adjustment. (R3)
- The paper lacks a thorough discussion of limitations and future directions. (R3)
- Many design choices, data curation steps, training details, and inference details are missing—only empirical results are given, which is insufficient. (R4) — *e.g., what are the specifics of CLIP-ft (fine-tuning on augmented data)? (R4 question)*
- No bias analysis is provided, yet bias is central to the introduction. (R4)
- No ablation or comparison using different key tools (LLM, detector/grounder, image generator) is performed, even though tool choice heavily affects generation quality. (R4)
- Lacking more solid analyses linking the augmented data to the observed bias and task performance. (R4)
- Reviewer questions that highlight missing experiments or criticism:  
  - Where do samples in Figure 1 come from? (real or generated?)  
  - In Figure 2 step 3, why is “tennis ball” dropped and “dog” selected as the grounding object?  
  - In Section 3.2, how are categories selected from the preset list? E.g., why is “shape” not selected for “grass”?  
  - In Section 3.3, how is the detected object masked?  
  - In Figure 3, what if none of the predicted objects fall inside the image?  
  - In Figure 4 (“blue boat” and “red boat”), what was the missing reference object? Why was boat already in the original caption?  
  - What is “other” in the preset attribute list?  
  - Should the problem be discussed from a causal perspective (counterfactual samples)?  
  - Any user study to assess image generation quality, prompt quality, or rationality of V-L relations?  
  - More details about CLIP-ft: is it simply fine-tuning CLIP on the augmented data? (R4)
- Font color in the paper is hard to read (prompt). (R4)
