## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Progressive Dataset Distillation (PDD), which synthesizes multiple small sets of synthetic images in stages, each conditioned on previous sets, and trains a model on the cumulative union. The method aims to capture training dynamics better than using a single synthetic subset. Experiments show improvements over baselines like IDC and MTT, but comparisons with state-of-the-art DREAM are missing and performance lags behind DREAM at IPC-10.

### Strengths
- The idea of progressive dataset distillation is interesting and novel: it captures training dynamics, reduces the complexity of synthesizing large datasets, and can be applied to slimmable dataset condensation (Slimmable Dataset Condensation, CVPR 2023). (R1)
- The writing is clear and coherent, with a well-structured motivation and sufficient explanation of comparisons. (R2)
- The experimental results are promising and effectively illustrate the efficacy of the proposed solution. (R3)
- As far as my knowledge extends, this is the first instance of synthesizing multiple small sets of distilled images with a conditioning mechanism to minimize bias and ensure training stability. (R3)
- The method can be applied to several dataset distillation methods and shows marginal improvement. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and detail issues (R1):**  
  - Fig. 1 does not clearly indicate the IPC used at each stage, nor the cumulative effect of progressive distillation. It is unclear how accuracy is measured across stages with varying amounts of synthetic data. The figure should explicitly show performance per stage and how IPC accumulates.  
  - The modification to baselines for PDD is unclear. For IDC, it is not clear how periodic random re-initialization is handled in the progressive setting (weights transferred or fully re-initialized at each stage). For MTT, it is unclear how checkpoints from the original dataset are used to initialize the network at each stage. A more precise description is needed.

- **Practical value limitations (R1):**  
  The method forces a multi-stage training regime on downstream use, introducing many hyperparameters, especially for cross-architecture settings, and reduces elegance. Performance gains are not significant in most cases, limiting practical value. The dependency on a specific training regime reduces flexibility and generalizability of distilled datasets. The paper should discuss these implications.

- **Weak evidence for discarding easy examples (R1):**  
  Table 6 shows marginal performance differences between using all examples and discarding easy examples, especially in later stages. More compelling evidence or detailed analysis is needed to justify this design choice.

- **Missing comparison with DREAM (R2):**  
  DREAM is the state-of-the-art in dataset distillation. The paper claims PDD can be applied to any dataset distillation method, so experiments augmenting DREAM with PDD are necessary. The paper should cite and compare with DREAM.

- **Performance lag behind DREAM at IPC-10 (R2):**  
  On CIFAR-10 IPC-10, DREAM achieves 69.4% accuracy, while PDD achieves only 67.9%. On CIFAR-100 IPC-10, DREAM achieves 46.8%, while PDD achieves only 45.8%. Why does PDD perform worse at IPC-10?

- **Simplicity and potential improvements (R2):**  
  The idea is straightforward. Is it possible to update the previous frozen subset with a small learning rate?

- **(Minority: R3 has no weaknesses. R2 is more critical, R1 is moderately critical.)**

- **Request for additional experiments (R3):**  
  Provide additional experimental results about "DM+PDD".
