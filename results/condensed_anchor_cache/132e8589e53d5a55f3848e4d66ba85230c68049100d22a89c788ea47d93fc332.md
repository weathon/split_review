## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Lyapunov dynamical systems theory to analyze the stability, robustness, and convergence of Predictive Coding Networks (PCNs). It proves Lyapunov stability, convergence to equilibrium, and robustness to bounded random perturbations. It shows that PC weight updates approximate quasi-Newton methods and provides theoretical bounds comparing PC to backpropagation (BP) and target propagation (TP), claiming PC is more stable and efficient. Numerical experiments on MNIST, fashion-MNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100 with a custom convolutional network support some claims.

### Strengths
- The use of dynamical systems and Lyapunov theory to analyze PC is interesting and novel (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper provides a solid theoretical foundation for PC, offering insights into its advantages over BP and TP (R2).
- The inference phase theorems (3.1–3.3) and the quasi-Newton approximation theorems (3.7, 3.8) are relevant and sound (R3).
- Numerical experiments are performed with relevant models and datasets and support some of the theoretical claims (R3).
- The writing is clear, error-free, and the natural-language reformulation of theorems makes the paper more accessible (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- A major concern raised by two reviewers (R3, R4) is that all analyses rely on a continuous-time deterministic approximation of the discrete update rules without justification. The actual PCN training uses SGD (random data selection); the dynamics are stochastic. The paper only proves convergence for the continuous-time deterministic counterpart, not for the actual discrete stochastic process. This is a significant gap requiring substantial work (R3, R4). Specific issues: overshoot may prevent V(x) from decreasing in discrete updates (R4); the fixed point x* depends on input and target, so each weight update changes x* and introduces noise; the mapping to a deterministic gradient flow needs hypotheses on learning rate and minibatch size that are not provided (R3).
- The paper implicitly assumes a single minimum x*/W*, which may not hold (R4).
- Theorems 3.1–3.6 are repetitive and convey similar results; the exposition would benefit from grouping or combining them under a common set of assumptions (R1, R2). The scope of each theorem (inference vs. training phase) is not clear until the appendix (R3).
- The paper lacks a proper literature review and fails to contextualize the results, making it hard to evaluate novelty (R1).
- The quasi-Newton analysis (Section 4) compares a modified PC update (not Eq. 4) to quasi-Newton, rather than Eq. 4 itself; therefore the claim that PC is closer to quasi-Newton than TP is not supported (R4). Additionally, the discussion does not address how the quasi-Newton nature relates to the stability of the actual training (R4).
- The accuracy of PC appears worse than TP in Figure 2, which contradicts Theorem 3.9; no explanation is provided (R1).
- No runtime comparison between PC and BP, and no discussion of when PC is preferred over BP (R2).
- The interaction between the two minimizations (inference over x and learning over W) is not addressed; argmin is not continuous, and perturbation in initial W may significantly impact the final result (R4).
- Robustness is characterized in the appendix as Lipschitz continuity with respect to input data, but robustness with respect to x is not addressed (R4).
- The assumptions for Theorem 3.6 (bounded perturbations) are not justified as realistic (R1).
- Definition 2.1 uses “stability” while Definition 2.7 uses “Lyapunov stability”; the authors should use consistent terminology and provide references for definitions (R1).
- Figure 3 has illegible labels/axes and text overlap (R1, R4); Table 1 lacks bold highlighting of the best test error (R3).
- The expansion in Theorem 3.1 to third order lacks justification (R4).
- The purpose of plots C and D in each figure is unclear (R2).
- The paper should clarify the training protocol: are Eq. (3) and (4) alternated with random training points? (R3). The free energy function should be explicitly written as F(x, x_0, x_L, W) to clarify which arguments are fixed in each theorem (R3).
- There is no clear distinction between Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 (R3).
- Typo in Section 2.1: “notably used to be formally prove the theorems” (R3).

Note: Reviewers 1 and 2 (score 5) are generally positive about the theoretical contributions but acknowledge presentation issues and redundancy. Reviewers 3 and 4 (score 3) are more critical, especially regarding the deterministic approximation and the strength of the theoretical support.
