## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes REVIP, a visual prompting method that extracts pixel-wise contextual information using panoptic segmentation and OCR models and embeds it as a spatial embedding map into MLLMs. The approach is designed to inject fine-grained external knowledge directly into the visual feature space, unlike existing methods that convert such knowledge into text prompts. REVIP can be integrated into various MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, Mipha-3B) and shows consistent performance improvement across 9–11 benchmarks.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel perspective: converting local contextual information (panoptic masks, OCR boxes) into visual tokens instead of text-based coordinate prompts. This differentiates it from prior work that adds region coordinates as text.
- The proposed method is simple and effective, showing consistent gains across multiple MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5, Mipha) and benchmarks.
- Comprehensive ablation experiments and analysis validate the effectiveness of the design choices (e.g., feature addition vs. concatenation in Table 1).
- The paper is clearly written; Figures 1 and 2 help explain the method and motivation.
- REVIP is easy to accommodate into existing MLLMs, requiring minimal architectural changes.

### Weaknesses
- The idea of using panoptic segmentation masks and OCR bounding boxes to enhance visual prompts is straightforward, and the overall novelty is limited.
- The method bears similarity to existing visual encoder augmentation works (e.g., DeepSeek-VL using multiple visual encoders) and visual prompting approaches (e.g., Set-of-Mark Prompting for visual grounding in GPT-4V). These should be discussed and compared to clarify the technical differences.
- Only limited comparisons to other visual instruction tuning paradigms are provided (only region cropping is considered). Missing comparisons to text-based coordinate representation schemes (e.g., LLaVA, VisionLLM, MiniGPT-v2).
- Performance heavily depends on the accuracy of panoptic segmentation and OCR models. The effect of using different segmentation/OCR methods on auxiliary visual prompt quality and downstream MLLM performance is not evaluated.
- Sensitivity to mask quality is not discussed: how does the quality of panoptic segmentation masks influence MLLM performance? Similarly, how does the model decide whether to use segmentation or OCR during inference?
- The method is only applied during supervised fine-tuning (SFT). It is unclear whether similar gains would be obtained if the auxiliary visual prompts were also used during pre-training, which would require generating them for all training images.
- Experiments are limited to natural images. Applicability to other domains (e.g., remote sensing, document images) is not explored.
- The claim that REVIP improves fine-grained comprehension (counting, localization) is not directly validated: most benchmarks are VQA, and there is no evaluation on referring expression comprehension/segmentation (REC/RES) tasks.
- Inherent limitations of panoptic segmentation masks: the vocabulary is pre-defined, making it hard to scale to new categories or domains; granularity is fixed (e.g., cannot handle finer-grained cases like human/object parts).
- In practical inference, the method seems to require the user to provide both the image and its panoptic segmentation, which is unreasonable. Additionally, the extra segmentation model increases computational complexity in data preparation.
- The handling of complex scenes with overlapping or ambiguous masks is not discussed. Why not apply the method to standard image recognition tasks (e.g., classification) to demonstrate broader potential?
- Figure 3 does not indicate which parts are trained or fixed during fine-tuning. Table 6 contains two figures in incorrect order (also noted by Reviewer 4).
- Technical questions remain: In the feature fusion, are embeddings concatenated along dimension dv or Nv? If concatenated along dv, why does the paper state that "the total number of image tokens remains unchanged"? Table 1 shows feature addition outperforms feature concatenation; this requires explanation since concatenation is more general. Also, the bolding in Table 7 (e.g., Qwen-VL on VQAT) does not seem to indicate the optimal value—what does the bolding mean?
- It is worth exploring whether other forms of external knowledge (e.g., depth maps) could further enhance MLLM capabilities.
