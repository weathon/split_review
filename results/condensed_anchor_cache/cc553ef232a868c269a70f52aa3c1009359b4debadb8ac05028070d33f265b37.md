## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MS-Diffusion, a layout-guided zero-shot multi-subject image personalization framework. It uses a Grounded Resampler (with entity word-embedding and bounding-box query initialization) and Multi-Subject Cross-Attention (masked cross-attention to prevent information leakage between subjects and backgrounds). A large-scale private dataset (3.6M videos yielding two views per subject) is used for training SDXL. Experiments show improved fidelity over baselines, but the novelty and margin of improvement are disputed.

### Strengths
- **First layout-guided zero-shot multi-subject personalization framework**: consolidates multi-subject accommodation, zero-shot capability, layout guidance, and frozen base model parameters (R1, R4, R5).
- **High-quality generation with layout control**: impressive qualitative results for both single- and multi-subject personalization, including complex interactions such as “virtual try-on” and spatial relationships (R1, R2). Alleviates the problem of combining natural objects in two‑thematic scenarios (R5).
- **Novel grounding resampler**: provides a performance boost over previous baselines through initialization with word embeddings and bounding‑box coordinates (R2). Decouples texture and position control (R4).
- **Clear and well‑organized writing**: motivation and framework diagram are easy to follow (R1, R4, R5). Experiments are sufficient (R4).
- **Data collection from video**: a novel approach to obtain paired training data (ref. image vs. GT image) (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of key components**: Local cross‑attention with masked attention is a common technique in layout‑guided generation (Subject Diffusion, GLIGEN); the paper does not differentiate its approach adequately nor justify why it is novel (R2, R4). The grounding resampler’s feature extraction (learnable query tokens) is also incremental (R4). The multi‑subject cross‑attention extends prior work (e.g., ELITE) but the differences are not highlighted (R3).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: Missing comparison with Subject Diffusion, GLIGEN, and the concurrent Identity Decoupling paper [1] (R2, R5). The choice of baselines appears arbitrary; a rigorous evaluation including methods with spatial control is needed (R2).
- **Marginal performance improvement**: In Table 2, the proposed method (trained on SDXL) shows nearly identical scores to SSR‑Encoder (trained on SD1.5) except for a higher CLIP‑T score (R2). CLIP‑I improvements are not significant (R5). The boost may come from SDXL’s multiple text encoders rather than the proposed architecture (R2). Gains in image quality and text alignment are limited given the framework complexity (R4).
- **Lack of ablations on critical design choices**: 
  * Data collection from video: no controlled experiment comparing video‑derived data vs. static image pairs to isolate its benefit (R3).
  * Grounding resampler initialization: unclear how much localization capability comes from word‑embedding+bounding‑box initialization vs. random initialization or using only one modality; details of bounding‑box feature encoding and learnability of queries are missing (R3).
  * Masked cross‑attention construction: insufficient step‑by‑step explanation of how attention masks are built, how queries/keys/values are handled, and the mathematical formulation (R3). The origin of image features injected into cross‑attention is not explicitly clarified (R3).
- **Missing results for >2 subjects**: no qualitative or quantitative examples with more than two subjects (R5). Missing results for scenes combining people and anime characters (R5).
- **Insufficient detail on key metrics**: The M‑DINO metric calculation is not fully explained; it is unclear how the product of multi‑subject DINO scores captures subject neglect, and what thresholds are used (R1).
- **Missing dataset statistics**: Percentage of multi‑subject samples beyond trivial cases (e.g., person+clothes) not reported (R3). Computational complexity (inference time, memory overhead) vs. baseline zero‑shot models not reported (R3).
- **Minor writing issues**: Unclear sentence in Section 2.2 (“few zero-shot multi-subject personalized models”); transpose notation missing for K in attention equations (R1). In Figure 2, “subject overcontrol” is mentioned but not further discussed or addressed (R2).
- **Qualitative examples are limited**: Only one interesting spatial/interaction case (virtual try‑on) shown; samples with spatial relations (on‑top) or object interactions (playing tennis) are missing (R3). Results in Figure 4/5 may be affected by detail loss from the CLIP image encoder (R5).
- **Labeling ambiguity**: It is unclear if results in Figure 4 come from zero‑shot or fine‑tuned models (R3).
