## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SPiT, a framework that replaces regular grid patch tokenization in Vision Transformers with online superpixel-based tokenization. Using a GPU-based superpixel method derived from “SuperPixel Hierarchy” with an additional regularization term, the model groups pixels hierarchically and extracts color, texture, and positional-histogram features for each superpixel. These tokens then feed a standard ViT (Small/Base) backbone. Evaluated on image classification and interpretability metrics, SPiT achieves competitive accuracy while providing more faithful attention maps than ViT and a random Voronoi baseline. However, concerns about limited novelty, reliance on handcrafted features, missing computational efficiency analysis, and insufficient baselines weaken the contribution.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and formulation** – Replacing rigid square patches with superpixels is a meaningful and straightforward idea. The paper provides a formal definition and proves that conventional ViT is a special case of SPiT (Proposition 1, 2). Writing is clear and structured. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Improved interpretability** – Superpixel tokens yield significantly better faithfulness metrics (e.g., multiple interpretability frameworks) and finer-grained attention visualizations compared to grid-based ViT. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Reproducibility** – Training details and hyperparameters are clearly provided in the appendix. (R4)
- **Extensive experiments and ablations** – The paper includes experiments on multiple datasets, comparisons with ViT and random Voronoi baselines, and several interpretability metrics. (R2, R3)
- **High originality and potential** (minority Reviewer 3, score 8) – The approach is seen as a highly promising starting point that could inspire further research in irregular tokenization for transformers. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** – Previous works [1,2] already combined superpixels with Transformers. More critically, SPiT relies on handcrafted, low-level features (color, gradient) for superpixel generation, which are **static and not learned**. This can cause permanent errors from the first layer, unlike methods [1,2] that use dynamically learned features. This likely explains the consistent accuracy drop in Table 1, especially on datasets with high intra-class variability. (R1)
- **Unconvincing classification results** – The paper itself admits “the results are not significant enough to warrant any clear benefit for any framework in particular on classification tasks.” SPiT often underperforms the ViT baseline. (R2) *Minority contrast: Reviewer 3 finds the experimental validation thorough, but acknowledges gaps in efficiency analysis.*
- **Missing runtime and computational efficiency analysis** – The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison of **total pipeline inference time** (superpixel generation + transformer) against ViT and RViT. Only superpixel generation time is briefly shown in Table 4; the overhead of superpixel extraction during **training** is not addressed. There is no breakdown of FLOPs or memory usage. The variable number of tokens per image also complicates batched execution (discussed only briefly). (R2, R3, R4)
- **Unclear token dynamics across layers** – It is not specified whether superpixel tokens remain fixed throughout the network or are hierarchically merged/refined. The paper lacks explanation of how hierarchical feature representation is handled. (R2)
- **Insufficient exploration of hierarchy level T** – The determination of T (set to 4 to match ViT-B/16 token count) is not justified. There are no ablation experiments varying T to show its effect on token granularity, number of tokens, computational cost, and accuracy. The variance in token count across images (and impact on stability) is not analyzed. (R3)
- **Limited baselines and comparisons** – The only non-ViT baseline is a random Voronoi tokenization (RViT). The paper should compare with other methods that also aim for flexible/input-dependent tokenization or improved interpretability, such as:
  - Super Token Sampling (Huang et al., CVPR 2023) – another superpixel-in-ViT method.
  - DINO (Caron et al., CVPR 2021) for fine-grained interpretability.
  - Token merging (ToME, Bolya et al., ICLR 2023) and dynamic/mixed-scale tokenization (Ronen et al., Havtorn et al.) that also address grid rigidity and efficiency.
  - Conventional superpixel methods like SLIC (which also use handcrafted features). (R1, R4)
- **Incomplete related work section** – Discussion of related tokenization improvements is very short; it does not adequately position SPiT against the above methods in terms of computational cost, feature quality, or interpretability. (R3, R4)
- **Practicality concerns** – Square patching is trivially efficient and allows easy position encoding transfer (e.g., FlexiViT). SPiT requires an extra, non-differentiable superpixel generation step per image; its practicality for large-scale or real-time deployment is unclear. (R4)
- **Incomplete sentence** – “We hope that our work inspires more research into the” ends abruptly (page 9). (R2)
- **Unclear significance of Propositions 1 and 2** – The notion of “equivalence” is not clearly defined; the proof only shows matching input/output dimensions, not functional equivalence. The conclusion of Section 2.4 is unclear. (R4)
- **Missing evaluation on dense prediction tasks** – Given the structure-preserving nature of superpixels, segmentation or detection would be more natural testbeds. Classification alone may not showcase the method’s strengths. (R4)
- **Questions about tokenization process** – Is the online tokenization differentiable? (R1) How does SPiT compare with mixed-scale tokenization approaches [1,2] that also mitigate grid rigidity? (R3)
