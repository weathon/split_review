## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes *Activation Addition (ActAdd)*, an inference-time method that modifies LLM intermediate activations during the forward pass to steer output properties (sentiment, topic, toxicity). It computes a *steering vector* as the activation difference between a contrastive prompt pair (e.g., “Love” vs. “Hate”) and adds it at a chosen layer. Experiments on sentiment shift, topic control, and detoxification use models including OPT, GPT‑2, LLaMA‑2, and LLaMA‑3. The method is lightweight (no gradient optimization, single prompt pair) and claims to preserve off‑target performance.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Activation engineering as perturbing activations during the forward pass is an important, intuitive concept. ActAdd’s steering-vector addition is original (with proper citation of concurrent work). (R1, R4)
- **Lightweight & inference‑time**: No gradient-based optimization is required; the method can be applied without modifying model weights, thus preserving performance on unrelated tasks. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Clear writing & well‑presented algorithm**: The paper is easy to follow and the algorithm is straightforward. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Compelling examples**: ActAdd successfully steers sentiment, topic, and reduces toxicity across multiple model architectures. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Convincing on included baselines**: Results show ActAdd outperforms some baselines (e.g., PREADD) on toxicity and sentiment tasks. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Outdated paper & novelty concerns** (R4, minority: R1/R3 consider it original): The paper refers to works from 2023 (Liu et al., Zou et al.) as “contemporary,” suggesting resubmission without major revisions. R4 notes that Zou et al. actually outperforms ActAdd on one task, and Liu et al. only cite ActAdd as related work. This undermines the claim that those works are based on the presented method. (R4)
- **Outdated models and baselines**: Most experiments use OPT, GPT‑2 XL, and LLaMA‑2; LLaMA‑3 runs lack baseline comparisons. Baselines like FUDGE and PREADD have been surpassed by newer white‑box transformation methods (e.g., Liu et al. 2023, Zou et al. 2023) that are not included. (R2, R4)
- **Inconsistent baselines and experimental setups**: 
    - In toxicity experiments, unsteered OPT perplexity differs substantially from the cited baseline paper (49.9 vs. 8.9) and toxicity differs (0.152 vs. 0.134), indicating different setups that cast doubt on ActAdd’s claimed fluency gains. (R2)
    - Perplexity for ActAdd models is measured with Davinci 002, while PREADD uses Davinci 001, making comparisons invalid. (R4)
    - Hyperparameters (top_p, temperature) appear to differ between ActAdd and baselines; non‑critical hyperparameters should be consistent. (R4)
- **Small, anecdotal benchmarks**: Only ~10% of the test splits for RealToxicityPrompts and Sentiment are used. The paper would benefit from larger benchmarks for steering/alignment (e.g., HelpSteer) and comparisons to alignment training. (R2)
- **Insufficient evaluation of general knowledge preservation**: ConceptNet tests only single‑word answers; longer‑form factuality (e.g., FactScore) is not assessed. (R2)
- **Redundant experiments**: Sections 4.1 and 4.2 largely confirm the method works; Tables 3 and 4 present similar findings. (R4)
- **Basic metrics insufficient**: Perplexity and cosine similarity do not fully capture fluency/relevance; human or LLM-based evaluation of outputs in Tables 3/4 is needed. (R4)
- **Omission of FUDGE in reporting**: Lines 378–380 omit FUDGE despite it performing better on some aspects and only slightly worse on others. (R4)
- **Missing related work comparisons**: The paper does not discuss the computational cost of computing the steering vector (requires partial forward passes for each layer) compared to methods that derive vectors from the model’s (un)embedding matrix (Sakarvadia et al., 2023). The layer‑specific nature of ActAdd vectors is a drawback; the paper should contrast with layer‑agnostic alternatives (Sakarvadia et al.) and discuss the need for costly layer search. (R3)
- **Code and reproducibility issues**: The provided code lacks scripts to reproduce experiments; the included repository link (playground.ipynb) is not anonymized, violating double‑blind review. (R2, R4)
- **Algorithm clarity concerns**: Notation in Algorithm 1 (e.g., `@`) is ambiguous. (R1)
- **Questions on hyperparameter and example selection** (overlapping with weaknesses):
    - Grid search for coefficient *c* and layer *l* is not fully described; it is unclear whether the same search was performed for baselines. (R1, R4)
    - Variance across steering vectors derived from different example pairs is not studied. (R1)
    - The hyperparameter “a” in Appendix C is unexplained. (R4)
    - Figure 3’s “P(steered completion contains wedding related words)” needs more explicit definition. (R1)
    - Figure 2’s x‑axis is unclear. (R1)
    - p‑values in Tables 3/4 lack explicit null/alternative hypotheses. (R1)
    - Figure 5’s “off‑target answer probabilities” is misleading (actually P@K). (R1)
- **Unsupported claim about composition**: The paper claims ActAdd can compose multiple traits (e.g., eloquence and math) but provides no experiments. (R1)
- **Unconvincing knowledge preservation experiment**: Section 4.5 should use recent open‑form reasoning benchmarks (GSM8k, MMLU‑Pro) instead of perplexity. (R4)

**Reviewer disagreement summary**: Two reviewers (R2, R4, scores 3,3) find the paper outdated, insufficiently novel, and with flawed or incomplete experiments. One reviewer (R1, score 8) considers the method original, significant, and well‑executed (though with clarity issues). Another (R3, score 6) is positive about the lightweight, weight‑preserving property but highlights missing related work and computational cost concerns.
