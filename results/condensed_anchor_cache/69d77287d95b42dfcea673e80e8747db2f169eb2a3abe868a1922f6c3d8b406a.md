## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CVLD, a framework for debiasing vision-language models (VLMs) using counterfactual image-text pairs. It uses an off-the-shelf LLM to generate a text prompt and then a text-based image editor (e.g., InstructPix2Pix) to modify the image according to the flipped protected attribute (e.g., male→female). The resulting counterfactuals are used to fine-tune a VLM (BLIP family) via three task-specific strategies (data augmentation for classification and captioning, modified contrastive loss for retrieval). Evaluation on image classification, retrieval, and captioning tasks shows improvements in fairness metrics while maintaining task performance, but only on gender bias.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and timely problem: bias mitigation in foundation models (R1, R3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).
- The use of existing LLMs and text-based image editors to generate counterfactuals is a smart and practical approach (R2).
- The method is grounded in formal definitions from the counterfactual fairness literature (R3).
- Evaluated across multiple tasks (classification, retrieval, captioning) with several fairness metrics (R1, R3, R4).
- Fine-tuning with counterfactuals shows a good trade-off between reducing bias and maintaining task performance (R2, R4).
- **Novelty (R1):** The method CVLD is novel as a debiasing approach, though other reviewers disagree (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Gender bias only:** Experiments focus exclusively on gender bias, despite the paper mentioning racial bias multiple times. This severely limits the assessment of generalizability. Missing experiments on other social biases (race, age) and non-social biases (e.g., water-land in Waterbirds, object co-occurrence, scene composition) (R1, R2).
- **Limited novelty / straightforward combination of tools:** The method is essentially an application of existing tools (LLMs + InstructPix2Pix) without developing new techniques for bias mitigation (R2). Moreover, a similar triplet-based counterfactual objective was already introduced by Agarwal et al. for graph learning (R4). *Contrast:* R1 considers the method novel.
- **Counterfactual quality and robustness:**
  - Generated counterfactual images are often noisy and do not reliably reflect the flipped attribute (e.g., Figure 3 shows no woman riding the rowboat after editing), raising doubts about whether debiasing is due to the attribute change or just noise (R4).
  - No quantitative evaluation of the data generation pipeline (realism, degree of attribute change, alignment between text and image) (R3).
  - Relies on external models (LLM, text-based editor) that may introduce or amplify other biases, as exemplified by the Bias-to-Text phenomenon (R1); this vulnerability is not discussed (R1, R2).
  - Very few sample counterfactuals shown, even in the supplementary (R2).
  - No analysis of how the amount of synthetic data affects debiasing performance (e.g., diminishing returns) (R3).
- **Unified claim is unsupported:** The fine-tuning procedures differ substantially per task (data augmentation for classification/captioning, contrastive loss modification for retrieval), so the framework is not truly unified or task-agnostic (R3). It is unclear how it would generalize to other tasks such as counting or object detection (R3).
- **No comparison with existing debiasing methods:** The paper does not benchmark against other VLM debiasing techniques (e.g., adversarial training, re-weighting), making it impossible to evaluate relative performance (R2).
- **Experimental presentation issues:**
  - Metrics are non-standard and it is not always clear whether higher or lower values indicate improvement (R3).
  - Tables are confusing: baselines (BLIP-PT, ResNet-50) and CVLD variants are in separate sections, and bolded numbers are not clearly explained, hindering comparability (R3).
  - Claim of “striking effectiveness” is overstated given the moderate improvements in fairness metrics (R3).
- **Data-intensive and computationally expensive:** The approach requires generating a counterfactual for every image-text pair and then fine-tuning the VLM, which is costly (R4). No comparison of computational cost with methods that do not rely on data generation (R3).
- **Clarity issues:** Figure 1 is not informative; the sketch and lock/unlock symbols are hard to parse; “M→F” is not explained (R1).
- **Missing analysis on hyperparameter λ:** The impact of the weighting hyperparameter on performance and fairness is not shown (R3).
- **Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms:**
  - How does the method handle multiple intersecting biases (e.g., gender and race simultaneously)? (R2)
  - The counterfactuals only modify the existing image but do not generate diverse versions; could diversity further improve debiasing? (R2)
  - If biases are not quantifiable via a binary attribute (e.g., spurious correlations like “camels in deserts”), does generating counterfactuals solve them? (R2)
