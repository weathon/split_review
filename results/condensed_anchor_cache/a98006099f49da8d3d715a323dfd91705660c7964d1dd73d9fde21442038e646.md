## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Phantom, a framework for backdoor poisoning attacks on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. The attack injects a single malicious document into the knowledge base, optimized in two stages: (1) retrieval-stage optimization ensures the document is retrieved only when a specific trigger token sequence appears in the query, using HotFlip; (2) generation-stage optimization crafts adversarial text to induce target behaviors (refusal, biased opinion, harmful behavior, passage exfiltration), using a multi-coordinate gradient (MCG) variant of GCG. Experiments cover multiple LLMs (Gemma, Vicuna, Llama), transfer to GPT-3.5/4, and a black-box attack on NVIDIA’s Chat with RTX.

### Strengths
- The experiments are extensive, covering multiple attack objectives, LLM architectures, and datasets.
- The security of RAG systems is an important and timely topic.
- The attack is demonstrated against a commercial black-box RAG system (NVIDIA Chat with RTX) and achieves various objectives.
- The optimization objectives are well formulated and clearly explained, and the empirical results look promising.
- The paper is well-written and well-organized.

### Weaknesses
1. **Threat model lacks clarity and practicality.**  
   - The attacker’s capabilities are inconsistent: Line 153 claims no knowledge of other documents is needed, yet the MCG technique (lines 274–275) requires access to top-k passages retrieved for m user queries (implying knowledge of the database). Appendix A.3.5 uses a consistent dataset (MS MARCO) for both optimization and attack, reinforcing this assumption.  
   - The scenario of local file system deployment is confusing; it is hard to justify how an attacker can insert malicious content into private user databases (e.g., spam emails are typically filtered beforehand).  
   - The assumption that the retriever’s embedding is accessible is strong; a totally black-box setting (both retriever and generator) needs discussion or empirical evaluation.

2. **Lacks comprehensive baselines and comparisons with prior work.**  
   - No comparison with prior work on retrieval-specific trigger documents (e.g., [1]) for Retrieval Failure Rate, nor for attack success rates against suffix generation methods (e.g., [2], which uses bi-level optimization for generation and retrieval).  
   - Even though the threat model is new, straightforward baselines should be included (e.g., the method from [1] can pre-define semantics of retrieved text to manipulate LLM output, though not optimized for generation).  
   - A comparison is needed to show the superiority of the proposed method.

3. **Limited technical novelty.**  
   - The two-stage framework is based on existing algorithms (HotFlip and GCG). The MCG variant differs only by minor modifications (lines 7–8 of the algorithm description), and the reasons for its improvement over GCG are not explained.  
   - The gap in technical contribution is not significant (Reviewers 1 and 2, and implicitly Reviewer 3).

4. **Loss modeling in MCG is problematic.**  
   - Equation 2 assumes only one passage is retrieved, but RAG systems typically retrieve multiple passages for context.  
   - Table 9 in Appendix A.2.26 shows that the position of \(S_{gen}\) strongly affects efficacy (prefixes outperform suffixes). In multi-passage settings, there is no guarantee the malicious passage is top-1; the paper does not justify why a string optimized on a single passage remains effective when multiple passages are concatenated.

5. **Missing experiments with diverse retrievers.**  
   - Only Contriever is used for end-to-end attack; no results for DPR, BGE, REALM, or ORQA (only retrieval failure rates are reported for DPR in the appendix).  
   - The high retrieval failure rates observed for DPR and Contriever-MS (a version fine-tuned on MS MARCO) suggest limited practicality. Explanation for these high failure rates, especially the discrepancy between Contriever and Contriever-MS, is needed.

6. **Attack on Chat RTX raises reliability concerns.**  
   - Given the poor transferability between retrievers noted in lines 1105–1107, the successful attack on NVIDIA’s black-box Chat RTX using a Contriever-based trigger is perplexing.  
   - The specific success rates achieved against Chat RTX should be reported.

7. **Imperceptibility and detectability of the injected content.**  
   - The optimized sequences \(S_{ret}\) and \(S_{gen}\) are likely non-meaningful strings with high perplexity, making them easy to detect (especially as their length increases). Appendices of extra query content (\(S_{ret}, S_{gen}, S_{cmd}\)) further reduce robustness.  
   - Case studies of backdoored content (examples) should be provided.

8. **Backdoored content proportion not specified.**  
   - The fraction of the database that consists of injected documents is not reported in the experiments. This proportion likely influences both retrieval rate and final success rate; an ablation study is needed.
