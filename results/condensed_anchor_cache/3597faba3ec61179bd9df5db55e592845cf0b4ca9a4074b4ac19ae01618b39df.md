## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a three-factor matrix factorization \(X \approx UDV^\top\) (and symmetric \(UDU^\top\)) with norm-ball constraints on \(U\) and \(V\) and a diagonal factor \(D\). It claims that this formulation exhibits a stronger implicit bias toward exactly low-rank solutions than the standard \(UV^\top\) factorization (called BM), with weaker dependence on initialization scale and learning rate. The idea is extended to neural networks by constrained layers and a diagonal component, and evaluated on a synthetic matrix sensing problem, small regression/classification datasets (MNIST, tabular UCI data), and transfer learning scenarios. Reviewers find the paper clearly written but raise major concerns about limited novelty, lack of theoretical explanation, insufficient empirical evaluation, missing comparisons with related methods, and questionable formulation constraints.

### Strengths

- **Well-written and clear**: The paper is pleasant to read, the introduction and setting are well established, and the main idea is conveyed straightforwardly.
- **Interesting empirical observation**: The proposed \(UDV^\top\) factorization is demonstrated to enjoy an implicit bias toward low-rank solutions even for relatively large initializations and learning rates, which is promising and motivates further theoretical study.
- **Promising effect**: Figure 1 shows singular values decaying very fast for the \(UDV^\top\) method, indicating a clear preference for low-rank solutions.
- **Novelty (with divergence among reviewers)**: One reviewer (Score 5) finds the idea simple, straightforward, and effective; another (Score 3) considers the approach novel (though based on SVD + projected gradient descent) and notes that the extension to neural networks is new. However, several reviewers question the novelty, arguing that the formulation is essentially depth-3 matrix factorization with constraints and that the low-rank effect can be explained by existing depth theory.

### Weaknesses

#### 1. Insufficient novelty and limited contribution
- The \(UDV^\top\) model is a depth-3 matrix factorization with spectral normalization on \(U,V\) and a diagonal constraint on \(D\). Prior work (e.g., [1905.13655], [2011.13772], [2406.04112]) shows that increased depth in matrix factorization suppresses residual singular values, leading to stronger low-rank bias. The paper does not compare with deeper factorizations (depth > 3) or discuss how the proposed model differs.
- Proposition 4.2 of [1] already explains improved compression using a three-factor decomposition with a Frobenius norm penalty, and the proof generalizes to diagonal \(D\). The paper does not clarify how it advances beyond [1] or discuss trade-offs between constraint and penalty formulations.
- The claim about “divergent dynamics” is not supported by experiments; the cited paper [2005.06398] only shows divergent behavior for matrix completion, not matrix sensing.
- The architecture for neural networks is simply a three-layer network with spectral normalization and a diagonal middle layer; one reviewer finds nothing new in this contribution.
- SVD-based pruning has been proposed in prior work (e.g., [1], [2], [3]), but the paper does not compare with these or other pruning/compression methods.

#### 2. Complete lack of theoretical analysis
- No theoretical insight is provided into why the \(UDV^\top\) factorization enjoys a stronger low-rank bias or why it depends less on learning rate and initialization.
- No analysis of gradient flow/dynamics, convergence rates, optimality conditions, or rank guarantees is given, despite the availability of analogous analyses for two-factor factorization (e.g., Li et al., 2020 [GLRL]).
- The paper does not discuss whether the observed low-rank bias is a direct consequence of the explicit constraints or arises from the underlying optimization dynamics (e.g., depth effect). Even an informal explanation of the mechanism is absent.
- The roles of the explicit constraints (norm balls, diagonal \(D\)) are not motivated theoretically. Why these constraints lead to low-rank solutions is not explained.

#### 3. Limited and weak empirical evaluation
- Experiments are conducted only on small-scale, non-standard, or outdated datasets (MNIST, a few tabular regression datasets). Larger-scale vision tasks (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) are missing.
- Comparisons with state-of-the-art compression or pruning methods are absent. There are no baselines such as weight decay on the \(UV^\top\) formulation (which has been proposed as a pruning strategy, e.g., openreview link) or standard pruning benchmarks (e.g., [2002.03231], [2402.19262]).
- For neural networks, the proposed method only compresses the last linear classifier head, leaving the base network uncompressed; its utility as a general pruning method is questionable.
- Results in Appendix A1 show that the BM algorithm obtains lower residual than the proposed method at the cost of higher rank, suggesting that the constrained loss landscape may exclude good minimizers. The paper does not explain this performance gap.
- In the transfer learning setting, the results do not convincingly show that the \(UDV^\top\) factorization outperforms a standard matrix factorization or a simple two-layer ReLU head.

#### 4. Formal and formulation issues
- The constraint \(\|U\|_F \le \alpha\) is redundant because scaling \(U\) down and \(D\) up leaves the product unchanged, making optimization problem (4) ill‑defined. If the constraint is meant to influence gradient descent updates, the paper should revise the presentation to avoid this redundancy.
- The paper does not compare with related factorizations (e.g., \(LDL^\top\) decomposition). Differences, advantages, and computational complexity relative to other decompositions are not discussed.

#### 5. Missing discussion of related theory and experiments
- No discussion of Li et al. (2020), which connects the gradient flow of \(UU^\top\) to greedy low-rank learning (GLRL). Since \(UDV^\top\) can be viewed as depth-3 GLRL, the paper should address whether that theory applies. Specifically, what happens if \(r = d\) (full-rank factor) – does the stronger low-rank bias persist? What happens when the constraints are relaxed (e.g., large \(\alpha\))?
- The effect of the rank of \(X\) on recovery quality is not explored; e.g., in Figure 1, how does varying \(r\) from 3 to 20 change the results?
- No loss landscape analysis is provided to justify the choice of constraints.

#### 6. Reviewer disagreement on significance (minority positive view)
- One reviewer (Score 5) considers the empirical phenomenon promising and the idea simple and straightforward, noting that it raises interesting follow-up questions. This reviewer finds the paper well-organized and the effect in Figure 1 compelling. However, other reviewers uniformly rate the paper lower (Score 3) and emphasize the gaps in novelty, theory, and evaluation. The minority voice underscores the potential, but the majority sees insufficient contribution and execution.
