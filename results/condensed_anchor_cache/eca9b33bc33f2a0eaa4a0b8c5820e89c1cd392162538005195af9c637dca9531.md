## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces grounded video caption generation (GROC), a task uniting video captioning with spatio-temporal object grounding. It contributes: (1) a manually annotated test dataset (GROC) with 1,100 clips; (2) an automatic annotation pipeline that uses GLaMM for frame-level grounding and an LLM to produce temporally consistent video-level captions and track objects by language on a randomly selected subset of HowTo100M, resulting in the 50k-video HowToGround training dataset; (3) a model, VideoGLaMM, which augments the image-level GLaMM with spatio-temporal adapters, a bounding box decoder, and a temporal objectness head, trained on HowToGround; (4) state-of-the-art results against still-image grounding baselines and ablations showing the importance of key components.

### Strengths
- The GROC task and the manually verified test set (1,100 clips) are important for advancing spatio-temporal grounding, with applications in video retrieval, open-world tracking, human-robot interaction, and embodied perception.
- The automated annotation pipeline uses an LLM to produce temporally consistent captions from frame-level GLaMM annotations, reducing manual cost.
- VideoGLaMM incorporates spatio-temporal adapters, a bounding box decoder, and a temporal objectness head, and ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of these components.
- The released HowToGround training dataset and GROC evaluation set could benefit the field if ethics concerns are addressed.
- The paper is clearly written (noted by one reviewer; multiple other reviewers disagree on writing quality).

### Weaknesses
- **Over-claim of task novelty.** The claim that GROC is a new task is rejected by multiple reviewers. Prior work: (a) “Grounded Video Description” (Zhou et al., CVPR 2019) frames the same task – generating video descriptions with object grounding – and is cited only in the appendix, not discussed in the main text or in the claims of novelty. (b) PG-Video-LLaVA also performs pixel grounding in videos with LLMs. (c) Ma et al. (ECCV 2020), Ma et al. (CVPR 2018), and Jiang et al. (AAAI 2024) are directly relevant and were not properly cited or compared. One reviewer called for a **complete reframing** of the contributions to properly acknowledge prior work and avoid overstating novelty.
- **Insufficient model comparisons.** The paper compares only against still-image caption+grounding baselines. Missing comparisons with: (a) InternVL and Qwen-VL (which output bounding boxes in text format); (b) PG-Video-LLaVA; (c) simple visual prompting (feeding frames with grounding boxes into a video LLM); (d) cascaded video captioning + object detection; (e) other VLM bbox methods such as MAIRA-2 and MedRG. No justification is given for why GLaMM was chosen over these alternatives.
- **Missing ablation and justification for dataset creation.** The paper does not ablate choices in the automatic pipeline: (a) using SAM-2 or an open-world detector instead of GLaMM; (b) omitting Step 3 (SVO triplet extraction); (c) varying train-set size. The effects of these choices on final model performance are unknown. Similarly, the advantage of including grounding information over plain video captioning is not isolated – an ablation that removes the grounding video encoder is needed.
- **Data pipeline limitations.** (a) Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) extraction from frame-level captions can omit important details (e.g., shirt color in Fig. 2), limiting downstream tasks like finding videos of orange shirts. (b) Since there is no alignment between SVOs across frames, two different instances with the same label (e.g., two people both wearing white and drinking beer) can be merged by the LLM into a single instance, and “tracking by language” (using only noun phrase text) cannot disambiguate them. (c) The pipeline captures only a portion of the video content, potentially missing concurrent events. (d) For videos with multiple events (e.g., chopping then cooking), the pipeline may prematurely assign bounding boxes to objects not yet interacting (e.g., pot before it is used). (e) No mechanism for rejecting ambiguous annotations is reported.
- **Temporal interaction evaluation missing.** The dataset and model may struggle with actions that require temporal understanding (e.g., forwards vs backwards walking); no quantitative evaluation on such actions is provided.
- **Lacks evaluation of generalization.** The model is only tested on the GROC test set (drawn from HowToCaption / instructional videos); generalization to other video domains is not shown.
- **Multiple reviewers questioned the choice of visual encoders.** Why are two different image backbones used for captioning and bounding box generation? Is there a specific reason?
- **No comparison with SAM-2.** SAM-2 is a natural alternative for video object tracking and was not considered in the model design or as a comparison.
- **Writing quality issues.** The introduction reads like a related-work section rather than a clear statement of the task. Redundancies, awkward phrasing (“Differently than the image-based task…”), poor paragraph transitions, and a broken reference (“Sec. ??” on L230, also L140, L262) are noted. Figures (e.g., Fig. 4 case studies) are considered overly simplistic by one reviewer.
- **One reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) found the model innovative and ablations effective, but this positive assessment is outweighed by the critical concerns above regarding novelty, comparisons, and dataset construction.**
