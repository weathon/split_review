## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SOTOPIA, an interactive, open-ended environment for simulating complex social interactions between agents (LLM-based or human) in diverse scenarios involving coordination, collaboration, exchange, and competition to achieve social goals. It also proposes SOTOPIA-EVAL, a multi-dimensional evaluation framework that scores interactions along dimensions such as GOAL, BEL, REL, KNO, SEC, SOC, FIN. Experiments compare several LLMs (including GPT-4) and humans, finding that GPT-4 performs best among models but still significantly behind humans on a subset of hard scenarios (SOTOPIA-hard) in terms of goal completion, social commonsense reasoning, and strategic communication. The paper also evaluates GPT-4’s ability to serve as an automated evaluator, finding partial alignment with human judgments.

### Strengths
- Provides an interactive, dialogue-based platform for evaluating social intelligence, unlike passive benchmarks (R1, R2). Includes characters, relationships, and scenarios in episodic flow (R1).
- Diverse task space through automatic generation of scenarios, goals, characters, relationships, and policies (R2). Enables evaluation from multiple dimensions beyond goal completion (R2).
- Involves human participants in role-playing, allowing direct comparison between AI and human social intelligence (R2).
- One reviewer (R3) rated the paper 8 (Accept) and praised it as very well-motivated, clearly written, well-organized, and rather complete, with careful design to promote diversity along several axes and a well-defined evaluation protocol. R3 highlighted the framework’s ease of extension (e.g., to multi-player, more dimensions) and considered the study of GPT-4 as an evaluator (though limited) a valuable methodology for measuring progress.
- The paper consistently answers reader questions, indicating strong structure (R3).
- The evaluation dimensions (GOAL, BEL, REL, KNO, SEC, SOC, FIN) can be useful for future social agent evaluations (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Potential bias from GPT-4 in generation and evaluation**: All scenarios and constraints are generated by prompting GPT-4, and evaluations are also performed by GPT-4 (or other LLMs). Since generated content may overlap with training data, it is unclear whether evaluations are biased (R1). R3 notes that the study of GPT-4 as evaluator is not conclusive: GPT-4 appears good only on the GOAL dimension and two others, yet the paper claims “most dimensions” (3/7 is not most) without clear justification; possible biases are not examined. The paper should commit to releasing the interaction dataset and human judgments to support future research on automated evaluation (R3).
- **Lack of quantitative definition and external validation for metrics**: The score ranges are hand-crafted with only positive/negative descriptions in prompts, not presented like Likert scales to humans; scores appear heuristic (R1). There is no external validation or comparison with established metrics from sociology or psychology (R2, R3). For example, how do GOAL, BEL, REL, KNO, SEC, SOC, FIN connect to sociological or psychological studies? (R2). No validation against human judgments for each metric (R2).
- **Some metrics questionably related to social intelligence**: Believability may reflect fluent dialogue generation rather than meaningful social interaction; social rules may capture memorization, not understanding (R1).
- **Insufficient comparison to existing benchmarks**: The paper lacks thorough comparison to related tasks like SocialIQA, PersonaChat, Multi-dialog, social relation QA, etc. It does not discuss whether solving SOTOPIA would transfer to other social capabilities (e.g., understanding social morality) (R2).
- **Limited model diversity and generalizability**: Experiments focus on GPT-based models; more models (including non-GPT, structure-based solutions) should be tested (R2). Generalizability to other AI models or real-world social interactions is not discussed (R2). Scaling to multi-agent interactions beyond two agents is not addressed (R1).
- **Missing details and analyses**: The distribution of social goals and relationships (e.g., types and number of collaboration vs. competition scenarios) is not provided (R1). Studies that vary single dimensions (e.g., relationship, personality, goal) to show expected shifts in conversation are missing (R3). The gap in Table 1 (human + SEC column) is unexplained (R3). The asymmetry in pairwise performance in Figure 3 is not explained (R3). Robustness to adversarial human behaviors is not studied (R3). No discussion of how to improve LLMs based on SOTOPIA (e.g., finetuning on successful interactions) (R3).
- **Clarity and completeness issues**: Table 2 states “The best performance for each dimension is bolded when significant” but does not specify the statistical test or significance level (R3). Limitations are in the appendix rather than the main paper (R3). No estimate of API call costs is provided, though this would be useful for reproducibility (R3). Minor grammar issues exist (e.g., “the literature inspire us”) (R3). No explicit statement of what will be publicly released (code, data, judgments), which is critical for a benchmark paper (R3). R3 notes that benchmark papers that do not release anything are pretty useless.
- **One reviewer (R1) expressed relatively lower confidence in the paper’s soundness (2 fair) and contribution (2 fair), while R3 found soundness and contribution excellent (4).** This contrast in overall assessment is noted.
