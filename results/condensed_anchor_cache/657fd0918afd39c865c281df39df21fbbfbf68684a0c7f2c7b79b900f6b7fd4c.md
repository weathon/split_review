## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ViML, a large-scale multi-modality-to-language dataset comprising 20M video clips from 290k trailers (films, news, gaming) with custom background music. A systemic captioning pipeline generates frame captions (CoCa), video captions (LLaVA), and music captions (MusicCaps) separately, then merges them along with object/background labels using Llama-2 to produce a multimodal caption. Two versions are provided: ViML-3M (3M high-quality clips) and ViML-Test (1k clips with manually adjusted captions). Evaluation benchmarks on video captioning, video generation, and music generation are presented.

### Strengths
- First multimodal captioning dataset to include musical captions alongside video captions, addressing the neglect of audio-visual correlations in existing datasets (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Large scale: 20M clips and 3M high-quality subset, good for training multimodal models and text-to-video/text-to-audio&video generation (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Data curation pipeline is sound and intuitive; data filtering strategy proven effective via ablations (Reviewer 1).
- Dataset features high image quality, aesthetic scores, and motion characteristics (Reviewer 1).
- Sophisticated captioning pipeline combining multiple state-of-the-art models with LLM fusion produces detailed multimodal annotations (Reviewer 3).
- Diverse content types (films, news, gaming) with custom background music enable fine-grained multimodal model training (Reviewer 3).
- Detailed evaluation scores assess audio-visual segment information from multiple perspectives with intuitive examples (Reviewer 4).
- Experiments on downstream tasks reveal high-quality nature of dataset and superior performance across various indicators (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Technical contribution is insufficient**: The caption pipeline is a trivial integration of existing uni-modal captioners (MusicCaps, CoCa, LLaVA) and an LLM merger (used by prior works), making the technical novelty low (Reviewer 1 – minority negative). Several similar data curation pipelines exist [1][2] (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of ablations on captioner choices**: Why CoCa for image captioner and LLaVA for video? Can LLaVA generate image captions given it outperforms CoCa? Why not use video-encoder MLLMs like VideoChat [3] or Video-LLaVa [4]? More clarification needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Effects of individual LLMs on final caption not discussed**: Since the pipeline is fully automated, the impact of each model (frame, video, music captioners, LLM merger) should be analyzed (Reviewer 2).
- **Music caption quality for very short clips**: 40% of clips <1 second and 72% <3 seconds; it is unclear how meaningful and correct the music captions can be for such short durations, especially given potential hallucination from automated LLMs (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also questions why not adopt stitching (as in Panda-70M) to lengthen clips (average 4.6s).
- **Hallucination accumulation across pipeline steps**: No proper checks on hallucination propagation from multiple LLM steps. Human evaluation is only on the small manually adjusted test set; it is unclear whether the test set itself inherits hallucinations from preceding automated steps (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental evaluation is inadequate**: 
  - Video generation baseline uses WebVid (old); newer datasets like LVD-2M [5] or Panda-70M [6] should be compared (Reviewer 1). 
  - VBench has 16 dimensions but only 9 are reported; rationale missing (Reviewer 1). 
  - For caption, additional datasets like Valor32k [7], FAVD [8], DiDeMo [9] (rich audio-visual content) should be tested (Reviewer 1). 
  - More diverse understanding tasks and benchmarks (e.g., ShareGPT4Video) needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Marginal improvements in Tables 4 and 5**: Results show only marginal gains, raising questions about practical value of the dataset (Reviewer 3).
- **Information loss in synthetic caption generation**: The automated pipeline may not adequately preserve original data information (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation of sound effects omitted**: The paper evaluates quality of background music and video but neglects sound effects, which often correlate more directly with video content (Reviewer 4).
- **Audio-video alignment evaluation confounded**: When using ImageBind to assess alignment, the method does not separate background music from sound effects. Since ImageBind’s train set (AudioSet) does not distinguish them, simultaneous presence likely impacts similarity scores (Reviewer 4).
- **Data source and overlap concerns**: Unclear if all videos are from YouTube and whether the data overlaps with existing datasets like HD-Vila or YT-Temporal-1b (Reviewer 1 – question).
- **Missing evaluations for sounding video generation and music generation**: The dataset is suited for these tasks but no experiments with SVG models (MM-Diffusion, MM-LDM) or text-to-music/video-to-music models are reported (Reviewer 1 – question).
- **Section 4.1 finding unclear**: Object/background labels score 6.02, showing more correctness than other captions – but why frame or music captions are poorer descriptions is not explained, nor how this highlights caption effectiveness (Reviewer 2 – question).
- **Table 5 metric disparity**: BERT similarity is high for pre-trained Video-LLaMA but much lower for fine-tuned and ViML-trained versions; this may indicate domain mismatch or caption style difference, not explained (Reviewer 2 – question).
