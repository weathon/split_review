## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces RECOMP (Retrieve, Compress, Prepend), a method to compress retrieved documents into summaries before prepending them to LM inputs in retrieval-augmented LMs. Two compressors are proposed: an extractive model that selects informative sentences and an abstractive model that generates query-focused summaries. Both are trained to optimize end-task performance while minimizing summary length. Experiments on language modeling and open-domain QA achieve compression rates as low as 6% with minimal performance loss, outperforming off-the-shelf summarization models and enabling transfer across LMs. The method freezes the LM and trains only the compressor, which is seen as interesting and beneficial for transfer.

### Strengths

- Clear presentation of the core idea; research topic is important and timely. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- Simple but clever approach; method is more than a model combination and thus novel. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- Well-designed experiments with well-chosen baselines; thorough analyses in Sec. 6. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Freezing the LM and training only the compressor makes the method transferable across different LMs. (Reviewer 1)
- Demonstrates effectiveness of both extractive and abstractive summarization for retrieval-augmented LMs. (Reviewer 3)
- Achieves prompt length reduction while largely maintaining performance; outperforms off-the-shelf summarization models. (Reviewer 3)
- Extensive experimental validation with comprehensive analyses. (Reviewer 1, 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing efficiency measurement**: Inference speedup is not reported. The number of saved tokens does not reflect actual system efficiency because the compressor (even if smaller) still processes all retrieved documents. This is a core missing piece to support the claimed motivation of improved efficiency. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Training cost not analyzed**: The abstractive compressor relies on a teacher LM (GPT-3.5) to generate training data, introducing significant overhead. The paper lacks a detailed trade-off analysis between this substantial upfront training cost and the potential inference gains. It is unclear whether the inference savings justify the training expense. (Reviewer 1; echoed by Reviewer 2 on overhead)
- **Practical usefulness in question**: Normal RAG without compression outperforms the proposed method in accuracy (albeit with more tokens). With growing context lengths of modern LMs, it is unclear whether the accuracy hit is worth the tokens saved. The computational overhead of training and running the compressors may negate the benefits in many real-world applications. (Reviewers 2, 3; also noted by Reviewer 1 on efficiency uncertainty) Two reviewers (1, 3) were notably more critical of the practical advantages, while two reviewers (2, 4) were more positive but still raised these concerns.
- **Performance improvement limited in many settings**: The method shows only marginal or no gains in several experimental conditions, which requires justification. (Reviewer 3)
- **Incomplete comparison of baselines**: Some baselines (DRP, Contriever) were not finetuned on the same dataset used to train the extractive compressor, creating an unfair comparison. (Reviewer 4)
- **Retriever dependency and missing analysis**: Only a BM25 retriever is used; results may be confounded by the retriever’s performance. The paper should present results with another retriever (e.g., Contriever) and include the distribution of oracle-retrieved documents (as done for extractive compression). (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing time or computational cost comparison**: Direct comparisons of end-to-end generation time (full retrieval without compression vs. compression+prepending) are needed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Limited exploration of context length limitations**: Given LM length limits, compression could allow including more retrieved documents in the prompt; this direction was not examined. (Reviewer 3)
- **Annotation agreement not reported**: For the manual evaluation of abstractive compressor outputs, inter-annotator agreement should be reported. (Reviewer 4)
