## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies prompt-injected data extraction from Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. It shows that instruction-tuned language models are vulnerable to copying their datastore contexts when instructed to do so, with larger and better instruction-following models being more susceptible. The attack works across open-source models (Llama2, Mistral/Mixtral, Vicuna, SOLAR, WizardLM, Qwen1.5, Platypus2) and production GPTs. Ablation studies examine factors like context position, chunk size, and retrieval window size. Mitigation through position-bias elimination (PINE) and safety-aware prompts is explored. Reviewers agree the work is thorough and practically important, but disagree on its novelty: some find the core attack obvious (simply asking the model to repeat its context) and the contributions more like a bug report or engineering characterization, while others praise the comprehensive empirical characterization and real-world implications for security policy.

### Strengths
- Important problem for practitioners, researchers, and policymakers (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation across multiple model families, scales, and system configurations (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Informative ablation studies on factors affecting extraction success, e.g., chunking decisions, retrieved context size, and context position (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Practical demonstration on production GPTs – a 100% success rate on 25 randomly selected custom GPTs with at most 2 queries, and verbatim extraction of 41% from a 77k-word book and 3% from a 1.57M-word corpus using only 100 auto-generated queries (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Analysis of different attacker policies, including black-box access and instruction injection (Reviewer 4).
- Thoughtful analysis of mitigation strategies (Reviewer 3; but criticized as underdeveloped by others).
- Good technical writing and clear structure overall (Reviewer 3; though two paragraphs are noted as too lengthy – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Core attack is obvious / lacks novelty.** Several reviewers note that telling an instruction-following model to repeat its context is an unsurprising vulnerability; the paper is described as more of a bug report or engineering characterization than a fundamental research contribution (Reviewers 1, 3, partially 4). Mitigation strategies are underdeveloped: one relies on a simple prompt and the other on prior work (PINE), and the paper does not engage deeply with architectural changes (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 characterizes the mitigations as “like putting a ‘please don’t enter’ sign on an unlocked door.”
- **Experiment setup is overly simplistic for open-source models.** The RAG prompts tested do not include more sophisticated guardrails (e.g., structured prompting that clearly separates system and user instructions, or prompts specifically designed to block context exposure); such setups might be more robust (Reviewer 2). The paper only tests a “naive RAG” without exploring state-of-the-art retrieval pipelines like HippoRAG or GraphRAG, which could significantly affect attack performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Production system scope is limited.** GPTs are not necessarily representative of commercial RAG systems that have multi-layer guardrails at various pipeline points. The paper would be significantly stronger if the attack were shown to be effective on a broader set of production RAG systems (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing discussion of risk scenarios.** Data extraction from a RAG system is not always a concern; the paper should clearly articulate for what use cases (e.g., proprietary or sensitive data) the leakage actually constitutes a risk (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of crucial controls / ablation checks.** Reviewers point to missing experiments: (a) confirming that the “new” Wikipedia articles used are truly disjoint from model training data (an n‑gram overlap check should be done) (Reviewer 2); (b) evaluating whether PINE mitigation affects downstream RAG QA performance (Reviewer 2); (c) addressing the case where the attacker does not have access to the GPTs’ system prompt (a likely realistic constraint) (Reviewer 2).
- **Readability issues.** Some paragraphs (e.g., lines 47–67 and 452–476) are too lengthy and should be broken up or shortened (Reviewer 1).
- **No exploration of test-time reasoning alternatives.** Reviewer 4 suggests that allowing the model to select/punish tokens non-greedily during inference could alleviate information leakage – this direction is not considered.
- **Disagreement on contribution depth.** Reviewer 2 rates contribution 4, considers the work important; Reviewer 3 rates contribution 3 and calls it a “well-documented proof-of-concept” rather than advancing the field’s understanding; Reviewer 1 rates contribution 2, citing lack of novel mitigation and limited impact without evaluation on advanced RAG pipelines; Reviewer 4 rates contribution 3, noting the method is simple prompt engineering without improving the causal LLM.
