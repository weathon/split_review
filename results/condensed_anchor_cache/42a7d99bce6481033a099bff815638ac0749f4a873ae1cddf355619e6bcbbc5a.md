## Merged Review

### Summary
KVMerger adaptively compresses the KV cache for LLMs in long-context tasks by clustering highly similar key states (cosine similarity) and merging them via a Gaussian kernel weighted function, while retaining top‑k attention tokens. Evaluated on LongBench and ZeroScroll across Llama2‑7B/13B‑chat and Mistral‑7B‑instruct, it is compared to H2O and CaM. Two reviewers (scores 5) found the method concise and promising; a third reviewer (score 3) was more critical, questioning the theoretical justification, missing baselines, and benchmark representativeness.

### Strengths
- Unique perspective: formulates KV cache merging as a constrained clustering problem (R1).
- Writing is easy to follow; method is concise and effective (R2).
- Simple to implement, well‑motivated, and clearly explained (R3).
- Empirically achieves notable memory savings, reducing GPU memory consumption (R1).
- Strong improvements over H2O and CaM; has potential to be a significant step forward (R3).
- Ablations around the choice of σ and the merging algorithm are convincing (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical justification unclear:** Sec 4.1 proves that value states have low similarity (due to lack of RoPE), but the method still merges value states and works. The claim that RoPE is required for high similarity (Lemmas 4.1, 4.2) is not well supported, and experimental evidence for the claim that value states are dissimilar (Lines 203‑205) is missing (R2, R3).
- **Missing baselines:** Only H2O and CaM are compared. Missing PyramidInfer, PyramidKV, SnapKV, quantization‑based methods, eviction methods, and hybrid approaches (quantization + merging) (R1, R2, R3).
- **Computational overhead unanalyzed:** Cosine similarity and Gaussian kernel merging may be computationally expensive for large models; the paper gives limited insight into overhead and does not compare inference throughput (R1, R2). (One reviewer notes simplicity, creating a contrast.)
- **Experimental details omitted:** Which dataset is used for merging set identification? Is merging applied only during the prefill stage or also during generation? (R2)
- **Analysis incomplete:** The claim that compression ratio is independent of dataset and persistent at the model level (Lines 267‑283) is contradicted by Table 1 (Mistral vs. Llama show different trends). Characterization of when the method works well (model, dataset) is lacking (R3). The objective function (Lines 311‑314) appears NP‑hard; no analysis is given of how close Algorithm 1’s solution is to this optimum (R2).
- **Benchmark limitations:** Most evaluated benchmarks (Wiki, QA, news) are memorization‑oriented rather than testing context use; broader in‑context learning tasks are needed. All KV compression methods work well on Llama, which was trained on many more tokens than Mistral (R3).
- **Missing ablation on top‑k attention tokens:** It is unclear how much gain comes from retaining tokens with top‑k attention scores (Line 323) and whether H2O and CaM also do this; if not, that could explain the improvement (R3).
- **No discussion of limitations:** The paper does not discuss scenarios where the high‑similarity assumption fails or the potential impact of merging on nuanced context understanding (R1).
- **Presentation issue:** The equal sign ‘=’ in Eq. (5) is used incorrectly (R2).
