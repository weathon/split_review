## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Moner, an unsupervised implicit neural representation (INR) framework for joint reconstruction and motion correction in undersampled 2D radial MRI. It integrates a quasi-static rigid motion model with the Fourier-slice theorem and a coarse-to-fine hash encoding strategy to recover artifact-free images without pre-training on large datasets. Evaluations are performed on simulated motion-corrupted brain MRI from fastMRI and MoDL datasets. All reviewers find the work interesting, well-written, and significant for the motion correction problem, with extensive experiments and ablation studies. However, major weaknesses include the exclusive use of simulated motion (no real-world validation), limited data diversity (only brain, similar contrasts), insufficient details on motion simulation and dataset selection, and reliance on rigid motion assumptions without testing non-rigid or intra-spoke motion. One reviewer (R2, score 6) is notably less positive, citing the lack of 3D and non-radial trajectory validation as a critical practical limitation; the other three reviewers (scores 8,8,8) are more positive but share several concerns. Reviewer confidence varies: two reviewers have high confidence (5), two have moderate confidence (3).

### Strengths
- **Unsupervised approach**: Eliminates the need for large pre-training datasets, enhancing generalizability across modalities and out-of-distribution data (all reviewers).
- **Motion model with Fourier-slice theorem**: Integrating a quasi-static motion model into image space via the Fourier-slice theorem is sound, enabling simple motion modeling and stable optimization (R1, R2).
- **Extensive evaluation and ablation**: Thorough comparisons with classical, supervised, and self-supervised baselines, plus ablations of the three core components (R1, R3, R4).
- **Well-written and well-motivated**: Clear background, motivation for each design choice, and good structure (R1, R3, R4).
- **Motion estimation stability**: The back-projection formulation effectively reduces artifacts in motion-corrupted scenarios (R2).
- **Efficiency and robustness**: Moner achieves superior out-of-domain performance and is 5–6× faster per 2D slice than the Score-MoCo diffusion baseline (R2, R4).
- **Originality**: First exploration of untrained neural networks (INRs) for joint reconstruction and motion estimation, with interesting extensions (coarse-to-fine hash encoding, projection-space loss) (R4).
- **Practical significance**: Addresses the real need for robust motion correction methods that do not require extensive training data from all domains (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **No real-world validation**: Evaluation is exclusively on simulated motion-corrupted data. Motion artifacts from field inhomogeneities, etc., are not modeled. Real-world in-vivo testing is considered mandatory for clinical applicability (R1). Reviewer R2 further notes that non-rigid motion and intra-spoke motion are not addressed, limiting practical realism.
- **Limited data diversity**: Only brain MRI with similar contrast/sequence is used. Whether the method generalizes to other body parts, contrasts, noise levels, or slice orientations is untested. Will the same optimization strategy (hash encoding, hyperparameters) remain stable? (R3). The choice of 25 test slices is unclear – are they from few subjects or many? Differences between fastMRI and MoDL datasets (e.g., axial vs. coronal, contrast differences) are not sufficiently described, making it hard to assess distribution shift severity (R4).
- **Insufficient motion simulation details**: The motion generation process is not fully specified. Are all spokes corrupted? What are the intervals between motion events? Does each spoke have its own motion state (i.e., 900 unknowns for 300 spokes)? How does the model scale with acceleration factor? (R1, R4). Realistic motion patterns in the brain (e.g., individual lines) are not clarified (R1). Trans-plane motion effects on optimization stability are unaddressed (R3).
- **Motion model assumptions**: Only rigid motion is handled; non-rigid deformations (e.g., tissue deformation) or motion within a single acquisition frame are ignored. These limitations are not fully acknowledged or quantified (R2).
- **Extensibility to 3D and non-radial trajectories**: The method is demonstrated only on 2D radial MRI. Untested on 3D MRI, Cartesian, or spiral trajectories, which are common clinically (R2). This significantly limits practical applicability (R2, R4).
- **Novelty claim for Fourier-slice theorem**: The Fourier-slice theorem was already used for MRI reconstruction (Catalan 2023). It should be properly cited rather than presented as a novelty (R1).
- **Lack of statistical significance**: Claims of “significant improvement” (e.g., Tables 2, 4) are not supported by standard deviations or statistical tests (R1, R4). R4 also suggests adding quantitative scores to qualitative figures (e.g., Fig. 4).
- **Image quality concerns**: Reconstructed images (e.g., Fig. 8) appear blurry and lack thin structures like vessels. Zoomed-in comparisons would help (R3). It is surprising that the supervised DRN-DCMB baseline performs worse than the unsupervised method; alternative supervised baselines should be considered (R3).
- **Computational speed analysis**: The speed comparison lacks detail. Why is the TV method (200 epochs) slower than the INR (4000 epochs)? What drives the speed difference vs. the diffusion model – network size or number of steps? (R4) The MLP size is not reported (R4).
- **Missing implementation details**: Post-processing steps (normalization, registration) not fully described for reproducibility (R1). The low-frequency bias in INR is claimed to be mitigated, but its dependence on network settings (activation, etc.) is not discussed (R1). It is unclear how data from multiple coils is handled during optimization (R3).
- **Question about high dynamic range**: The paper claims that existing methods overlook the high dynamic range problem (line 151), but some mentioned works (e.g., Huang 2023) do address it – this claim needs clarification (R1).
- **Why Moner outperforms Score-MoCo on OOD data**: The reasons are not fully explained; is it an inherent advantage of INR over diffusion-based priors? (R2).
- **Benefits of coarse-to-fine hash encoding**: The specific advantage of this encoding over traditional techniques for motion correction is not thoroughly justified (R2).
