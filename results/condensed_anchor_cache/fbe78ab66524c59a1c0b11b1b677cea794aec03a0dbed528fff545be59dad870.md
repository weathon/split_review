## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents WenXinGPT, a 7B-parameter multimodal LLM for orthopedic medical consultations in Chinese, using a three-stage training pipeline (pretraining, domain-specific SFT, multi-disciplinary consultation). It introduces a new 16-category orthopedic dataset from Peking Union Medical College Hospital. Evaluation compares against GPT-3.5 and XrayGPT using ROUGE. Reviewers agree the dataset and multi-agent consultation concept are interesting, but find major flaws in technical detail, evaluation, experimental design, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- New comprehensive dataset of orthopedic surgery data covering 16 distinct categories from a medical institution; if made public, valuable for future research (R1, R2, R3).
- Addresses a significant gap in non-English healthcare by targeting Chinese orthopedic domain (R2).
- Multi-Department Consultation (MC) framework with feedback from multiple experts aligns with real-world medical consultations and improves output robustness/interoperability (R2, R3).
- Multi-round discussion among medical professionals (multi-agent design) mimics joint expert consultation (R2, R3).
- Dataset includes diverse medical information that could benefit the community (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing multimodal details**: Paper claims "multimodal large model" for medical image diagnosis but provides no technical details on visual processing, multimodal integration architecture, or how images (e.g., X-rays) are handled (R1).
- **Flawed evaluation**: 
  - Compared primarily against GPT-3.5 (text-only) using only text-based ROUGE metrics; does not include relevant multimodal baselines like MiniGPT-4, LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl, Opus, or GPT-4 (R1, R2).
  - ROUGE alone is insufficient to capture medical report quality (interpretability, usability); human evaluations and GPT-based assessments are missing (R2).
  - No BLEU scores reported (R3).
  - Limited to one institutional dataset; generalizability to other medical datasets or specialties is not shown (R1, R2).
  - Only compares with GPT-3.5 and XrayGPT; other general LLMs and medical LLMs are absent (R3).
  - With few-shot CoT+SC, the model performs better than WenXinGPT itself; no discussion on how to improve WenXinGPT (R3).
- **Missing ablation studies**: No analysis of how NAS or MC strategies affect performance, leaving effectiveness unclear (R1, R2). No ablation on the multi-agent consultation mechanism (R2).
- **Dataset details insufficient**: Number of records/tokens, statistical analysis, composition, proportion allocated to pretraining vs. SFT, number of participants, and how D_consultations were acquired are all unspecified (R2, R3).
- **Implementation details missing**: 
  - Prompts used for MC framework and tasks for SFT not provided, harming reproducibility (R2).
  - How CoT, SC, few-shot, zero-shot strategies are implemented unclear (R3).
  - The claimed "underlying prompt system" is not explained (R3).
  - How human feedback for RLHF was acquired not described (R3).
- **Multi-turn dialogue not clearly explained**: Mechanism for initiating/maintaining multi-turn discussions is unclear; the provided example does not illustrate consensus reaching (R2, R3). The "consensus among the interdisciplinary team" in the example case is not shown (R3).
- **Misleading attribution of architecture**: The paper claims a "7B decoder-only LM" without initially citing DeciLM-7B; it is unclear whether the authors developed GQA and NAS or simply used DeciLM's existing implementation. The advantage over Llama 3 (which already uses GQA) is unsupported (R1, R3).
- **Hardware inconsistency**: Paper states "16 A100 GPUs (32GB)" but A100 only comes in 40GB and 80GB variants; model used needs clarification (R1).
- **Domain justification weak**: Exclusive focus on orthopedics not justified; limited discussion on adaptability to other medical specialties or non-Chinese datasets (R2).
- **Ethical considerations incomplete**: Handling of Protected Health Information (PHI) not clearly described; no explanation of PHI removal or validation techniques (R2).
- **Paper writing quality poor**: Missing parts (e.g., prompt system, dataset details, acquisition of D_consultations), misleading claims, and unclear experimental design (R3).
- **Unaddressed question**: Why not simply translate existing English-language medical LLMs to Chinese? Would that lead to decreased performance? (R3)

*Note: Reviewer 2 is more positive overall (calling it "valuable groundwork" and rating contribution 3), while Reviewer 1 is the most critical (contribution 1, soundness 1) and Reviewer 3 is in between (contribution 2). All three reviewers gave a score of 3, reflecting significant concerns despite an interesting concept.*
