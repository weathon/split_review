- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OML-AD, an online prediction-based anomaly detection framework that wraps an online forecasting model and uses dynamic thresholding on prediction errors to handle non-stationary time series. Reviewers agree the work is a combination of existing ideas (prediction error + adaptive threshold) with limited novelty. Experiments are considered insufficient: too few datasets, outdated baselines, and missing ablations. One reviewer praises the clear writing; another calls it horrible and demands a complete rewrite.

### Strengths
- **Online anomaly detection is practically important** for data streams and edge devices (R1).
- **Lightweight plugin** that transforms a basic online forecasting model into an online anomaly detection model, enabling adaptability to concept drift (R2, R3).
- **Good coverage of online learning methods broadly** (R1).
- **Results support the overall claim** that OML-AD outperforms SARIMA and Prophet (R1).
- **Integrated into the widely-used River library** (R4).
- **Simple and straightforward** approach (R4).
- **Writing is generally clear and well-structured** according to one reviewer (R2); however, another reviewer strongly disagreed (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and technical depth** – The method essentially computes prediction error (forecast vs. actual) and applies a dynamic threshold; the threshold update (using μt, σt) is taken from prior work. Multiple reviewers consider this a trivial combination of known components with too little innovation (R1 W1, R2 W1, R3, R4 W2).
- **Insufficient baselines** – Only two baselines are used: SARIMA and Prophet (both from 2018). Missing comparisons with dedicated time-series anomaly detectors such as **SAND** (VLDB 2021) and other state-of-the-art methods (R1 W2, R2 W3, R3). Reviewer explicitly asks for comparisons with recent SOTA anomaly detection models (R2 Q3).
- **Narrow and weak dataset selection** – Experiments use only one synthetic dataset and one real dataset with injected synthetic anomalies. Needed: more real-world datasets with varied concept drifts (no drift, slow, fast, random) and different anomaly types. The **KDD Cup 250 time series** is suggested (R2 W2, R4 W3).
- **Missing ablation studies** – No ablation to isolate the effect of the threshold mechanism, base model choice, or dynamic update (R3).
- **Missing threshold method experiments** – The methodology describes three threshold methods (simple μt/σt based on normality assumption, and extreme value theory), but only the first is tested. Experiments with the other two are needed (R2 Q2).
- **Performance dependency not addressed** – MAE/MSE of OML-AD is entirely determined by its internal forecasting model; the paper should compare against models with similar MAE/MSE to show that its anomaly detection is not just an artifact of better forecasting (R2 Q1).
- **Lack of challenging examples** – The synthetic dataset (Figure 2) exhibits mild concept drift; reviewers ask for concrete scenarios where OML-AD succeeds but other models fail (R2 Q4).
- **Missing proper evaluation benchmarks** – The paper does not reference or follow recent benchmarking works: **TSB-UAD** (VLDB 2022) for datasets, and **Volume Under the Surface** (VLDB 2022) for evaluation measures (R1 W3, W4).
- **Writing quality is severely criticized** – One reviewer states “the writing is horrible” and that “the whole paper needs to be rewritten completely,” with redundant introduction, related work, and preliminaries, and unclear key points (R4 W1). This contrasts with another reviewer who found the writing clear (R2 S2); the negative view is strongly held and includes a demand for complete rewriting.
