## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Test-Time Ensemble (TTE), a method that enhances test-time adaptation (TTA) by constructing an ensemble network via weight-averaging of multiple TTA models and incorporating dropout for representation diversity. TTE also proposes a debiased and noise-robust knowledge distillation scheme to stabilize optimization and prevent collapse. It is designed to integrate seamlessly with existing TTA methods, improving adaptation across various distribution shifts. Extensive experiments show consistent performance gains over baselines in corruption, continual, and non-i.i.d. scenarios.

### Strengths

- Good writing quality; the paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and clearly motivated.
- Insightful perspective: treating TTA as a domain generalization problem and uncovering linear mode connectivity among TTA models, which simplifies and enhances adaptation.
- The proposed test-time ensemble problem has practical applications and goes beyond prior TTA formulations.
- TTE uses weight averaging to capture model diversity without the computational burden of managing multiple models; dropout further promotes diversity.
- Simple and effective baseline methods that can be implemented on top of multiple TTA methods (demonstrated across various backbones).
- State-of-the-art performance: integration with TTE consistently outperforms baseline TTA models across challenging scenarios (corruption, continual, non-i.i.d.).
- Extensive experiments include multiple TTA scenarios and ablation studies verifying the effectiveness of the proposed modules.
- The method is easy to implement and has potential to benefit the community, especially if code is released.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty of components**: TTE combines well-established techniques (ensemble, dropout, knowledge distillation) that have been used in TTA or self-supervised learning. The paper does not clearly articulate which specific modifications or novel contributions arise from the combination, and the weight-averaging and two-branch structure resemble existing methods (e.g., pytorch-image-models). The uniqueness of the approach is not sufficiently justified.
- **Theoretical depth lacking**: The linear mode connectivity insight is claimed to guide the method, but the analysis does not explain *why* models generated during TTA are linearly connected or under which conditions this holds. The connection to the proposed ensemble strategy remains superficial.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and interaction**: The method involves multiple hyperparameters (momentum coefficient, dropout ratio, temperature) whose interactions are not systematically studied. For example, how momentum and dropout jointly affect convergence, or how the temperature in knowledge distillation interacts with these settings, is unclear. A robustness analysis is needed.
- **Computational cost analysis missing**: The paper claims weight averaging does not add computational burden, but no detailed analysis of time/space complexity or memory overhead is provided. Even if weights are averaged, storing multiple model snapshots may increase memory; this should be quantified and compared to other TTA methods.
- **Knowledge distillation debiasing limitations**: The debiased and noise-robust KD scheme does not clearly explain the origin of the bias (spike phenomena in accuracy curve) nor how it connects to the bias. It is also insufficient for handling incorrect pseudo labels; the paper lacks a mechanism or discussion of alternatives when the ensemble produces wrong pseudo labels.
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**: 
  - No comparison with other local ensemble methods (e.g., SWA combined with TTA) in Table 1.
  - Table 3 (continual TTA) only shows TTE applied to DeYO, not to other TTA variants; it is unclear if the continual improvement generalizes. The performance in continual TTA (e.g., DeYO from 28.1 → 3.7 → 7.2 with TTE) seems too stable—could be due to implicit reset or ensemble with the zero-shot model, but this is not controlled across baselines.
  - Table 4 (non-i.i.d.) column V2 shows almost all results as 68.9 (DeYO and DeYO+TTE); more diverse batch sizes and additional methods with TTE should be added (possibly in supplementary).
- **Inconsistent improvement in continual TTA**: It is not shown whether TTE improves performance consistently across all adaptation steps or only initially. The method’s ability to mitigate catastrophic forgetting is not explicitly demonstrated: does it prevent loss of previously acquired knowledge, or just mask immediate performance drops?
- **Unclear description in Section 3.2**: The debiased knowledge distillation subsection is vague; how the bias arises and its relation to the accuracy spike is not well explained. Also, in Equation 5, there is no hyperparameter to balance the terms, which is likely needed.
- **Minor issues**: Typos in introduction ("lager" → "larger", "Adaptvie" → "Adaptive").
- **Question about extension**: Can the benchmark construction method be extended to dense prediction tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation)? This is not addressed.
- **Question about tiny CoTTA results**: In continual TTA with non-i.i.d. conditions, why are CoTTA’s results only 2.2% and 3.4%? This needs explanation.
- **Batch size impact**: The role of batch size (especially in continual TTA) is not explored, despite known sensitivity (e.g., “Bag of Tricks for Fully Test-Time Adaptation”). The results in Table 4 may be influenced by a fixed batch size that favors some methods.
