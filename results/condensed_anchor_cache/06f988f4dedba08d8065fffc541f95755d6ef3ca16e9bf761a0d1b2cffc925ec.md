## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces pFedGRP, a personalized federated learning framework for “Flowing Data Heterogeneity under Restricted Storage,” where each client experiences time-varying data distributions and limited storage. The method uses category decoupled auxiliary models to reconstruct and replay local data distributions, then performs personalized aggregation and knowledge transfer. Experiments on MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and EMNIST with eight baselines show accuracy and forgetting metrics.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem formulation.** The challenge of data distributions changing over time per client under restricted storage is relevant and practical. This extends the pFL setting to a more realistic dynamic scenario.
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation.** Testing on five datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, EMNIST) with eight baseline methods spanning FL, pFL, and FCL. Metrics include average accuracy (AA) and average forgetting measure (AFM).
- **Algorithm novelty.** The idea of combining category decoupled replay with personalized aggregation is novel (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation.** Generally well-written and organized (Reviewer 1); introduction and related work give clear motivation.

**Minority positive (Reviewer 4, rating 6, confidence 2):** Finds the algorithm novel and experimental studies solid. (Reviewer 3, rating 1, confidence 4, is the most negative, see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and motivation issues.**
  - Readability suffers from overly complex notation (e.g., **θ_{A^{t-1, *}_{j, c‘’}}**) and dense description. No algorithm pseudocode in the main text (Reviewer 3). Methodology lacks step-by-step explanation of how auxiliary models are trained/updated and how they interact with local models during personalized aggregation; loss function design is unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - The motivation for why pFedGRP is more effective than existing methods is unclear. The claim that no prior pFL works address “flowing data heterogeneity” is insufficiently supported; existing work on concept drift/shift [1,2] is not reviewed or compared (Reviewer 2, 3).
  - The goal is contradictory: line 164-165 states “customize personalized models for each client,” yet clients send models to the server for “personalized aggregation” and “improving generalization.” What is the role of a “personalized global model” (Reviewer 3)?
  - Privacy claims (line 96) are unfounded. Auxiliary models transmitted to the server and other clients could allow an adversary to generate data similar to a client’s data (Reviewer 3).

- **Resource analysis and scalability missing.**
  - No analysis of memory footprint, number of parameters, or storage for auxiliary models and generated replay data. No communication cost (parameter count, MBs) or computation cost (FLOPs, time, energy) reported (Reviewer 1, 3). The method seems unscalable: each client receives auxiliary models, a personalized model, and an aggregated global model per round (Reviewer 3).
  - “Restricted storage” is not clearly defined or motivated until the experiments; it remains unclear why the experimental setup (e.g., 150 tasks on MNIST) constitutes “flowing data heterogeneity under restricted storage” (Reviewer 2).

- **Replay quality control and forgetting.**
  - The framework’s success hinges on replay accuracy, but there is no mechanism for detecting or correcting errors in generated replay. Ablations are missing (e.g., introducing noisy or out-of-distribution samples, regularization methods) (Reviewer 1).
  - pFedGRP performs poorly on forgetting metrics, undermining the claim of “mitigating catastrophic forgetting.” The good accuracy may stem from the restricted storage setting (no need to memorize) rather than the proposed mechanism (Reviewer 2).
  - It is unclear whether the approach remembers multiple past distributions or only the most recent one (Reviewer 3). Missing ablation on the rate of data drift (how performance changes with different speeds of distribution shift) (Reviewer 1).

- **Missing comparisons and benchmarks.**
  - No comparison to methods from concept drift/shift literature [1,2] that also address per-client distribution change over time (Reviewer 3).
  - No evaluation on popular non-IID benchmark settings (Dirichlet-based label distributions like Dir(0.5), Dir(0.1); quantity-based like C=1,2,3) (Reviewer 4, citing [Li et al., ICDE 2022]).

- **Other clarity and validity concerns.**
  - The “Local Data Distribution Reconstruction Scheme” appears to be a general continual learning solution; its connection to FL-specific challenges (communication, heterogeneity, privacy) is not explained (Reviewer 3).
  - Y-axis in Figure 1 is unlabeled (Reviewer 3). Equation 1’s objective (client vs. server) is ambiguous (Reviewer 3). The claim that uploading weights reduces communication bandwidth and meets real-time requirements is questionable (Reviewer 3).
  - Is it assumed that all clients participate every round? (Reviewer 3). What is the rationale for the grouping strategy on CIFAR100/EMNIST62? (Reviewer 3). What are practical examples of “tasks gradually changing”? (Reviewer 3). Can the method handle conflicting past and current distributions (e.g., label reversal)? (Reviewer 3).
  - The paper would benefit from a step-by-step breakdown of training and update procedures for auxiliary models, including optimization algorithms and parameters (Reviewer 1).
