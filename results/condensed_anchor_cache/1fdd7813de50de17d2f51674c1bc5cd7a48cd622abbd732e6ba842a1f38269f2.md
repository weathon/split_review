## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BREAD, which integrates block coordinate descent (BCD) with landscape correction for LLM fine-tuning. It identifies two BCD issues: (1) wasteful backpropagation through inactive blocks and (2) frozen blocks narrowing the optimization landscape. BREAD unfreezes inactive blocks using low-rank updates (LoRA for Adam, LOMO for SGD). Experiments on 8B and 70B models claim memory savings and competitive downstream performance. Reviewers find the problem identification interesting and the demonstration via a three-layer neural network insightful. However, they raise substantial concerns about theoretical grounding (limited to a shallow regression network, missing convergence analysis), limited technical novelty (combination of existing low-rank methods like LoRA, LOMO, GaLore with BCD), insufficient experiments (missing baselines like LOMO, missing wall-clock time comparisons, lack of statistical significance, no ablation on hyperparameters), and clarity of presentation. One reviewer (R2) is slightly more positive about the overall contribution but still identifies important gaps, including an error in Proposition 2.

### Strengths
- First to identify the issue in BCD where frozen blocks far from optimality can interfere with optimization of the active block.
- Provides theoretical demonstration of this issue via a regression problem on a three-layer shallow network.
- Shows empirical improvements over existing memory-efficient baselines (BAdam) on LLaMA-3.1-8B and 70B models.
- The method is easy to follow and interesting; the combination of BCD with low-rank landscape correction is innovative.
- The two identified BCD issues are intriguing, and the experimental results are promising.
- The paper is structured well.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited theoretical scope**: The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1 and 2) is only for a three-layer regression network; it does not cover general L-layer deep neural networks nor classification losses like cross-entropy. (R1)
2. **Missing convergence analysis**: No convergence guarantees are provided, even for convex optimization. Establishing such a rate is acknowledged as challenging but considered crucial for optimization literature. (R1, R2)
3. **Error in Proposition 2**: In the example, the expressions \(Ce_1=W_3^{(1)}-y\) and \(y-W_3e_1=y-W_3^{(1)}\) do not subtract to zero; also a typo in the proof: \(C = [y - W_3^{(1)}, 0, \dots, 0]\) should be corrected. (R2, R3)
4. **Unjustified global conclusion from Proposition 1**: It is unclear why \(z_3^*\) in Case II must have a negative entry; a single step yielding higher loss does not imply eventual failure, and the claimed conclusion that the BCD subproblem excludes useful search directions is not justified. (R4)
5. **Limited technical novelty**: BREAD is essentially a combination of LoRA and BCD (for Adam) and LOMO and BCD (for SGD). The core idea of using low-rank updates to correct the landscape is not fundamentally new given existing low-rank adaptation methods (LoRA, GaLore). BREAD is more similar to GaLore than to true BCD because it performs SGD/Adam on inactive layers via LoRA; it is unclear how it achieves lower memory cost than GaLore. (R3, R4)
6. **Missing baseline for LOMO**: Results for the LOMO optimizer are not included in the experiments, despite being a key memory-efficient baseline. (R3)
7. **Missing quantitative results for variants**: Tables 2 and 3 do not include performance of BREAD-partial, BREAD-SGD, and BREAD-SGD-partial. (R3)
8. **Marginal improvement and insufficient experiments on other architectures/tasks**: Performance gains in the main LLaMA-3 experiment appear marginal. Experiments on other architectures (e.g., Phi-3, Mistral) and tasks are needed. The evaluation setup is not fully aligned with MathInstruct regarding out-of-domain datasets and number of shots. (R1, R3)
9. **Slower than BAdam in epoch GPU hours (Table 1) and no wall-clock time comparison**: Figure 3 shows learning curves vs. iterations; wall-clock time curves are needed for fair comparison. (R1)
10. **Missing experiment to test search space expansion**: An extreme example does not conclusively show BCD is worse in general; the authors should measure the change in magnitude per layer and compare BCD vs. BREAD to substantiate that BREAD expands the search space. (R4)
11. **No rationale or results for block partitioning ordering strategies**: The BAdam paper studied various ordering strategies (Appendix B.1); this work does not address or present similar analysis. (R2)
12. **Unexplained behavior of BREAD-SGD-partial**: It shows convergence similar to BREAD-SGD, yet the reason is not explained. (R2)
13. **Lack of statistical significance**: Standard deviations across multiple random seeds are not reported, making it difficult to assess robustness. (R3)
14. **Missing ablation studies on hyperparameters**: The effects of the number of blocks (D), the number of correction steps (K), and the rank (r) on performance are not explored. (R3)
15. **Clarity and presentation issues**:
    - Algorithm 1: unclear whether two separate Adam instances are used; how matrices U and V are initialized and used; what constitutes a single iteration; how one epoch of training data is sampled. (R3)
    - Notation: not all notation in Section 2 is properly introduced; what \(n\) represents in Section 4.2 is ambiguous. (R3)
    - Experimental details: the choice of alpha for LoRA with rank 80 is unusual and not justified. (R3)
    - Minor: In Table 2 (Llama 3.1-8B, SimulEQ, 4-shot), the best result is incorrectly highlighted; note that mathematical fine-tuning falls under instruction tuning. (R3)
