## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a framework for efficiently learning large sets of 3D scene representations using a micro–macro decomposition of Tri-Planes. It operates in two stages: (i) training a compression model (latent-space autoencoder) on a subset of scenes to learn a shared global (macro) tri‑plane, then (ii) training per‑scene (micro) tri‑planes on the resulting smaller latent representations. Experiments on ShapeNet Cars (144 views) and Basel Faces (45 views) show reduced training time and memory footprint compared to per‑scene Tri‑NeRF, with similar rendering quality. Reviewers are sharply split: two find the work not competitive due to weak baselines, limited scope, and weak motivation (scores 1,1), two find the approach useful and novel for scalability (scores 5,5), and one is neutral (score 3) citing insufficient comparisons and ablations.

### Strengths
1. **Novel decomposition**: The explicit decomposition into a global (macro) tri‑plane shared across scenes and a scene‑specific (micro) tri‑plane is an interesting and unique approach in the NeRF context, where scene‑specific and globally shared information are seldom jointly optimized.
2. **Clear methodology**: The paper is well‑written and well‑organized; the two‑stage training pipeline and micro‑macro Tri‑Plane design are clearly explained.
3. **Efficiency gain**: The proposed training strategy substantially reduces computational time and memory for training NeRFs on large sets of scenes compared to independent per‑scene Tri‑Planes, as shown on ShapeNet Cars and Basel Faces.
4. **Originality**: The two‑stage pipeline and micro‑macro decomposition serve as a novel technique for concurrent reconstruction of large object sets.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient baselines and comparisons** (raised by Reviewers 1,2,5 with high confidence):
   - Only compared against per‑scene Tri‑Planes. Missing comparisons to:
     - Recent efficient NeRF variants (InstantNGP, NeRFLight, Plenoctrees).
     - 3D Gaussian Splatting methods (3DGS, FlashGS, gsplat).
     - Feed‑forward reconstruction pipelines trained on large scene sets (LRM‑variants, multi‑view 3D Gen, MVSplat, Pixelsplat, Splatter Image, CAT3D, SparseFusion, SRT, UP‑SRT, RUST, MVSNeRF).
     - Distillation‑based methods (Plenoctrees, [1], [2]).
   - No comparison to an EG3D‑style single network that generates tri‑planes from a latent code (Reviewer 3 suggests this ablation, varying feature dimensions to match micro‑macro capacity).
   - The comparison with Tri‑Planes is potentially unfair because the proposed method increases model capacity; an apple‑to‑apple comparison requires aligning capacity.

2. **Limited experimental scope** (Reviewers 1,4,5):
   - Experiments only on single‑category object‑level datasets (cars, faces) with relatively uniform variation. No evaluation on real‑world scenes (e.g., ScanNet), multi‑category datasets, varying textures and lighting, or large spatial depth.
   - The method claims “scaled inverse graphics” for large sets of 3D *scenes* but is only demonstrated on objects; scalability to complex, scene‑level data is unverified.
   - No demonstration of what the macro tri‑plane actually learns (e.g., renderings or 3D models from macro alone). This makes it unclear whether the macro captures meaningful global features.

3. **Weak motivation for the problem** (Reviewer 2, confidence 5):
   - The stated goal of reducing resource costs at inference time is arguably better solved by existing fast methods (3DGS, InstantNGP – seconds), lower‑memory NeRFs (NeRFLight – 10‑30 MB), or distillation into compact representations (Plenoctrees). The proposed method still requires 31 hours of pre‑training + 2.2 min per scene, and 360 MB memory, which is orders of magnitude slower and larger than these alternatives.
   - The introduction highlights the prohibitive cost of creating NeRF datasets, but the method still requires constructing a large dataset for Stage‑1 pre‑training, so this problem is not addressed.

4. **Misleading memory claim** (Reviewer 2, confidence 5):
   - The paper claims memory savings by reducing trainable features per scene (F/F_mic). However, the global (macro) features must still be stored in memory to decode each scene, so there is no actual memory saving from the core technical contribution.

5. **Insufficient ablation and analysis** (Reviewers 3,4,5):
   - No ablation on the effect of splitting data between the two stages (ratio scenes in Stage‑1 vs Stage‑2) on rendering quality and convergence speed.
   - No ablation comparing the two‑stage pipeline versus direct training on all scenes (what quantitative benefit does Stage‑2 bring?).
   - No analysis of adding more stages beyond two.
   - Lack of ablation on the macro tri‑plane representation: e.g., render from macro only to assess what it captures.
   - The use of disjoint subsets (line 134‑138) is not justified; why can’t the same scenes be used in both stages?

6. **Clarity issues** (Reviewer 4, confidence 5):
   - “S describes N scenes drawn from a common distribution” (line 134‑135) – not specified whether common distribution means same object category or scene type (e.g., living room).
   - Definitions of x_{i,j} and p_{i,j} are unclear; they likely refer to images and camera poses, but notation is ambiguous.
   - “Encoded ground truth image and latent image” (line 230‑231) needs clarification: are these latent codes of ground truth images?
