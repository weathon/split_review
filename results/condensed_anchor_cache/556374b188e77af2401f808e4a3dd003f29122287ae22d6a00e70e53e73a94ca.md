## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes AdamQLR, a modification of Adam that incorporates damping and learning rate selection heuristics from K-FAC. The method estimates curvature via a second-order Taylor expansion and updates the damping factor λ and learning rate α based on the ratio ρ between actual and predicted loss reduction. AdamQLR is evaluated on Rosenbrock, UCI Energy/Protein, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, and (in the appendix) ImageNet and Penn Treebank, comparing tuned and untuned versions against SGD, Adam, and K-FAC.

### Strengths

- The idea of combining first-order and second-order optimization approaches is interesting and addresses an important research direction (R1, R3).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to understand (R1, R4).
- Code is provided (R4).
- The algorithm modification is clearly described, and the motivation for adopting K-FAC’s heuristics is well explained (R2).
- The experimental setup and hyperparameter search space are described clearly (R2).
- Optimal hyper-parameters are listed in Table 2 (R4).
- An ablation study for the Levenberg-Marquardt heuristic is included (R4).
- Extensive numerical experiments are conducted (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and significance**: The paper lacks novelty and originality, consisting of a facile combination of existing heuristics from K-FAC applied to Adam (R4). R2 finds the theoretical result insignificant, as it merely moves heuristics from one optimizer to another without evidence that it should perform better in theory.
- **Experimental results are not convincingly strong**: On most tasks (UCI Energy/Protein, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, Penn Treebank), AdamQLR does not outperform Adam or K-FAC in terms of generalization performance vs. runtime, contradicting the abstract (R4). K-FAC achieves the best test loss/accuracy in most experiments (R2, R4). On larger models/datasets (ImageNet, Penn Treebank), AdamQLR is outperformed by all other optimizers (R2). Results are too close and not strongly in favor of AdamQLR (R1).
- **Inadequate evaluation and reporting**: Figure 2 is unclear; the paper should provide tables with training loss, test loss, convergence steps, and generalization gap (R1). Accuracy values are not reported for classification tasks, making it hard to assess final performance from plots alone (R1, R4). Only performance vs. wall-clock time is given; performance vs. epochs should also be shown (R2). The generalization gap (overfitting) is not analyzed (R1). Tasks used are too simple; results on complex tasks like ImageNet are not convincing (R1, R4). The Rosenbrock function example adds little value and is unfair because it uses the Hessian explicitly (R2, R4).
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: The paper does not show results for an untuned version of Adam (R2) or an untuned version of K-FAC (R2). It is therefore unclear whether untuned AdamQLR truly performs similarly to tuned Adam. R4 suggests that the research flow should first have tested K-FAC without the heuristics that are being transferred.
- **Increased hyperparameter burden**: AdamQLR adds hyperparameters (λ, or w_dec and w_inc) beyond Adam’s β₁, β₂, ε (R2). R3 asks about the principle for setting w_dec and w_inc and why the threshold values 3/4 and 1/4 for ρ are chosen. The approach requires considerable tuning, suggesting numerical instability (R4). The learning rate clipping (α_max) introduces another heuristic not present in K-FAC, making comparisons unfair (R4).
- **Computational efficiency claims**: Adapting K-FAC heuristics to Adam is not natural because Adam uses a diagonal Hessian proxy, while AdamQLR requires full Hessian-vector products. This incurs an additional backward pass (not just a forward pass as claimed), so calling AdamQLR computationally-efficient is misleading (R4).
- **Specific experiment issues**:
    - **Fashion-MNIST**: The claim that K-FAC overfits much earlier is wrong – K-FAC actually achieves the best test accuracy faster than all methods; stopping earlier would make K-FAC the best (R2).
    - **SVHN**: The training loss for Untuned AdamQLR shows frequent large decreases that are unexplained; this may be due to more frequent learning rate decay than other optimizers (R4).
    - **Batch size**: The batch-size comparison is unfair because different batch sizes yield different numbers of optimization steps and require manual scaling of the learning rate (R4). The section on batch size seems redundant since larger batch sizes trivially improve convergence (R2).
- **Momentum modelling**: The paper omits K-FAC’s momentum heuristic, which minimizes the quadratic approximation directly, and instead uses Adam’s momentum for the gradient. This is a fundamentally different use of momentum and should be discussed as an omitted heuristic (R4).
- **Learning rate behavior**: AdamQLR sometimes increases the learning rate, which is problematic in stochastic optimization because decaying the learning rate is needed to control gradient noise and ensure convergence (R4).
- **Manuscript inconsistencies**: The abstract and introduction claim competitive generalization performance vs. runtime, but the results do not support this (R4). Statements such as “outperforms classical damping from e.g. K-FAC” are unclear and not substantiated (R4).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**:
    - How is the curvature matrix C in Eq. 1 computed? The paper states only one additional forward pass, but Hessian-vector-products require an additional backward pass (R3, R4).
    - How does AdamQLR behave on NLP tasks? (R4)
