## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes MoreauPruner, a structured pruning method for LLMs that uses the Moreau envelope to estimate weight importance, aiming for robustness against weight perturbations (e.g., between bfloat16 and float16). Reviewers generally found the paper well-written and the research question interesting, but raised significant concerns about limited novelty, marginal performance gains over baselines, restricted model scale, missing baselines, and unclear motivation for the importance of perturbation robustness. The most positive reviewer (score 6) appreciated the clever use of the Moreau envelope and theoretical insight, but noted that the pruner does not achieve significant improvements over competitors (Tables 2–6) and that the main impact is resilience to perturbations. The most negative reviewer (score 3) considered the novelty incremental due to substantial overlap with prior work (MoreauGrad [ICCV 2023]), and argued that the method only improves gradient-based pruning, which is not competitive with SOTA structured pruning methods (e.g., An et al. AAAI 2024, SliceGPT, SLEEP). Other reviewers echoed concerns about marginal improvements, limited model scale, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths

- Well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4, R5).
- The key research question—robustness of pruning masks to weight perturbations—is interesting and thought-provoking (R2, R4).
- The use of the Moreau envelope is mathematically well-grounded and provides theoretical insight into robustness (R1, R4).
- Thorough experiments and ablation studies demonstrate the method’s effectiveness under weight perturbations (R2).
- Raises awareness of instabilities in gradient-based pruning due to data format changes (R2).
- Empirically shown to handle parameter perturbations, e.g., across bfloat16/float16 transitions (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty & overlap with prior work:** The theoretical contributions and use of the Moreau envelope show significant overlap with MoreauGrad [Zhang & Farnia, ICCV 2023]. Definitions 1,2,3 and Algorithm 1 lines 1–9 are nearly identical to those in [1] (R3, R5). This makes the contribution incremental rather than groundbreaking (R3, R5).
- **Marginal performance improvements:** MoreauPruner does not achieve significant perplexity or accuracy gains over previous methods (Tables 2–6). At low pruning ratios (e.g., 5%), it yields higher perplexity than LLM-Pruner (R4, R5). At higher ratios, improvements are marginal (R1, R2, R4, R5). The most impactful result is robustness to perturbations, not overall accuracy (R1).
- **Restricted model scale and families:** Experiments focus on small LLMs (~7B parameters: LLaMA-7B, LLaMA-13B, LLaMA3-8B, Vicuna-7B). Reviewers asked for evaluations on larger models (e.g., 70B) and other families such as Mistral, Qwen, OPT, BLOOM, LLaMA-3.1, or Gemma-2 (R2, R3, R4).
- **Missing important baselines:** Several recent state-of-the-art structured pruning methods for LLMs are not compared, e.g., Fluctuation-based Adaptive Structured Pruning [An et al. AAAI 2024], SliceGPT [Ashkboos et al. 2024], and SLEEP [Shen et al. 2024] (R5).
- **Unclear motivation for perturbation robustness:** The paper does not convincingly explain why inconsistency across minor weight perturbations (e.g., bfloat16 vs. float16) is problematic. Different pruning masks can be equally good (citing Srinivas & Babu 2015) (R4). Table 1 does not strongly support the instability claim—most perplexity differences are minimal except at 20% pruning on PTB (R2). The need for robustness beyond format transitions remains unconvincing (R3). Reviewer 4 suggests framing motivation from other sources of perturbation (e.g., pruning itself affects gradient evaluations, LoRA updates perturb weights).
- **Missing computational cost analysis:** No reporting of required memory, time, or effort to obtain pruning masks. The paper mentions 30 minutes pruning on CPU, but does not specify the model size, whether hyperparameter search is included, or why CPU is chosen over GPU (R4, R5 questions). Comparison to other robust pruning methods on computational efficiency is needed (R3).
- **Writing and clarity issues:** Terminology “structural pruning” should be “structured pruning”; several typos (“anti-intuitive” → “counterintuitive”, “easy obtained” → “easily obtained”, “effiently” → “efficiently”, “envelop” → “envelope”, “hyper-paramter” in Figure 4 caption) (R4). The introduction of Section 4.1 (Moreau-Yosida regularization) is abrupt with little motivation (R2). Theorem 1 and 2 appear unrelated to the empirical method (R2).
- **Limited perturbation scope:** Robustness is only demonstrated for bfloat16/float16 transitions, which is a niche scenario. More generalized perturbation testing (e.g., weight noise, LoRA updates) is needed (R3, R4).
- **Lack of experiments at higher pruning ratios:** Reviewers request experiments at 70–90% pruning, as attempted by recent algorithms (R1).
- **Clarifications needed:** How Soft Grad and GS pruners enforce sparsity (R1). Whether the GST function is effectively a soft L1 regularizer (R1). Calibration dataset assumption (line 158) – clarify if it is a general assumption (R1). Distinguish evaluation of fine-tuned vs. non-fine-tuned models (R1). Need more fine-grained precision experiments (R1).
