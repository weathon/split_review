## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reformulates image attribution as a submodular subset selection problem, aiming to produce more interpretable explanations using fewer, fine-grained regions. It introduces a novel submodular function guided by four scores (confidence, effectiveness, consistency, collaboration) and a greedy search algorithm. Experiments on Celeb-A, VGG-Face2, and CUB-200-2011 show improvements over SOTA methods, especially for incorrectly predicted samples (e.g., 81.0% gain in average highest confidence vs. HSIC-Attribution). The method is also used to discover causes of prediction errors.

### Strengths
- Novel reformulation of the image attribution problem as a submodular subset selection problem, enabling higher interpretability with fewer fine-grained regions (R1, R2, R3).
- Significant quantitative improvements are reported: 4.9% and 2.5% average gains in Deletion/Insertion over HSIC-Attribution for correct predictions; an 81.0% gain in average highest confidence and an 18.4% gain in Insertion for incorrect predictions (R1, R2).
- The method effectively identifies regions that cause model prediction errors, aiding in debugging and model improvement (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The four interpretability clues (confidence, effectiveness, consistency, collaboration) are well motivated and supported by theoretical and empirical results (R1).
- The approach has a different search-based mechanism compared to perturbation- and gradient-based methods (R4).
- Theoretical guarantees for the submodular function are provided (R1, R3).
- Detailed analyses and additional experiments/visualizations are included in the appendix (R4).
- Evaluation on face recognition and fine-grained recognition tasks demonstrates practical applicability (R1, R3).
- Inclusion of an ablation study is valued (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of related work citation and differentiation**: The paper does not cite decomposition methods (e.g., Noroozi et al., 2016 for self-supervision; Redmon & Farhadi, 2018 for object detection) or standard patch/superpixel segmentation techniques. The differences from these region-partition approaches need to be explicitly stated (R1).  
- **Unclear use of saliency maps in sub-region division**: How the a-priori saliency map A guides sub-region selection and groups semantically meaningful regions is not explained (R1).  
- **Missing justification for scoring metrics**: The scoring functions rely on cosine similarity/distance without citing relevant literature (e.g., Deng et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2018) or clarifying how they are adapted/offer advantages over other distance measures (R1).  
- **Insufficient explanation of individual score roles**: The specific role of each score (e.g., s_colla. using cosine distance) in the overall objective is not clearly articulated; the paper should explain how they interact to achieve the desired attribution (R1).  
- **Greedy search algorithm as prior art**: The greedy subset selection algorithm is standard (Wei et al., 2015) and novelty versus existing work should be clarified (R1).  
- **Missing submodular optimization reference**: The paper omits a critical reference *Fujishige (2005)*; this undermines the claim of novelty in submodular optimization (R1).  
- **Missing results for LIME and Kernel SHAP on CUB**: Table 1 does not report these methods for CUB. Their absence needs justification (technical limitations?) and, if applicable, they should be included (R3, R4).  
- **Incomplete evaluation of incorrect-prediction analysis**: Section 5.3 validates only on ResNet; additional backbones (VGGNet, MobileNet, EfficientNet) are needed to demonstrate generalizability across architectures (R4).  
- **Contradictory effect of a-priori saliency map**: Table 2 shows that the method without an a-priori saliency map sometimes outperforms the version with it in average highest confidence. An ablation study on partition sizes (e.g., 8×8, 12×12) without the a-priori map is required (R4).  
- **Ablation on parameter k**: The number of selected sub-regions (k) is not ablated; its impact on attribution quality is unknown (R1).  
- **Algorithm 1 typo**: The input *k* is not used; line 3 should likely use *k* instead of *n* (R3, R4).  
- **Method classification**: It is unclear whether the method (excluding the prior saliency map) is white-box or black-box (R3).  
- **Minor textual issues**:  
  - “at the level of theoretical aspects” is wordy (R2).  
  - Phrasing of image attribution definition can be improved (R2).  
  - “fine-grainedness” is not a standard word (R2).  
  - Redundant word “datasets.” in introduction (R2).  
  - “the image attribution algorithm” implies a unique algorithm; use specific names or plural (R2).  
- **Figure 1 ambiguity**: The left plot’s blue curve dips near 0 around 0.8—explanation needed (R2).  
- **Visualization clarity**: The method claims to use *fewer* regions, but figures sometimes show *more* highlighted regions—clarification needed (R2).  
- **Algorithmic complexity**: A runtime/complexity comparison with HSIC-Attribution is missing (R2).  
- **Relation to attention**: The paper should relate image attribution to attention mechanisms in neural networks (R2).  
- **Meaning of “fine-grained interpretation regions”**: This term is not defined (R2).  
- **“Validity” of submodular function**: The meaning should be clarified (R2).  
- **Section 4.1 presentation**: The sub-region division procedure should be presented as an algorithm for clarity (R1).  
- **Figure 1 labels**: Input I and attribution map A should be labeled in Figure 1 (R1).  
- **Clarify variable M** in problem definition (sections 3 and 4.2) (R1).  
- **Redundancy in lemmas/theorem**: Lemma 1, Lemma 2, and Theorem 1 duplicate prior definitions/theorems; Theorem 1 is from Nemhauser et al. (1978) and should be cited rather than restated (R1).  
- **Notation set**: A set of notations would improve readability given the broad scope (R1).  
- **Limitations of the method**: A discussion of limitations is absent (R3).
