## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LOZO and LOZO-M, low-rank zeroth-order optimization algorithms for memory-efficient fine-tuning of LLMs. The core contributions are a low-rank ZO gradient estimator and a lazy sampling strategy that reuses the perturbation matrix over several training steps. The paper provides theoretical convergence guarantees by framing LOZO as a subspace optimization method. Experiments on OPT models up to 30B show that LOZO outperforms existing ZO methods (e.g., MeZO) and approaches FO performance while using less memory.

### Strengths
1. **Novel and well-motivated algorithm**: The low-rank ZO gradient estimator and lazy sampling strategy directly address a critical limitation of prior ZO methods (e.g., MeZO) in capturing low-rank gradient structures; the approach is clearly outlined and innovative. (R1, R2, R4)
2. **Momentum integration with negligible overhead**: LOZO-M incorporates momentum that requires almost no extra memory cost, a clever and practical extension. (R1, R2)
3. **Solid theoretical convergence guarantees**: The paper provides convergence analysis by interpreting LOZO as a subspace optimization method; the proof appears correct and reasonable. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
4. **Clear writing and presentation**: The problem, methodology, and results are well-organized and clearly presented. (R3, R4)
5. **Extensive experiments on medium and large LLMs**: Convincing quality gains and memory savings are demonstrated on OPT models up to 30B, with comparisons to MeZO, ICL, and LoRA. (R2, R4; note R1 finds model diversity limited – see weaknesses)

### Weaknesses
1. **Memory efficiency claims insufficiently demonstrated**  
   – The paper lacks direct memory comparisons with first-order full fine-tuning (FFT) and FT-LoRA, which weakens the claim of memory efficiency relative to FO methods. (R1)  
   – Moreover, the memory savings over MeZO appear marginal: Table 1 shows LOZO reduces memory from ~3–7.42 GB (MeZO) to 2.84 GB, which does not constitute a substantial practical reduction. The core issue is that the low-rank approximation, while theoretically sound, does not translate to a significant memory footprint advantage beyond what MeZO already provides. (R3)

2. **Limited evaluation benchmarks**  
   – Experiments are primarily on SuperGLUE, which is insufficient to demonstrate generalizability. The evaluation lacks critical benchmarks for common sense reasoning (e.g., MMLU) and complex reasoning (e.g., GSM8k), raising concerns about whether the method enhances high-level cognitive abilities. (R1, R2, R3)  
   – The paper claims LOZO can handle long-context tasks, but SuperGLUE does not support this claim. (R1)

3. **Lack of model diversity**  
   – All experiments are conducted on a single model family (OPT). Scalability and effectiveness on other architectures (e.g., Llama) are not demonstrated. (R1, R2)  
   – Questions remain about memory savings and performance on larger models (e.g., OPT-13B or Llama-70B). (R3)

4. **Unfair comparison and unclear hyperparameter control**  
   – The learning rate used for LOZO differs from that for MeZO (see Table 4), making it difficult to isolate the impact of the low-rank perturbation from hyperparameter tuning. A controlled experiment using the same learning rate (or a detailed analysis of its effect) is needed. (R4)  
   – An ablation study on hyperparameter choices for *N* (number of iterations for lazy sampling) and rank *r* is missing. (R2)  
   – The total training steps for LOZO vs. MeZO are unclear: Figure 2 may represent epochs or shots, and LOZO may have more steps due to the additional interval in each epoch. (R4)

5. **MeZO-LoRA discrepancy contradicts prior work**  
   – In the experiments, MeZO-LoRA performs worse than MeZO, contrary to the MeZO paper (Table 18 of that paper) where MeZO-LoRA usually performs better. This is especially puzzling for small models like RoBERTa where LoRA should help (only ~0.8 M trainable parameters). The discrepancy needs explanation and raises reproducibility concerns. (R4)

6. **Limited improvement relative to variance**  
   – The performance improvement of LOZO over MeZO is modest, especially considering the large variance of ZO methods across random seeds. (R4)  
   – The benefit of momentum on large-scale models (LOZO-M) is unclear; momentum may become less important due to high estimation variance. (R4)

7. **Missing wall-time convergence analysis**  
   – The paper does not provide evaluation loss vs. wall-time figures, which would better demonstrate whether the low-rank perturbation improves convergence speed in practice (more critical than number of steps). (R4)

8. **No evaluation on complex LLM capabilities**  
   – There are no experiments assessing the impact of LOZO on instruction following, reasoning, or other complex tasks, limiting the practical utility claims. (R2, R3)
