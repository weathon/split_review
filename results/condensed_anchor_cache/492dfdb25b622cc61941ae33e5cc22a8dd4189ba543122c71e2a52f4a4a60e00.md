## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes a one-layer Transformer trained on 5-digit integer addition, proposing a mathematical framework that decomposes the task into subtasks (Base Add, Make Carry 1, Make Sum 9, Use Carry 1, Use Sum 9) and examining loss curves, attention patterns, and ablation experiments. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: two reviewers (scores 8) find the work novel and well-organized, providing solid mathematical interpretation and interesting visualizations that support trust in Transformer mechanisms. The other two reviewers (scores 3) criticize the lack of mathematical rigor, poor writing, limited generalizability, and missing quantitative evaluations. One reviewer (score 3) notes the paper is technically solid but suffers from insufficient clarity and experimental completeness.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important problem of opening the black box of Transformers, a topic of significant and growing importance.
- The decomposition of integer addition into subtasks (BA, UC1, US9) and analysis of per-task loss provides inspiration for future improvements and model understanding.
- Paper is well-organized with a clean, easy-to-follow basic idea (per Reviewer 4).
- Interesting visualization of training loss curves showing semi-independent learning of digit positions, and meaningful attention visualizations (per Reviewer 2).
- Solid theoretical framework and interpretation of model workings (per Reviewer 2). One reviewer (score 3) acknowledges the work as technically solid with moderate to high impact.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of mathematical rigor and formal definitions.** The analysis framework lacks a precise mathematical formulation; conclusions about digit-specific parallel streams and algorithms are not formally linked to weights, activations, or computations. The paper does not specify mathematical properties, assumptions, or how the framework relates to observed behavior. (Reviewers 1 and 3)
- **Insufficient clarity and poor writing.** Descriptions of Figure 4 and Figure 8 are difficult to comprehend; the connection between figures and text is weak. The mathematical framework is not presented in an easily digestible way, lacking concrete step-by-step examples. (Reviewer 3) Multiple typographical errors in the text: “d_e-dimensional embeddings” (typo), “detailing identified circuits” (unclear identification process), “Surveys like  overview techniques…” (missing citation), and grammar errors. (Reviewer 1) Figure 3 caption ambiguity: “layer 11” likely refers to row 11, sub-figures 0.0, 0.1, 0.2 are not labeled; row/column numbering should be consistent. (Reviewer 1) Section 7’s plain English analysis is difficult to follow; “independent of every other digit” and “most impact on loss” are not formally defined with statistics. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited generalizability and scope.** The methodology only applies to a one-layer Transformer on integer addition; extension to multi-layer models, other operations (subtraction, multiplication), or different attention mechanisms is not addressed. The analysis is tightly coupled to this specific architecture and task, requiring new analysis for even slightly different settings. (Reviewers 1 and 3) The paper does not justify why a single-layer architecture is sufficient for drawing general conclusions about Transformers. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing quantitative evaluation and baselines.** Test accuracy of the trained model is not provided. The “Prediction Analysis” section lacks specific metrics and comparisons to baseline models, making it difficult to assess significance of findings. (Reviewers 3 and 4) No exploration of using the trained model for addition of integers with more digits. (Reviewer 4)
- **Incomplete consideration of digit 0.** The mathematical framework does not treat digit 0 as a special case (e.g., Make Sum 0/Use Sum 0), which is intuitive for human calculation and may affect the model’s behavior. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unsubstantiated claims about autoregressive generation.** Claim that “Transformer models always process text from left to right” is inaccurate; attention masking need not be left-to-right, and non-autoregressive generation is possible. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing formal definitions for phases and loss averaging.** Phases 1, 2, 3 are not formally defined. The loss averaging (per digit or per digit average) is not detailed. The “mathematical framework” description does not clarify whether it characterizes digits only or links formally to loss. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minority contrast:** While two reviewers (scores 8) praise the mathematical explanations and visualizations as clear and solid, two reviewers (scores 3) find those same explanations lacking in rigor and insufficiently formalized—this disagreement is not resolved in the manuscript.
