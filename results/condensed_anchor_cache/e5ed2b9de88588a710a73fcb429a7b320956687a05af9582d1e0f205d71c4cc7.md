## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PyramidKV, a KV cache compression method for LLMs, based on the observation that attention scores are more uniform in lower layers and more peaked in higher layers (termed “Pyramidal Information Funneling”). PyramidKV dynamically allocates cache budget: more cache for lower layers, less for higher layers, using a linear schedule. Experiments on LongBench (8k context) and Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) show it matches full KV cache performance with 12% cache and outperforms baselines under extreme compression (0.7% cache, e.g., 20.5 absolute accuracy gain on TREC). With only 128 KV cache entries, LLAMA-3-70B achieves 100% Acc. on NIAH.

### Strengths
- The observation of pyramidal attention patterns across layers (lower layers have uniform attention; higher layers concentrate on critical tokens) is valuable and insightful.
- The method is straightforward, uses different compression rates per layer, and can be combined with other KV cache algorithms.
- Under extreme compression (e.g., 0.7% cache, 128 KV entries), PyramidKV significantly outperforms existing methods (e.g., 20.5 absolute accuracy improvement on TREC) and preserves long-context capabilities (100% Acc. on NIAH for LLAMA-3-70B with 128 cache).
- Experiments are comprehensive, covering LongBench tasks, NIAH, and multiple models (including LLAMA-3-70B). Several challenging long-context scenarios are examined.
- Paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow regarding setup and results.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The observation that attention becomes more skewed in higher layers is not new; similar patterns and linearly decreasing cache allocations have been described in prior work (e.g., InfiniGen, MagicPIG, PyramidInfer). Extending existing methods to use layer-wise sampling ratios is straightforward, limiting novelty (Reviewer 1, 4; both note this).
- **Suboptimal performance at moderate budgets**: When the KV cache budget is 2048 (not extremely compressed), PyramidKV does not consistently outperform baselines in Table 1. No ablation is provided to explain why the method fails to gain advantage at moderate compression (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Allocation strategy insufficiently justified**: 
  - The paper does not explain why a *linear* schedule (arithmetic sequence) is chosen over other possible schedules, or what observations motivate this specific allocation (Reviewer 1, 4).
  - The allocation formula uses a hyperparameter \(\beta\) (given \(k_0 = 2k_{\text{total}}/m\) and \(k_{m-1} = k_{\text{total}}/(\beta m) - k_0\)). It is unclear whether \(\beta\) is determined by the budget sum constraint or is a free parameter; empirical validation of the linear allocation versus alternatives (e.g., nonlinear or learned allocations) is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing long-context evaluation**: Models are primarily tested with 8k context length; tests with sequences >128k are absent. This is important for validating the method’s scalability (Reviewer 3).
- **Potential masking by attention averaging**: The attention analysis averages across heads per layer, which might obscure "massive attention" (retrieval heads) present in lower layers. Additional experiments per head are needed to confirm the pyramidal pattern is not an artifact of averaging (Reviewer 2).
- **NIAH task specification unclear**: The paper does not state which NIAH variant was used (e.g., repetitive sentences vs. long corpus), reducing reproducibility (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons with recent methods**: Under extreme compression, comparisons with Minference (or similarly recent techniques) are lacking (Reviewer 3).
- **No analysis of which tasks benefit vs. underperform**: Reviewers ask for insights on task categories where pyramidal allocation is most/least effective (Reviewer 4). This could help understand the method’s scope.
- **Practical implementation concerns**: It is unclear whether PyramidKV can be efficiently implemented in real systems (e.g., vLLM/SGLang) due to frequent arbitrary memory allocation/release, potential memory fragmentation, and speed-up changes under tensor/pipeline parallelism (Reviewer 5).
- **Hyperparameter selection**: How to choose per-layer budgets (e.g., the exact sequence of cache sizes) to ensure optimal accuracy is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues**: Many figures are not vector graphics (blurry when enlarged); Figure 3 is repetitive with Figure 1; Figure 2 is hard to interpret (histogram/CDF would be better); Section 5 has too many subsections; Figure 5’s grid and axis labels are too small; right half of Table 2 is not clearly explained (Reviewer 1).

**Minority disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (rating 3) finds the paper lacks novelty and the empirical results are unimpressive overall, while Reviewer 5 (rating 8) praises its long-context capabilities and potential for real systems. Most reviewers (4 of 5) agree the observation is valuable but note novelty and performance limitations.
