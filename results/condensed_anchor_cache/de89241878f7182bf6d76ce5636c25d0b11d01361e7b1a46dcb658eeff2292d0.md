## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles training instability in continuous-time consistency models (CMs). The authors first unify EDM and Flow Matching into a simplified diffusion formulation called TrigFlow, which normalizes the noise and data schedules. Building on this, they analyze the continuous CM objective and identify multiple sources of instability: inappropriate time embedding scaling, the use of standard AdaGN layers, high variance in the target norm, and unbalanced loss terms. They propose targeted fixes: replacing Fourier time embeddings with positional embeddings, replacing AdaGN with Adaptive Double Normalization, adding tangent normalization and a linear warm-up on the time derivative, and introducing an adaptive weighting function. For scaling, they contribute JVP Rearrangement and a JVP of Flash Attention to reduce the memory and compute overhead of forward-mode differentiation. The resulting method, sCM (sCT for training from scratch, sCD for distillation), achieves state-of-the-art one‑ and two‑step FID scores on CIFAR-10 (2.06), ImageNet 64×64 (1.48), and ImageNet 512×512 (1.88), narrowing the gap to the best diffusion models to within 10%. The model is scaled up to 1.5B parameters on ImageNet 512×512 and matches or surpasses adversarial methods, score distillation, and autoregressive models at similar sampling budgets.

### Strengths
- **Novel unification and analysis.** TrigFlow provides the first theoretical unification of EDM and Flow Matching, simplifying the connection between schedule parameters and model scaling. The paper presents the first gradient‑based analysis of continuous‑time CMs, identifying the root causes of training instability (Reviewers 1,2,4,5).
- **Comprehensive, empirically supported fixes.** Each proposed stabilization technique (positional time embeddings, Adaptive Double Normalization, tangent normalization, linear warm‑up, adaptive weighting) is motivated by a specific source of variance or instability and backed by ablations (Figure 5 and Appendix) (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Strong experimental results.** sCMs outperform all previous CMs and other one‑/two‑step generative methods, achieving FIDs within 10% of the best diffusion models. The method scales to 1.5B parameters on ImageNet 512×512. Scaling behavior is studied across model sizes and datasets (Reviewers 1,2,4,5).
- **Efficient training with large models.** JVP Rearrangement and JVP of Flash Attention substantially reduce the memory and compute cost of forward‑mode differentiation, making large‑scale continuous‑time CM training feasible (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear presentation.** The paper is well‑structured, with intuitive motivation for each contribution. The appendix provides further insights (Reviewers 1,2,3,5).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ablation for Adaptive Double Normalization.** Reviewers 3 and 5 note that the paper only claims it “removes instability in CM training” without showing a direct comparison (e.g., training curves, FID) to standard AdaGN. An ablation similar to Figure 5 is requested.  
- **Adaptive weighting hurts two‑step performance.** Figure 5(b) shows that the adaptive weighting function slightly improves one‑step FID but worsens two‑step FID. The paper does not explain why or propose an alternative design for the two‑step setting (Reviewers 3,5).  
- **No ablation for tangent warm‑up.** The linear warm‑up of the time derivative term is introduced without an ablation comparing training dynamics (gradient magnitudes, loss curves) with and without it (Reviewer 3).  
- **sCT vs. sCD behavior at scale not explained.** On ImageNet 512×512, sCT (training from scratch) performs significantly worse than sCD (distillation), while on smaller datasets sCT is better. Multiple reviewers (1,3,5) ask for an analysis: is the gap due to increased variance from the latent encoder/decoder? Do CMs require a different latent space? Why does scaling (model size, batch size) not close the gap at 512×512?  
- **Comparison with discrete‑time CMs on cost.** Continuous‑time training requires JVP computation, which adds time and memory overhead even with Flash Attention. Reviewer 5 requests a detailed comparison with discrete‑time ECMs (ECMs) in terms of training convergence, memory usage, and inference cost.  
- **Comparison with recent flow models.** Reviewer 4 asks for comparisons with more recent flow methods (Tong et al. 2024, Kornilov et al. 2024) and rectified flows with two generation steps, even on a small dataset.  
- **Missing compute/parameter details for fair comparison.** Table 1 does not include the number of parameters or number of training updates for each baseline (Reviewer 4). This info is needed to judge whether comparisons are compute‑matched.  
- **Fairness of discrete‑time CM baseline in Figure 5(c).** Reviewer 5 questions whether the discrete‑time CM baseline used a fixed number of steps or an optimally scheduled step schedule. If the latter, the comparison may be unfair to continuous‑time CMs.  
- **TTUR not applied to VSD baseline.** In Figure 7, the paper uses TTUR (from DMD2) for its models but does not apply it to the VSD baseline. Reviewer 5 requests a VSD+TTUR comparison to ensure a consistent evaluation.  
- **Conditioning on guidance scale.** sCD conditions the generator on the guidance scale; Reviewer 5 asks whether the VSD baseline also conditions on the guidance scale for a fair comparison.  
- **Generalization to text‑to‑image.** The method is only tested on class‑conditional image generation; Reviewer 3 questions how the techniques transfer to text‑to‑image (more complex architectures, data, and training procedures).  
- **Limited discussion of limitations.** Reviewers 2 and 3 note that the paper does not clearly state the limitations of the method beyond being 10% behind diffusion models. Are there failure modes, residual instabilities, or datasets where the approach breaks?  
- **Completeness of architecture analysis.** The section on positional embeddings (Section 4.1) is considered insufficiently self‑contained (Reviewer 2). Figure 3 is seen as not very informative (Reviewer 2).  
- **Typos and minor inaccuracies.**  
  - Line 362 “cause instability” → “causes instability” (Reviewer 2).  
  - Line 126 norm missing square (Reviewer 4).  
  - Line 122 `z_t` does not depend on time (Reviewer 4).  
  - Line 201/202: `c_skip` and `c_out` may be mis‑stated (`sigma_t` and `alpha_t * sigma_d`?) (Reviewers 4,5).  
  - Equation (20) in the appendix: `D_theta(x_t) = hat{D}_theta(hat{x}_t(x_t))` likely missing hat (Reviewer 4).  
  - Paragraph lines 924‑938 needs elaboration on geometric invariance (Reviewer 4).  
  - Line 266: `c_noise` formula may be `1/4 log(sigma_d * tan t)`? (Reviewer 5).
