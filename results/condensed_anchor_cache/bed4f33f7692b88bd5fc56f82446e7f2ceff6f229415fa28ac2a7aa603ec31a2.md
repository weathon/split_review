## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a knowledge distillation (KD) method using cosine similarity applied over batch predictions (one vector per class across samples), combined with a Cosine Similarity Weighted Temperature (CSWT) mechanism. The authors report favorable results on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. Reviewer opinions diverge: Reviewers 1 (score 3) and 2 (score 5, raised after author response but still below acceptance bar) are substantially more negative, citing fundamental methodological and clarity issues. Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more positive, praising the novelty and efficiency, while acknowledging a dependence on batch size. Despite the disagreement, all reviewers express significant concerns that prevent acceptance.

### Strengths
- The concept of using cosine similarity for individual class information transfer is a departure from traditional KD techniques and offers a novel perspective. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- The paper clearly identifies the research gap between existing logits-based KD methods (which use KL divergence over class predictions) and the proposed batch-level cosine similarity approach. (Reviewer 2)
- The scale-invariant property of cosine similarity is leveraged to allow the student to learn direction rather than being bound to a fixed distribution, potentially enabling more efficient learning. (Reviewer 3)
- The Cosine Similarity Weighted Temperature (CSWT) technique adjusts temperature adaptively based on cosine similarity, which may refine knowledge transfer for each sample. (Reviewer 3)
- Comprehensive experiments, including entropy analyses and ablation studies, are conducted. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison with KL-divergence-based methods (e.g., SHAKE, DKD; both 2022).**  
  The paper lacks a direct comparison and discussion explaining how the scale-invariance of cosine similarity offers advantages over distribution matching with KL divergence. The theoretical explanation of why relaxing the strict matching constraint benefits knowledge transfer is insufficient. (Reviewer 1)

- **Insufficient exploration of the relationship between the proposed method and existing KD techniques.**  
  The paper only provides result comparisons without delving into the theoretical underpinnings of why cosine similarity might be a better metric for transferring “dark knowledge” than KL divergence. A deeper related-work discussion of differences is needed. (Reviewer 1)

- **Unclear motivation and poor explanation of the loss function and temperature parameter.**  
  * The temperature parameter `T` is introduced in Eq. (3) but its specific setting is never clarified.  
  * In Eq. (9), two loss functions are introduced with only one balancing factor, causing confusion.  
  * The role of temperature in the cosine similarity loss and how it affects learning dynamics is not explained. (Reviewer 1)

- **Critical notation and methodological clarity issues regarding the cosine similarity loss.**  
  * The meaning of “[:,j]” and “[i,:]” in Eqs. (2) and (3) is not defined; the colon notation is ambiguous.  
  * Given both student and teacher predictions are matrices `R^{B×C}`, it is unclear how cosine similarity (Eq. (1)) is computed between matrices.  
  * It appears the loss focuses on only one class `j` per batch (confirmed by Eq. (9)), which should be explicitly clarified earlier. (Reviewer 2)

- **Dependence on batch size and dimensionality of class vectors.**  
  Cosine similarity is scale-invariant, but the significance of a given similarity value depends on the vector length (number of samples in batch × number of classes). For example, a value of 0.4 in a 200‑d vector may be large, while 0.7 in a 5‑d vector may not imply similarity. Since the vector length is determined by batch size, different batch sizes yield different student models, even for the same task. This is a limitation when batch size must be adjusted due to memory constraints. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)

- **Figure 4 lacks explanation.**  
  The experiment’s purpose and why batch size affects performance are not explained; the paper should relate this to the underlying principles of cosine similarity KD. (Reviewer 1)

- **Overall writing issues.**  
  * Inconsistent tense usage.  
  * Mixed abbreviations (e.g., Fig. vs. Figure, Table vs. Tab.).  
  * Citation formatting errors.  
  The paper requires careful proofreading. (Reviewer 1)

- **Experimental results are not fully convincing due to unclear methodology and definitions.**  
  Even after the author’s response, the batch-wise cosine similarity mechanism remains unclear, raising doubts about the validity of the extensive experimental comparisons. (Reviewer 2)

- **Question: applicability to out-of-distribution tasks is not addressed.**  
  It is unclear whether the proposed learning paradigm can be extended to out-of-distribution or open-set scenarios. (Reviewer 3)
