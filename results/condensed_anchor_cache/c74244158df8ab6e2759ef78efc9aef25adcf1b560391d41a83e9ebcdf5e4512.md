## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CoMRes, a semi-supervised long-term time series forecasting method built on Pathformer. It introduces a consensus promotion mechanism that aligns supervised predictions from multiple multi-resolution views (obtained via augmentation strategies: Time Warping, Interpolation, Noise Injection) with an aggregated prediction. The approach aims to capture unseen temporal dynamics, reduce error accumulation in long horizons, and improve robustness. The paper also evaluates autoregressive and non-autoregressive decoding schemes. Experiments on multiple datasets and limited-resource scenarios show CoMRes outperforms supervised baselines, though ablation results raise questions about the contribution of individual components.

### Strengths
- The paper is clear, well-written, and understandable (R1, R2).
- The concept of promoting consistency among multi-resolution views is interesting and a novel perspective for time-series forecasting (R2, R3).
- The experimental evaluation covers most commonly used datasets and an exhaustive set of baselines; limited-resource scenarios are a valuable addition (R1, R2).
- The ablation study is in-depth and investigates various components (R4).
- Empirical results indicate the method provides some lift over baselines (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Variance not reported:** No error bars or variance are given for any result, making it impossible to assess whether improvements are statistically significant, especially given small performance differences (R1, R3).
- **Inconclusive ablation results:** In Table 1, MRes with consensus training often does not consistently outperform the MRes baseline (without consensus loss); on several tasks the ablated version performs better. CoMRes significantly underperforms Pathformer on one dataset. This weakens the argument that consensus promotion and/or augmentations are beneficial (R2, R3, R4). The paper lacks clear experimental evidence showing the individual-view models become more consistent or that the unsupervised loss improves the primary task (R3).
- **Missing important baselines:** Comparisons with recent methods like TimeMixer, TiDE, PatchTST, D/NLinear, iTransformer, and other advanced transformers are absent, reducing the strength of the evaluation (R2).
- **Clarity issues:**
  - The title and abstract do not clearly explain why the approach is called “semi-supervised,” given that labels can be constructed by shifting the time window. The role of augmented data and challenges with temporal dynamics need clarification (R2).
  - The statement in lines 031-033 that transformers are superior in forecasting is misleading; multiple recent works (Das et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2023) show transformers are not always state-of-the-art (R2).
  - Table 1 is difficult to read; the meaning of red/blue coloring is not stated in the caption (R2).
  - It is unclear what “multiple resolutions” vs. “augmentations” specifically refer to in the method. The distinction and usage points need a more detailed description (R2).
- **Limited multivariable evaluation:** The limited-resource experiments do not cover datasets with many variables (e.g., Traffic, Electricity), which is a limitation for generalizability (R2).
- **Novelty concerns:** The architecture heavily borrows from Pathformer, adding only an MLP per view and a consensus loss. The consensus-based loss is a natural ensemble regularization; the contribution appears incremental (R4).
- **Computational overhead:** The extra MLP layers and (M+1) forward passes per sample may impose nontrivial training/inference cost; no efficiency analysis or discussion of limitations is provided (R4).
- **Missing analysis of design choices:**
  - The use of Euclidean distance for consensus between aggregated and per-view predictions is assumed without justification; other measures (e.g., soft-DTW) could be considered (R2).
  - No discussion of whether more complex augmentations or other augmentation strategies would work; limitations of the chosen augmentations are not addressed (R2).
  - It is unclear whether MRes (the ablated version) is considered a baseline or part of the method; clarification needed (R2).
- **Robustness not demonstrated:** Claims of robustness compared to traditional supervised learning are not supported by explicit robustness experiments (error propagation, noise sensitivity) (R2).
