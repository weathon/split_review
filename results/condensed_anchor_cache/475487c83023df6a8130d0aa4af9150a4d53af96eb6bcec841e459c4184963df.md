## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a continual learning method based on pathway protection and graph matching for model fusion. It trains a new model per task, then fuses the previous and new models by selecting protected channels and aligning them via graph matching (treated separately for shallow and deep layers). Experiments are reported on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet under both class-incremental (CIL) and task-incremental (TIL) settings.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and relevance** (Reviewer 1: score 5, very positive; Reviewer 2: score 3, positive): The method is well-motivated. It addresses the crucial issue of catastrophic forgetting while asking whether parameter isolation is truly necessary for protecting task knowledge.
- **Empirical performance** (Reviewer 1: very positive; Reviewer 2: positive; Reviewer 3: positive): Outperforms existing baseline methods. Strong results are shown on ResNet with CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet.
- **Evaluation scope** (Reviewer 1): The method is evaluated under both CIL and TIL settings, which is thorough.

### Weaknesses
- **Heuristic layer split** (Reviewer 1): The method treats shallow and deep layers differently, but it is not explained what constitutes “shallow” and “deep.” This choice is heuristic, with no optimal solution for identifying these layers.
- **Overclaim and lack of evidence** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3): The phrase “pathway protection is all you need” (line 80-81) is an overstatement. The method still suffers from forgetting and underperforms memory-based methods (Reviewer 1). No strong empirical evidence is provided against parameter isolation methods; only weak experiments are shown (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 notes the same statement lacks rigorous support.
- **Unclear distinction from prior work** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2): It is unclear how “pathway protection” differs from the hard-attention mechanism in HAT (Reviewer 1: [1] Overcoming catastrophic forgetting with hard attention to the task, ICML 2018). The concept is also conceptually similar to Piggyback, Packnet, and MEAT (Reviewer 2: [1] Meta-Attention for ViT-Backed Continual Learning, CVPR 2022).
- **Significantly underperforms a strong baseline** (Reviewer 1): The method performs significantly worse than the method in [2] (A Theoretical Study on Solving Continual Learning, NeurIPS 2022). For CIL on CIFAR-100: [2] achieves 65.1% (10 splits) and 60% (20 splits); for CIL on Tiny-ImageNet: 48.9% (5 splits) and 47.1% (10 splits). For TIL on CIFAR-100: 93.0% (10 splits) and 95.3% (20 splits); for TIL on Tiny-ImageNet: 68.4% (5 splits) and 74.1% (10 splits). All results for [2] are without a memory buffer.
- **Poor clarity and writing** (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3): The language is perplexing. The term “misaligned fusion” in the abstract is never defined (Reviewer 2). Many details appear only in Figures (e.g., L1, L2, L3, L4 – not explained in text). The difference between “appending” and “matching” is unclear (Reviewer 3). Design choices are not properly motivated (Reviewer 3). The experiments discussion is poor; tables are not always referenced in the text, and wide claims are made without discussing exact numerical results (Reviewer 3).
- **Confusing analogy in graph matching** (Reviewer 2): Line 239-241: “Graph matching bears resemblance to a quadratic assignment problem… with the objective of establishing correspondences between the nodes in an image and the edges connecting these nodes.” The reference to “nodes in an image” is unclear. Line 246: “a deep network is conceptualized as an image.” No explanation is given for this analogy.
- **Limited to small convnets, no large-model experiments** (Reviewer 3, also inherent in Reviewer 2’s question): Only experiments on small convnets are shown. Most recent parameter isolation methods (e.g., SPG, SPU) are designed for large models, where parameter isolation excels. The method is not tested on Transformers (explicit question from Reviewer 3).
- **Contrasting reviewer opinions** (Reviewer 1 score 5 vs. Reviewer 2 and 3 score 3): Reviewer 1 is notably more positive about the method’s motivation and empirical gains over baselines, but still acknowledges the significant gap to [2] and the heuristic layer split. Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 are more critical, emphasizing the poor writing, lack of clarity, overclaims, and limited experiments. Reviewer 3 considers the soundness and presentation very low (both 1).
