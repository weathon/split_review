## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Semantic Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (SVQ) as part of the Neural Language of Thought Model (NLoTM). The model uses slot attention to obtain N object-centric features, divides each slot’s hidden dimension into M groups (attributes), and learns a shared M×K codebook. Each attribute per slot is quantized to the nearest code in its corresponding group. An autoregressive transformer learns a prior over these discrete codes for generation. Experiments on synthetic 2D and 3D datasets (CLEVR variants) evaluate downstream tasks, out-of-distribution generalization, and generation quality. Results are compared against patch-based VQ-VAE, dVAE, and continuous object-centric models like GENESIS-v2 and SysBinder. The paper claims to learn hierarchical, composable, semantic representations aligned with objects and their properties.

### Strengths
- The writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow; figures illustrate the main ideas effectively.
- The motivation for learning hierarchical discrete object-centric representations aligned with semantic attributes is interesting and well-articulated.
- The proposed SVQ models discrete abstraction and object-level representations simultaneously, unlike standard VAE/VQ-VAE.
- The semantic prior obtained from discrete latent codes is used directly for generation, and the visualization results (e.g., superior generation quality over baselines) are impressive.
- Codebook analysis results show learned structure and are considered impressive by one reviewer.
- Qualitative results in Figure 6 demonstrate disentanglement of color, position, and size on synthetic data.
- Downstream tasks are well-designed, including probing whether the model has learned underlying generative rules (considered a novel evaluation).
- Experiments are extensive, covering multiple datasets and settings, with reasonable baselines and clear analysis.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited to synthetic data**: All experiments are conducted only on synthetic, simple datasets (CLEVR variants). It is unclear whether the factor-based approach generalizes to more realistic, complex scenes.
- **Unfair or incomplete baseline comparisons**:  
  - Table 4 compares only dVAE and VQ-VAE Indices with SVQ Indices; it does not include VQ-VAE features or dVAE features (e.g., using code IDs to index decoder weights).  
  - Table 5 compares classification/attribute-prediction accuracy but VQ-VAE and dVAE are not designed for object-centric tasks; SysBinder shows comparable or even better performance, undermining the claimed advantage.  
  - Only a few baselines (GENESIS-v2, VQ-VAE, dVAE) are selected for generation quality evaluation; VQ-VAE/dVAE may require larger model capacity to handle these tasks, making the comparison less convincing.
- **Weak generation results**: The generation results in Figure 4 are surprisingly bad given that VQGAN and DALL-E perform well on real data even with smaller hyperparameters; this raises concerns about the current results.
- **Missing standard object-centric metric**: The main metric FID is orthogonal to object-centric decomposition; object-centric papers typically measure Adjusted Rand Index (ARI). The paper does not compare ARI with slot-attention or its variants.
- **Lack of ablation studies**: No ablation on critical hyperparameters, e.g., varying the number of blocks M (what happens when M is smaller than the actual number of attributes?), codebook size, or number of slots. This makes it hard to understand which components are critical.
- **No visualization of learned semantics**: The paper claims to learn semantic representations (shape, color, etc.) but never illustrates or visualizes the actual codebook vectors or their correspondence to semantic attributes. Without such analysis, the claim is unconvincing.
- **Novelty concerns relative to prior work**: The idea of disentangling object-level slots into block-level factors and using an autoregressive transformer decoder has been introduced in prior work SysBinder. The paper does not clearly articulate the specific differences in the disentanglement mechanism, and the performance margin over SysBinder is not large.
- **Reproduction difficulties**: Hyperparameters (number of blocks, vector dimensions, etc.) are not specified for SVQ or the baselines. The artificial constraints used for “Generation Accuracy” and the design of the odd-one-out downstream task are not explained, raising concerns about validity and generalizability.
- **Missing experiments**:  
  - It is not shown whether SVQ preserves geometry structure as patch-based VQ-VAE does (important for applications like mask image modeling).  
  - No demonstration of controllable generation by manipulating codes in the learned codebook.  
  - Applicability to general 2D image generation (e.g., Stable Diffusion) is unclear.  
  - Sensitivity analysis of results to codebook size and number of blocks is missing—these hyperparameters are known a priori for CLEVR but not for real-world datasets.
- One reviewer noted that a concurrent paper (arXiv:2309.15505) presents a similar group-level quantization idea, which could provide inspiration.
