## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces LST-Bench, a benchmark for long sequence time-series forecasting (LSTF) that evaluates 11 models (Transformer-, MLP-, CNN-, and RNN-based) on 7 existing datasets (4 ETT, weather, traffic, electricity) and 7 new 15-minute-level datasets from the power industry. Experiments reveal two phenomena: “Degeneracy” – predictions with low MSE/MAE but repetitive, simplistic patterns that fail to capture future dynamics – and near-optimal performance after only one training epoch. The authors call for further investigation and redefinition of evaluation metrics. The paper is easy to follow and provides detailed experimental setups, but multiple reviewers note limited novelty, shallow analysis of the observed phenomena, and concerns about dataset diversity and completeness.

### Strengths
- **First dedicated LSTF benchmark after deep learning adoption** – serves as a potential resource for future research (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Identification of Degeneracy and rapid convergence** – highlights a critical, underappreciated failure mode that can mislead standard metric comparisons (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Clear, well-organized experimental comparison** – provides rankings across models and datasets, useful for practitioners choosing models for long-horizon tasks (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **New electricity-industry datasets** – may be of value to the community (Reviewers 1, 3); one reviewer considers their collection a strength, while others question their diversity and documentation (see Weaknesses).
- **Thorough and transparent experimental configuration details** (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Low novelty and originality** – the work applies existing models to mostly existing datasets; no new technical framework or methodological insight (Reviewers 1, 3). A minority reviewer (Reviewer 1, score 1) considers the contribution very low; others rate it fair but also note marginal novelty.
- **Lack of in-depth analysis of observed phenomena** – the paper documents Degeneracy and one-epoch convergence but does not investigate their underlying causes (e.g., theoretical explanation, impact on generalization) or provide comparative visualizations of one-epoch vs. fully-trained predictions (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). [Reviewer 3 explicitly asks: “What is the underlying theoretical cause of Degeneracy?”]
- **Insufficient documentation of the new dataset** – only source and sampling rate are given; no information about data characteristics, preprocessing, or potential domain diversity; the 7 new series all come from equipment monitoring, limiting cross-domain coverage for a benchmark (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Missing model architecture descriptions** – each model should be described for completeness (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited evaluation metrics** – only MSE and MAE are used; the benchmark should include MAPE, CVRMSE, NMBE to improve generality (Reviewer 3).
- **Hyper-parameter sensitivity not addressed** – the use of default hyper-parameters leaves open whether adequate tuning could mitigate Degeneracy or improve results (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation/writing quality** – the structure resembles a technical report; the writing is not compelling for a conference paper (Reviewer 4).
- **Need for clearer vision of how to address the issues** – the paper stops at empirical evidence without proposing even partial mitigations or concrete future directions (Reviewer 4). [Reviewer 3 asks “How to improve the paper’s novelty?”]
