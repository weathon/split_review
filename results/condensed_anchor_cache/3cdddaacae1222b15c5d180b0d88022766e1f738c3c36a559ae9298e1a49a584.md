## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes using stochastic Lagrangian and stochastic action as direct loss functions for solving the Schrödinger bridge problem (SBP), incorporating physical constraints (Fisher information, cross-entropy) into the Lagrangian. The authors connect the problem to stochastic mechanics, derive an effective Lagrangian, and present an IPF-based algorithm. Evaluation is limited to a single 2D toy spiral example without any comparison to existing methods. Most reviewers (scores 3,3,3,3) find the paper not ready for publication due to weak experiments, lack of novelty, and poor presentation. One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, praising the mathematical elegance and the introduction of Yasue and Guerra‑Morato Lagrangians, but still notes awkward phrasing, reproducibility concerns, and missing convergence analysis.

### Strengths

- Introduces formalism from stochastic mechanics (Yasue and Guerra‑Morato Lagrangians) to the SBP, a perspective that could be novel and interesting to the generative model community.
- Provides an interpretation of the Schrödinger bridge as a heat equation in imaginary time.
- Shows how to derive an IPF‑based algorithm from stochastic mechanics theory.
- Incorporates necessary physical constraints (Fisher information) into the training loss.
- The paper deals with mathematically elegant concepts (Chen, Georgiou, Pavon 2021) and Lagrangians that deserve wider recognition (minority positive view, Reviewer 4).
- Uses theoretical underpinnings to drive practical algorithmic applications (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient experiments**: Only a single 2D toy example is shown, with no evaluation on more complex tasks. The results are described as “underwhelming” and show poor matching of marginal distributions at initial/final times. No evidence is provided that the algorithm restores true Schrödinger bridge trajectories.
- **No comparison with existing methods**: The paper does not compare to standard SB solvers (De Bortoli et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2022) or recent alternatives (Tong et al. 2023, Shi et al. 2023, Liu et al. 2023, Somnath et al. 2023, Neklyudov et al. 2023, Gushchin et al. 2023). The proposed method’s advantages over these approaches are never discussed.
- **Novelty concerns**:
  - Theorems 1, 3, 4 are known results; only Theorem 2 might be novel. It is unclear whether the authors claim these as their own.
  - The IPF‑based algorithm and objectives are already known (Chen et al. 2021, Eq 18, 19).
  - The combination of Yasue, Guerra‑Morato, and cross‑entropy Lagrangians is described as “ad‑hoc”; a more principled derivation is needed.
- **Missing important comparisons**: The paper does not compare the proposed formulation (including imaginary‑time interpretation) to the original approach of Chen et al. (2021a), leaving the benefits unclear.
- **Presentation and language**:
  - Numerous typos (e.g., “ßaction”, “Schrödinge” in the title), missing “the”, and amsmath errors (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - Awkward phrasing (e.g., Theorem 4: “Stochastic action minimization … is associated Fisher information production”).
  - Unnecessary historical material and repeated well‑known calculations in the appendix obscure the contribution.
  - Sparse citations; important statements lack references; missing literature like Neklyudov et al. 2023.
- **Missing analysis and justification**:
  - The sense of (weak) convergence for the iterative minimization in Section 5.1 is not specified.
  - Theorem 2’s connection to the SBP is not explained.
  - The paper claims an “intuitive grasp of the loss function” but does not make this intuition clear.
  - The keyword “single‑cell, trajectories” appears in the keywords but is never discussed in the paper.
  - No discussion of how the method scales with system dimension.
  - The relevance to generative models is not elaborated.
- **Reproducibility**: The toy example is not clearly reproducible (e.g., missing details).

*Note: The minority positive reviewer (score 5) acknowledged these weaknesses as well, though with lower severity.*
