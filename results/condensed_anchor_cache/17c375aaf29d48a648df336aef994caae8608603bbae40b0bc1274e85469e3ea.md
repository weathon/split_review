## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Unitention, an end-to-end trainable module that uses cross-attention to combine a sample's deep features (individual) with dataset-level universal features from a class-specific prototype bank updated via exponential moving average. The module is inserted after a backbone and outputs the sum of both feature streams. Experiments on ImageNet-1K, iNaturalist 2018, and 1D signal classification show performance gains across ConvNets and Vision Transformers.

### Strengths

- Well-written, easy to follow, and well-organized.
- The idea is simple, logical, and easy to understand.
- Novel perspective: attending to the entire dataset (holistic dataset representation) rather than per-image processing.
- Extensive experimental evaluation across multiple architectures (ResNet, ViT, Swin) and modalities (image, 1D signals).
- The training-free study helps justify the design.
- The method obtains healthy performance gains (e.g., up to 2.3% on fine-grained tasks) and shows improvement over baselines.
- Limitations are discussed.
- The exploration of universal–individual interaction is interesting and may inspire future work.

### Weaknesses

- **Related work omission and missing comparisons**: The paper ignores a large body of prior work on head design and feature encoding (e.g., NetVLAD, Compact Bilinear Pooling, MoNet, Query2label, Sot, Residual Attention, NOAH). Comparisons to these methods (or at least a discussion of complementarity) are necessary.
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: Parameters, FLOPs, and inference speed for models with and without Unitention are not reported (e.g., ResNet-50/101/152, ViT-Small, Swin-Tiny). The computational and storage demands of the 1000×C class-level feature bank need evaluation.
- **Limited task generality**: Experiments are restricted to single-label global-feature-based classification. The method is not evaluated on multi-label classification, object detection, segmentation, or other downstream tasks that would test transferability.
- **Overclaimed relation to codebook/k-NN**: The universal bank is label-dependent (class-specific prototypes), unlike traditional unsupervised codebooks. Also, Unitention aggregates all class prototypes rather than selecting the most relevant (as in k-NN), which may limit discriminative focus. These differences should be clearly highlighted.
- **Limited migration ability**: The universal bank relies on label annotations, making it difficult to transfer to downstream tasks with different categories (or where labels are unavailable). This limitation should be more clearly stated in Section 2.2.
- **Algorithm clarity**: The “training-free” inference aspect is unclear—how is the classifier formed at test time? The paper should explicitly describe the transition from training to inference.
- **Fairness concern**: Using dataset-level contextual information during testing (the universal feature bank) may be unfair, as testing should evaluate per-image performance without extra data.
- **Hyperparameter analysis**: The temperature τ is fixed to 1 in the final model, yet Table 4 shows gains with momentum 0.8 and varying τ (rows 7,9,10,11). The reason for this choice is unexplained.
- **Ablation and design questions**:
    - In the ablation, how is the feature bank updated when the number of class centers is less than the number of classes?
    - What is the effect of replacing the feature bank with trainable parameters (updated by gradient)?
    - Does the backbone with Unitention still show gains when fine-tuned on detection/segmentation?
- **Table 1 clarification**: Is the k-NN accuracy in Table 1 obtained from a model trained with only a k-NN classifier?
- **Typo**: Section 3.2 has a typo: “BL* in tab:imn” should be “BL* in tab:1”.
