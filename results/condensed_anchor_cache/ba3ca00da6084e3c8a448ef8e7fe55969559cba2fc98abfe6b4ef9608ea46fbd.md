## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MCoT-Memory, a framework integrating dynamic scene graph updates (Evolving Scene Graph-Driven Chain of Thought) with a stepwise confidence-driven memory retention mechanism that uses an expert model to selectively retain high-quality experiences. The approach augments MLLMs with structured environment history and past reasoning traces for long-horizon embodied task planning. The paper also introduces ExtendaBench, a benchmark of 1,198 tasks across VirtualHome and Habitat 2.0 with four horizon categories. Experiments show performance gains over baselines (CoT, CCoT, DDCoT) on long tasks, but the method is only evaluated fully on VirtualHome; Habitat results are partial.

### Strengths
- Addresses a meaningful gap in long-horizon task planning for embodied agents.
- The core idea—combining a dynamic scene graph representation with selective memory retention to provide structured interaction history—is intuitive and reasonable.
- The selective retention mechanism (Stepwise Confidence-Driven Memory Retention) is noted by one reviewer as interesting and novel, though another reviewer sees it as a combination of existing techniques.
- The proposed ExtendaBench and its automatic generation pipeline are valuable to the community, and the task-generation process includes careful review and sequential refinement to ensure quality.
- Evaluation on ExtendaBench demonstrates advantage over naively using LLMs and over baselines like CoT, CCoT, and DDCoT. Ablation studies verify benefits of the three components (evolving scene graph, CoTm, memory bank).

### Weaknesses
**Benchmark justification and comparison** (raised by all reviewers):
- No detailed comparison to prior embodied task-planning benchmarks [1–4] is provided. The paper does not discuss what planning horizons prior work used, nor does it justify the necessity and advantages of ExtendaBench over existing long-horizon benchmarks (e.g., BEHAVIOR, ThreeDWorld Transport Challenge, MFE-ETP). One reviewer states that “just changing the scene and setting up a new benchmark” is not convincing.
- No justification is given for choosing VirtualHome and Habitat 2.0 as the two simulators, and it is unclear whether Habitat 2.0 provides different insights compared to VirtualHome.

**Limited novelty** (minority view: one reviewer finds selective retention novel; two others see limited novelty):
- The framework combines existing techniques (scene graphs, Chain of Thought, memory retention) without fundamentally new concepts, and the paper provides no insight into why scene graphs specifically are helpful.

**Missing experiments and ablations** (all reviewers):
- On Habitat 2.0, only GPT-4o is evaluated; there are no results for open-source VLMs (LLaVA, Qwen) even though LLaVA is used elsewhere in the paper.
- Ablation studies on Habitat are absent.
- No comparisons to other memory-augmented or agentic frameworks for long-horizon task planning, such as Voyager [1], Describe-Explain-Plan-Select [2], MP5 [3], or other task-planning agents [4].
- Experiments on additional well-known task-planning benchmarks (beyond ExtendaBench) are missing.
- The method shows only marginal improvements on the easiest Habitat tasks (Table 2), raising concern about generalizability across environments and baselines.
- Failure mode analysis is missing; given that all baselines perform poorly on the benchmark, the paper should discuss why tasks are hard and where memory augmentation still fails.

**Insufficient explanation and missing details** (reviewers 1,2,3):
- The dynamic scene graph construction and update process (Section 3.1.3) is not well explained. No illustrated example of a scene graph is given, and the tailored prompts for generation and updating are not provided.
- It is unclear whether experiments used privileged information from the simulator for scene graph construction (e.g., position, state, rooms). The paper states LLaVA is used, but LLaVA performs poorly on simulated environments, so the quality of generated scene graphs may be poor and could explain limited performance.
- The memory retention logic is not well explained: the MLLM-based judge for stepwise and overall scoring lacks details (filtering threshold, how threshold was decided, analysis around different thresholds). There is no mechanism described to guarantee objectivity or fairness of scores (e.g., majority voting).
- The execution phase is not described: how the agent interacts with the simulation after generating an action to produce the next observation.

**Dataset analysis and quality concerns** (reviewer 3):
- No analysis of task diversity, task types (e.g., are all tasks multiple-object rearrangements?), or human evaluation of dataset quality is provided. Only two examples are given in the appendix, and one Habitat example contains language instruction mistakes, raising concerns about overall data quality.

**Related work omission** (reviewer 4):
- Several prior agents for long sequential tasks in embodied scenarios that also use multiple memory mechanisms (Voyager, Describe-Explain-Plan-Select, MP5) are not discussed or compared.

**Typos and presentation issues** (reviewer 1):
- Abstract begins abruptly (“Existing methods excel in short-horizon tasks”) without specifying which methods.
- Opening quotation marks face backward throughout the paper.
- Line 320: “bu” should be “by”.
- Figure 2, “Refinded Task and Plan” box: arrow points in the opposite direction.
- Inconsistent symbols: ESG vs. $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{SG}}$.
