## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents BLIP-3-Video, a multimodal vision-language model that uses a temporal encoder (e.g., learnable spatio-temporal pooling or Token Turing Machines) after a perceiver-resampler to compress inter-frame visual tokens into as few as 16–32 tokens. Evaluated on MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, TGIF-QA, and NExT-QA, it achieves competitive accuracy compared to much larger models (e.g., 34B) while being smaller (4B, based on Phi-3) and more efficient. One reviewer (score 8) is very positive about the efficiency and ablation; another (score 5) finds insufficient novelty and limited benchmarks; the other two reviewers (scores 6) are moderately positive but raise concerns about evaluation diversity, scalability, and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency-accuracy tradeoff:** Using only 16–32 visual tokens (vs. 4608 in competing models) gives strong performance on video QA, with an impressive tradeoff on MSVD-QA (Reviewer 1). This makes training and inference computationally efficient (Reviewer 2, 4). Reviewer 3 calls it a compelling finding that 32 tokens suffice for QA and captioning.
- **Extensive temporal encoder ablation:** The paper thoroughly explores different designs (learnable spatio-temporal pooling, Token Turing Machines, grouped TTM) and validates the choices (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 highlights that the ablation demonstrates flexibility in design.
- **Competitive performance despite smaller size:** BLIP-3-Video outperforms both parameter-heavy and visual-token-heavy models on several video QA benchmarks (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 notes it outperforms models in Fig. 1 on both QA and captioning.
- **Important research direction:** The idea of highly compact video representation for LLMs is promising and could benefit downstream tasks (e.g., captioning for text-to-video generation) (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity:** The paper is well-written, well-organized, with clear explanations and visual aids (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Originality (minority view):** Reviewer 4 considers the temporal encoder an innovative approach to efficiency. (This contrasts with Reviewer 1’s novelty criticism.)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty (major concern from Reviewer 1, echoed by Reviewer 4):** The only improvements to Token Turing Machines are (1) time-stamped positional encodings and (2) grouped TTM. These are minor changes; the core TTM mechanism is largely unchanged. Reviewer 4 questions the core novelty compared to LLaMA-VID and notes that similar architectures (Video-LLaVA, LLaVA-VID, LLaVA-NEXT) already explore this direction. The paper does not provide ablation studies specifically for these novel TTM modifications (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited and unconvincing evaluation benchmarks:** Only tested on MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, TGIF-QA, NExT-QA – benchmarks that focus on simple understanding (object presence, basic actions) rather than complex video reasoning (temporal relationships, event causality, nuanced interactions) (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4). Missing evaluation on VideoMME and MVBench (Reviewer 1, 4). The model uses the VideoChatGPT instruction set but is not evaluated on that benchmark (Reviewer 1). Further, there is no evaluation on non-QA video tasks such as action recognition, temporal segmentation, or fine-grained temporal understanding (Reviewer 2). The datasets also lack diversity in camera motion, occlusions, and rapid scene changes (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability concerns with uniform 8-frame sampling:** The model uniformly samples only 8 frames per video. This may miss critical temporal details in complex or rapidly changing scenes (e.g., a gymnastics routine with take-off, mid-air movements, landing) (Reviewer 2). The paper does not analyze scaling to longer videos, nor does it provide computational cost (memory, inference time) or discuss the temporal encoder’s ability to maintain relevant information over extended periods (Reviewer 2, 4). It is unclear whether 16–32 tokens encode enough information for long diverse scenarios requiring multiple details or nuanced temporal reasoning (Reviewer 2). There is no built-in mechanism to prevent information loss during token pooling (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing/insufficient ablations and architectural clarity:** Section 2.2 is confusing: it is not clear whether BLIP-3-Video uses both spatio-temporal attentional pooling and TTM, nor whether the perceiver-resampler is placed before the temporal encoder (Figure 2 is ambiguous) (Reviewer 3). A simple and promising ablation – fine-tuning the existing perceiver-resampler itself as a temporal encoder instead of compressing per-frame tokens – is missing (Reviewer 3). Implementation details such as the number of layers, hidden units, and activation functions in the temporal encoder are not provided, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Fixed token count regardless of text query:** The compression to 32 tokens is not text-query-guided; every query receives the same compressed representation, which may be suboptimal for different questions (Reviewer 3). It is also unclear whether the model can dynamically produce different token counts (e.g., 32 vs. 128 tokens) as a hyperparameter or if separate models are required (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete comparisons with other efficient models:** The paper lacks comparisons to other efficient video VLM approaches (e.g., knowledge distillation, sparse attention) and does not provide detailed trade-off analyses (FLOPs, memory footprint, inference latency) versus state-of-the-art models (Reviewer 4). The current accuracy-only comparisons are insufficient for judging practical applicability in resource-constrained settings (Reviewer 4).
