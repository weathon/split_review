## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a GPU-accelerated large-scale microscopic traffic simulator for transportation system simulation and optimization. The simulator uses CUDA-based SIMD parallel processing, a two-phase parallel process, and a linked-list-based vehicle detection index to achieve high performance (84.09Hz, 88.92× speedup over CityFlow on a scenario with 2,464,950 vehicles). It adopts IDM and randomized MOBIL models to improve realism of average road speeds. The simulator provides Python APIs for controllable microscopic and macroscopic objects and supports five representative optimization scenarios: traffic signal control, dynamic lane assignment within junctions, tidal lane control, congestion pricing, and road planning. Benchmarks with rule-based, reinforcement learning, and black-box optimization algorithms are run across four cities. An open-registration web platform (moss.fiblab.net) is provided.

### Strengths
- **GPU acceleration and performance**: The parallel design using GPU features and linked-list-based vehicle detection index is promising. The simulator achieves substantial speedup (88.92×) over CityFlow on large-scale scenarios with millions of vehicles. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Practical application value**: The simulator supports multiple transportation optimization scenarios (traffic signal control, dynamic lane assignment, tidal lane control, congestion pricing, road planning) and provides predefined evaluation metric APIs, facilitating research and application in traffic system optimization. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Realism improvement**: The use of IDM and randomized MOBIL models yields more realistic average road speeds compared to CityFlow. (Reviewer 2)
- **Comprehensive benchmarking**: The paper benchmarks rule-based, reinforcement learning, and black-box optimization algorithms across five scenarios in four cities, demonstrating practical applicability. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well-organized, with clear problem statement, system design, and technical implementation. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **High reviewer confidence in value**: Reviewer 1 strongly believes many researchers can take advantage of this platform. Reviewer 2 notes the work is good in its realm and shows superiority over existing simulators. (Reviewers 1, 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Narrow scope and limited audience**: The scope is too narrow—only a small subset of the learning community will use this simulator. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing related works and comparisons**: The paper does not discuss or compare with recent GPU-accelerated simulators such as GPUDrive (Kazemkhani et al., 2024), CityFlowER (Da et al., 2024), GEMSim (Saprykin et al., 2019), or multi-GPU approaches (Jiang et al., 2024). The realism benchmarking does not include CityFlowER, which also incorporates more realistic driving models. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear technical contribution and architecture**: It is unclear which specific components (e.g., two-phase parallel process, linked-list indexes) mainly drive the efficiency gains. A summary paragraph at end of Section 3 stating core contributions and quantifying each component’s impact would help. (Reviewer 2)
- **SIMD homogeneity assumption and dependency management**: The claim that vehicles are “highly homogeneous” and compatible with SIMD is not sufficiently justified. Vehicle interactions in congestion create dependencies that must be managed to avoid compromising realism. No citations or discussion distinguish the SIMD implementation from existing techniques. (Reviewer 3)
- **Simplicity and realism concerns**: The simulator excludes intersection interactions, pedestrians, bicycles, public transport, and intersection overlap. The impact of these exclusions on realism is not validated. Evidence from prior research or justification for minimal impact is missing. (Reviewer 4)
- **Performance discrepancy with CBLab**: The reported performance of CBLab appears to contradict its original paper at scales over 1,000 vehicles, with CityFlow performing comparably or better. The paper does not explain whether this is due to different datasets or experimental setups. A detailed comparison using similar datasets (e.g., from CBLab) is needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient scenario validation and motivation**: The five scenarios lack individual effectiveness analysis. It is not explained how the results provide insights for actual urban traffic planning or policy decisions. Empirical validation showing that the simulator can reproduce real-world policy changes would strengthen motivation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of user-friendly reproducibility and code documentation**: The provided code and instructions (README.md) are insufficient for reproduction. Two of three source codes only have installation instructions; the benchmark code’s GitHub link is missing or the target version is unspecified, preventing access to necessary datasets. More detailed step-by-step guidance is required. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing analysis of memory usage and long-term evaluation**: The paper could include memory usage analysis (Reviewer 1). There are no experiments evaluating the simulator under long-term urban traffic pattern changes, e.g., reproducing outcomes of real-world policy changes. (Reviewer 4)
- **Questions on modularity and extensibility**: It is not clear whether the car-following model can be made selectable (currently fixed to IDM). The modularity of vehicle behavior models and what is involved in adding multiple selectable models is not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Uncertainty/stochasticity for robustness validation**: The paper does not describe functionality for adding uncertainty or stochasticity to vehicle models or traffic objects to validate algorithm robustness or the simulation-to-reality gap. (Reviewer 2)
- **Resource contention with AI/ML training**: Since the simulator and AI/ML training both use GPUs, resource constraints are expected on non-high-performance servers. This issue is not addressed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Comparison to commercial/game simulators**: The paper does not differentiate from commercial services (e.g., Aimsun) or urban simulation games (e.g., Cities: Skylines) that support user-customized plugins. (Reviewer 4)
- **Reference inaccuracies**: There are citation errors: a 1992 Dynasmart reference cannot be verified; an arXiv paper (HEBO) has an updated version; and a duplicate reference entry for TransformerLight. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minority more positive opinion**: While three reviewers scored 5 and one scored 6, the more positive reviewer (2) acknowledged the practical value and superiority but still raised the technical contribution clarity and modularity concerns. (Reviewers 2 vs 1,3,4)
