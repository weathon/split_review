## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a teleportation-based optimization acceleration method using null space gradient projection. Reviewers generally find the problem interesting and the approach architecturally flexible, extending teleportation beyond MLPs to CNNs and Transformers. However, multiple reviewers raise significant concerns about clarity of the projection mechanism, lack of wall-clock runtime comparisons, missing comparisons to other teleportation methods, hyperparameter sensitivity, limited generalization improvements, and citation inaccuracies. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, finding the idea neat and experiments comprehensive, while three reviewers (score 3) have substantial reservations regarding theoretical justification, empirical validation, and presentation.

### Strengths

- The idea of using layer input-space null projection (building on GPM, Saha et al. 2021) for teleportation is neat and a novel extension (R1).
- The method extends teleportation to a broader range of architectures (CNNs, Transformers) without relying on group actions, making it architecturally flexible (R1, R2, R4).
- The approach is more efficient than symmetry-based teleport methods (R1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2).
- The introduction effectively provides background context and situates the study within related work (R3).
- The idea of doing gradient ascent on the gradient norm while projecting onto the level set is interesting (R3).
- Experiments cover a variety of scenarios, showing faster convergence and training performance at least as good as baselines (R1, R4). Code is attached to reproduce results (R4).

### Weaknesses

- Wall-clock runtime comparison is missing. The computational cost of SVD and null space projection per layer is not quantified, making it unclear if convergence gains justify extra overhead (R1, R2, R4). A runtime comparison with non-teleportation optimizers (SGD, Momentum, Adagrad, Adam) is absent (R2). Direct wall-clock comparison with previous teleportation methods (symmetry-based, Zhao et al. 2022, Mishkin et al. 2024) is needed (R1, R2, R4).
- The explanation of why projecting the gradient of the teleportation loss onto the Residual Gradient Space (RGS) keeps parameters in the loss-invariant level set is unclear (R2). The approximation scheme lacks clarity, particularly its implementation, theoretical guarantees, and the effect of singular value threshold on accuracy (R4). The paper claims an approximate projection but does not provide pseudocode for the approximation algorithm or bounds on deviation from the true level set (R4).
- The paper mixes layer-wise and global operations without justification. Equations (4)–(5) and Algorithm 1 describe global updates, but Sections 3.1–3.3 suddenly use layer-wise projections. The equivalence of projecting the entire parameter vector π and projecting layer subsets separately is not justified, and the notation for π is inconsistent between global and layer contexts (R3).
- The claim “the gradient of the teleportation objective function resides within the space spanned by the input data” is incorrect and misleading. It should refer to the space spanned by the *rows* of the input data matrix for each layer (R3).
- Missing comparison to other state-of-the-art teleportation methods: group action-based methods on MLPs (Zhao et al. 2022) and Mishkin et al. (2024) on applicable models (R1, R2).
- Hyperparameter sensitivity is not studied: the robustness of teleportation hyperparameters and SVD thresholds is unclear (R1).
- The paper shows minimal gains in generalization; improvements are primarily on training loss. Practical utility is questionable without test-time performance enhancement (R4). The authors acknowledge this but do not explore it.
- The solutions reached with teleportation may differ qualitatively from those without; LMC curves (Frankle et al., 2019) to check barriers between solutions are missing (R1).
- Several citations are inappropriate or incorrect: Hessian (Sun et al., 2019), Adam (Kashyap, 2022), ReLU (Agarap, 2018), MLP (Taud & Mas, 2018), CNN (Li et al., 2021), multi-head self-attention (Wen et al., 2022) are not representative or are outdated (R1, R4). Also, “symmetry teleportation” is cited as state-of-the-art without justification (R4).
- The order of multiplication in the unnumbered equations between (7) and (8) for MLP, CNN, and Self-Attention differs (x*δ vs δ*x). The authors should explain whether this affects theoretical claims (R3).
- Presentation issues: figure titles say “ImageNet” instead of “TinyImageNet”; “input space” should be clarified as “input space of layers” (R1).
