## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multi-task reinforcement learning (MTRL) method to mitigate task performance imbalance. It augments the PaCo parameter-compositional approach [Sun et al., NeurIPS 2022] with task-specific embeddings and a task-aware modification of prioritized experience replay (TA-PER). Experiments on Meta-World show improved average success rates and more balanced task performance compared to prior methods. All three reviewers rated the paper 3 (Reject), with confidence 4. They acknowledge the problem identification and empirical gains but find the contributions incremental, the writing poor, and many implementation details missing.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly identifies the task performance imbalance problem in MTRL and provides a comprehensive benchmark of existing methods (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed approach (shared representation + task-based PER) is simple and reasonably formulated to address imbalance (Reviewer 3).
- TA-PER achieves notable improvement on MT10 tasks of Meta-World, demonstrating practical effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
- The ablation study supports the efficacy of both the shared representation and the task-based PER components on MT10 (Reviewer 3).
- The idea of using data sampling strategies to handle performance imbalance is interesting and leads to empirical improvements on MTRL benchmarks (Reviewer 1).
- The paper presents a detailed writeup of related works covering multi-task learning, MTRL, and off-policy learning (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution:** The method is not fundamentally different from PaCo [1]; adding task-specific embeddings and modifying PER are minor modifications (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 3 explicitly states the contribution seems incremental and the motivation insufficient for ICLR.
- **TA-PER is a minor modification of standard PER** (Reviewer 1), and the replay buffer sampling appears to be a generic improvement to the underlying RL algorithm rather than something specific to MTRL (Reviewer 2).
- **Questionable effectiveness of the embedding learning:** Figure 5 suggests that the proposed embedding learning method (triplet loss) is not effective (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 asks for visualizations comparing representations with and without the unique feature loss.
- **Missing complexity and memory analysis:** Storing a set of parameters for representations likely requires significant memory and computation, but the paper does not discuss this (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient experimental scope:** The paper should demonstrate performance on challenging MT50 tasks to establish efficacy for imbalanced performance (Reviewer 1), and should include comparisons with methods like CARE on MT50 (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experimental details:** Hyperparameters, architecture, and policy parameterization are not provided (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 2 asks: How is the policy parameterized? With the extra embedding, do you use a larger policy? What is “average sampling”?
- **Poor writing and incorrect notation (Reviewer 2):** Section 3 gives a poor overview of MTRL (describes MDP basics in detail but devotes one line to MTRL). Notation errors: transition probability is defined as a distribution (should be deterministic); reward function incorrectly written as \(R(a_t|s_t)\); variable \(s\) is used for both state and shared features. The paper jumps into PaCo’s solution strategy without context, forcing the reader to consult [1] to understand the notation.
- **Typographical errors (Reviewer 1):** (1) Last line of page 4, \(r_a\) should be \(u_a\). (2) Eq. (2), \(\phi_{targ}\) is redundant given the parameter set. (3) Notation \(s_i\) for task parameters is confusing with state \(s\). (4) \(w_i\) is ambiguous between importance weight for PER and vector weight in shared features.
- **Redundancy of extra embedding:** Since task ID is provided as part of the state, it is unclear why an additional task-specific embedding is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Need for clarification on unique feature learning:** How are unique features learned? Reviewer 3 recommends visualizations of representations with and without the unique feature loss.
- **Unclear behavior in Figure 1(b):** In steps 5–10, the black line moves out of the black zone – why does that happen? (Reviewer 3)
- **Generalization question:** Does the unique representation help with task generalization? (Reviewer 3)
- **Disagreement on presentation and soundness:** Two reviewers rated presentation as 2 or 1 (fair/poor) and soundness as 2; one reviewer rated both presentation and soundness as 3 (good). This reflects the poor writing and missing details acknowledged by the majority.
