## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a backpropagation-free method for training neural PDE solvers for time-dependent problems. It separates space and time variables: a neural network ansatz is used for the spatial domain, with hidden-layer weights and biases randomly sampled (Extreme Learning Machine), and the time-dependent coefficients are solved using classical ODE solvers. The approach uses a boundary-compliant layer and an SVD layer to improve conditioning. Variants include ELM-ODE and SWIM-ODE (adaptive sampling). Experiments on Advection, Euler-Bernoulli, Nonlinear diffusion, and Burgers’ equations show that the method outperforms physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in accuracy and training speed by up to 5 orders of magnitude.

### Strengths
- The writing is clear, well-structured, and the literature review is thorough.
- The experiments cover a diverse set of PDEs with challenging features (high-frequency temporal dynamics, long time spans, shocks, non-linearities) and include comparisons to PINNs and, where applicable, classical numerical solvers.
- The method achieves substantially lower relative errors than PINNs (often 1–5 orders of magnitude improvement) and significantly faster training times.
- The framework is mesh-free and does not require backpropagation, which simplifies implementation for certain settings.
- The paper introduces practical techniques for handling boundary conditions (boundary-compliant layer) and improving the condition number of the ODE system (SVD layer).
- The approach is novel, combining separation of variables, random weight sampling, and classical ODE solvers into a hybrid scheme that can achieve accuracy comparable to classical numerical methods.

### Weaknesses
- **Random weight sampling and stability:** The influence of the distribution (uniform, Gaussian, etc.) and variance of the randomly sampled weights on solution stability and convergence is not analyzed. Table 2 shows that the proposed method has larger standard deviations than PINNs, yet no explanation or mitigation is provided. (Reviewers 2, 5)
- **Missing ablation studies:** The necessity and impact of the SVD layer, the effect of the number of hidden neurons on accuracy and computational cost, and the trade-offs between ELM-ODE and SWIM-ODE are not systematically studied. Guidance on when to choose one strategy over the other (e.g., linear vs. nonlinear PDEs, parabolic vs. hyperbolic) is lacking. (Reviewers 2, 5)
- **Limited scope of experiments:** All numerical examples are in one or two spatial dimensions with small ranges. Despite the introduction’s emphasis on mesh-free advantages for high-dimensional and complex spatial domains, no experiments on higher-dimensional PDEs (d > 2) are presented. This leaves questions about computational scalability (pseudo-inverse cost for large N in high dimensions) and practical benefit over mesh-based methods. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Lack of theoretical foundation:** No discussion of universal approximation properties of ELM in the PDE solver context, nor analysis of n-width or spectral decay of the snapshot matrix. The paper does not provide theoretical justification for why random weight sampling works for this problem class. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited applicability:** The method is presented as mesh-free, but in practice the pseudo-inverse of the feature matrix (size N × (M+1)) becomes expensive for high-dimensional domains where many collocation points N are required. Moreover, the method cannot handle inverse or grey-box problems. The advantage over classical mesh-based solvers (FEM, spectral methods) is not directly demonstrated; for periodic boundary conditions, using sin(kx)/cos(kx) or FEM basis functions may be equally or more efficient. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Unaddressed question about higher-order time derivatives:** The paper does not clarify whether the method can handle PDEs with second or higher-order time derivatives (e.g., wave equation). (Reviewer 5)
- **Minority critique on direction:** One reviewer (score 5) views the zeroth-order optimization approach as a “huge step backward” for AI-based PDE solving, arguing that backpropagation’s efficiency with many parameters is fundamental and that random sampling scales poorly. This concern, while not shared by all reviewers, highlights a disagreement about the significance of the contribution. (Reviewer 1)
