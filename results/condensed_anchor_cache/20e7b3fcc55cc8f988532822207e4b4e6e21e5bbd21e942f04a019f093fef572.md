## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a future-constrained decoding strategy that uses a language model to estimate constraint satisfaction during generation, guiding the beam search toward outputs that satisfy natural-language constraints (e.g., keywords, toxicity avoidance, factual correctness). It is evaluated on CommonGen, toxicity reduction, and factual QA (ALCE). Reviewers generally agree the idea is intuitive and tackles an important problem, but they raise serious concerns about novelty, the magnitude of improvements, the absence of human evaluation, missing analyses, and presentation issues. There is clear disagreement: one reviewer (R1, score 6) is more positive, while two others (R2, R3, both score 3) are more critical; R4 (score 5) is moderately positive but notes several weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Important problem**: Controlling undesirable behaviors (toxicity, hallucinations) in LLM text generation is a key challenge (R1, R4).
- **Intuitive and flexible method**: The use of future constraints as a self-evaluation signal is interesting; it can handle diverse constraint types (keywords, toxicity, factual correctness) (R2, R4).
- **Clear explanation (per some reviewers)**: R1 and R3 praise the clarity and succinctness of the core idea and the formula for the satisfaction score.
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The paper tests three tasks with multiple backbone models and investigates hyperparameter impact (R3).
- **Demonstrated constraint satisfaction**: Results show improved adherence to constraints with modest fluency tradeoffs (R4). The analysis of the satisfaction score on constructed benchmarks is useful (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The constraint scoring function is very similar to existing controllable text generation methods, and the paper does not adequately compare against them, making the contribution incremental (R1, R3). R2 considers the contribution excellent, but this is a minority view.
- **Marginal and inconsistent improvements**: Gains over greedy/beam search are small, often only help smaller LLMs (R1), are sometimes absent (R2), and overall are not convincing given the method’s added complexity (R2, R3). Factual correctness results are particularly noisy and may be sensitive to the prompt or factual claim (R4).
- **Missing human evaluation**: No human evaluation of output quality, naturalness, or hallucination reduction. Reliance on n-gram overlap metrics (e.g., BLEU) is unreliable for strong LLMs (R2, R3, R4).
- **Missing analyses**:
  - **Decoding time trade-off**: The method calls an LM for each beam and each timestep; this slowdown is not reported or compared to baseline decoding speed (R2).
  - **Longer text generation**: Only short texts are tested; error accumulation over longer sequences (e.g., multi-paragraph essays, extended dialogues) is not studied (R4).
  - **Missing dataset**: For ALCE, the QAMPARI dataset is omitted (R2).
  - **No case study**: Concrete examples showing how the method reduces hallucinations would strengthen the paper (R3).
- **Ambiguity in method definition**:
  - The definition of the constraint satisfaction score is unclear: the formula \(R(y_{\le t}, C(x))\) approximates \(\log p(C(x) \mid y_{\le t})\), but the benefit of this specific definition and the addition of the `<SEP>` token are not justified (R3). It is also unclear whether the `<SEP>` token is the same across LLMs or repurposed from pretraining (R1).
- **Potential failure modes not explored**: The satisfaction score relies on an LLM to predict future constraint satisfaction; the paper does not analyze failure cases such as overconfidence, sensitivity to input variations, or how the choice of LLM and prompt engineering affect accuracy (R4). The frequency of degenerate sequences during inference is also not reported (R1).
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Figures 2, 4, and 5 are not self-contained: they show previously unintroduced models and undefined terms (e.g., “Ranking accuracy”) (R2). R3 notes that figures are not vector images, causing distortion when enlarged.
  - The paper needs proofreading (R2). (R1 and R4 found the paper well-written, while R2 and R3 rated presentation poor.)
- **Focus on faithful decoding but uses n-gram overlap metrics**: Given the paper’s emphasis on faithfulness, using metrics like BLEU (rather than faithfulness-specific ones) is questionable (R2).
