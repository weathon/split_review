## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Adaptive Feature Aggregation (AFA), a method to ensemble multiple text-to-image diffusion models at the feature level. A lightweight Spatial-Aware Block-Wise (SABW) aggregator dynamically combines block-wise intermediate features from multiple U-Net denoisers, adjusting contributions based on prompts, initial noise, denoising steps, and spatial locations. Only SABW is trainable (~50M parameters); base models are frozen. Experiments on COCO 2017 and DrawBench Prompts show improvements in FID, IS, and CLIP scores, and tolerance to fewer inference steps.

### Strengths
- **Novel dynamic feature-level ensembling** that goes beyond static parameter merging and timestep-specific expert selection (e.g., ERNIE-ViLG 2.0, eDiff-I, MEME). The method adaptively aggregates features from multiple models, keeping strengths while suppressing weaknesses.
- **Comprehensive state conditioning**: dynamically adjusts contributions based on prompts, noise, denoising steps, and spatial locations, enabling more fine-grained control than existing approaches.
- **Well-designed SABW aggregator** with spatial attention maps that provide interpretability and insight into how different models contribute across contexts and timestamps.
- **Practical training**: only the lightweight SABW is trainable; all base models are kept frozen.
- **Strong quantitative results** on COCO 2017 and DrawBench Prompts, with improvements in FID, IS, and CLIP scores over both single models and baseline merging methods. Improvements are consistent across multiple metrics.
- **Tolerance to reduced inference steps**: AFA maintains performance with fewer steps, partially offsetting the per-step computational overhead.
- **Thorough ablation studies** and clear visualization of spatial attention patterns. The paper is well-structured with informative figures and tables.

### Weaknesses
- **Severe architecture limitation**: The method is only applicable to diffusion models with *identical U-Net architectures* (same number of layers, same feature map dimensions). It cannot ensemble models with different architectures (e.g., DiT, transformer-based diffusion, SDXL, SD 3.0, Flux) or models that differ in layer count or feature dimensions. Multiple reviewers flag this as a critical constraint.
- **Missing comparison with related mixture-of-experts methods**: The paper does not directly compare with ERNIE-ViLG 2.0, eDiff-I, or MEME, which also use multiple expert models for dynamic denoising (e.g., timestep-specific selection). A comparison on metrics (FID, IS, CLIP), computational efficiency, and qualitative differences would clarify whether spatial-aware aggregation offers advantages over or complements timestep-based expert selection.
- **Scalability and overfitting risks**: Only three models are ever ensembled; there is no analysis of performance trends, diminishing returns, or computational cost as the number of models increases. Moreover, if base models have correlated features or biases (e.g., trained on similar data), the adaptive aggregation may overfit and generalize poorly. Additional models could also become computationally prohibitive.
- **Unclear computational efficiency**: While AFA tolerates fewer inference steps, the per-step cost is higher due to the SABW module. The paper compares AFA with fewer steps against baselines with full steps, but does not compare both under the same reduced-step setting to isolate the efficiency gain. Detailed measurements of memory, runtime, and hardware are missing. Reviewers also ask about optimizations (pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation).
- **Potentially unfair experimental setup**: The SABW aggregator is trained on JourneyDB (a high-quality dataset), but the baseline models are *not* fine-tuned on the same dataset. This makes direct comparisons less fair. Additionally, the claim that SDv1.5 fails to understand number prompts (e.g., “single clock”) is not reproducible in at least one reviewer’s tests.
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No analysis of why spatial-aware aggregation outperforms timestep-specific expert selection, nor guidelines on when one may be more appropriate. A theoretical framework (e.g., information flow, gradient propagation) is missing.
- **Limited scope of evaluation**: Only SDv1.5-based models are used; newer architectures (DiT, SDXL, Flux) are excluded. The method focuses on general generation capability, not on combining styles/concepts, which is a distinct goal in model merging – the paper does not clearly delineate this difference.
- **Presentation and reproducibility issues**: Repeated phrases, unclear explanation that SABW uses a composition of modules rather than typical self-attention, missing references, small fonts, and incomplete experimental details (hardware, memory, time). Evaluation sample size ambiguous (“4 images”).
- **Handling conflicting contributions**: No discussion of how the aggregator resolves conflicts when different models produce contradictory outputs (e.g., different spatial arrangements or semantics).
- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (ratings 5) are more critical of the experimental fairness, architecture limitation, and lack of generalizability, while two others (ratings 8) find the method highly novel and empirically strong. The noted reproducibility issue and unfair training setup are particularly concerning to the more critical reviewers.
