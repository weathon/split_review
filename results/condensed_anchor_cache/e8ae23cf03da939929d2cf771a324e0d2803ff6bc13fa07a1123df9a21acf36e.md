## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies how down-scaling LLMs via weight pruning affects two core capabilities: recalling facts from pre-training (parametric knowledge) and processing information provided in context (ICL). Experiments on OPT-13B/30B and LLaMA-13B/33B with SparseGPT and Wanda pruning, plus dense down‑scaling, show a striking disparity: moderate pruning (>30%) degrades fact recall, while ICL remains robust up to 60–70% sparsity. The topic is timely and the experimental design (multiple model families, sparsity levels, task categories) is generally well‑thought. However, reviewers raise concerns about evaluation validity, limited methodological detail, missing comparisons (structured pruning, other aspects like reasoning/instruction following), and insufficient discussion of tradeoffs and related work.

### Strengths
- **Important question** – Investigating how pruning (and down‑scaling more broadly) affects LLMs’ parametric vs. in‑context knowledge is highly relevant as model sizes grow (R1, R3, R4).
- **Useful evaluation framework** – Separating fact recall from ICL with dedicated benchmarks (closed‑book QA, open‑book QA, overriding QA, learning tasks) and using counter‑factual evidence is a sensible approach (R2, R4).
- **Multiple settings** – Experiments cover 6 base LLMs (OPT‑13B/30B, LLaMA‑13B/33B), 9 sparsity levels per model, and two pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) plus dense down‑scaling, lending robustness to the findings (R2, R4).
- **Clear main finding** – The observed dichotomy (fact recall degrades at moderate sparsity, ICL is resilient up to high sparsity) is striking and likely useful for practitioners (R4).
- **Well‑written and structured** – The paper is easy to follow and presents the results clearly (R4). Reviewer 4 rates soundness 4 and contribution 4, reflecting a more positive view than R1/R2/R3 (who rate both 2); this contrast suggests the work is seen as solid by at least one reviewer.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation validity of counter‑factual ICL** – It is not convincingly verified that the in‑context evidence truly contradicts pretraining data for LLaMA/OPT, since the training corpora are not fully released. Unlike prior work (Neeman et al. 2022, Longpre et al. 2021) that fine‑tunes models to guarantee counter‑factual answers, this paper does not fine‑tune, making the results uncertain (R3).
- **Metric issues** – Exact‑match accuracy with greedy decoding is problematic for instruction‑tuned LLMs, which tend to produce verbose completions; this biases results downward. The paper should justify the use of greedy decoding and discuss this limitation (R3).
- **Missing comparisons and ablation**  
  - Only unstructured pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda) is evaluated; structured pruning methods (e.g., LLM‑Pruner) are not tested, leaving a gap (R1, R4).  
  - The paper does not compare its zero‑shot fact‑recall results to those already reported in the SparseGPT paper (which showed high sparsity could retain zero‑shot performance) – why the discrepancy? (R3).  
  - Dense down‑scaling of 30B→~13B underperforms a naturally‑trained 13B model, but the reasons are not discussed (R2).  
  - The set of tasks is small; other key capabilities (instruction following, heavy reasoning, NLI, classification, summarization) are not included (R1, R2).  
  - For ICL, only synthetic learning tasks with scalar labels are used; a natural‑language ICL task would strengthen the conclusions (R4).  
  - The GINC dataset (Xie et al.) for ICL is not considered (R3).  
- **Limited technical detail** – The pruning methods themselves are described only briefly (even in the appendix); more detail on the pruning process and the down‑scaling comparison would help (R4).  
- **Lack of clarity in Section 5** – Notation (K, x, D=4, N=32) is introduced without explanation, making the section hard to follow (R4).  
- **Missing inference speed/accuracy tradeoff** – Since pruning is primarily used for speed, a plot of accuracy vs. inference speed (or latency) would be valuable (R2).  
- **Presentation issues** – The introduction reads as disjointed; the three bold‑faced items should be woven into a coherent paragraph. Claims about down‑scaling introducing biases need softening, and the paper should better contrast its contributions with prior scaling studies, the Wanda paper’s ICL results, and Longpre et al. 2022 (R3).  
- **Generalization concerns** – The findings are empirical and may not extend to all LLMs, pruning methods, or tasks (R1).  
- **Data release** – Releasing the exact dataset versions used for benchmarking would improve reproducibility (R4).
