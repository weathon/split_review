## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose a forecasting model that combines discrete state space hidden Markov models with recurrent neural networks, trained using a VQ-VAE-inspired procedure with separate emission models per quantized hidden state. Evaluations on several stationary and non-stationary datasets show comparable or superior performance to SOTA methods including PatchTST/64.

### Strengths
- Novel combination of VQ-VAEs and RNNs with a latent-conditioned observation model (Reviewer 1).
- Introducing finite state HMM for forecasting is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- The formulation of the model and the learning method is sound (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive evaluation against multiple baselines on multiple datasets with multi-seed comparison and grid search (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Tests on both stationary and non-stationary datasets (Reviewer 3).
- Good presentation with comprehensive training and architecture details (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Lack of ablation studies to provide intuition for why the model performs well compared to baselines (Reviewer 1).
- The method’s performance is similar to PatchTST/64, but the paper does not clarify practical advantages (e.g., training efficiency, runtime) that would motivate choosing the proposed model over a Transformer-based alternative (Reviewer 1).
- Hyperparameter search for baselines was limited to learning rate and batch size; a search over network size (analogous to the authors’ hidden size grid search) would strengthen the comparison (Reviewer 1).
- The two-stage alternating training of emission and prior models may be suboptimal in the time series context; it could prevent convergence to a global optimum, as the emission model might overfit to the current prior and vice versa (Reviewer 2).
- The paper lacks details on how discrete latent variables are handled during backpropagation (e.g., straight-through estimator or Gumbel-Softmax). This omission hurts reproducibility and makes it hard to assess the robustness of the learned representations (Reviewer 2).
- Only one Transformer baseline (PatchTST/64) is considered; many other Transformer architectures for forecasting exist, and a more comprehensive comparison is needed to demonstrate true effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
- The method’s performance on long context forecasting is not evaluated (Reviewer 2, question).
- Writing quality issues: a large blank space on Page 2; grammatical errors such as “...where T stand for…” should be “stands” (Section 3.1.3); “their associated MASE” should be “MASEs” (Table 1 caption); the second equation in Section 3.2.2 should be MAE; subject-verb disagreement in “the accuracy ... provide uncertainty quantification” (Section 3.2.3) (Reviewer 3).
- In Figure 1, it is unclear whether the hidden state trajectory values (e.g., 2,2,1,1,3,...,3) are fixed or flexible in the implementation (Reviewer 3, question).
