## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a training-free multi-agent debate defense against jailbreak attacks on Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It uses two agents: one with full image access and one with partial/cropped image access. They debate to reach a consensus, aiming to preserve correct semantics on clean inputs while rejecting attacks. Experiments report reducing attack success rate from 100% to 22% (and further to 18% with GPT-4o and 14% with enhanced perspective). The paper tests multiple debate strategies (message passing, critical debate, persuasive debate) and claims better response quality than baselines (MLLM Protector, SmoothVLM).

### Strengths
- The training-free defense mechanism is practical for endpoint deployment.
- The topic of using multi-agent debate for VLM security is interesting and novel in this context (one reviewer noted novelty as a strength, though another found the framework itself not new).
- The approach achieves a significant reduction in attack success rate (from 100% to 22%) while maintaining low refusal rates and better response quality than baselines.
- Experiments are comprehensive and clear, with multiple debate strategies investigated (message passing, critical debate, persuasive debate) and statistical analysis across different topics.
- The writing is clear and well-organized, making the method easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient sample size**: Only 20 samples per scenario, which is not adequate for robust conclusions.
- **Baseline selection inadequate**: Only two baselines (MLLM Protector, SmoothVLM) are compared, without justification for their sufficiency. Missing comparisons with many other relevant defenses such as prompt-based methods, self-evaluation, self-defense, self-reminder, input perturbation (query rewriting), fine-tuning-based methods, and no-image-input heuristics. Also missing ablation on single-round vs multi-round debating (e.g., majority vote from round 1, moderator summarizing without debate).
- **Lack of clear evaluation criteria**: The definition of a “successful attack” is not explicitly stated; the indicator function used for this is unclear. The paper claims 100% ASR for baselines but does not otherwise mention this value in the main content.
- **Over-reliance on GPT-4 for evaluation**: Quality scoring (0–5) and attack success assessment are done solely by GPT-4 without human validation or consistency checks. This introduces potential bias and lacks rigorous justification.
- **Quality of response claim not rigorously validated**: The paper claims maintained response quality, but the scoring method using GPT-4 lacks detail and the method is not evaluated on standard capability benchmarks (e.g., on MM-SafetyBench the models may not need to analyze images).
- **Missing stronger attacks and defenses**: White-box attacks are not considered, and common defenses like refusal training are not included.
- **No computational cost or efficiency analysis**: The debate framework is resource-intensive, yet the paper does not discuss inference time, computational overhead, or practical feasibility.
- **Limited novelty concern** (minority reviewer): One reviewer found the multi-agent debate framework not new, as it is well-explored in previous work, and the paper simply applies it to defense tasks without clear advantage demonstrations. Other reviewers found it novel for VLM security.
- **Lack of ablation studies on components**: No ablation on the impact of different components of the proposed method.
- **Grammar errors**: Several grammar issues, e.g., “other debater's answers” → “other debaters' answers”, “can notebly decrease” → “can notably decrease”, “Additionlly” → “Additionally”.
