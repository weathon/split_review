## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces CHRONOS, a self-supervised learning (SSL) framework for time series (specifically ECG signals) that aims to capture three distinct temporal attributes: static, short-term, and long-term dynamics. It extends the DEBS approach by using two projection spaces, multiple contrastive/consistency losses, and a selective optimization scheme that masks low-similarity features per objective. Evaluation is on four ECG datasets across arrhythmia detection, gender identification, age regression, and a PhysioNet Challenge. Performance is reported against Mixing-Up, TF-C, PCLR, BYOL, and DEBS. Reviewers note genuine ideas and thorough experiments but raise significant concerns about insufficient related work, missing comparisons, methodological clarity, and limited evaluation outside ECG.

### Strengths

- **Novel conceptual contribution.** The idea of explicitly modeling static, short-term, and long-term dynamics in self-supervised time series learning is not fully explored in the literature and has potential for other domains (e.g., video analysis). The integration of SSL with a teacher-student paradigm (inspired by BYOL) and separate projection heads for different temporal granularities is a genuine innovation (Reviewer 2: score 5, confidence 4; Reviewer 1: score 3, confidence 3; Reviewer 4: score 3, confidence 3).
- **Well-designed experiments.** The evaluation is performed across four independent datasets and four diverse downstream tasks that explicitly require different types of features (static vs. dynamic). The experiments are clear and well-organized, with noticeable performance gains over most baselines (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2).
- **Ablation studies.** The authors include ablation for the long-term loss component and the selective optimization mechanism (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4; Reviewer 1).
- **Feature analysis.** Efforts are made to study learned features by examining their dynamics across recordings and importance for different tasks (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4 appreciates this direction but notes clarity issues).
- **Detailed implementation description.** The paper provides a relatively detailed explanation of the experimental setup (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2, though others question clarity).
- **(Reviewer 2, minority pro-positive view):** The framework communicates complex ideas and is understandable to knowledgeable readers. The overall quality is adequate, with potential for significant field contribution.

### Weaknesses

- **Grossly insufficient related work.** The introduction and related work only cite four works, omitting many essential SSL-for-time-series papers (e.g., Franceschi et al. NeurIPS 2019, Cheng et al. 2020, Mohsenvand et al. ML4H 2020, Tonekaboni et al. ICLR 2020, Kiyasseh et al. ICML 2021, Yeche et al. ICML 2021, Eldele et al. IJCAI 2021, Fan et al. ICASSP 2021). The review lacks depth, fails to discuss current challenges, and does not properly situate CHRONOS against the state of the art (Reviewer 4, strong; Reviewer 2, consistent).
- **Limited generalizability evaluation.** The method is motivated by and evaluated only on ECG data. The claim of general applicability to other time series domains is unsupported. Evaluation on standard benchmarks like the UCR archive or FD-a/FD-b tasks (used by TF-C) is necessary (Reviewer 1; others echo).
- **Missing comparisons with strong time-series models.** No comparison against time-series-specific transformer models (e.g., FormerTime, Informer) is provided. The encoder used is from DEBS; upgrading the encoder might yield improvements across all SSL baselines (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity of the methodology is insufficient.** Several reviewers find the writing confusing and incomplete:
  - Relationship between projector, predictor, teacher, and student is not explained (Reviewer 3).
  - It is unclear which representation is used for downstream tasks (Reviewer 3).
  - Eq. (5) computes an inner product between static and dynamic spaces, implying they are in the same space, contradicting the claim of two separate spaces (Reviewer 3).
  - Despite claiming three types of dynamics (static, short-term, long-term), the model only learns two representations (static and short-term) (Reviewer 3).
  - The loss functions lack clear motivation: why use two different records in Eq. (2)? Why compare projection to prediction rather than both projections? What are i and j in Eq. (3)? How are hyperparameters determined? (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4).
  - The loss set appears problematic: Eq. (2) and (3) drive similarity only, and Eq. (5) only encourages difference between dynamic and static features. The covariance loss (Eq. (6), borrowed from VICReg) may be the main driver of performance but requires ablation (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4).
  - No notation section is provided, and the figures lack descriptive captions (e.g., no description of Figure 1 components; Figures 2 and 3 color scheme unexplained) (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4).
- **Missing experiments and ablations.**
  - Ablation for the covariance loss is missing, so it is impossible to attribute performance gains to the proposed disentanglement vs. this regularization (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 3).
  - No ablation comparing two projection spaces vs. a single space to justify the design choice (Reviewer 4).
  - No ablation for the short-term loss window parameter (Reviewer 4).
  - In the ablation study (Section 5), the removal of the long-term loss is only tested with a 25/50/25 time-scale ratio; would the long-term loss add more benefit at higher long-term ratios? (Reviewer 1).
  - Consider an ablation where selective optimization is replaced by random loss selection at each pre-training step (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Weak statistical evidence for empirical results.** The reported small mean differences with large variances (Table 1) may not be statistically significant. Statistical tests (e.g., p-values) are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity of figures.** Figures showing feature importance and overlap use numbers instead of feature names, limiting interpretability (Reviewer 1). The meaning of colored bars in Figures 2 and 3 is not explained (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation quality is poor.** Multiple grammatical errors and typos (e.g., “electrocardiagram,” “timeseries,” redundant phrases, incorrect use of “a” vs. “an”). Sentences are difficult to follow. Acronyms are defined in the abstract but not redefined upon first use in the main text. Vague value statements like “achieve excellent performance” should be replaced with specific values (Reviewer 2, strong; Reviewer 1 notes nits).
- **Unclear novelty relative to DEBS.** The manuscript has similarities to the DEBS paper (including section structure) without clear referencing. The specific innovations of CHRONOS beyond DEBS need explicit clarification (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 notes CHRONOS extends DEBS but does not quantify).
- **Lack of intuitive explanation.** No intuitive explanation is given for why the proposed methodology should be effective (Reviewer 2).
- **Section structure and readability.** The introduction should provide a roadmap of sections. Sections III and IV lack introductory paragraphs setting reader expectations (Reviewer 2).
- **Pretraining dataset choice not justified.** The choice of Sleep Heart Health Study for pretraining is not explained; the authors do not try alternative pretraining datasets (Reviewer 1, question).
