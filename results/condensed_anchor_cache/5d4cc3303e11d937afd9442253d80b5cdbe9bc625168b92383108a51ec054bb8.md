## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a theoretical framework for disentanglement in diffusion models under an ℓ2 score approximation, introduces an implicit bottleneck variable, applies the theory to voice conversion as a canonical disentanglement problem, and presents an adapt-and-vote scheme for domain adaptation. Reviewers find the topic important but raise major concerns about presentation, unclear contributions, weak experimental validation of the theory, and feasibility of the theoretical assumptions. One reviewer (Rating 5, Confidence 3) is notably more positive, praising the mix of theory and experiments and the bounds on ensemble improvement rate, while the other three (Ratings 3,3,3) see significant flaws.

### Strengths
- The problem of disentanglement in diffusion models is important and timely (Reviewer 4).
- The paper provides theorems showing that conditional diffusion models can learn disentangled representations under certain assumptions (Reviewer 1).
- The use of conditional diffusion models for voice conversion to create zero-shot adaptation datasets, and the adapt-and-vote scheme to improve downstream classification performance, is an interesting experimental paradigm (Reviewer 1).
- The bounds relating ensemble improvement rate (EIR) to speaker distortion are considered impressive (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments on synthetic data lend some support to the theoretical claims, and results on real speech tasks with different voice conversion models and aggregation schemes are provided (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The paper offers a good mix of theory and applied results, appreciated by one reviewer (Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 2 (Rating 5) is more positive than the majority, finding the overall approach solid and the experimental results convincing, in contrast to the other reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and readability**: The paper is very difficult to parse; notation is used before being defined, many symbols (e.g., γ, q_γ, d, χ, ζ, η, θ̂, G_<t, t*, Θ^{a→b}) are undefined or inconsistently defined, and there are numerous typos and formula mistakes (e.g., wrong drift term signs, missing time differentials, mixing forward/reverse time, missing definitions of distance measures). Multiple reviewers (1, 3, 4) note that the interleaving of information-theoretic results and voice conversion details leads to heavy notation and a confusing narrative, making the contributions unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Implicit bottleneck variable**: The central concept is ambiguous because the functions η, ζ, γ are not unique, and the definition relies on a critical timestep t* whose existence is not guaranteed. Assumptions about ζ*’s properties (e.g., I(ζ*;X)) may be infeasible (Reviewer 4). The role of the implicit bottleneck in the theoretical framework is not sufficiently justified.
- **Unclear contribution and motivation**: The paper does not clearly state what its main contribution is (e.g., is it the adapt-and-vote scheme? The theoretical bounds?). The motivation for studying disentanglement in the chosen setting is weak, and the paper lacks a focused narrative (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Experimental shortcomings**:
    - The theoretical framework is tested only on synthetic data (linear subspace models); no real voice conversion experiments validate the disentanglement theory (Reviewer 4). The claim of describing general diffusion models is unsubstantiated by the experimental scope.
    - The linear subspace model (Equation 18) for speech features is borrowed from discriminative tasks and is unlikely to yield high-quality generative voice conversion; results do not extrapolate (Reviewer 4).
    - The real-data experiments correspond to ensembling theory (Section 3), not disentanglement. The conclusion that “adding target speakers reduces speaker distortion” can be explained by reduced variance rather than the theoretical framework (Reviewer 4). Two other conclusions (“Different VC excels at different tasks” and “Tradeoff between classifier accuracy and diversity”) are interesting but not explained by the theory (Reviewer 4).
    - No baselines or SOTA performance numbers are reported for the classification tasks; the methods used are only described in the appendix, not the main text, and the significance of the results is not established (Reviewer 3).
    - Missing comparisons to other out-of-distribution techniques; no demonstration of disentangling without voice cloning (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of generalization**: The focus on speech tasks is limiting; it would be interesting to see if conclusions hold for other modalities (e.g., image detection/segmentation/recognition) (Reviewer 2).
- **Assumption justification**: Key assumptions (e.g., Assumption 2) are not justified, and their necessity and implications are unclear (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Multiple unresolved technical questions**: 
    - The relationship between A and Z_t in Formula (4) – both seem to capture content but are not clearly distinguished.
    - The definition of the implicit bottleneck in Formula (13) mixes forward and reverse diffusion times, leading to confusion.
    - In Definition 3, why “max” instead of “sup” for unbounded Z?
    - The dimensionality mismatch between G (abstract speaker identity) and Ĝ (speaker embedding) in Assumption 2 is unaddressed.
    - How the random speaker embedding ρ is generated and its relation to the target distribution β is not explained; what is the best way to select ρ?
    - The bound in Theorem 4 gives an upper bound on the ensemble error rate, but the minimum improvement from voting is more interesting and not provided.
    - The formula (5) for the score function is incorrect for Variance Preserving diffusions (missing a(t) factor).
