## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an online prompt optimization framework for text-to-image diffusion models to generate minority (low-likelihood) samples. A learnable token is appended to the user prompt and optimized during inference to maximize a likelihood-based objective that encourages low-density features while preserving semantics. Experiments are conducted on SDv1.5, SDv2.0, and SDXL-Lightning. Reviewers’ overall scores range from 3 to 6, with one minority reviewer (score 3) finding the novelty limited and the experiments unconvincing, while others (scores 5-6) find the approach well-motivated and methodologically sound but note missing analyses.

### Strengths
- **Generalizability across models**: The method is validated on multiple T2I architectures (SDv1.5, SDv2.0, SDXL-Lightning, including distilled backbones), demonstrating broad applicability. (R1, R3)
- **Semantic preservation**: By optimizing a single learnable token placed at the end of the prompt, the approach improves diversity without significantly compromising text alignment or image quality. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Theoretical grounding**: The paper provides a clear intuition, starting from a naive application of prior work [Um & Ye 2024], carefully analyzing its weakness (Eq. 5), and proposing a novel objective to address it. (R1, R3)
- **Clear presentation**: The writing is well-organized, easy to follow, and explains motivations and design choices clearly. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Flexible objective framework**: The method allows different objective functions to achieve varied functionalities (e.g., bias mitigation). (R2)
- **Bias mitigation potential**: Quantitative results show the method can reduce bias in generated images. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty (minority reviewer)**: One reviewer (score 3) finds the work a small incremental step over [Um & Ye 2024]; the core idea of optimizing a token embedding is adapted from that prior work, with only stability and controllability enhancements. No qualitative or quantitative comparison is provided against the naive approach of Eq. (5), which the authors claim has theoretical issues. (R4; contrast: other reviewers value the analysis and solutions for [Um & Ye 2024] as a strength.)
- **Missing statistical significance**: The paper reports e.g. a 0.17 drop in PickScore on SDv1.5 (Table 1) but provides no standard errors or confidence intervals. Without these, it is impossible to judge whether differences are meaningful or ties. (R3)
- **Incomplete image quality analysis**: The authors claim minimal compromise to image quality but do not provide quality metrics (e.g., FID). (R1) Additionally, the method appears to trade off image quality (PickScore) for prompt alignment (CLIPScore) – a trade-off also achievable by varying CFG. The paper uses a fixed CFG of 7.5; the frontier of CLIPScore vs. image quality should be explored by varying CFG in the base model to check if MinorityPrompt is strictly better or outside that frontier. (R3)
- **Unconvincing quantitative results**: Improvements are mostly observed on SDv2.1 (Table 1), while gains on other models are limited or absent. (R4) The lack of improvement is not explained.
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**:
  - No comparison against direct prompt engineering (e.g., using "old man" instead of the bias example in Fig. 1). (R2)
  - No qualitative comparison against DDIM+null baseline. (R4)
  - For fairness/bias mitigation experiments, baselines such as Iti-Gen, FairDiffusion, and aDFT are not compared. (R3)
  - The method should be tested on different samplers (ODEs, SDEs). (R2)
- **Efficiency and step count**: The method introduces additional (online) training; no efficiency analysis is provided. (R2) Performance changes with diffusion models using fewer steps (e.g., distilled models) are not studied. (R2)
- **Metric concerns**: Precision and recall are inadequate for diversity evaluation; the authors should use metrics from Naeem et al. (2024) instead. (R4) Also, CLIPScore improves even though the text input remains unchanged – why? (R4) A user study is suggested to measure diversity and quality. (R4)
- **Token placement**: The paper states (line 296) that placing the placeholder token at the end yields the best performance, but does not explain why. (R4)
- **Limited ablation on token count**: Would optimizing more than one token lead to better results? (R4)
- **Prompt specificity**: The evaluation should use prompts that explicitly correspond to minority instances to demonstrate the method's advantage; otherwise any generated image may be considered reasonable and not necessarily a minority sample. (R2)
- **Experimental setup detail**: The paper uses 10k images for SD1.5/2 and 5k for SDXL-LT – why this difference? This is relevant to statistical significance. (R3, question overlapping with weakness 1)
