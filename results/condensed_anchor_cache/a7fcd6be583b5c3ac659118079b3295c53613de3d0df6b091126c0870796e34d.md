## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a backdoor attack using Visible, Semantic, Sample-Specific, and Compatible (VSSC) triggers, generated via an automated pipeline that employs large language models to select trigger objects and diffusion models for seamless insertion. The goal is to achieve robustness against visual distortions (blur, compression, noise) and practical deployment in physical scenarios. Extensive experiments evaluate effectiveness, stealthiness, and robustness.

### Strengths
- The concept is interesting and inspiring; it could make backdoor detection more challenging (Reviewer 1).
- Presentation is motivating and easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- The use of a large language model to autonomously determine trigger objects and a diffusion model for inpainting is an evolution of prior physical attacks (e.g., Chen et al., 2017), where triggers were manually selected (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed method achieves successful attacks with a modest poisoning ratio of 5% (Reviewer 2).
- The trigger has been evaluated on various datasets and compared with many existing works (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation does not fully support claims.** The trigger generation pipeline (especially the LLM-based selection described in Section 4.2 Stage 1) is not clearly assessed in the experimental section; it is unclear how the triggers used in evaluation are selected (Reviewer 1).
- **Performance is not compelling.** In Tables 3, 4, and 9, several baseline methods outperform the proposed approach. Many bold (best) values are from baselines, contradicting the paper’s textual claims. This weakens the argument that the proposed method is better than existing attacks (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Ambiguous metrics.** In Tables 3 and 4, the lowest ACC is highlighted, but an effective backdoor attack should have both high ACC and high ASR. The highlighting choice is confusing (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited robustness analysis.** The distortion tests cover only blurring, compression, and noise. Other physical attack papers consider additional distortions such as transformations, shrinking, and padding, which are missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation on poisoning ratio.** The paper does not study the influence of different poisoning ratios on attack performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Potential trigger class conflict.** If the LLM proposes a trigger object that belongs to a class in the training dataset, it could cause misclassification or confusion. The paper does not explain how to avoid this (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty and depth are limited.** The VSSC trigger is essentially a “physical” trigger generated digitally, similar to the seminal backdoor attack (Chen et al., 2017) where only tens of face images with sunglasses were used. That work used weak blended triggers, making the claimed advantage of a low poisoning ratio less novel. The proposed generation method appears unnecessary in practice (Reviewer 3).
- **Cost and domain limitation.** Training a generative model is costly; using off-the-shelf models like Stable Diffusion limits the application domain (e.g., it cannot generate realistic tumors in medical images or handle hyperspectral imagery without expensive fine-tuning). This is supported by the ASR vs. PR curves in Figure 6 (Reviewer 3).
- **Methodology lacks rigor.** The trigger assessment method (vision-language model) lacks clear justification—no evidence that models outperform humans in recognizing the injected trigger objects, and it is unclear why a trigger recognizable by an object detector would still be learned by the classifier. The entire pipeline appears heuristic (Reviewer 3).
- **Omission of relevant works.** The generative model used is not state-of-the-art (e.g., Cones, Liu et al., 2023). Visible, sample-specific triggers have been studied in earlier defenses (Xiang et al., 2020) (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation details missing.** The algorithm description in Section 4.2 would benefit from pseudo-code instead of natural language. Adopted techniques (image editing, dense caption) are only cited; brief descriptions would improve readability (Reviewer 1).
- **Reviewer disagreement on contribution.** Reviewer 1 finds the work inspiring (contribution score 3), Reviewer 2 moderate (contribution 2), and Reviewer 3 assesses contribution as poor (contribution 1). The minority view (Reviewer 3) is significantly more negative regarding novelty and practical utility (Reviewer 3).
