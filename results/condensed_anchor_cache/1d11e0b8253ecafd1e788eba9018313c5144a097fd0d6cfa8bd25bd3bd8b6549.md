## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies distribution shifts in Machine Learning Force Fields (MLFFs) and proposes two test-time refinement strategies: radius refinement (RR) to align test graph structures with training distributions via spectral graph theory, and test-time training (TTT) using cheap priors (e.g., sGDML) to adjust representations. Reviewers generally found the problem well-motivated and the analysis of OOD types valuable, but raised concerns about practical utility, accuracy remaining far from chemical accuracy, assumptions about test data availability, experimental scope, and limited novelty of TTT. One reviewer (R4, rating 8) was notably more positive than others (ratings 5–6), highlighting the first application of test-time refinement to MLFFs while also emphasizing methodological assumptions and need for clearer justification.

### Strengths
- The problem of distribution shifts in MLFFs is important and well-motivated, especially for AI-for-Science and extrapolation tasks like MD simulations (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper identifies three primary origins of domain shift in MLFFs, most of which are not explicitly categorized in prior literature (R4).
- It is the first work to apply test-time refinement strategies (RR and TTT with cheap priors) to MLFFs (R4).
- Establishes practical criteria and metrics (e.g., force norm, graph Laplacian eigenvalues) for diagnosing distribution shifts, with valuable analysis of different OOD flavors (R1, R3).
- The proposed methods show meaningful improvements over large-scale foundation models and pre-trained baselines on several benchmarks (R1, R2, R3).
- The radius refinement approach (searching for the most similar graph structure by tuning cutoff radius) is interesting (R2).
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Accuracy far from chemical accuracy and limited practical utility**: Force MAE/RMSE improvements remain magnitudes away from the ~1 kcal/mol/Å (~43 meV/Å) threshold required for reliable simulations. Most results do not reach this threshold, and the methods are of more academic than practical interest (R1, R2, R4). R2 states that the proposed method “could hardly be useful in practice,” while R1 notes that gains are modest and do not bring OOD samples to in-distribution performance. R4 (rating 8) agrees that accuracy is insufficient for practical use, calling the method “more of academic than practical interest,” but still finds the work promising.
- **Unclear assumptions about test data and evaluation protocol**: The methods assume access to test structures and, for TTT, require a cheap prior to generate pseudo-labels, which conflicts with standard ML evaluation where test data is strictly held out (R2, R4). This setup needs to be explicitly justified and its limitations acknowledged in the abstract and introduction (R4). R2 adds that if the test set has unseen elements or novel substructures, using a pre-trained MLFF may not be ideal even with these strategies.
- **Radius refinement (RR) may introduce potential energy discontinuities and lacks MD validation**: Adjusting the cutoff radius can cause sudden force changes, a well-known issue for MLFFs. The paper does not examine RR’s impact on MD simulations (R3, R4). Furthermore, the MD analysis only uses NVT (canonical) simulations, which can artificially stabilize dynamics; a fair comparison should use NVE (microcanonical) simulations to test energy conservation (R4).
- **Laplacian spectrum alignment motivation needs more intuition**: The justification for aligning graph spectra lacks theoretical or intuitive grounding. It would help to clarify why this alignment is expected to mitigate distribution shifts (R1). R2 also questions how multiple training molecules’ eigenvalue distributions are aggregated and suggests that similarity to any single training molecule suffices; case studies showing how radius changes affect connectivity would strengthen the argument.
- **TTT is not novel**: Using low-cost priors for test-time training/fine-tuning of MLFFs has been explored in prior work (R2).
- **Missing experiments and incomplete analysis**:
  - No results combining MACE+TTT and GemNet-T+RR (R4).
  - GemNet-T was trained on only 10% of the data; it would be more informative to compare models trained on identical data (R3).
  - MD simulations were only performed for TTT, not RR, despite RR’s potential impact on dynamics (R3, R4).
  - It would be interesting to test SPICE-trained models on MD simulations with unseen molecules as a more direct generalization task (R3).
  - The effect of OOD-ness on MD stability when using TTT is not explored (R3).
- **Statistical significance not reported**: Small improvements in Table 1 (e.g., 26.75 vs. 26.0) may be due to random fluctuations; statistical analyses are needed (R1).
- **Missing related work summary**: The main paper should include at least a brief summary of related work (R2).
- **Critical content relegated to appendix**: Sections on “Necessity of Proper Pre-training for Test-Time Training” and “Notes on the Prior” are in the appendix but appear to be important for understanding the method’s validity (R4).
- **Questions on model choice and prior**: MACE, GemNet-T, and NequIP were chosen; it is unclear if they represent the broader MLFF landscape (R4). The prior (sGDML) may itself have generalization issues on OOD samples; using semi-empirical methods (e.g., DFTB) could offer better generalization (R1). R4 also asks whether TTT is more accurate than directly training a model on prior labels from classical force fields.
