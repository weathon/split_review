## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the problem of reconstructing a static background and 360° objects by fusing multiple neural radiance fields (NeRFs) of the same scene with different object arrangements. It proposes a decomposition into segmentation/alignment (using point cloud methods) and radiance-field fusion via a novel scalar volumetric visibility field and visibility-aware rendering. Experiments are conducted on a synthetic dataset (objects on a flat tabletop) and a real dataset in a similar controlled setup. The method is motivated by the idea that occluded parts become visible across different scene configurations.

### Strengths
- **Problem motivation and setting.** The paper studies an interesting and largely ignored problem in NeRF: handling invisible/occluded parts by observing the same objects in multiple arrangements (R1, R3, R5). The setting is well-motivated, even though the current assumptions are strong (R1, R5).
- **Clarity and presentation.** The writing is clear, easy to follow, and discusses assumptions thoroughly (R2, R4). The proposed method is simple and straightforward (R2).
- **Proposed visibility field and visibility-aware rendering.** The introduction of the visibility field as a volumetric representation to quantify visibility, and the use of visibility-aware rendering for fusion, are interesting and sound (R3, R4, R5). The fusion approach is novel and reasonable (R3).
- **Experimental demonstration.** The method shows effective results for reconstructing objects with 360° visibility on both synthetic and real datasets (R2, R3, R5). The creation of these datasets is a positive step (R5). Ablation studies on lighting, number of scenes, and object placement are included (R2, though some are considered unnecessary).
- **Practical problem.** Multi-scene registration and reconstruction is a practical problem worth solving (R4). The idea of capturing/scanning a scene with different configurations is reasonable because invisible parts can only be obtained when they become visible (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of quantitative evaluation.** No quantitative metrics are provided (R1, R4). The paper should include segmentation metrics (e.g., 2D IoU), reconstruction quality (PSNR, 3D Chamfer distance), and comparisons to baselines (R4). Without numbers, it is hard to assess actual performance or failure modes (R1). The paper claims “consistently satisfactory” results, but that is nearly guaranteed with clean inputs (R1).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations.** There is no comparison to a baseline method, such as training a single NeRF per component across all videos (shared representation) instead of per-video (R4). Ablation studies are incomplete: the effect of removing the visibility composition, comparison of max vs. weighted selection, and the impact of different selection strategies are not shown (R4). The ablation on lighting is too simple and does not reflect real-world directional light sources, shadows, or view-dependent specularities (R2).
- **Weak experimental setup (toy examples).** All experiments use very simple, controlled scenes: flat background, diffuse surfaces with no view-dependent effects, no directional light sources, no shadows or reflections, and objects easily separable (R2). The setting is too ideal to generalize to in-the-wild data (R1, R2, R4, R5). Real-world results are reported as blurry (R5), indicating quality issues that are not analyzed. The background is assumed unchanged across videos; real-world backgrounds may change (e.g., tablecloth) (R4).
- **Strong, unrealistic assumptions.** The method assumes fixed illumination across all scene variants, which is unrealistic for many real applications (R2, R5). No modeling of scene illumination or shadows leads to artefacts (Figure 4 shows darker/shadowed parts) (R5). It also assumes accurate pose alignment for all objects, but registration noise is not analyzed (R1, R3). The background must be flat? (R1). The method cannot handle dynamic objects (R2). If one can control the scene, capturing each object independently may be simpler than this setup (R2, R5).
- **Limited technical novelty.** The segmentation and alignment components rely on traditional point-cloud registration and clustering (DBSCAN) (R4, R5). The visibility calculation is straightforward (R4, R5). Once objects are segmented and aligned, one could directly reconstruct an object NeRF using self-calibration; the benefit of the visibility field is unclear (R2). The fusion approach, while interesting, may not push the field sufficiently (R5).
- **Scalability concerns.** The method trains a separate NeRF per video (M videos × N objects?); this is computationally expensive and redundant when geometry/appearance is consistent across variants. A single representation per component from all videos would be more efficient (R4). The method also likely requires dense observations for each NeRF (R2).
- **Robustness of segmentation and alignment.** Object segmentation relies on heuristic clustering (k-means or DBSCAN) based on point clouds. This works in simple scenes but is unlikely to survive more complex settings, textured objects, or reflection/textless regions (R3). NeRF point-clouds can be noisy in such cases (R3). Registration failure (e.g., partial scans, non-rigid objects) is acknowledged as a limitation but not evaluated (R4).
- **Missing analyses and reviewer questions.** Several reviewer questions overlap with criticisms that should be part of the experimental evaluation:
  - How many images/viewpoints are needed? (R1)
  - How does the method perform when the background is not flat? (R1)
  - How robust is it to noise in point-cloud registration? (R1, R3)
  - Why not use semantic segmentation or object features for matching? (R3)
  - Why not pre-capture the background alone and each object separately? (R3)
  - Do you need to predefine the number of objects? (R3)
  - How exactly is the clean background obtained? (R3)
  - The term “scene” is confusing; “video 1…N” would be clearer (R4)
  - Fig. 3 bottom-right subfigure: should points near ray end be red (not visible)? (R4)
  - Eq. 4: it would be helpful to show the exact temperature-scaled softmax equation (R4)
