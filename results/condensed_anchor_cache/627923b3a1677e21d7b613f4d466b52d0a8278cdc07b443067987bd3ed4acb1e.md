## Merged Review

### Summary
LEMoN uses multimodal nearest neighbors to identify inconsistent image-caption pairs, leveraging distances in the latent space of contrastively pretrained models. The method is evaluated on classification (CIFAR10/100), captioning (COCO, Flickr30k), and a medical dataset, using synthetic noise injection. It shows improvements over baselines in label error detection, though downstream captioning gains are often marginal. A theoretical analysis is provided.

### Strengths
- The research problem is important: large noisy image-caption datasets are widely used and filtering them is valuable for downstream model reliability.
- The proposed metric (leveraging multimodal nearest neighbors) is novel, well-motivated, and intuitive. The approach is a training-free method that outperforms existing metrics (e.g., CLIP similarity) in label error identification in most synthetic-noise settings.
- The paper provides a clear, well-organized presentation that is easy to follow. The high-level idea and the derivation of the scoring function (Eq. 2) are explained reasonably.
- Theoretical justification is given (though reviewer 4 raises flaws – see weaknesses). Most reviewers found the reasoning plausible and supportive of the method.
- Experiments are conducted across a wide range of datasets (image classification, image-caption, medical) and include ablation on hyperparameters (neighborhood size \(k\), radius \(r\)), showing robustness.
- The method shows notable improvements in detection performance, especially among training-free approaches, and the impact on downstream classification and captioning is evaluated.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical flaws**: Reviewer 4 identifies several significant errors in the theoretical justifications (Theorems 4.1, 4.2, Proposition A.1):
  - Theorem 4.1 contains contradictory conditions (\(\eta\) defined as normal but also satisfying \(|\eta| > \epsilon\)).
  - Proposition A.1 Part 3 inequality does not hold because the condition \(y' \ne y\) is omitted, leading to \(\mathbb{E} = 0\).
  - Frequent interchange of labels and embeddings (e.g., line 913 replacing embedding of label \(y'\) with label \(y\) and an additive term, violating Assumption 1).
  - Expectation operator lost when transitioning between equations (lines 915–917).
  - Variance error in Theorem 4.2 proof: \(\frac{1}{k} \text{Var} E\) instead of \(\frac{1}{k^2} \text{Var} E\).
  These flaws limit the strength of the theoretical claims. (Note: other reviewers called the theory “reasonable” – this disagreement should be considered.)

- **Marginal downstream improvements**: On captioning tasks (Table 4), the improvement of LEMoN over the CLIP similarity baseline is trivial. This weakens the practical motivation, as label-error detection is primarily useful if it improves downstream performance. On the web-collected dataset CC3M (Appendix I.12), there is no clear gain, which is particularly concerning given the paper’s motivation.

- **Lack of component ablation**: The contribution of each term (\(d_{mm}\), \(s_m\), \(s_n\)) is not clearly reported. The paper does not provide a comparison using only a single-side nearest neighbor term (e.g., only image-side or only text-side), nor a breakdown of how the three terms interact across datasets. This makes it hard to understand why the method works better on some datasets than others.

- **Sensitivity and parameter tuning**: The paper does not systematically explore the sensitivity of LEMoN to the choice of neighborhood size \(k\) and radius \(r\) beyond a few values. Symbols such as \(r\), \(D\), \(k\) in Section 3 are not defined until later, making the exposition harder to follow.

- **Synthetic-noise evaluation only**: All image-caption experiments use artificial noise (e.g., replacing object names). Real web-crawled datasets contain more diverse noise types (e.g., captions focusing on a specific aspect, varying detail levels). The method’s behavior on such real-world noise is not validated; the limited experiments on CC3M show no improvement.

- **Missing comparisons and baselines**:
  - The source code does not include implementations of baseline methods, so how their hyperparameters were tuned is unclear.
  - A recent related work [1] (VDC) proposes a similar multi-modal scoring approach; a detailed comparison is missing.
  - On the area under the accuracy/filter-out-rate curve (Appendix I.12), results on only two datasets suggest that Deep k-NN may be a more effective alternative. This undermines the claim that LEMoN is superior and the evaluation is insufficient to generalize.

- **Unimodal dataset application unclear**: For unimodal datasets like CIFAR10/100, it is not specified how text nearest neighbors are defined (e.g., there are no captions; the method seems designed for multimodal data and the adaptation is not explained).

- **Inference speed and scalability**: No analysis of runtime or scaling to billion-scale datasets is provided, leaving feasibility unclear.

- **Figure 3 quality**: The lines overlap too much and are difficult to distinguish.

- **Curious result on Flickr30k**: CLIP similarity outperforms LEMoN on this dataset, which is not explained.

- **Marginal improvement on captioning downstream** (noted by multiple reviewers): The improvements over CLIP similarity in the captioning downstream task are very small; analysis of why LEMoN does not help more is missing.
