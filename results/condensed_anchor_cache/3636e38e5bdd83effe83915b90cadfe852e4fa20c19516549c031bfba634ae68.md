## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge good performance across multiple image restoration tasks (deraining, deblurring, dehazing, desnowing, low-light enhancement) and sufficient experiments. However, the majority (ratings 3,3,3) find limited novelty: the architecture is an incremental combination of existing techniques (modulation, Transformer, DFFN from FFTTransformer, SMB block resembling RGT) without clear motivation or analysis. One reviewer gave a higher rating (5) but still notes lack of substantial new insights. Key concerns include: missing ablations (parameter sharing, CMB, SMB), insufficient comparisons with recent SOTA (e.g., AdaRevD, SegFFTformer, AutoDIR, InstructIR) and missing standard tasks (denoising, SR), unclear efficiency claims (higher FLOPs than prior work), and experiments that are not fully aligned with the paper's claims (e.g., all-in-one task not originally designed for, inconsistent table formatting).

### Strengths
1. Sufficient experiments on ten datasets for five image restoration tasks (image motion deblurring, deraining, dehazing, desnowing, low-light enhancement), plus all-in-one experiments. (R1, R2, R3)
2. Good performance results on several benchmarks. (R1, R3)
3. High-level design incorporates interesting concepts: feature downscaling for efficient processing, parameter sharing, channel-wise operations in upper UNet layers and spatially-focused in deeper layers. (R2)
4. The paper is well-structured and easy to read. (R3, R4)
5. One reviewer (R4) found the approach novel, particularly the bio-inspired parameter-sharing technique and the revisit of Transformer and modulation design. (R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and incremental engineering**: The proposed attention block is largely a combination of existing Transformer blocks and modulation designs, not newly proposed. The DFFN module closely resembles the FFN from FFTTransformer [CVPR 2023] and the SMB block resembles RGT [ICLR 2024]. No clear reasoning is given for why the specific combination is beneficial. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
2. **Missing ablation studies**: Ablations for key components (parameter sharing, CMB, SMB) are not provided or are insufficient. Table 8 shows only marginal improvements from parameter sharing, and there is no comparison between networks with and without parameter sharing to validate its effectiveness. (R2, R3, R4)
3. **Efficiency claims unsupported**: Despite claiming efficiency, the proposed network uses higher FLOPs than prior work (e.g., Tables 4 and 7). The design of extreme downsampling in the channel attention block is unusual; features are already reduced in U-Net. The rationale for using downsampled features in channel attention is unclear. (R2)
4. **Incomplete and unfair experimental comparisons**: 
   - Missing standard image restoration tasks: denoising and super-resolution (SR) are omitted, despite the paper claiming "image restoration." (R1)
   - Table 4 omits recent CVPR 2024 SOTA methods on GoPro: AdaRevD (34.64 dB) and SegFFTformer (34.48 dB) outperform the proposed method. (R3)
   - For all-in-one, comparisons should focus on recent all-in-one methods (e.g., Perceive-IR, AdaIR, AutoDIR, InstructIR) rather than general methods like MPRNet. (R1, R4)
   - The two model versions (S and B) have huge computational cost differences; base is used for deblurring without clear justification. Explanation of how versions are designed is missing. (R1)
   - Unfair: In Table 7, Ours-S outperforms FSNet and ConvIR (Base); clarification needed. (R3)
5. **Parameter sharing motivation and role**: The motivation for parameter sharing is unclear, with no compelling rationale for why it should be effective. The attention block is described as "shared" memory, but the FFN has far more parameters, making this description questionable. (R2, R3)
6. **Unclear experimental settings and missing details**:
   - Table 9 is poorly written: contains typos like "denoising," details of manually split datasets are not illustrated, and there is inconsistency between 3 tasks in the main paper and 5 tasks in the appendix. (R1)
   - Validation is confined to public datasets; real benchmark datasets are not tested, limiting generalizability assessment. (R4)
   - All-in-one settings lack computational cost (FLOPs/params) reporting. (R1)
   - The method is not designed specifically for all-in-one, yet it is included; the reason is unclear. (R1)
   - Missing results on additional benchmarks: defocus deblurring (DPDD), dehazing (ITS, OTS, DenseHaze), and RealBlur-R/J datasets were requested. (R3)
7. **Underwhelming visual comparisons**: Visual comparisons are not comprehensive; more detailed visual evidence supporting claimed improvements is needed. (R4)
8. **Presentation and consistency issues**: 
   - Figure 2 x-axis differs from other figures, impacting clarity. (R3)
   - Equation 8's specific contribution to image restoration is not clarified. (R3)
   - In Table 10, which model version ("Ours") is used is not specified. (R4)

**Reviewer disagreement note**: While most reviewers (R1, R2, R4) rated the paper low (3) and emphasized limited novelty and insufficient analysis, one reviewer (R3) gave a higher rating (5) but still noted similar concerns about lack of novelty, missing baselines, and marginal improvements. The positive reviewer (R4) considered the design contributions novel, but the majority disagreed. Efficiency claims were contradicted by one reviewer's FLOPs analysis.
