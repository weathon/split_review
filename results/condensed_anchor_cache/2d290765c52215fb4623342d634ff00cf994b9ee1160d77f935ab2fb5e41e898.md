## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the BCNAS-SNN framework to automatically search for optimal placement of backward connections (BCs) in spiking neural networks. Reviewers had mixed assessments (two scores of 5, one score of 3). Positive reviewers praised the novelty of investigating global backward connections, the two-step search strategy, and the interesting finding that searched BCs prefer front layers. The more critical reviewer found the experimental validation insufficient and requested additional comparisons and baselines. Several weaknesses were pointed out regarding limited performance gains, incomplete search analysis, and missing experiments.

### Strengths
- First comprehensive investigation of varying effects of global backward connections in SNNs (Reviewer1).
- Introduces a two-step search strategy to reduce the search space of BCs (Reviewer1, Reviewer3).
- Novel method to search for optimal BCs in SNNs; the research topic is forward-looking (Reviewer2, Reviewer3).
- Reveals an interesting phenomenon that searched BCs prefer to connect to front layers, which may inspire future SNN architecture design (Reviewer3).
- Well-written and organized, with clear definitions, figures, and tables (Reviewer3).

### Weaknesses
- Missing precise definition of "BCSNN": is it an abbreviation for "Backward Connection SNN" and does it refer to subnets extracted from a supernet? (Reviewer1)
- Lacks analysis of time series data; unclear if BCs benefit static data. Reviewer requested experiments on time series data such as DVS gesture recognition (Reviewer1).
- Search process analysis is not comprehensive:
  * Is 100 epochs sufficient for supernet convergence? No evaluation of correlation between subnet performance from supernet vs. training from scratch (Reviewer1).
  * Number of different seed values used in ablation study not reported; slight improvement might be due to random seed variation rather than search method (Reviewer1).
  * Experiment 8 of ablation: no comparison with random search strategy in step 2; evolutionary search should be compared to random search (Reviewer1).
  * Provide a plot of search iteration versus accuracy to illustrate the evolutionary search process (Reviewer1).
- Figure 1 has a missing part on the right side (Reviewer2).
- Experimental validation insufficient: need additional experiments on non-residual structures such as VGG (Reviewer2).
- Compare the method’s application in ANNs vs SNNs (Reviewer2).
- Add a comparison with accuracy without backward connections in Table 1 (Reviewer2).
- Performance increment limited compared to prior works: on CIFAR-100, Guo et al [1] achieves 79.51% vs this work's 78.59% average; on CIFAR10-DVS, 82.60% only 0.2% higher than Deng et al [2] (Reviewer3).
- Would be more convincing to include results on the ImageNet dataset (Reviewer3).
- Need to demonstrate extra training cost of the architecture search; NAS typically requires more training, and performance improvement is subtle; is the extra cost worth the small gain? (Reviewer3)
- Explain why architecture search is performed specifically on backward connections and why adding BCs improves performance; what is the advantage over other NAS methods? (Reviewer3)
- One reviewer questioned whether the observed front-layer preference is because adding such connections alleviates gradient explosion or gradient disappearance in deep networks (Reviewer3).
- Is the method constrained by computational resources, or are there plans to further optimize its efficiency? (Reviewer2)
