## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a “diversity coefficient” for pre-training text corpora, computed as the expected cosine distance between Task2Vec embeddings of random batches. The authors claim that higher diversity causally leads to better downstream evaluation performance, based on controlled interventional experiments with GPT-2 and LLaMAv2 (44 models, 51M–7B parameters). The metric is applied to several public datasets and compared to theoretical lower/upper bounds; synthetic experiments show it increases with vocabulary size and number of latent concepts. Reviewer scores are split: two reviewers give 5 (positive but with reservations) and two give 3 (critical). While all acknowledge the importance of data diversity, the majority of reviewers find the experimental evidence for the core causal claim unconvincing and the evaluation insufficient.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important and potentially impactful problem: understanding the effect of pre-training data diversity on LLM performance. (R1, R3)
- Combines concepts from vision (Task2Vec) with language modeling to create an original measure for textual data diversity. (R1, R3)
- Provides a tool to measure pre-training data diversity; the diversity results in Table 1 align well with community intuitions (CC-derived datasets more diverse than non-CC Pile subsets). (R2, R3)
- Some evidence that diversity influences model performance (specifically perplexity), demonstrating a potential relationship with generalization. (R4)

### Weaknesses
**Experimental evidence for causal claim is highly unconvincing (R3, R4)**
- Section 3.1 experiments that claim “higher diversity leads to better evaluation performance” use only three datasets (PubMed, USPTO, PubMed+USPTO), and linear regressions on three datapoints. For most GPT-2 experiments, PubMed+USPTO has similar or higher loss than PubMed while also having higher diversity. (R3)
- The relationship does not hold consistently: in some cases performance declines with increased diversity, which the authors do not address. (R4)
- Only 2 pre-training datasets are used; they are highly unusual and not representative of current LLM pre-training corpora (e.g., C4, OpenWebText, The Pile, RedPajama, SlimPajama, RefinedWeb, Dolma, FineWeb, DCLM). The results would be more convincing with a single architecture/scale and many pre-training datasets. (R3)
- Potential confounding factors are not accounted for: merging datasets changes other characteristics (token frequency distribution, sentence length) that could affect training dynamics and performance besides diversity. (R4)

**Evaluation metric is insufficient (R2, R3, R4)**
- Performance is measured only by cross-entropy loss / perplexity on C4 and OpenWebText2. It is unclear if correlation generalizes to downstream tasks (question answering, GLUE, etc.). (R2)
- Perplexity on a given evaluation set may simply reflect similarity to the pre-training data (e.g., PubMed may yield lower PPL on C4 because it is more similar, not because it is more diverse). Work on pre-training data quality has moved to “early-signal benchmarks” (FineWeb, DataComp-LM). (R3)
- Even if all results showed a consistent trend, cross-entropy alone does not provide a robust evaluation; task-specific metrics (accuracy, F1, BLEU) and statistical significance tests are missing. (R4)

**Inadequate comparison with existing baselines (R2, R3)**
- The Vendi Score (another diversity measure) is mentioned only in the appendix; it should be compared in the main experiments (e.g., Table 1) to assess relative merits in computational cost, sensitivity, and correlation with performance. (R2)
- Other simpler embeddings (N-gram, mean GPT-2 last layer embedding) should be tested to justify the choice of Task2Vec; core results (Figures 2,3,4) should be replicated with these baselines. (R3)

**Motivation for Task2Vec embeddings is unclear (R1, R3, R4)**
- Why use cosine distance between Task2Vec embeddings? What are the tradeoffs compared to other textual embedding methods? (R1)
- The claim that the metric is “interpretable” is problematic – it outputs a single black-box number just like other metrics, and the value range (lower bound ~0.05, upper bound ~0.4) is not intuitive. (R4)
- Task2Vec is not fully described in the main paper (including prior usage and intuition). (R2)

**Other empirical results (synthetic experiments, cross-diversity) are less significant (R3)**
- Showing that concatenating datasets increases diversity (Sec 3.3), that cross-diversity is higher than single-dataset diversity with well-separated histograms (Sec 3.4), and that diversity increases with vocabulary/concepts in synthetic data (Sec 3.5) are reasonable checks but not compelling contributions. They lack baselines with simpler embeddings. (R3)

**Inaccuracies in claims (R4, minor agreement from other reviewers)**
- L.59: Claim of a “paradigm shift” is factually incorrect; many previous studies have explored data diversity. Several relevant papers were overlooked (e.g., arXiv:2307.12532, 2205.06253, 2407.15724, 2103.03399v2, 2311.08695, 220a Fang et al., 2403.00553). (R4)
- L.111: Assertion that probe network parameters are “most important” for solving the task may be incorrect; needs substantiation. (R4)
- L.266+269: Bounds described as “theoretical” are actually empirical. (R4)
- L.66: “latent concepts” is unclear. (R4)
- L.191-192: Argument about lower bound is unclear. (R4)
- L.197: “non-special” token needs clarification. (R4)
- L.209: “formal diversity” is unclear. (R4)

**Questions that overlap with criticisms/missing experiments**

- Which timestep t is used to compute the FIM matrix for sequences in a batch? Are steps averaged or only the last step? (R2)
- Sec I.2 should mention the data used to pre-train GPT-2. (R2)
- L.751: “this more sophisticated aggregation method” – which method? (R2)
