## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel weight initialization method for feedforward neural networks (FFNNs) and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) using the tanh activation function, grounded in fixed-point analysis to prevent activation saturation. Experiments on classification datasets (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10) and PDE problems show improved early accuracy and convergence speed compared to Xavier initialization, with better robustness to network depth and size. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical contribution and promising results but raise concerns about limited baselines (only Xavier, no comparison with Xavier+layer normalization), missing hyperparameter details, lack of evaluation on other architectures (CNN/RNN), stability issues, and insufficient experimental evidence to guarantee generality.

### Strengths
- Novel weight initialization method specifically designed for tanh-based neural networks, using fixed-point analysis to prevent activation saturation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Strong theoretical backing: mathematical analysis with lemmas, proofs, and propositions showing activations do not vanish with increasing depth (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Experiments demonstrate faster convergence and improved performance in PINNs for solving PDEs, with consistent robustness to network depth and size variations (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Well-designed experiments spanning multiple network configurations, datasets (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10), and applications (PINNs for differential equations) (Reviewer 4).
- Paper is clearly written with consistent notations and organization (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baselines**: Compared only with Xavier initialization; missing comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods (e.g., He, LeCun) and with Xavier + layer normalization, which is known to handle the diminishing activation problem (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Significance concern**: Since Xavier + layer norm already solves diminishing activations, the problem addressed may be less relevant; no comparative analysis with or without layer norm was performed (Reviewer 4).
- **Universality**: Only tested on FFNN and PINN; unclear if method generalizes to CNNs or RNNs due to different gradient flow characteristics (Reviewer 3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and missing details**: Optimal α in Eq. 2 is empirically set to 0.085 without theoretical justification; the paper does not define α clearly (Reviewers 1, 3). Missing details on threshold parameter for FFNNs, learning rate, batch size, optimization algorithm, and training strategy (supervised vs. unsupervised) (Reviewer 3).
- **Stability and consistency**: In Figure 3 (c) and (d), Xavier sometimes performs comparably, and the proposed method’s accuracy decreases after 6 epochs, raising concerns about training stability and robustness across runs; longer training and multiple seeds are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Equation 2 mean issue**: The paper claims a_i^(k+1) follows a normal distribution with unit mean, but when N_{l-1} > N_l, the mean becomes greater than 1, potentially causing tanh saturation; this case is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Analysis of learned representations**: No exploration of weight matrices, feature maps, or embeddings to show whether the method leads to meaningful and discriminative features (Reviewer 3).
- **Overfitting in deeper networks**: In Table 2, as hidden layers increase, loss increases, indicating overfitting; suggested to combine with state-of-the-art architectures (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only 20–40 epochs trained; unclear if networks converge or how accuracy changes with more epochs (Reviewer 2). Few datasets/PDEs considered; improvement may not be general (Reviewer 2).
- **Vanishing gradient drawback of tanh**: Using tanh exclusively throughout the network has known vanishing gradient issues for very high and low inputs (Reviewer 1).
- **Complex formulation**: Equation (1) is more complex than standard methods, complicating implementation (Reviewer 1).
- **Impact dependent on exclusive tanh use**: No strong support that the method’s benefit in PINNs fundamentally relies on exclusive tanh usage (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing reproducibility**: Code not provided; results may not be reproducible without it (Reviewer 2).
- **Handling of x=0 case**: In Appendix A.1, when x=0, it is unclear how the method avoids the vanishing problem; the strategy should be highlighted (Reviewer 3).
- **Context-dependent α**: The optimal α may vary across architectures, datasets, and tasks, and interacts with hyperparameters like learning rate and batch size, complicating tuning (Reviewer 1).
