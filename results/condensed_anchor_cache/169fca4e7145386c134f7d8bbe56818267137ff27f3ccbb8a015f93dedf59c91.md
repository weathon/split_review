## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Alignment from Demonstrations (AfD), a framework that casts LLM alignment as an imitation learning problem. It uses forward and reverse KL divergence minimization and introduces a tailored reward model (Init-SFT RM) to address heterogeneity. Experiments on the HH-RLHF dataset (Harmless and Helpful tasks) compare SFT, RLHF, and BoN sampling with learned discriminators. The goal is to replace preference-based data with high-quality demonstrations.

### Strengths

- **Unified theoretical framework:** The paper effectively frames LLM alignment as distribution matching within a sequential decision-making process, linking forward KL (SFT) and reverse KL (discriminator-as-reward) objectives. This provides a fresh, rigorous perspective on alignment (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Init-SFT RM insight:** The identification of the heterogeneity problem in naive discriminator-as-reward and the proposal of Init-SFT RM (extrapolating over a reward model initialized from SFT) is a concrete solution with strong empirical performance. This insight may generalize beyond LLM alignment (Reviewer 1).
- **Reduced dependence on preference data:** AfD bypasses expensive preference annotations, offering a scalable alternative using demonstration data (Reviewer 2).
- **Clear exposition (minority positive):** One reviewer found the paper exceptionally well-written with crystal-clear takeaways (Reviewer 3). The use of colored boxes aids navigability (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns (majority):** Multiple reviewers question the novelty. Several key components are well-known: the MDP formulation for LLMs (Sec 2.2, Reviewer 4), SFT equals behavioral cloning (Sec 3.1, Reviewers 3, 4), and using model generations as negatives (Sec 3.2) is already done in SPIN and DITTO (Shaikh et al., 2024) (Reviewer 4). The observation that SFT on high-quality demos can outperform RLHF on the same number of demos (Sec 4.1) is not surprising and echoes existing results (Reviewers 3, 4). Overall, the work is seen as unifying existing ideas rather than introducing a new framework (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing implementation details and confusing experiments:** Important details about extrapolating the learned reward model (Sec 3.2) are omitted from the main text. Consequently, the results in Sec 4.3 are confusing—the only apparent difference from Sec 4.2 is the use of GPT-4 as a judge instead of the golden reward model (Reviewer 1). The presentation is dense and buries contributions; the theoretical material could be moved to an appendix to highlight core mechanics (Reviewer 2). The experimental structure is hard to follow, e.g., baselines in Sec 4.1 are unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of understanding of behavioral differences:** The analysis of mass-covering (forward KL) vs. mode-seeking (reverse KL) behavior is hand-wavy. It merely characterizes datasets as “less divergent” (harmless) and “more divergent” (helpful) without deeper investigation. A proper analysis of how these divergences affect alignment outcomes is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Overlap with SPIN / need for clearer comparison:** The unique contribution of AfD relative to SPIN is unclear. SPIN uses an implicit reward model (DPO-like objective), whereas AfD uses an explicit reward model. The paper should clarify the advantages of explicit reward modeling in terms of policy performance and robustness, and include a theoretical or empirical comparison (Reviewers 2, 4). A reviewer explicitly requested moving the SPIN comparison to the main paper (Reviewer 3).
- **Efficiency claims unsubstantiated:** The claim that AfD is more efficient than traditional RLHF lacks justification. The provided pseudocode resembles standard RLHF (reward model training + optimization). Concrete details on computational overhead reduction (e.g., fewer iterations, smaller models, more efficient data usage) are missing (Reviewer 2). The amount of demonstration data used is not reported, making it impossible to assess the cost vs. performance tradeoff (Reviewer 4).
- **Suboptimal design choices:** 
    - The preferred RM strategy compares \(\pi_{SFT}\) to \(\pi_{init}\). The optimal discriminator in that case is known in closed form: \(d^*(x,y) = \log \pi_{SFT}(y|x) - \log \pi_{init}(y|x)\). Learning a separate discriminator for BoN sampling may only degrade performance compared to using this log ratio directly (Reviewer 3). A reviewer asks to compare to the closed-form discriminator in the BoN experiments (Reviewer 3, Question 1).
    - The final policy evaluation relies on BoN with the learned RM; a proper policy optimization step on the learned RM would be more informative (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete justification for theoretical framing:**
    - The paper claims BC is only action matching, not trajectory matching. However, the KL divergence between expert and learner trajectory distributions expands to cancel dynamics, leaving the standard BC objective. Thus BC always minimizes forward KL between trajectory distributions; the distinction needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
    - The framework is described as “inverse RL” but the paper matches demonstration distributions rather than uncovering an underlying reward function. The use of the term IRL is confusing (Reviewer 1, Question 1).
- **Missing citations and related work:** Several closely related works are omitted: maxEnt IRL (Ziebart), token-level MDP (Rafailov et al., “From r to Q*”), DITTO / “Show don’t tell” (Shaikh et al.), mode-seeking behavior (Tajwar et al.), and IRL for LLMs (Wulfmeier et al.) (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and formatting issues:** The citation format is misused throughout the manuscript (Reviewer 1). The paper is overly dense and hard to follow due to too many subjects (Reviewers 2, 4). A minor point: Eq. 2 should be framed as KL-constrained / MaxEnt RL rather than standard RL (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer-posed questions that overlap with weaknesses:**
    - Why is the golden reward model used for evaluation (Sec 4.1) instead of GPT-4? (Reviewer 2)
    - How compatible is AfD with RLHF? Should we expect further improvement from RLHF after AfD, given that both are mode-seeking? (Reviewer 1)
    - If the golden RM is used for BoN, would the win rate be 1? (Reviewer 3)
    - Is the model sampled from just \(\pi_{SFT}\)? What is the theoretical interpretation of BoN with the closed-form discriminator? (Reviewer 3)
    - Why does the win rate decrease with higher \(N\) for some lines in Figure 4? (Reviewer 3)
