- Decision: Reject

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a post-hoc OOD detection method: train a normalizing flow on the penultimate-layer activations of a pretrained classifier, then threshold on the learned density. The authors report strong Far-OOD AUROC (e.g., 98.2% on ImageNet-1k vs. Textures) and discuss training pitfalls (e.g., early stopping, normalization of latent representations). Reviewers agree that the problem is important, the writing is clear, and the exploration of latent-space properties is interesting. However, most find the contribution limited, the experimental evaluation insufficient, and the novelty undermined by prior work showing that normalizing flows work well on embeddings.

### Strengths
- **Problem importance and clarity.** The paper addresses a real, high-value problem and is clearly written with an in-depth motivation of OOD detection (R1, R2).
- **Key insights for making flows work.** The discussion of training pitfalls (normalizing latent representations, early stopping) and the demonstration that normalizing flows can achieve competitive OOD detection when applied to latent space are appreciated (R1, R4).
- **Strong Far-OOD results.** The reported 98.2% AUROC on ImageNet-1k vs. Textures is highlighted (R1).
- **Post-hoc applicability.** The method can be applied to any pretrained classifier (R3, R1).
- **Novelty of hybrid approach (minority view).** One reviewer finds the combination of generative and discriminative OOD detection novel and interesting (R5); another notes that investigating the role of flows in OOD is an important direction (R1).
- **Preliminary analysis of latent space properties.** Section 6.5 (compactness of ID representations vs. OOD performance) is considered interesting (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and contradiction with prior work.** The core idea – that normalizing flows perform much better on latent features than on raw images – is already established in Kirichenko et al. (2020), which the paper itself cites. This contradicts the paper’s claim that “normalizing flows have historically performed very poorly for OOD detection” and undermines the motivation (R1, R2, R4). The paper also states in Sec. 2.3 both that “normalizing flows … have historically performed very poorly” and that “Zhang et al. demonstrate strong OOD detection performance using normalizing flows in image classification” (R2).
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation and baselines.**  
  * Missing key discriminative baselines: ODIN, Mahalanobis (R1), RankFeat (NeurIPS 2022), ASH (ICLR 2023) (R5).  
  * Missing generative OOD detection methods: Provable Guarantees (AAAI 2022) (R4), Tied VAEs (ICLR 2023), HOS (ICLR 2023) (R5).  
  * No error bars reported; AUROC uncertainty could be obtained via bootstrapping or cross-validation (R1).  
  * CIFAR10 benchmark uses only SVHN as OOD data, whereas the standard practice includes six datasets (LSUN-crop, LSUN-resize, iSUN, Places365, SVHN). The limited OOD scenarios make average performance questionable (R5).  
  * Hypotheses in the discussion (e.g., compactness of class representations correlating with OOD performance) lack quantitative evidence; only two data points (supervised vs. unsupervised backbone) are shown (R1).  
  * Ablation studies on hyperparameters and training configurations are missing (R2).
- **Missing implementation details and poor paper structure.**  
  * The method section is too short (half a page) and omits critical details: mean/variance of the target Gaussian, whether the classifier weights are frozen, whether cross-entropy loss is still enforced, and how the transformed distributions are matched to Gaussian marginals (R5).  
  * Section 3.2 is unclear and its connection to experiments is not explained (R4).  
  * The discussion section (Sec. 6.2, 6.3) refers to normalization and early stopping without introducing them in the method section (R5).  
  * Some figures (Fig. 1, Fig. 2) are not referenced in the body text; Table 4 has typos (R2).
- **Contradiction regarding OOD data requirement.** The abstract claims the method “avoid[s] researcher bias in OOD sample selection” and is “fully unsupervised,” yet Sec. 6.2 recommends evaluating on a “representative OOD dataset” to determine early stopping (R2). If early stopping is necessary for good performance, the authors must explain how the stopping point was chosen for the reported results (R1, R2).
- **Lack of comparison to other latent-space methods.** The paper does not discuss or compare with other works that exploit the latent space of deep generative models (e.g., VAEs with lower-dimensional latent spaces, which might be more “lightweight”) (R3).
- **Minority reviewer notes.** While one reviewer found the hybrid approach novel, all reviewers except the most positive one (score 5) rate the contribution as poor (1 or 2) and soundness as fair (2). Even the positive reviewer considers the paper premature due to contradictory statements and insufficient experimental depth.
