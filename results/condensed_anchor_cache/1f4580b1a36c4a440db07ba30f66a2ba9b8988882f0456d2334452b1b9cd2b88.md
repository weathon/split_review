## Merged Review

### Summary
SafeDiffuser embeds Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) into the denoising diffusion process to enforce safety constraints during trajectory generation. It proposes three variants (ROS, RES, TVS) and evaluates them on maze path planning, legged locomotion, and 3D manipulation. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty and importance of adding safety guarantees to diffusion planning, but raise substantive concerns: the method is computationally expensive, relies on strict assumptions that clash with the stochastic/time-varying nature of diffusion, has many hyperparameters, and lacks comparisons to strong safe-RL and planning baselines. One reviewer (score 5) finds the approach overly restrictive and difficult to scale, while others (scores 8,8,6) view it as effective but with significant limitations.

### Strengths
- Safety guarantees are important for the safe planning community, and this may be the first safety method for diffusion-based planning (R1, R3).
- The formulation of CBFs within diffuser models is novel, and modifying the denoising steps appears more effective than classifier-free guidance for satisfying constraints (R2, R3).
- The systematic approach for selecting the appropriate variant (ROS, RES, TVS) based on safety constraint properties is provided (R2).
- Results show improvements in safety-related metrics while maintaining comparable task performance; the method can be trained on unsafe data yet reliably generate safe trajectories during testing (R2, R4).
- The extension to handle “local traps” (e.g., local minima) is effective (R3).
- Simulation results demonstrate effectiveness and robustness across diverse domains (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Computation and speed:** Solving Quadratic Programs (QPs) at each diffusion step makes SafeDiffuser significantly slower (by an order of magnitude) than vanilla diffusion (R2, R3).
- **Theoretical assumptions and applicability of CBFs:** CBFs are designed for time-invariant, deterministic dynamics, but the diffusion process is time-varying (alpha bar changes) and inherently stochastic (due to added noise). The paper does not adequately address this mismatch, and the extension to diffusion neural networks requires Lipschitz assumptions that are not verified (R4, R3). This calls into question the suitability of CBFs for this problem (R4, also questioned in R1).
- **Overly strict invariance condition and complex design:** The method enforces safety at every intermediate denoising step, whereas only the final (t=0) trajectory matters. This is unnecessarily restrictive (R4). The design introduces many tunable hyperparameters ($\epsilon$, $w$, $r$, $\gamma$, $\sigma$), making tuning difficult (R4). Furthermore, the choice of three methods (ROS, RES, TVS) instead of a single general solution adds complexity, and the user must pick based on the unsafe set’s convexity—hard to determine in high-dimensional tasks (R1, R4).
- **High trap rates and unclear metrics:** On some benchmarks, the method gets stuck in “local traps” 39%–100% of the time. The distinction between “trap rate 1” and “trap rate 2” is not explained (R1). This raises practical concerns about reliability.
- **Missing comparisons and benchmarks:** Important baselines are not included:  
  – Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT) for offline safe RL (R2).  
  – Potential-based diffusion motion planning (Luo et al., 2024) (R4).  
  – More standard safe RL methods using Lagrangian objectives (R1).  
  Additionally, the paper uses D4RL rather than the dedicated safe offline RL benchmark (Liu et al., 2023) without justification (R2).
- **Limited experimental scope:** Only Maze2D-large is used for maze tasks; only the Medium-Expert variant is shown for locomotion, and Cheetah is omitted (R2). Scaling to highly nonlinear dynamics (e.g., drone/quadcopter) is not demonstrated, and the method’s ability to generalize to new, unseen safety constraints is untested (R2, R4).
- **Requires predefined, differentiable constraints:** Safety specifications must be differentiable, and the unsafe set must be known to choose the correct variant—this limits applicability to settings where constraints are not explicitly given or differentiable (R3, R4).
- **Missing analysis of estimation error:** The diffusion dynamics are estimated online; the paper does not analyze how errors in this estimation affect safety guarantees or practical performance (R3).
- **Motivation and justification gaps:** Several conceptual decisions lack clear rationale:  
  – Why apply CBFs during denoising instead of trajectory planning steps? (R2)  
  – Why not use Control Lyapunov Functions or reachability-based methods when only the final trajectory’s safety matters? (R4)  
  – Could the method be improved by continually retraining on safe data? (R3)  
- **Presentation weaknesses:** The writing is sometimes difficult to read, contains unusual terminology (e.g., “local traps”), and has typos (e.g., line 470 “physical simulator”, line 474 grammar errors) and excessive underlining (R1).
