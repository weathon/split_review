## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Open-CK, a dataset for combustion kinetics created using Fire Dynamics Simulator (FDS) simulations of industrial fire parks. The dataset covers 300 different fire scenarios with multiple physical fields (temperature, pressure, etc.) and includes both single-ignition-source (SIS) and three-ignition-source (TIS) cases. The authors evaluate a range of machine learning architectures—vision backbones (U-Net, ViT, ResNet), spatio-temporal predictive models (ConvLSTM), and operator learning frameworks (FNO)—to establish benchmarks for three downstream tasks: capturing continuous combustion kinetics changes, neural PDE solving for temperature and turbulence, and reconstruction from sparse observations. Reviewers note the dataset fills an important gap and involves substantial computational effort (64 NVIDIA A100 GPUs). However, concerns are raised about lack of verification/validation of the FDS simulations, limited description of dataset size and scenario selection, reliance on a single geometric layout, strong language in claims, and missing discussions on data maintainability and limitations. Reviewer 1 (score 5) finds the paper not yet suitable for ICLR, while Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 (scores 8, 6, 6) recognize the contribution but request clarifications and additional analyses.

### Strengths
- **Novel and necessary dataset**: Addresses a critical lack of open, high-resolution data for fire dynamics and combustion kinetics, particularly for industrial settings, in contrast to existing satellite-based wildfire datasets (Reviewer 4).
- **Substantial computational effort**: 64 NVIDIA A100 GPUs used to generate 300 scenarios with multiple physical fields (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive benchmarking across diverse ML architectures—vision backbones (U-Net, ViT, ResNet), spatio-temporal models (ConvLSTM), and operator learning (FNO)—provides a strong baseline for future SciML research (Reviewer 4).
- **Interesting application of multi-physics coupling**: Simulation over complex boundaries/plant layouts with consideration of multiple fields (temperature, pressure) enables exploration of physical coupling phenomena (Reviewer 3).
- **Use of LPIPS and natural image metrics**: Adoption of LPIPS and similar metrics for evaluating physical field predictions is intriguing (Reviewer 3).
- **Open practices and documentation**: Open repository, good documentation, and discussion of future directions and limitations (Reviewer 2).
- **Motivation and related work**: Good coverage of prior work and motivation for the dataset (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of verification and validation**: The paper does not provide any verification or validation of the underlying FDS methodology, mesh resolution, or simulation technique for the industrial park domain (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient description of dataset size and structure**: It is not clear how many samples constitute the dataset (e.g., are there 300 time series of varying lengths?), and how the “300 fire scenarios” relate to the parameters in Table 1. The project website was inaccessible to some reviewers, preventing further inspection (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 1).
- **Overly strong language in claims**: Claims such as being the “first combustion CFD benchmark” are overstated; existing efforts like Sandia’s Engine Combustion Network and BLASTNet (Chung et al., NeurIPS 2023) provide open combustion benchmark data. The statement “Open-CK involves several PDEs” is also questionable since the listed equations are conservation laws. These should be moderated (Reviewer 2).
- **Only one geometric layout considered**: The dataset uses a single industrial park layout; without diverse geometries, the richness needed for generalizable SciML research may be limited (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited discussion of data maintenance and access**: No details on how the dataset will be maintained, stored, or accessed long-term (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of domain background and scenario rationale**: The narrative does not adequately describe the problem of combustion kinetics, the representativeness of the chosen scenarios for real-world fires, and the reasoning behind selecting each scenario in Table 1 (why each is important, representative, and challenging) (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient motivation for multi-physics coupling challenge**: The paper does not clearly explain why modeling the coupling between multiple physical fields is particularly challenging and crucial in this context (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis of model efficiency vs. performance**: It would be insightful to include scatter plots of MSE vs. FLOPs, MSE vs. parameters, SSIM vs. (FLOPs, parameters) for the architectures in Table 2 to understand scaling behavior and identify useful architectures (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited discussion of limitations**: There is limited discussion of the work's limitations, both in dataset design and model evaluation (Reviewer 1).
- **Odd phrasing**: The repeated use of “supercomputers” in italics and other phrases sound odd and should be revised (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions about real-world applicability and adaptation**: It is unclear how models trained on Open-CK would generalize to real-world fire dynamics, what deficiencies they might have, and how they compare to existing reduced-order models (e.g., theoretical fire dynamics models) in terms of physical consistency and accuracy (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 also asks about enriching the dataset with diverse geometric layouts.
- **Representativeness of scenarios**: How were the 300 scenarios conceived and selected? How representative are they of real-world fire development dynamics? (Reviewer 4).
