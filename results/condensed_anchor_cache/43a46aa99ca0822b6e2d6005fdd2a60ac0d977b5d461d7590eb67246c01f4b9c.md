## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Energy-based Conceptual Diffusion Models (ECDMs), integrating diffusion models and Concept Bottleneck Models within an energy-based framework to enable concept-based generation, interpretation, debugging, intervention, and imputation. It uses energy networks and pretrained diffusion models to define joint energy over instructions, concepts, and images, providing conditional probabilities for various tasks. Experiments on CUB, AWA2, and CelebA-HQ show improvements in generation quality and concept accuracy.

### Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to read, with a clear organization.
2. Novel integration of concept bottleneck models with diffusion models through an energy-based framework, adding probabilistic interpretability to generative processes.
3. Comprehensive theoretical framework with detailed proofs.
4. Supports multiple practical applications: generation, interpretation, debugging, intervention, and imputation in a unified manner.
5. Strong empirical results across three datasets (CUB, AWA2, CelebA-HQ) with clear quantitative improvements over baseline methods in both generation quality (FID) and concept accuracy.
6. The model allows users to modify generated images based on specific concept-level controls, offering interpretable editing capabilities.
7. The framework is versatile and applicable to any conditional data generation task.

### Weaknesses
1. The formulation maps a text prompt to a deterministic concept probability vector, independent of the generated image. This lacks image-dependent variation (e.g., "polar bear" size should vary across different generated images). With binary concept labels, the concept energy model's optimal output given y would be binary; it is not explained why a learned mapping is needed over a simple logic mapping (e.g., "polar bear" -> paws=1, big=1).
2. The paper fails to acknowledge pioneering energy-based diffusion works ("Diffusion Recovery Likelihood", "Cooperative Diffusion Recovery Likelihood") and related EBM composition works ("a theory of generative convnet", "Implicit Generation and Generalization in Energy-Based Models").
3. Lacks comparisons with related interpretable frameworks like COMET or CBGM that also use energy-based models for concept-based diffusion.
4. Code for reproducibility is not provided.
5. The experiments rely on a single fixed pretrained stable diffusion model; other diffusion backbones are not explored.
6. The method suffers from limited precise regional control over concept-based edits, and the energy-based approach is computationally intensive, especially during joint optimization steps (as acknowledged by authors).
7. Evaluation metrics lack diversity scores or user-study-based interpretability measures; only FID, class accuracy, and concept accuracy are reported.
8. The significance is unclear—the method appears to be a straightforward combination of concept bottleneck models and standard conditional diffusion models.
9. In the concept-based generation procedure (Eqs. 11-13), equation (11) maximizes the mapping energy E_map rather than the full joint energy E_joint = E_map + E_concept, despite both terms depending on concept c. The rationale for this choice is not explained. Additionally, maximizing E_map with respect to binary c is an integer programming problem whose efficiency is not addressed.
10. Several implementation details are missing or unclear: how the number of concepts K is determined (is it fixed?), how concept embeddings v_k are modeled, and how the generation process (Eqs. 12-13) differs from a standard conditional diffusion model (which it seems to only replace conditioning input y with c obtained from y).
11. Missing analysis on scaling with number of concepts, computational overhead compared to standard diffusion models, potential extension to continuous concepts, and robustness to out-of-distribution samples.
12. Some terminology ("pivotal inversion", "energy matching inference") could be better explained for clarity.
