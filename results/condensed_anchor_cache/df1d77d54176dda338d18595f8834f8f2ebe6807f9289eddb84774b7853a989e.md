## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UMKL-G, an unsupervised multiple kernel learning method for graphs that produces a weighted sum of kernels by preserving ordinal relationships (similarity order) among graphs via a probability simplex. The authors provide theoretical guarantees on stability, robustness, and generalization. Experiments on graph clustering tasks (eight molecular datasets) are reported. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: one reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) finds the work novel and theoretically high-quality, while another (score 3, confidence 2) finds the core definition unclear and the paper difficult to follow. Others (scores 5 and 6) see value but note limitations in novelty, evaluation, and clarity.

### Strengths
- Multiple kernel learning in an unsupervised setting is a relevant and nontrivial problem (R1).
- The approach offers a novel solution to an understudied problem (R2).
- The theoretical analysis is comprehensive, covering stability, Lipschitz continuity, robustness to kernel perturbations, and generalization, with detailed proofs (R2, R4).
- The method is applicable to arbitrary kernels, not just graph kernels (R2).
- The concept of preserving ordinal relationships among graphs in an unsupervised setting addresses a unique aspect of unsupervised kernel learning (R3).
- Theoretical proofs for stability, robustness, and generalization strengthen the foundation (R3).
- Empirical validation across eight datasets provides reasonable breadth (R3).
- UMKL-G outperforms existing methods on graph clustering benchmarks, demonstrating robust performance on real and synthetic datasets (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Core definition unclear (R1, high severity)**: Definition 1 is imprecise and appears self-referential, referring only to learned kernel values \(k\) without specifying an external reference ordinal relationship. It is unclear whether the initial kernel values \(k_{ij}(w_0)\) are fixed or change during learning, and what conditions (triplet \((i,j,r)\)) define preservation. This makes the core mechanism difficult to understand; R1 could not follow the paper and recommends rejection.
- **Intuitions insufficiently justified (R2)**: Why does the probability simplex P lead to a more "accurate" representation of the data's inherent geometry? Is "accurate" meant as better reflection of the manifold or simply improved downstream performance? This claim needs clarification.
- **Evaluation limited (R2, R3, R4)**: 
  - Only clustering task tested; no supervised or other tasks despite claims of broader applicability (R2). 
  - Theorems from Section 4.6 (robustness to perturbations, generalization) are not empirically validated (R2). 
  - Comparisons with modern GNN-based clustering methods are limited (R3, R4). Existing techniques like sparse-UMKL and GCN-based methods already achieve comparable results in unsupervised scenarios (R3).
  - Scalability not addressed; testing on large datasets is missing (R4).
- **Baseline details lacking (R2)**: The statement about "learn[ing] graph representations and kernel weights simultaneously" via GCN needs elaboration: architecture, optimization procedure, joint learning implementation. Also, the simple baseline of equal weights (1/M) is not mentioned (R2 question).
- **Table inconsistency (R2)**: Some numbers in Table 1 do not match the supplementary (e.g., ACC for BZR and MUTAG). Definitions of clustering metrics should be in the appendix (R2).
- **Incremental novelty (R3, R4)**: The methodology heavily relies on established probability simplex and MKL techniques; the main contribution (ordinal preservation) seems an incremental adaptation rather than a breakthrough (R3). It is unclear how this differs significantly from existing MKL approaches beyond the focus on ordinal relations (R4).
- **No evidence for UMKL-X (R3)**: The paper proposes extension to broader data types but provides no experimental or conceptual support beyond graph tasks, reducing confidence in generalizability.
- **Stylistic issues (R4)**: Large parts of the introduction and Section 2 are redundant; writing is dense and technical, making the text difficult to follow (R4). R1 also rates presentation as 1.
- **Hyperparameter and sensitivity questions (R2, R3)**: 
  - Why does any parameter \(o>1\) give exactly the same performance for the selected clustering metrics? Can this be generalized? (R2)
  - How sensitive is the method to initial weight settings and the power hyperparameter \(o\) in the kernel concentration step? (R3)
  - How does UMKL-G handle datasets with minimal ordinal relationships or uniform graph similarities? (R3)
- **Ground truth for ACC (R2)** – What ground truth was used for the clustering accuracy metric?
- **No supervised test (R2)** – Although Section 4.5 claims the learned composite kernel can be used in supervised tasks, no graph classification experiments are provided.
- **Choice of GCN for baseline (R2)** – Elaboration needed on representing graphs with GCN for the baseline methods.
