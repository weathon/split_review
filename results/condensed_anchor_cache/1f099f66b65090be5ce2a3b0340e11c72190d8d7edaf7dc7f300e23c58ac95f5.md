## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces DyAug, a graph data augmentation framework for dynamic graphs that combines causal subgraph rationalization (separating rationale from environment) with three types of environment replacement (spatial, temporal, spatial-temporal) to improve performance, generalization, and robustness while maintaining temporal consistency. Reviewers acknowledge the relevance of dynamic graph augmentation and the strong empirical results (0.89%–3.13% performance gains, 6.2%–12.2% robustness improvements). However, there are notable concerns about overclaimed novelty relative to prior dynamic OOD works, insufficient evaluation on continuous-time dynamic graphs and recent baselines, scalability/complexity inaccuracies, heuristic augmentation choices, limited adversarial ablation, and missing ablations for robustness scenarios. **One reviewer (R3) is more critical, questioning the novelty and the distinction from existing OOD methods, rating soundness and contribution lower (2 each).**

### Strengths
- **Focus on dynamic graph augmentation** – The paper extends graph data augmentation to the less explored dynamic setting, where temporal consistency is critical for real-world applications (R1, R2, R4).
- **Well-motivated temporal consistency approach** – DyAug explicitly ensures that augmentations do not disrupt the natural evolution of dynamic graphs, an issue not previously addressed by static GDA methods (R1, R2, R3).
- **Novel combination of rationalization and multi‑dimensional augmentation** – The paradigm of separating causal subgraphs (rationale) from non-causal environment and applying spatial, temporal, and spatial-temporal replacements in latent space is innovative (R2, R3).
- **Strong empirical results** – Extensive experiments on six benchmarks with three backbones show consistent performance improvements (0.89%–3.13%) and robustness under adversarial attacks (6.2%–12.2% ↑) and temporal distribution shifts (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Thorough comparison with existing GDA methods** – The paper demonstrates significant improvements over multiple static GDA baselines (R1).
- **Clear presentation** – Figures and structure effectively convey the key concepts (R3).
- **Potential impact on robust dynamic GNNs** – The method contributes to improving generalization and robustness in dynamic graphs (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Overstated novelty regarding dynamic graph augmentation** – Several prior works on out-of-distribution handling in dynamic graphs (e.g., [1,2,3] in R3) already incorporate augmentation strategies tailored for dynamic graphs. The paper should thoroughly discuss and differentiate DyAug from these works, and its claim of being the first GDA method for dynamic graphs is not fully justified (R3).
- **Methodological similarity to existing OOD works** – The approach of constructing a causal mask to separate rationale/environment and then perturbing spurious factors is conceptually similar to techniques used in dynamic graph OOD methods. The paper lacks a clear articulation of how DyAug’s causal discovery and perturbation differ (R3).
- **Insufficient explanation of temporal consistency guarantee** – It is unclear which specific modules or mechanisms within DyAug ensure that augmentations do not disrupt temporal consistency. The evidence in Section 4.2 (Observation 1) is considered insufficient; a more detailed justification is needed (R3).
- **Incomplete baseline comparisons** – 
  - The evaluation omits recent dynamic GNN models such as TGAT, ROLAND, and DyGFormer, which limits generalizability (R1, R4).
  - Notable graph rationalization methods (DIR, GREA) were excluded due to compatibility issues without thorough justification (R2).
- **Limited evaluation on continuous-time dynamic graphs (CTDGs)** – DyAug is only tested on discrete-time graphs; its applicability to CTDGs remains unexamined, and adaptation may be challenging (R1, R2).
- **Scalability and complexity concerns** – 
  - The method introduces additional complexity from causal mask estimation, contrastive loss, and consistency loss. A detailed analysis of computational overhead per component and memory footprint is missing, raising doubts about application to large graphs (R2).
  - The complexity analysis in Section 3.6 is potentially inaccurate: the similarity calculation in the consistency loss likely scales as O(|E|) rather than constant, making the overall complexity O(ϖ T |E|) (where ϖ is window size, T is number of timesteps) (R4).
- **Heuristic augmentation selection strategies** – The spatial, temporal, and spatial-temporal replacements rely on heuristic choices without a clear rationale, which may not generalize across diverse dynamic graph structures and temporal dynamics (R2).
- **Limited adversarial robustness evaluation** – 
  - The adversarial attacks used are not state-of-the-art (R1). Testing against more advanced attacks (e.g., temporal dynamics-aware attacks [4] from R1) is suggested (R1, R2).
  - Ablation experiments are missing for OOD generalization and for attack scenarios other than structure attacks (e.g., feature attacks, Nettack). This prevents assessing the contribution of each component (temporal-conditioned rationalization, augmentation, consistency loss, contrastive loss) under these scenarios (R4).
- **Insufficient ablation and hyperparameter analysis** – The ablation study is not exhaustive; sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., window size w in consistency loss, rationale generation methods) is not explored, and the potential for the rationale-environment separation to introduce new spurious correlations is not addressed (R2).
- **Implementation ambiguity** – In Section 3.4, the transformation \(\overline{M}=1_N-M\) (with \(1_N\) an all‑one matrix) would yield a dense matrix, which is computationally prohibitive. It is likely the intended expression is \(\overline{M}=A-M\) where \(A\) is the adjacency matrix; this should be clarified (R4).
- **Source code repository is expired** – The code is not currently available, hindering reproducibility (R4).
