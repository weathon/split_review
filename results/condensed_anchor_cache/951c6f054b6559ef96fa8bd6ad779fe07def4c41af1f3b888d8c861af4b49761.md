## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces calibration attacks—a new class of adversarial attacks that miscalibrate a model’s confidence scores without altering its accuracy. Four attack goals are defined: underconfidence, overconfidence, maximum miscalibration, and random confidence attacks. The attacks are implemented by designing new loss functions and using existing white-box (PGD) and black-box (Square attack) algorithms. Comprehensive experiments on image classifiers (ResNet, ViT) across datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet) demonstrate effectiveness even with few queries, and the paper explores defenses including two new methods. Reviewers note the novelty of the problem, extensive empirical evaluation, and clear writing, but also raise substantial concerns about technical originality relative to prior attacks, insufficient significance justification, missing baselines, and presentation clarity. One reviewer (score 1) is strongly negative on originality; three reviewers (scores 5) find the problem novel but agree that technical contributions are limited.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem**: The paper proposes a new attack objective—miscalibrating confidence without changing accuracy—which is distinctive from typical misclassification attacks and addresses an overlooked vulnerability (reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive evaluation covering white-box and black-box attacks, multiple models/datasets, and defenses. The coverage of attack variants, design choices, and defensive methods is thorough (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- **Clear writing**: Paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and provides a good survey of related work (reviewers 1, 2).  
- **Non-conventional threat model**: The attack can cause harm with smaller perturbations than misclassification attacks and may be less detectable, especially for random confidence attacks (reviewer 3).  
- **Exploration of defenses**: The paper studies existing calibration methods and adversarial defenses against these attacks, and proposes two tailored defenses, which adds to the contribution (reviewers 1, 2, 3; though one reviewer notes that defenses may be attack-specific).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The core mechanism (manipulating logits to change confidence) is straightforward and largely builds on existing attack algorithms (e.g., Square attack, PGD). There is little analysis of how different logit manipulations affect specific calibration metrics, and the differences from prior work (notably Galil & El-Yaniv 2021, which attacks confidence scores directly) are minor—only adding overconfidence, using different attack algorithms, and considering margin, but the essential idea is similar (reviewers 1, 2, 3). One reviewer (score 1) states that the failure to cite Galil & El-Yaniv (2021), Zeng et al. (2023), and certification works (Kumar et al. 2020, Emde et al. 2023) undermines originality.
- **Insufficient significance justification**: The paper motivates calibration attacks with only an autonomous driving example and reports only calibration errors in experiments. It does not demonstrate a concrete downstream impact (e.g., how miscalibrated confidence affects real-world decisions), making it hard to assess the practical significance of this threat (reviewers 1, 2).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Important robust-calibration baselines are omitted: Gaussian randomized smoothing for certified confidence scores (Kumar et al. 2020), Neural Clamping (Tang et al. 2022), and Multi-domain Temperature Scaling (Yu et al. 2022) should be compared (reviewers 3, 4). Additionally, the defense methods are tested only against the proposed attacks; it is unclear if they improve general calibration (pre-attack results are not superior to standard calibration methods) (reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:  
  - Algorithm 1 and the defense descriptions are dense and hard to parse; pseudocode or diagrams would help (reviewer 1).  
  - Section 5 confusingly mixes calibration methods (TS, Splines, DCA, SAM) with defenses (AAA, AT, CA AT, CS), risking misinterpretation (reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 1: caption states red/blue bars represent accuracy/confidence but the vertical axis is labeled “accuracy”; PNG format should be vector (reviewer 4).  
  - The calculation of average confidence in Table 1 is unclear (e.g., why accuracy is not ECE ± average confidence); authors should clarify (reviewer 4).  
  - Code for reproducibility (especially Table 1) is not provided (reviewer 4).
- **Threat model concerns**: The maximum miscalibration attack requires knowing ground-truth labels for all attacked inputs. The paper does not discuss whether this assumption is realistic—if ground truth is cheaply available, the classification problem may be easy (reviewer 3). Related questions: can the attack work with only predicted class labels (query efficiency)? Can it transfer to other classifiers or be mounted as a training-time attack? (reviewers 2, 3).  
- **Missing experiments and ablations**:  
  - No analysis of how different logit-manipulation strategies affect calibration error metrics (reviewer 1).  
  - No downstream task evaluation (reviewer 2).  
  - Underconfidence attack only minimizes margin between top-two classes; entropy maximization across all classes could be a stronger variant (reviewer 3).  
  - Defenses are only tested under maximum miscalibration; results for random confidence attack are missing (reviewer 3).
