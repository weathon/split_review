## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers assessed the paper with scores 6, 3, 5, and 3, indicating significant disagreement. Reviewers 1 (score 6, confidence 3) and 3 (score 5, confidence 4) were more positive, finding the hierarchical clustering approach and taxonomy-guided generation interesting and effective. Reviewers 2 (score 3, confidence 4) and 4 (score 3, confidence 5) were more critical, raising major concerns about evaluation methodology, experimental fairness, missing baselines, domain generality, hallucination risks, reproducibility, and presentation. All reviewers acknowledged the importance of the task and some novelty, but the lower-scoring reviewers view the current validation as insufficient.

### Strengths
- The framework addresses a real need given the rapidly increasing volume of academic papers and the burden on researchers to produce accurate, complete literature reviews.
- The graph-based hierarchical clustering method (HiReview) that jointly considers textual content and citation network structure is a relatively novel approach, differentiating this work from existing review generation methods.
- The taxonomy-then-generation strategy naturally mimics how humans write literature reviews by first organizing concepts hierarchically, which is an effective design for generating long, structured reviews.
- The joint training of a graph neural network for clustering and a pretrained language model for topic generation ensures consistency between clusters and topics, a technically novel contribution.
- The evaluation framework assesses sub-components of the model, providing insight into the contribution of each part to overall performance.
- The paper is clearly written and the methodology is presented in a structured, easy-to-follow manner (noted by reviewers 1 and 3; though reviewer 4 noted typos and formatting issues – see weaknesses).
- Experimental results show substantial improvements over baseline methods (LLM prompting, RAG, AutoSurvey) on the constructed dataset, demonstrating the framework’s potential.

### Weaknesses
**Evaluation methodology and fairness**
- Experiments rely exclusively on LLM-based scoring (e.g., scores out of 100). The reliability of such automated evaluation for long outputs is questionable – reviewers asked whether an 80‑point passage is always better than a 75‑point passage (Reviewer 1). More rigorous evaluation (e.g., human judgments or side-by-side comparisons) is missing.
- The experimental setting is not strictly fair: AutoSurvey retrieves papers from a database, while HiReview uses the 2‑hop neighbor citation network of the human‑written survey paper. The former is a more realistic setting for literature review writing (Reviewer 1). The paper does not justify why this scenario is appropriate or what happens when no 2‑hop network is available.
- There is no comparison of the clustering method with that of GraphRAG (Reviewer 4).
- The paper does not compare the generated topic taxonomy against existing taxonomies (e.g., MAG labels) or against established topic taxonomy construction baselines (Reviewers 3 and 4). Justification for constructing a custom taxonomy rather than using an existing one is lacking.
- The quality of the generated taxonomy is not evaluated properly: the example in the Appendix shows that “continual TC” is placed under “applications of continual learning” instead of under “continual fine‑tuning” (Reviewer 2). This misclassification indicates that automatic metrics like BERTScore or LLM Score may hide serious errors. The paper also does not evaluate hallucination risks or incorrect information in the generated reviews (Reviewer 2).
- The statement “Distillation-based methods focus on transferring knowledge from previous models to new ones” is not completely correct; distillation is about teacher‑student knowledge transfer (Reviewer 2).

**Missing ablations, baselines, and analysis**
- The paper does not compare inference costs against baseline methods. Given the complexity of joint training and large‑scale processing, such a comparison is needed (Reviewer 3).
- A highly relevant prior work (“Hierarchical Catalogue Generation for Literature Review”, EMNLP 2023 Findings) is not cited or compared (Reviewer 4).
- The paper lacks analysis of how the method performs across different research domains. Domain characteristics (e.g., citation patterns, review structure) may affect effectiveness, and this is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- There is insufficient detail on the granularity and structure of the input topic query Q, making it hard to assess adaptability and scalability (Reviewer 3). Examples of input queries are not provided, and the code/dataset repository link was inaccessible for two reviewers (3 and 4).

**Robustness and reproducibility**
- The pipeline is highly reliant on LLMs for both content generation and evaluation, leading to potential high variance depending on the model used in each stage (Reviewer 2). The added joint training and multiple components increase complexity, posing reproducibility challenges (Reviewers 2 and 4).
- Robustness to noise in the input query is not tested (Reviewer 3).
- The paper does not discuss how the method would be applied if retrieved papers are automatically obtained rather than extracted from an existing citation network (Reviewer 1).

**Presentation and technical errors**
- Several typos: page 8 “opf” should be “of”; Figure 1 “Retreive” should be “Retrieve”; Table 3 “HiCluserting” should be “HiClustering” (Reviewers 1 and 2).
- Some claims in the introduction lack supporting citations (e.g., lines 031‑041 on manual reviews being labor‑intensive; lines 045‑047 on LLMs for this task) (Reviewer 4).
- The abstract statement “research on incorporating LLMs for automatic document generation remains limited” is not accurate (Reviewer 4).
- The relevance of discussing hallucination (lines 047‑050) is unclear since the paper does not address it (Reviewer 4). Similarly, the inclusion of PEFT in the related work section seems unrelated; the focus should be on automatic literature review generation (Reviewer 4).
- Abbreviations are not defined before use (e.g., PLM in line 128) (Reviewer 4).
- Equations (1) and (2) are insufficiently explained: what are \(p_\theta\) and \(r\)? Does \(R\) increase with the number of neighboring nodes? (Reviewer 4).
- What is \(T_u\) in line 121? (Reviewer 4)
- Line 232: what does \(G_l'\) belongs \(G_l\) mean? (Reviewer 4)
- The paper does not provide examples of the generated surveys compared to standard human‑written surveys, nor examples of the generated hierarchical structure (Reviewers 1 and 4).
- RQ3 has no explicit answer; research questions appear more like engineering questions than research ones (Reviewer 2).
- Table 2 reports only LLMScores – why? (Reviewer 2)
- Table 3: accuracy is evaluated on what? (Reviewer 2)
- The text encoder used to convert titles is not specified (Reviewer 2).
- Use consistent tenses (line 052 vs. 055) and list citations in chronological order (Reviewer 4).
- Use vector graphics (PDF) instead of raster images (PNG) and do not arbitrarily modify the paper template font sizes (Reviewer 4).
