## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces big.LITTLE Vision Transformer (bLViT), a dual-transformer architecture with high-capacity “big” (P) blocks for important tokens and lightweight “little” (E) blocks for less critical tokens, routing tokens dynamically to reduce computation while maintaining performance. Experiments on image classification and segment anything (SAM) are conducted. Reviewers note the motivation is clear and the design is intuitive, but raise significant concerns about unfair comparisons due to reliance on knowledge distillation, lack of actual speed benchmarks, missing related works, and insufficient analysis.

### Strengths
- The dual P-E block design is interesting and leverages varying token importance (R1). The integration of performance and efficiency blocks is straightforward and effective (R3). The P-Block and E-Block are reasonable, leading to less computational cost without compromising accuracy (R4).
- The Semi-Cross Attention mechanism allows important tokens to gather information from all tokens (R1).
- The motivation addresses the sub-optimal performance and computational complexity caused by omitting unrepresentative tokens; the methodology is intuitive and accessible (R3).
- Improving ViT efficiency is an important topic, and this work proposes a new architecture (R2).
- The ablation study on model design is comprehensive and discusses contributions of each component (R2).
- The paper is well organized and written with clear logic that facilitates understanding (R3, R4).
- Experiments on image classification and SAM verify the effectiveness of the proposed method (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparison due to distillation:** The method relies on distillation from a pretrained vanilla model that is not described (R1, R2). Without distillation, the model achieves only 80.8 (Table 3), which is worse than EViT-B@0.6 in Table 1, making comparisons in Table 1 unfair (R2). The significance of distillation in SAM results (Table 2) likewise undermines the meaningfulness of those comparisons, and it is unclear why distillation helps so substantially in SAM (R2).
- **Missing latency/FPS benchmarks:** The paper only reports theoretical FLOPs; it is unclear how this translates to actual latency or FPS (R1, R3, R4). Actual speed-up on GPU/CPU should be compared to state-of-the-art methods (R1, R3, R4).
- **Missing comparisons to relevant work:** Several related efficient architectures are not compared: FastViT (R1), EfficientViT (ICCV 2023) (R2), TinySAM and EfficientViT-SAM (R4). Parameter counts are missing from Table 1 (R2).
- **Inconsistency in algorithm/figures:** The description in Algorithm 1 includes a fuse operation after attention and before FFN, but this is not shown in Figure 2 (R2).
- **Hyperparameter and design choices need clarification:**
  - The role of the extremely small gamma (0.0001) is unclear (R2).
  - The introduction of new hyperparameters may incur substantial tuning cost, potentially undermining the intended reduction in computational complexity (R3).
  - The rationale for P-block parameterization at 10N vs E-block at N is not explained (R3). The design principle for the P-E blocks and whether the 1:1 stack ratio is optimal should be discussed (R1).
  - The specific structural designs employed in the performance and efficiency blocks are not described (R3).
- **Lack of in-depth analysis:**
  - No analysis of which tokens are utilized and how to optimize the use of important tokens (R3). Given that Figure 3 shows minimal variation in identified important tokens across layers, it is necessary to justify per-layer processing (R3).
  - The impact of token fusion on overall performance is not quantified (R1).
  - How the dual transformer modules affect the self-attention mechanism is not discussed (R1).
- **Low-level task applicability:** The effectiveness for low-level tasks like super-resolution and denoising is not addressed (R3).
- **Figure readability:** Characters in figures (e.g., Figure 1) are too small (R4).
