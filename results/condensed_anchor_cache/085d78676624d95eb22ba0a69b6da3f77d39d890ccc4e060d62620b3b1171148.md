## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces CLIPMem, a hold-one-out metric to quantify memorization in CLIP models, filling a gap in multimodal memorization research. Using CC3M and COCO, the authors find that CLIP’s memorization lies between supervised and self-supervised paradigms, with mis-captioned samples showing the highest levels. The text encoder contributes more to memorization than the image encoder, suggesting mitigation should focus on the text domain. They propose strategies (e.g., caption augmentation, removal of memorized examples) that reduce memorization while preserving or improving utility, defying the typical trade-off. Reviewers largely agree the metric is novel and the analysis is well-organized, but some find the empirical findings unsurprising and note several methodological limitations. One reviewer (score 6) was more positive; two (score 5) found the contributions modest.

### Strengths

- Introduces CLIPMem, a new metric for quantifying memorization in multimodal settings, adapted from Feldman et al.’s supervised learning leave-one-out approach (R1, R2, R3).
- Provides a clean separation of training and test splits and uses reasonable datasets (CC3M) (R1).
- Empirical analysis reveals that memorization is more significant in the text encoder than the image encoder, a novel and actionable insight (R1, R2, R3).
- Proposes mitigation strategies (caption augmentation, removal of memorized samples) that reduce memorization while preserving or even enhancing model utility, challenging the traditional memorization–utility trade-off (R1, R2).
- Highlights the risks of training with uncurated, mis-captioned data, offering practical guidelines for real-world multimodal training (R2).
- Well-organized and easy to follow (R3).
- Literature on memorization is well highlighted (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Applicability and generalizability**: CLIPMem requires retraining on specific splits and is not applicable to off-the-shelf CLIP models (R1). The metric and findings may not transfer to other multimodal architectures with different fusion mechanisms or generative objectives; sensitivity to architectural variations is not explored (R2). Experiments are limited to COCO and CC3M; it is unclear how findings generalize to other large-scale or domain-specific datasets where caption characteristics differ (R2).
- **Clarity of CLIPMem definition**: The paper does not clearly explain how the leave-one-out strategy is applied in the multimodal setting—whether per modality separately or jointly—and needs more details on the exact computation for vision-only vs. joint vision+text (R1).
- **Low linear probe accuracy**: Accuracy in Tables 1, 5-a, 5-b, 6-a/b is quite low, raising concerns about the quality of learned representations and whether the memorization results are meaningful for poorly performing models (R1).
- **Unconvincing noising results**: Table 5-b results are not convincing—almost all values are within the same ±std range, trends are unclear, and error bars are large (R1).
- **Lack of baseline for noisy sample removal**: The metric’s effectiveness for removing noisy samples is only compared with random replacement; additional baselines (e.g., naive CLIP similarity) are needed (R3).
- **Diffusion-based image augmentation**: Table 1 uses diffusion model augmentation for images, which may cause distribution shift and does not provide clear intuition about image augmentation (R3).
- **Model size not discussed**: The paper does not examine how model size affects memorization; using a larger image encoder might shift more memorization to the image side, potentially changing the conclusion that text encoder memorizes more (R3).
- **Findings are not surprising**: That augmenting text improves CLIP has already been observed, and that augmenting datasets mitigates memorization is expected. These points reduce the impact of the work (R3).
- **Computational cost of mitigation strategies**: The proposed caption augmentations and variation generation likely add computational overhead, but the paper lacks a detailed analysis of memory usage and training time increases (R2).
- **Figure readability issues**: Figure 1 text and images are very hard to read (suggest larger and fewer examples); Figure 3 should be made larger with shared y-axis and increased font sizes (R1).
- **Missing experiments / reviewer questions**: 
    - Is CLIPMem bounded? (R1)
    - What dimension is kept constant in Table 1? Are the total training samples seen the same? (R1)
    - It would be interesting to evaluate infinite data regimes (no repeated data) rather than classical K-epoch runs, as in language modeling. (R1)
