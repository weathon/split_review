## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of why FixMatch (and similar SSL algorithms) generalizes better than supervised learning (SL) on deep neural networks, specifically CNNs. It characterizes FixMatch’s learning as a two-stage process: first learning like SL, then learning missing features through unsupervised learning from augmented data under a “multi-view” data assumption. Based on these insights, the authors propose Semantic-Aware FixMatch (SA-FixMatch), which uses Grad-CAM to mask out highly semantic regions during strong augmentation. The theory is extended to FlexMatch, FreeMatch, Dash, SoftMatch. Experimental results on common SSL benchmarks support the analysis and show improved performance from SA-FixMatch.

### Strengths
- Provides a novel theoretical analysis of FixMatch on multi-view structured data distributions, demonstrating its advantage over SL and explaining how it learns all discriminative features while SL captures only a random subset (lottery ticket hypothesis). The two-stage learning characterization is insightful.
- The analysis is sound and applies to realistic neural networks (3-layer ConvNets) without overly strict assumptions (Reviewer 4). In contrast, Reviewer 1 finds the augmentation assumptions unrealistic – this disagreement is preserved in Weaknesses.
- The proposed SA-FixMatch is an intuitive, elegant, and effective improvement that aligns with the theory; it shows strong gains, including impressive improvements from CutOut-SA on recent FixMatch derivatives (Reviewer 2). Experimental validation on standard SSL benchmarks is included.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and concise, with good figures/tables; it highlights contributions and differences from prior work (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The theory not only explains FixMatch’s success but also predicts new results (SA-FixMatch), and the framework extends to other FixMatch-like methods.
- SA-FixMatch is simple to implement (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Artificial augmentation assumptions:** The theoretical analysis assumes an augmented view that can selectively remove either noise or one of two feature patches while retaining the other, and that feature and noise patches are perfectly separable. This is unrealistic; real augmentations like random cropping or masking affect all patches indiscriminately. The assumption that augmentation “knows” which patch is a feature is too strong (Reviewer 1). (Reviewer 4, conversely, finds the assumptions not overly strict – this contrast is preserved.)
- **Motivation concern:** The core question – why FixMatch generalizes better than SL – is questioned because SSL uses 9× more data (unlabeled) than SL in typical semi-supervised settings, so better performance from more data seems obvious. A fairer comparison would be SSL vs SL with the same total training samples (i.e., labeling the unlabeled data for SL) (Reviewer 3). The authors are asked to address why the analysis is significant beyond this data advantage.
- **Novelty of SA-Cutout:** The proposed SA-Cutout (masking semantic regions) is not entirely novel; similar guided augmentation exists in “Crafting Better Contrastive Views for Siamese Representation Learning” (CVPR 2022) and gradient-based masking approaches like “Adversarial Dropout for Supervised and Semi-supervised Learning” (AAAI 2018) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 finds it elegant and effective, noting the disagreement.
- **Computational overhead:** SA-FixMatch uses Grad-CAM for semantic-aware augmentation, which adds significant computational cost during training and introduces an extra hyperparameter to tune, potentially limiting scalability (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues (Reviewer 4):**
  - Non-standard notations (e.g., \(Z_l\) for labeled dataset instead of \(\mathcal{S}\)).
  - Ambiguous variable usage: \(i\) is used to index both patches and classes (e.g., lines 126-128), making derivations hard to follow.
  - Opaque definitions: Definition 1 (multi-view data assumption) is dense and needs more gradual introduction with concrete examples; the distinction between single-view and multi-view samples is unclear.
  - Minor: In theorems (e.g., Theorem 4), phrasing “for any \((x,y) \sim D\) with probability …” should be “with probability over the selection of \((x,y) \sim D\)”.
- **Typos:** Line 87 incorrectly cites “FixMatch (Xie)” instead of “FixMatch (Sohn)” (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing prior references:** The analysis closely follows Allen-Zhu and Li [2023] but misses earlier works on augmentation and feature learning (Wen and Li, ICML 2021; Wen and Li, NeurIPS 2022) (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms/missing experiments:**
  - Can augmentation be agnostic to patch content? What would be the impact of a uniformly random mask? Could distribution-agnostic augmentation achieve similar results? (Reviewer 1)
  - Could multiple cutouts be used to enforce exactly one classifying feature per strong augmentation? (Reviewer 2)
  - Can the theory extend to more architectures (ResNets, ViTs)? Would that change results or imply certain architectures generalize better with SL? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why does the margin in Theorem 4 scale as \(\log(k)\) (where \(k\) is number of classes), implying better margin for more classes? (Reviewer 4)
  - What polynomial degree and leading coefficient are expected for \(T = \text{poly}(k)/\eta\) (iterations to convergence)? (Reviewer 4)
