## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the TimeStep Master (TSM) paradigm for efficient fine-tuning of diffusion models via LoRA. TSM addresses the limitation of using a single shared LoRA across all timesteps by introducing two stages: (1) a fostering stage that partitions the diffusion timestep range into intervals and trains separate LoRA modules on each interval (TimeStep LoRA experts), and (2) an assembling stage that combines these experts through a designed asymmetrical mixture of experts with core-context collaboration, where the smallest-interval expert serves as the core without gating and larger-interval experts serve as context with time-dependent gating. Experiments are conducted on domain adaptation, post-pretraining, and model distillation tasks across UNet, DiT, and MM-DiT architectures and image/video modalities. TSM achieves state-of-the-art results on all tasks.

### Strengths
- Identifies a key limitation of applying a single LoRA uniformly across all diffusion timesteps, given the significant differences in feature distributions at varying noise levels. Partitioning timesteps into intervals with dedicated LoRA modules enhances adaptability.
- The two-stage design (fostering then assembling) is innovative and effectively captures noise-level variations while maintaining efficiency through shared experts and a collaborative learning function.
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple tasks (domain adaptation, post-pretraining, model distillation) and across diverse architectures (UNet, DiT, MM-DiT) and modalities (image, video). Results show consistent improvements and strong generalization.
- The paper is well-structured, with clear illustrations (figures) and compelling qualitative and quantitative results.
- Reviewer 3 specifically notes that the method maintains relatively low computational costs and addresses text adherence deterioration in LoRA-tuned models.
- The asymmetrical mixture-of-experts design with core-context collaboration is highlighted as particularly novel and effective.

### Weaknesses
- **Rationale and clarity issues:** The rationale for using different scales of intervals in the assembling stage is not clearly explained (Reviewer 1). Equation (7) uses confusing subscript notation for $\epsilon$ and requires a more rigorous mathematical definition of $\epsilon$ and its interaction with timestep-specific LoRA parameters (Reviewer 2). The claim that prompt misalignment errors (e.g., incorrect spatial relationships, attribute binding) are caused solely by using a single LoRA across timesteps is not fully justified; these issues also appear in pre-trained and fully fine-tuned models, and more explanation or experiments (e.g., fine-tuning only UNet, not the text encoder) are needed to validate the claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:**
  - No comparison of the asymmetrical MoE against a standard (vanilla) MoE (Reviewer 1). In Figure 3, if a single scale with \(n=8\) intervals were used, could a vanilla MoE with top-2 gating already learn to combine context and core experts? (Reviewer 1, question).
  - Table 1 does not include results for the proposed method on the base SD2 model; the direct improvement over SD2 without additional modifications cannot be assessed (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 2 lacks widely-used FID scores for post-pretraining, making cross-comparison with works like Pixart-Alpha impossible (Reviewer 2).
  - No comparison with non-LoRA methods that address prompt misalignment, e.g., Training-Free Structured Diffusion Guidance, BoxDiff, Cross-Attention Guidance, or LayoutDiffusion; nor against other LoRA combination methods like Linear Arithmetic Composition, Reference Tuning-Based Composition, or Mixture of LoRA Experts (Reviewer 3).
  - Reviewer 4 requests experiments on additional tasks (e.g., image restoration, image segmentation) to demonstrate broader applicability.
- **Computational overhead and practical applicability:**
  - The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of computational overhead from the assembling stage (extra FLOPs, memory footprint, latency) during both training and inference, hindering assessment of practical deployment in resource-constrained environments (Reviewer 2).
  - The two-stage design introduces additional hyperparameters (interval selection, router architecture) that require careful tuning, which may limit generalization to tasks outside evaluated benchmarks (Reviewer 4).
  - The cumulative effect of multiple LoRA modules and the inherent computational cost of iterative diffusion inference still pose scalability challenges; extra parameters further limit deployment on resource-constrained devices (Reviewer 4).
  - Reviewer 4 notes that improvements in color and shape metrics in Table 1 are modest relative to the added complexity, raising questions about practical significance. (Reviewer 3 also points to examples in Figs. 4 and 5 where prompt adherence appears poor.)
  - Reviewer 3 asks whether 1 M additional parameters is accurate and requests clarification on which components (e.g., gating mechanisms) account for this increase.
- **Ablation and parameter analysis:**
  - Table 6 shows performance improves with 8 experts in stage 1, but the paper does not explore diminishing returns or potential degradation with excessive experts, nor the trade-off between performance and computational cost (Reviewer 2).
  - The choice of LoRA rank \(r\) as a hyperparameter is not justified; it is unclear why all attention blocks share the same rank or how performance changes as a function of \(r\) beyond the values tested (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4 question).
- **Visual quality concerns:** In Figure 4, the “in the style of Sci-Fi” prompt appears poorly followed; in Figure 5, some images (e.g., a black-and-white image) show poor prompt understanding (Reviewer 3).
