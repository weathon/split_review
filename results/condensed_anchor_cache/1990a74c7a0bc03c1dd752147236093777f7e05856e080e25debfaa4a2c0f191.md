## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents SC-OmniGS, a self-calibrating omnidirectional Gaussian splatting system that jointly optimizes 3D Gaussians, camera poses, and a differentiable omnidirectional camera model (trainable focal length and distortion coefficients) by minimizing a weighted spherical photometric loss. The main technical contributions are the derivation of backward gradients for spherical-image pose refinement (Eqs. 13–14) and the differentiable camera model enabling ray-wise distortion handling. The method achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis on synthetic (two datasets) and real-world 360° data, outperforming prior omnidirectional (OmniGS) and NeRF-based self-calibration approaches.

### Strengths

- **First system for self-calibrating omnidirectional radiance fields** (Reviewers 1, 4). Jointly optimizes 3D Gaussians, omnidirectional camera poses, and camera models – a novel combination.  
- **Differentiable omnidirectional camera model** with trainable focal length and angle distortion coefficients (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Enables ray-wise distortion handling and adapts to consumer-grade 360° cameras.  
- **Pose gradient derivation** (Eqs. 13–14) using chain rule on the sphere (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Makes direct pose optimization for spherical images tractable.  
- **Spherical photometric loss weights** ensure spatially balanced optimization across varying pixel densities (Reviewer 2).  
- **Thorough experiments**: comparisons on two datasets, cutting‑edge method OmniGS, multiple initialization schemes (Tab. 1), and perturbation analysis (Fig. 5) (Reviewer 4).  
- **State‑of‑the‑art novel view synthesis** quality among omnidirectional and self‑calibration methods (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
- **Sound motivation**: modeling the full sphere directly mitigates distortion/aliasing introduced by cube‑map projection (Reviewer 4).  
- **Well‑structured paper** – methodology and findings are easy to follow (Reviewer 2; reviewer 4 notes some figures need better captions – see Weaknesses).  

### Weaknesses

- **Limited technical novelty** (Reviewer 1, score 6): self‑calibration of 3DGS has been well‑studied, and omnidirectional GS already exists; the combination is practical but not scientifically large. The gradient derivation (Eqs. 13–14) is a straightforward extension of perspective cases; similar computations appear in SfM bundle adjustment for spherical cameras (e.g., Metashape, OpenMVS). (Confidence 3.)

- **Misleading terminology** (Reviewer 2, score 8): the method is called “self‑calibrating” but requires SfM initialization; it is more accurately a joint optimization, not classical auto‑calibration (e.g., from absolute dual quadrics). (Confidence 3.)

- **Missing pose accuracy evaluation** (Reviewers 2, 3): only rendering quality (PSNR) is reported. Without comparing optimized extrinsic parameters against ground truth, it is unclear whether gains come from pose optimization, the camera model, or the 3DGS representation. Request: quantitative and qualitative pose comparisons on both synthetic and real datasets. (Confidence 3–4.)

- **Missing intrinsic parameter evaluation** (Reviewer 2): show whether focal length and distortion parameters improve after optimization relative to initial estimates. Clarify whether these parameters are shared across all views or optimized per view. (Confidence 3.)

- **Missing comparisons with 3DGS‑based no‑pose methods** (Reviewer 3, score 6): baselines such as COLMAP‑Free 3D GS (CVPR 2024), NoPE‑NeRF, and LocalRF should be included because the compared NeRF‑based methods were designed for noisy poses, not unposed images. (Confidence 4.)

- **Missing ablation of anisotropy regularizer** (Reviewer 3): the paper does not ablate the anisotropy regularizer loss. (Confidence 4.)

- **Incomplete Table 1 results** (Reviewer 3): no experiments with random initialization and estimated depth are reported. (Confidence 4.)

- **Failure case analysis needed** (Reviewer 3): the method cannot be trained from scratch on real‑world multi‑room scenes (even though real datasets have more views). An analysis of why this fails and under what conditions is recommended. (Confidence 4.)

- **Figure captions too brief** (Reviewer 4, score 8): Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 require readers to refer back to the main text, disrupting the flow. (Confidence 4.)

- **Lack of geometric visualizations** (Reviewer 4): only rendered images are compared; depth or point‑cloud visualizations are missing. (Confidence 4.)

- **Dataset clarity** (Reviewer 4): in datasets like 360Roam, are the 110 training and 37 test views all omnidirectional, or are additional perspective images used as auxiliary data? (Confidence 4.)
