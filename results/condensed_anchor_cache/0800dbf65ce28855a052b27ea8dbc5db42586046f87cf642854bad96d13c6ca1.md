## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes EvoPress, an evolutionary search framework for dynamic (non-uniform) LLM compression that is provably convergent and has low sample/evaluation complexity under linear fitness. The authors observe that error monotonicity (end-to-end error proportional to sum of per-layer errors) does not hold for LLMs. EvoPress is applied to three compression approaches: unstructured sparsity, structured pruning/layer dropping, and quantization with dynamic bitwidths. Experiments on Llama, Mistral, and Phi models show improved performance over prior non-uniform compression methods. Three reviewers are broadly positive (scores 8, 8, 6), while one reviewer is strongly critical (score 3).

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical performance** across multiple compression types (unstructured pruning, structured pruning/layer dropping, quantization) on Llama, Mistral, and Phi models. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Flexible and architecture-agnostic**: the method works with different model architectures and compression techniques. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- **Simplicity and computational efficiency**: effectively minimizes costs when working with larger models; design is compelling. (Reviewer 2)
- **Theoretical analysis** provided, including convergence guarantees; this bonus analysis may be of interest beyond LLM context. (Reviewer 1; but see weaknesses below)
- **Well-written** paper with clear presentation of arguments. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical foundation misalignment**: Theorem 1 assumes a linear fitness function, which is unlikely to hold for LLMs (highly non-convex, discrete search space). This makes the theorem’s relevance to the practical algorithm unclear. (Reviewer 4, confidence 3)  
  *Reviewer 1 (confidence 2) also notes the theoretical results seem somewhat disconnected and suggests exploring the function form of $f$ in real experiments or including a simulation example to demonstrate significance.*
- **Evaluation lacks systematic rigor**:
    - Results are shown only at one sparsity level (70% in Table 2); performance gains at other sparsity levels are not reported. This makes it difficult to assess robustness and generalizability, and to compare fairly with other methods across the sparsity spectrum. (Reviewer 4, confidence 3)
    - Average and standard deviation across multiple runs are not reported; without this, it is unclear whether improvements are statistically significant. (Reviewer 1, confidence 2)
    - Variability of the optimal compression profile across runs is not discussed; it is unknown whether the search gets stuck in local minima. (Reviewer 2, questions 2)
- **Scalability and generalization concerns**:
    - The algorithm uses an elitist, exploitation-focused strategy with single-offspring selection and minimal mutations (one mutation shown nearly optimal). This resembles random coordinate descent; which properties of evolutionary search are essential remains unclear. (Reviewer 2, confidence 3)
    - The approach may work well when the optimization manifold remains relatively convex, but for multi-modal compression techniques or non-convex landscapes, there is risk of local minima and poor generalization beyond controlled benchmarks. (Reviewer 2, confidence 3)
    - It is unclear whether the hyperparameters used in the heuristics (mutation operation, multi-step selection) transfer well across tasks, datasets, and models. (Reviewer 3, confidence 2)
- **Algorithm presentation**: No overview figure summarizing EvoPress steps and the multiple heuristics; this would improve clarity. (Reviewer 3, confidence 2)
- **Step size and selection stages**:
    - How is the step size in a single dimension determined (e.g., 1M weights for unstructured sparsity)? Is it fixed or adaptive? (Reviewer 2, question 4)
    - The selection process uses only two stages; were more stages tested and found ineffective? (Reviewer 2, question 5)
