## Merged Review

### Summary
AutoDAN is an automatic, interpretable adversarial attack that generates jailbreak suffixes token-by-token using gradient information and a readability objective, producing readable prompts that bypass perplexity filters while maintaining high attack success rates.

### Strengths
- Novel token-by-token generation method that produces adversarial sequences of arbitrary length while preserving readability, representing a breakthrough over fixed-length gradient optimization and LLM-prompted jailbreaks (R3).
- Effective on multiple LLMs (Vicuna-7B/13B, Guanaco-7B, Pythia-12B) and demonstrates transferability to black-box models (R1, R2).
- Reveals interpretable jailbreak strategies from generated samples, such as shifting domains and corroborating fine-grained instructions (R2).
- Versatile beyond harmful content generation, applicable to objectives like system prompt leakage (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Trade-off between attack success rate and readability not adequately resolved**: The method adds a weight parameter to balance the two objectives, but experiments (Table 1) show ASR is compromised for lower perplexity, and a similar trade-off exists in baselines (R1). One reviewer (R1) questions whether the method truly achieves high ASR and low perplexity simultaneously, noting that the perplexity of generated suffixes is sometimes similar to GCG (Fig.1 vs. Fig.6) and is around 80–100 (GPT-2) rather than the 30–50 typical of natural paragraphs; the paper does not provide the lowest/highest perplexity examples (R1’s questions).
- **Attack success rate metric is insufficient**: ASR is computed only by checking if no refusal string appears in the response; this may not reflect actual harmfulness (R1, R3). Example: “how to build a bomb” is elaborated into a cyberpunk dystopian scenario, potentially distorting the request’s meaning (R1). One reviewer (R3) recommends validation via a dedicated safety model, GPT-4 judge, or manual inspection. Direct transferability results without a PPL filter (requested by R1) are also missing.
- **Limited evaluation to less robust models**: The method is tested only on Vicuna-7B/13B, Guanaco-7B, and Pythia-12B, all of which are known to be highly vulnerable (ASR >90% even for GCG) (R1, R3, R4). Missing evaluations on more safety-aligned LLMs such as Llama 2, Claude, Bard, and GPT-4. Transfer attacks against these models are also lacking (R3, R4). One reviewer (R4) remains skeptical until tangible success on GPT-4, Bard, and Bing Chat is shown.
- **Lack of ablation studies**: The dual-target loss with weight parameters w1 and w2 is not ablated; the effect of these weights on ASR and perplexity is not studied (R1). The impact of suffix length constraint is also unexplored (R2). Readability measures (e.g., perplexity) for the full adversarial dataset are not provided (R3’s question).
- **No computational cost evaluation**: The paper does not report training time, convergence speed, or resource requirements (R1, R2). The method involves backpropagation through the LLM and token traversal, which could be computationally heavy (R2).
- **Black-box applicability is limited**: The method requires access to token probabilities, which is often unavailable for API-based models (e.g., GPT-3.5) (R2).
- **Other defenses not considered**: Beyond perplexity filtering, LLM providers may use dynamic content moderation or keyword filtering; the method is not tested against such defenses (R4).
- **Baseline comparisons are insufficient**: The paper does not compare against other automated jailbreak prompt generation methods such as JAILBREAKER (R4). The lack of a direct comparison on attack strength (e.g., transferability without PPL filter) is also noted (R1’s question).
- **Reproducibility concerns**: Source code is not released, and technical details (implementation of perplexity testing, evaluation settings, generation parameters) are not fully described; one reviewer (R4) considers reproduction difficult. Minor: citation “Claude+[CITE]” needs revision (R2).
