## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Di-Fusion, a fully self-supervised denoising method for diffusion MRI (dMRI) that leverages latter diffusion steps and an adaptive sampling process. Unlike prior approaches, it uses a single-stage framework without an explicit noise model, training a network to predict one DWI from a linear combination of two DWIs with different diffusion encodings, with time-dependent coefficients and iterative refinement. The method aims to improve SNR without clean reference data and claims state-of-the-art performance on microstructure modeling, tractography, and other downstream tasks.

### Strengths
- **Novel diffusion-like modeling for dMRI denoising**: The idea of learning a relationship between two DWIs with different diffusion encodings to denoise each other is interesting (R3, score 5, confident 4; R5, score 5, confident 4). R3 notes this is a creative application of diffusion modeling, and R5 finds the algorithm appears novel.
- **Stabilization of the diffusion process**: R4 (score 10, confident 4) highlights that the fusion approach stabilizes issues found in DDM². R2 (score 8, confident 3) adds that compared to DDM², Di-Fusion shows comprehensive improvements and is simpler to implement, requiring no additional denoiser training.
- **Avoids hallucination by training only later diffusion steps**: R3 explicitly lists training only later steps as a strength, as it reduces hallucination.
- **Flexibility with data and noise models**: R2 notes the method relies on N2N training and pixel shuffling, not explicit noise models, suggesting generalization potential to other denoising scenarios (e.g., cryo-EM). This is seen as a strong practical advantage.
- **Thorough and comprehensive experiments**: R2 emphasizes the extensive comparisons and analyses on downstream tasks (DTI metrics, tractography), making the work methodologically well-rounded. R1 (score 6, confident 2) appreciates results across multiple real and simulated datasets, suggesting generalizability.
- **Good reproducibility and code quality**: R1 and R4 note that code was released, is well-written, informative, and uses open-source standards, aiding reproducibility.
- **Paper is easy to follow**: R1 finds the paper easy to follow, though other reviewers disagree (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: Multiple reviewers note that key denoising methods are absent:
  - Random-matrix theory approaches by Veraart et al. (Neuroimage 2016) are a widely used baseline and should be compared (R5, confident 4).
  - Patch2Self2 (CVPR 2024) and MPPCA should be added (R4, score 10, confident 4).
  - Noise2Score and Recorrupted2Recorrupted are other recent self-supervised methods that should be compared (R3, confident 4).
  - R1 suggests two additional papers: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.00042 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.05794.
- **Theoretical/mathematical foundations are questionable** (R3, confident 4):
  - The assumption that DWIs with different diffusion encodings share the same underlying clean image is inaccurate; in reality, different diffusion directions produce different contrasts. The implications of this mismatch are not addressed.
  - The claim that the method does not require an explicit noise model is an overstatement: the J-invariance assumption implicitly assumes zero-mean, conditionally independent noise, and the additive noise model (x = y + n₁) may not hold for low-SNR magnitude images at high b-values.
  - The derivation of the linear combination relationship (Eq. 31→32) is not accurate: F_θ predicts x₀, but the equation includes an additional σₜ²·z term, so x_{t-1} is not a simple linear interpolation. The connection between theory and implementation is unclear.
  - Mathematical inconsistencies: Eq. 7 mixes a joint distribution (q(x_{1:T}|xₜ*)) with a Gaussian on the right side. The definition of forward vs. reverse process is inconsistent (Line 160 vs. Figure 1).
  - J-invariance requirement is violated because the input to F(·) in Eq. 9 (xₜ*) is a linear combination of x and x', and x serves as the supervision signal, creating a correlation that breaks conditional independence.
- **Misleading or inaccurate statements about MR physics** (R3): The claim that “noise predominantly originates from physical interferences” is vague and inaccurate—the work deals with thermal noise. The statement that “different clinical applications require varying numbers of diffusion vectors . . . which makes modeling the noise distribution challenging” is incorrect; acquiring more vectors does not affect noise modeling difficulty.
- **Quantitative evaluation limited to simulations** (R5, confident 4): All quantitative results use simulations that likely favor the proposed algorithm’s assumptions. R2 also questions the DTI comparison: instead of comparing DTI metrics from noisy denoised images against each other, the authors should compute a clean DTI metric from more DWI data as a ground truth reference (R2, score 8, confident 3).
- **Qualitative results are not convincing** (R4, R5): R5 states that the few example images shown on real human data appear cherry-picked, and it is unclear whether advantages hold over large collections. R4 adds that it is hard to see large qualitative advantages over Patch2Self.
- **Missing speed, memory, and hardware details**: R4 (score 10, confident 4) notes that the paper claims to “outperform the other methods” yet provides no speed or memory comparisons. R4 requests reporting time and memory usage, and specifying what GPUs were used for training.
- **Language and presentation issues**: R1 and R4 both note that a thorough language review is needed (e.g., strange phrasing: “The MRI, including …”, “Consequently, the denoising technique plays a crucial role”). R3 adds that the overall presentation lacks clarity, with inaccurate mathematical statements and inconsistent terminology.
- **Unclear practical requirements**: R4 asks what the minimum number of B₀ and DWI volumes is required, and whether the method can work with a single B₀ scan. This information is missing.
- **Unexplained design choices**: R3 questions why the Fusion process dynamically adjusts the starting point of the forward process based on t (xₜ* depends on t), which is unconventional. R3 also asks for clarification on the “drift” term highlighted in Eq. 5 and how it emerges.
