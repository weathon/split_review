## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ControlAR, a framework that enables spatial control (edges, depth, segmentation) in autoregressive (AR) image generation models. It uses a lightweight ViT-based control encoder to transform control inputs into tokens and a conditional decoding method that fuses these tokens with image tokens via addition in intermediate layers. The method shows strong qualitative and quantitative results, but reviewers raise concerns about missing comparisons, pathway generality, evaluation limitations, and presentation of efficiency gains.

### Strengths

- **Simple and effective formulation**: The approach of adding control features to image token features at selected layers is elegant, straightforward, and achieves high-quality generation with low additional training cost (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Novelty for AR models**: Very few prior works tackle controllable generation in AR models; ControlAR is a novel extension of ControlNet-style conditioning to autoregressive generation (Reviewer 2).
- **Good ablations**: The paper provides detailed ablations on training strategy, fusion strategy (cross-attention vs. addition), placement and number of control layers, and control encoder choices (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Strong results across multiple tasks**: Quantitative and qualitative results are strong on class-to-image and text-to-image settings for edges, depth maps, and segmentation (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Arbitrary-resolution generation**: The method enables AR models to generate at arbitrary resolutions, overcoming fixed-resolution limitations (Reviewer 1).
- **Smaller than ControlNet**: The control encoder is lightweight (22M parameters) compared to typical ControlNet models, though this comparison is disputed (Reviewer 2 vs. Reviewer 4).
- **Good experimental details**: The paper includes thorough setup descriptions and clear demonstrations that conditional decoding outperforms conditional prefilling (Reviewer 2).
- **Visualization quality**: Generated images look convincing (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Equation (4) is not well defined**: Writing \(q_i + C_{i+1}\) with \(q_i\) as a discrete token (belonging to a codebook) and \(C_{i+1}\) as a continuous feature is mathematically improper. In addition, the control features are added at intermediate layers, not to the input embedding as implied (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with recent AR models**: The paper should compare against Lumina-mGPT, Cm3leon, or Anole, especially for segmentation-to-image tasks, and discuss potential integration with these models (Reviewer 1).
- **Pathway choice concerns**: ControlAR trains separate models for each control task (e.g., Canny, depth, segmentation), while the "conditional prefilling" approach could support a unified generative model across modalities (text, multiple images, flexible relationships). If the goal is only specific local controls, diffusion methods are already mature. The paper does not justify why the AR community should adopt this restricted formulation over a more general one (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of failure case analysis**: The paper should discuss representative failure cases to better understand limitations (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited structural diversity analysis**: Spatial conditions like segmentation maps and Canny edges impose strong constraints; the paper does not explore whether some diversity can be incorporated during conditional decoding (Reviewer 1).
- **Efficiency comparison is misleading**: Comparing 22M (ControlAR) to 361M (ControlNet++) parameters is unfair because diffusion and AR models have fundamentally different architectures and generation mechanisms. The paper should compare inference time, FLOPs, or other relevant metrics within similar architecture families (Reviewer 4).
- **Quantitative improvements are marginal**: The gains in Tables 1 and 2 over prior work are small (Reviewer 4).
- **Use of pretrained encoders is standard**: The choice of CLIP, DINOv2, or SAM weights for the control encoder is a standard practice; the paper does not justify why this constitutes a novel contribution (Reviewer 4).
- **Color saturation / contrast issues**: Some output images show unrealistic color saturation or excess contrast. It is unclear whether this is caused by the control layers or the base AR model (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient details on multi-resolution training**: Section 4.2 lacks specifics on the multi-resolution dataset size, architecture design for positional encoding, and how the control encoder (e.g., VIT-S with fixed global position encoding) handles arbitrary resolutions (Reviewer 3).
- **No text-image alignment evaluation**: The paper should evaluate whether the generated images match the text prompts when text is used (Reviewer 3).
- **Resolution control without a control image**: Can ControlAR or an extension enable arbitrary-resolution generation even when no edge/depth/segmentation map is provided? How? (Reviewer 3).
- **Comparison with resolution-aware prompts**: The paper claims ControlAR offers "easy" arbitrary-resolution generation, but it requires additional training just like resolution-aware prompt methods. A direct quantitative or qualitative comparison would strengthen the claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor**: Double comma in the 3rd contribution bullet (Reviewer 3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) finds no weaknesses severe enough to reject and considers the work essential, while Reviewer 4 (score 5) views the efficiency comparison as misleading and the improvements as marginal. Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 6) raise multiple specific concerns but acknowledge the method's value.
