## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the representational collapse/oversmoothing in Transformers, where token representations become overly similar across layers. The authors propose Twicing Attention, which replaces the standard attention matrix \(A\) with \(2A - A^2\), drawing inspiration from the kernel twicing procedure in nonparametric regression and the connection between self-attention and low-pass non-local means (NLM) smoothing filters. The method is theoretically shown to slow eigenvalue decay and preserve token diversity. Experiments on image classification (ImageNet) and language modeling tasks show performance gains, but improvements on clean data are modest. One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, praising the theoretical depth and complete proofs; another (score 5) was more critical, questioning the problem framing and empirical scope.

### Strengths
- Well-written, easy to follow, and fluent presentation with clear formulae and background motivation (R1, R3, R4).
- The Twicing Attention mechanism is simple and easy to implement (R1).
- Novelty: introduces a new attention mechanism (Twicing) to slow eigenvalue decay associated with representational collapse (R2).
- Strong theoretical foundation: provides mathematical validation connecting self-attention to NLM smoothing filters, with thorough proofs that Twicing attention (\(2A - A^2\)) has superior theoretical properties in preserving token diversity and slowing representational decay (R1, R2, R3, R4). The research angle via NLM is interesting and has theoretical significance (R3).
- Empirical validation across multiple modalities: experiments on both language models and vision models (R2). Includes attention heatmap visualizations that validate the claim of preserved token diversity (R4). The work is informative and complete (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Over-explanation and verbosity: the core idea (compute \(2A - A^2\)) is simple, but the paper over-explains it (pages 7–8 could be condensed; Algorithm 1 is redundant with 12 lines) (R1). This space could have been used for a more thorough study (R1).
- Limited improvement on clean data: gains on clean settings like ImageNet (Table 1) are modest and not substantial enough to justify the added complexity and computational overhead of Twicing Attention (R2, R4). A more significant performance leap would be expected (R2).
- Additional computational cost: Twicing has the same \(O(N^2 d)\) complexity but involves an extra matrix multiplication (Algorithm 1, line 7), increasing overhead by ~50% (R2). The efficiency comparison in Table 4 shows little difference in speed and GFLOPs, which is inconsistent; a detailed efficiency analysis is needed (R2). The empirical gains come at the cost of additional computation, making it hard for practitioners to adopt (R4).
- Lack of comparison with alternative solutions: no comparison with existing methods that address oversmoothing, such as regularization strategies (R2). The number of test datasets and comparison methods is too few (R3). Dense tasks like segmentation, which are more direct for evaluating structure of patch representations, are missing (R4).
- Missing ablations and analyses: no systematic investigation of applying Twicing at different layer depths or intervals (R2). Visualizations are only on later layers and few heads; visualizations on earlier layers and more heads would strengthen the claim (R4). Performance on more extreme cases (very large LLMs, non-classical Transformer structures like Linear Transformer) is not evaluated (R3).
- Narrow problem framing: the premise of representation collapse may not be inherent; DINO (Caron et al., 2021) and Darcet et al. (2024) show that self-supervised training can produce diverse representations and that apparent collapse may be a nuanced information distribution pattern (R4). The paper does not address this context.
- No comparison of alternative kernel twicing candidates: the paper only uses \(2A - A^2\) without studying other possibilities and comparing them on benchmarks (R1).
