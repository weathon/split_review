## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a meta-learning approach to improve the scalability of latent force models (LFMs) for nonlinear dynamical systems. It uses a deep kernel along with a task representation (derived from a function embedding) to infer latent forces without requiring per-instance numerical ODE/PDE solvers. Reviewers disagreed on the overall quality: two gave score 5 (positive about novelty and scalability) and two gave score 3 (critical of framing, experimental clarity, and practicality). The method is claimed to be faster than alternatives (DeepLFM, Alfi), but several reviewers question the experimental setup, the meta-learning framing, and the practical assumptions.

### Strengths

- The problem is important and challenging: modeling complex scientific scenarios with nonlinear dynamics, relevant to deep learning and other sciences.
- The idea of improving LFM scalability by incorporating Deep Kernels and GPs is reasonable, enforcing a strong Bayesian structure.
- The method is novel in providing a numerical-solver-free approach for dynamical system solving, combining task representation with a deep kernel in a meta-learning scheme.
- The use of task representation to generalize LFMs to new tasks and the expressive power of deep GP kernels are positive features.
- The method is technically sound and popularizes a dimensionality-reduction viewpoint specific to physical models.
- Clear motivation and mathematical description.
- Experimental results appear to show efficiency/scalability over compared baselines (explicitly faster than Alfi and DeepLFM in certain settings).
- The manuscript is well-organized.

### Weaknesses

**Meta-learning framing and related work**
- The paper places itself in the meta-learning field but does not compare with any other meta-learning method, nor mentions GP-based meta-learning approaches in the related work. [R1, R2]
- The definition of “task” (each data instance) may be non-standard for multi-task learning; the method can be seen as a parametric+non-parametric framework trained via likelihood maximization without specific meta-learned parameters, requiring better justification. [R2]
- It is unclear what meta-knowledge is learned and used (only the task representation?); the encoder for task representation is undermotivated. [R3]

**Practicality of latent force assumption**
- The assumption that latent forces are available (or observable) is less practical. In the ODE benchmark, the latent force is inferred from Alfi, an unpublished preprint software; the reliability is not discussed, nor are alternative ways to collect latent forces. [R2, R3]
- The paper should clarify how the latent force is obtained and why it is observable; the practical feasibility is questionable. [R3]

**Experimental design and clarity**
- The empirical evaluation is weak: only low-dimensional tasks are used, not showcasing the power of LFMs; high-dimensional experiments are missing. [R2, R4]
- The description of experiments is unclear: two ODEs (mRNA, LV) are mixed together; task definition and actual execution are unclear; insufficient experimental details (e.g., dataset creation, integrator, uniform sampling). [R2]
- The proposed method (DKLFM) is compared only with DeepLFM and Alfi. DeepLFM is significantly better in Table 2 (higher MSE?), yet it is not included in the same type of comparison as Figure 6. [R1]
- The comparison between Alfi/DeepLFM (reported on 20 examples) and DKLFM (reported on 256 examples) may be unfair; elaboration on dataset sizes and whether computation times are averaged is needed. [R1]
- The output MSE on the second benchmark (PDE?) appears very poor; the scalability of the method is not explicitly discussed beyond speed. [R2]
- The paper focuses on inference speed but does not report training cost: training DKLFM requires expensive generation of training datasets, not needed for single-task methods like DeepLFM; a comparison of training costs would be informative. [R4]

**Kernel choices and ablation**
- Only the RBF kernel is used in experiments; an ablation study over different kernels (e.g., Matern, spectral, cosine) is missing, nor is a justification for RBF provided. [R1]

**Model formulation and presentation**
- Model formulation needs clarification: the missing observation (output) likelihood; confusion between latent force $\mathbf{f}_n$ and function $f$; inconsistent use of symbols between GP preliminaries and model formulation. [R3]
- Figure captions lack sufficient description (e.g., what colors represent, what error bars indicate, why test points have no observations in Fig 4b). [R3]

**Metrics and additional comparisons**
- Using only MSE is insufficient; NLL or ELBO would be more informative in the GP setting. [R1]
- Missing comparison with other meta-learning approaches (e.g., GP-based) beyond DeepLFM. [R1]
- No comparison between different choices of embedder (e.g., set encoders) or specification of the deep kernel architecture. [R2]
- No discussion of how the method would handle non-Gaussian or more complex distributions (suggestions: flow-based approaches, neural ODE). [R1]
