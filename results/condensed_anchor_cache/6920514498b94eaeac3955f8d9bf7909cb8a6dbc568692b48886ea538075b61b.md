## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel training strategy for self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SSMDE) that targets reflective surfaces. It introduces a reflection-aware triplet mining loss to penalize photometric error minimization in reflective regions identified at the pixel level, and a reflection-aware knowledge distillation method that allows a student model to learn selectively from reflective and non‑reflective regions. Key claimed contributions: the first end‑to‑end method specifically for SSMDE on reflective surfaces; simple, integrable components (triplet mining loss, distillation); and state‑of‑the‑art performance validated on multiple indoor datasets (ScanNet, 7‑Scenes, Booster). Reviewers are not unanimous: two rate the paper 6 (confidence 4 and 5) while one rates it 8 (confidence 5), leading to contrasting views on performance and generalizability.

### Strengths
- Well‑written and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 3).
- First end‑to‑end method for self‑supervised monocular depth estimation targeted at reflective surfaces; the triplet mining loss is simple and can be integrated into any SSMDE framework (Reviewer 1).
- The method is general: both the triplet mining loss and the reflection‑aware knowledge distillation are simple and can be applied to most self‑supervised depth estimation frameworks (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The reflection‑aware knowledge distillation enhances accuracy on reflective surfaces while preserving high‑frequency details of non‑reflective surfaces (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments are conducted on multiple indoor datasets (ScanNet, 7‑Scenes, Booster) with cross‑validation and show state‑of‑the‑art performance (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 2 adds that the method achieves state‑of‑the‑art results; Reviewer 3 disagrees on the degree of improvement (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of evaluation on outdoor and standard indoor datasets.** The paper only tests on indoor datasets (ScanNet, 7‑Scenes, Booster) and omits common benchmarks like KITTI (outdoor) and NYUv2 (indoor) where reflective surfaces also exist. Reviewer 1 questions why outdoor experiments were omitted and whether the method adapts when a pose network is involved (rotational camera movements). Reviewer 3 asks for verification on those datasets to confirm generalizability.
- **No proof that reflective regions produce low disparities.** The assumption that reflective regions have small disparities (rather than random or larger errors) is not proven; Reviewer 1 requests evidence or references to prior work supporting this claim.
- **Fundamental concern about Eq. (8) identifying reflective surfaces.** The photometric error magnitude used in Eq. (8) is not a reliable measure of depth accuracy, let alone a reliable indicator of reflective surfaces (Reviewer 3). This undermines the core reflection‑localization mechanism.
- **Marginal overall performance.** In Table 4, all comparison methods show limited improvement or even worse results after incorporating the proposed idea; Reviewer 3 finds the performance improvement too small to be convincing. (Contrast: Reviewer 2 calls the performance state‑of‑the‑art.)
- **Outdated baselines.** The selected baselines (Monodepth2 2019, HRDepth 2021, MonoViT 2020) are early methods, not current state‑of‑the‑art. Reviewer 3 is concerned about effectiveness when integrated into recent frameworks (e.g., RA‑Depth, GasMono, Large Kernel Attention) and asks for verification on those.
- **Missing ablation on pixel‑level reflection regions \(M_r\).** An ablation study is needed to analyze the contribution of the pixel‑level reflection regions to both the triplet mining loss and the knowledge distillation (Reviewer 2).
- **Additional computational overhead.** The paper does not analyze whether the proposed triplet mining strategy and reflection‑aware knowledge distillation introduce extra inference / training overhead (Reviewer 2).
- **Confusing description of relative pose computation.** Lines 246‑247 state that relative pose \([R|t]_{s2r}\) is obtained by computing the inverse of \([R|t]_{s2r}\) – a likely typo that needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related work on depth completion.** Since depth completion is an extended task from depth estimation, the related work section should include depth completion methods [1‑5] (Reviewer 2).
