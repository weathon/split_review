## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Progressive Visual Relationship Inference (PVRI), a method that combines rough visual appearance with fine-grained visual cues to improve scene graph generation. PVRI has three steps: Known Cue Collection (KCC) using LLMs (GPT-4) to gather descriptive cues, Unknown Cue Extraction (UCE) to extract visual cues not defined in text, and Progressive Inference (PI) that refines relationship predictions. Experiments are conducted on Visual Genome and Open Images V6 datasets, claiming improvements in accuracy and interpretability for distinguishing subtle visual relationships.

### Strengths
- The overall paper structure is complete and the content is substantial.
- The paper proposes a new method that complements rough visual appearances by using an LLM to extract detailed descriptions of cues; this is an innovative attempt to capture fine-grained features via LLM.
- The author demonstrates effectiveness through extensive experiments, including ablation studies that verify the importance of the KCC and UCE strategies.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor presentation and redundancy.** Lines 93–97 are almost identical to the abstract and to lines 161–192. The interpretation of “Progressive Inference” is confusing and does not reflect the concept of “progressive.” The whole article is messy and does not highlight keypoints. Redundant descriptions appear, e.g., lines 470–485 repeat what is obvious from Table 1, while explanations of experimental background and results are insufficient.
- **Limited novelty and missing related work.** Many existing works (e.g., LLM4SGG [CVPR 2024], Zero-shot Visual Relation Detection via Composite Visual Cues from Large Language Models [NeurIPS 2023], Less is More [arXiv 2023]) already use LLM-generated visual cues to assist SGG in distinguishing similar relation categories. The author does not discuss these efforts. Two main innovations (geometric space representation and LLM-provided cues) are not the latest; see Li’s paper above.
- **Low performance.** Recall@K and mean Recall@K are lower than PE-Net-Reweight (Table 1 of Prototype-based Embedding Network, CVPR 2023).
- **Missing comparison with state-of-the-art baselines.** The paper only compares with a few methods (e.g., BGNN from 2022). Recent SGG baselines such as VETO [ICCV 2023], HiKER-SGG [CVPR 2024], and HIKER-SGG (hierarchical knowledge) should be included. Since the paper discusses the hierarchical tree structure of HiKER-SGG, it makes sense to compare with that model.
- **No complexity or cost analysis.** The method uses costly GPT-4 for *all triplets*, plus CLIP and BGNN—all heavy models. There is no analysis of training and inference time, and the union-region similarity calculation requires N×N cropping (N = number of objects), which is very time-consuming. A complexity analysis is necessary.
- **Trade-off between mR@K and R@K appears more pronounced** than in other baselines. The harmonic or simple average of R@K and mR@K (widely used in SGG [Data Transfer ECCV 2022, VETO ICCV 2023]) should be reported to show the effectiveness of the proposed model.
- **Lack of evidence for interpretability.** The Introduction claims the method addresses lack of interpretability, but no analysis is provided to demonstrate how interpretability is enhanced.
- **Insufficient ablation studies.** There is no ablation study for the Progressive Inference (PI) component or the robustness of multiple description generation. The experiments lack comparisons with some latest methods (e.g., HiKER-SGG) — why?
- **Rough figures.** Fig 1 is not a vector image; text is unclear; borders of some pictures are not processed. The pipeline figures do not explain the method well and are unintuitive.
- **Questions as weaknesses.** (1) What would happen if GPT-4 were replaced with an open-source LLM? Can the method generalize to other LLMs? (2) The trade-off issue is also a missing analysis. (3) Missing complexity analysis is a criticism.
