## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SAM into multimodal image segmentation for the first time, proposing FusionSAM with Latent Space Token Generation (LSTG) and Fusion Mask Prompting (FMP). LSTG encodes RGB-T modalities via vector quantization; FMP fuses these through cross-attention to produce prompts that guide pixel-level segmentation. Evaluated on MFNet and FMB (RGB-T) datasets, it achieves a 3.9% mIoU improvement over prior SOTA and outperforms SAM/SAM2. Code is provided.

### Strengths
- First work to extend SAM to multimodal image segmentation in natural images (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Good performance on multiple public datasets (MFNet, FMB) with promising improvements (Reviewer 1).
- Quantitative results show notably higher segmentation mIoU (3.9%) than previous best methods, including SAM and SAM2 (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Code provided in supplementary for reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- Detailed descriptions of proposed methods with mathematical formulations and architectural diagrams (Reviewer 4).
- Paper is well-organized and clearly written (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  *Contrast: Reviewer 2 strongly suspects LLM-writing and rates presentation 1, finding the method section very poor (see Weaknesses).*

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and theoretical insight**  
  - The design and losses are unintuitive; the paper describes “what” without explaining “why” – it reads as “we tried it and it worked” (Reviewer 1).  
  - Motivation is unclear and could be better articulated (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation quality** – Reviewer 2 strongly suspects the paper was written by an LLM, and points to multiple unclear elements in the method section:  
  - Figure 2 includes a top‑right subfigure with no explanation.  
  - Terms like `Fusion Conv` and `Cross Conv` in Figure 2 are undefined.  
  - Lines 300–301: “are then processed through a convolutional layer” – unclear how this is achieved.  
  - $z_f^q$ in Equation 12 and the correspondence of Equations 12–13 to Figure 2 are unclear.  
  - The “points prompt” and “Mask Token” in Figure 2, and “10‑point mask prompts and 1‑box mask prompt” in Lines 345–346 are unexplained.  
  - How Equation 7 is computed is not described.  
  - Line 288: why fused features are called “fusion mask” is confusing.
- **Title mismatch** – The title says “multimodal,” but the paper only addresses RGB‑infrared segmentation, not RGB‑depth or RGB‑event (Reviewer 3).
- **Replication/similarity concern** – The top‑right section of Figure 2 closely resembles Figure 2 from "Multi‑interactive Feature Learning and a Full‑time Multi‑modality Benchmark for Image Fusion and Segmentation" (Reviewer 3).
- **Dataset and baseline coverage**  
  - Only two datasets (MFNet, FMB) are used; more datasets with varying conditions (lighting, complex backgrounds) are needed (Reviewer 4).  
  - Missing comparisons with more multimodal fusion/segmentation baseline methods (Reviewer 4).  
  - Differences between SAM/SAM2 and FusionSAM in Table 1 are unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing computational complexity metrics** – Tables lack FLOPs, parameters, model size, and FPS. Since SAM is a foundation model, these are important (Reviewer 3).  
  - The inference computational cost vs. the original SAM is not analyzed (Reviewer 4, question).
- **Analysis of results**  
  - On MFNet, the method obtains 0 mIoU on the “Guardrail” class (Table 3); this failure is not analyzed nor are possible fixes discussed (Reviewer 1).  
  - On FMB, FusionSAM only slightly surpasses MRFS – the trade‑offs (efficiency, generalizability) vs. MRFS are not discussed (Reviewer 1).  
  - The paper claims large improvements on heat‑insensitive classes (e.g., Curve, Car Stop, Color Cone ~20 points), yet improvement on Building is only 0.1% – such disproportionality needs explanation (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete ablation and feature analysis**  
  - More ablation experiments are needed for each component in LSTG and FMP, not just Table 2 (Reviewer 2).  
  - In Table 2, the first model omits the LSTG module – how does it then extract latent features for FMP? (Reviewer 2).  
  - There is no quantitative or visual analysis of the quality of the fused features (Reviewer 4).
- **Reviewer disagreement** – Reviewers 1 and 4 assign scores of 5 (accept) with higher confidence in the paper’s contribution and presentation, while Reviewers 2 and 3 give scores of 3, citing major writing flaws, missing details, and questionable motivation. The writing quality is particularly contested: Reviewer 3 calls it “fluent and easy to understand,” whereas Reviewer 2 finds it very poor and suspects LLM generation.
