## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces RiTTA, a benchmark for audio event relation modeling in text-to-audio (TTA) generation. Contributions include: (1) an audio event relation corpus covering spatial, temporal, count, and compositional relations; (2) an audio event category corpus with 25 commonly heard categories; (3) a multi-stage relation-aware evaluation framework (MSR-RiTTA) with new metrics; and (4) a fine-tuning framework to improve relation modeling in existing TTA models. Experiments benchmark seven recent TTA models (e.g., AudioLDM, Tango) and show improvements after fine-tuning.

### Strengths
- The paper systematically addresses a previously underexplored area: modeling relations between multiple audio events in TTA (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The proposed corpus and evaluation benchmark are important for future research on audio event relations, enabling TTA with better comprehension (Reviewer 3).
- The dataset construction process is explained in detail using temporal relations as an example, and new evaluation metrics are proposed (Reviewer 1).
- The multi-stage, relation-aware evaluation framework (MSR-RiTTA) offers more targeted metrics than existing TTA evaluation standards (Reviewer 2).
- The research is rigorous in dataset construction and experimental validation, demonstrating clear improvements in relation modeling ability (Reviewer 2).
- The fine-tuning strategy shows significant improvement on benchmark tests, indicating potential for real-world applications like virtual reality and immersive media (Reviewer 2). *Note: Reviewer 2 expresses lower confidence (1) but is more positive in strengths.*

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset scope and generalizability:**
  - The corpus contains only 25 audio event categories, which seems too limited to generalize to other categories with relations; previous datasets like AudioCaps and Clotho have over 100 classes (Reviewer 3).
  - Expanding each example to only 5 events via ChatGPT is insufficient (500 events suggested); the dataset construction does not appear to require significant effort and feels somewhat toy-like (Reviewer 1).
  - The dataset targets mono-channel audio, making spatial distance relations (e.g., 1m vs 7m) difficult to measure accurately; multi-channel audio (e.g., a car driving left to right) would increase diversity and realism (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Innovation and approach limitations:**
  - The main contribution is the benchmark and dataset, not a novel generative modeling advance; the fine-tuning framework only adapts existing TTA models, which limits novelty (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper is not the first to explore audio event relation modeling—it extends temporal relations to four types in a data-driven way, but no new TTA framework that jointly models relations is proposed (Reviewer 1).
  - Performance decline on “not” relations (Figure 7) suggests the approach is insufficient for complex relation modeling (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation and experiments gaps:**
  - The evaluation benchmarks only single-strategy TTA models; no testing in more complex scenarios (e.g., virtual reality, interactive environments) or with multimodal generation models or models optimized for extended sequences (Reviewer 2).
  - Out-of-domain scenarios are not considered when forming the test set (Reviewer 1, Question 2).
  - Results on previous test data from baseline models are not reported; it is unclear whether fine-tuning affects original generation quality (Reviewer 3, Question 2).
  - The model’s ability to handle audio lengths other than 10s is not demonstrated (Reviewer 3, Question 3).
- **Missing technical details:**
  - The fine-tuning strategy description is too general, lacking specific training settings, hyperparameters, number of iterations, loss function choice, and parameter tuning procedures (Reviewer 2).
  - The construction of non-temporal relations (e.g., spatial) is not clearly explained; clarification on how corresponding text-audio pairs were created is needed (Reviewer 1, Question 1).
  - The RiTTA dataset is not provided in the supplemental materials (Reviewer 1, Question 3).
- **Feature embeddings for evaluation:**
  - FAD and FD use VGGish embeddings, which are known for poor performance; modern backbone models should be considered (Reviewer 3).
