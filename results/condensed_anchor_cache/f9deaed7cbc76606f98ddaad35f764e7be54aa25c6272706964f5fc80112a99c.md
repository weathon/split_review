## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces an entity-centric reinforcement learning approach for goal-conditioned object manipulation from pixels. The method combines Deep Latent Particles (DLP) as an object-centric perception module with a transformer-based Entity Interaction Transformer (EIT) for the policy and Q-function. A goal-conditioned reward uses Chamfer distance between particles from current and goal images, avoiding explicit correspondence matching. The method is evaluated on tabletop robot pushing tasks in IsaacGym, using multiple static camera views. Results show effective learning of structured representations and policies, scalability to many objects, and compositional generalization (e.g., trained on 3 objects, tested on >10). Ablations and comparisons to baselines (SMORL, unstructured RL) are provided.

### Strengths
- Concept is elegantly simple and straightforward, combining existing components (DLP + transformer MBRL) into a coherent RL architecture.
- Clear and well-written presentation with extensive supplementary material (ablations, visualizations).
- Experiments in IsaacGym demonstrate robust performance and validate compositional generalization.
- Scalability to many objects in goal-conditioned tasks from raw pixels.
- No explicit matching between goal and current object representations is required; the design is permutation-invariant.
- Multi-view observations help resolve depth ambiguities.
- Conditional goal transformer is soundly implemented for actor-critic RL.
- Clever use of Chamfer distance on particle representations as a reward signal.
- Ablations study the contribution of Chamfer reward, object-centric latent state, and multiple views.
- Examples and animations confirm the system works in practice.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only one environment type (colored cubes in a tabletop pushing task) is tested. Missing evaluations on non-cube objects (e.g., YCB objects), more cluttered scenes, occlusions, varying lighting, or real-world setups (kitchen environment) – raised by multiple reviewers. One reviewer (score 6) noted that particles effectively assume point-mass objects, which restricts generalization to different shapes or properties.
- **Missing related work and baselines**: Several relevant methods are not discussed or compared:
  - SRICS, DAFT-RL, NCS, STOVE (as included in DAFT-RL).
  - Interaction Networks, Graph networks (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al.).
  - Object-centric perception works (Traub et al., Driess et al.).
  - Prior particle-based control (Levine et al.).
  - Recent many-object manipulation works (not from images) – cited in review.
  - Large-language-model based baselines (e.g., Palm-E).  
  One reviewer noted several citations appear as arXiv despite conference publication.
- **Insufficient ablations and analysis**:
  - Ablation using alternative OCR methods (e.g., slot attention) instead of DLP is missing.
  - The impact of individual DLP components (e.g., background features) on policy learning is not studied; potential reliance on spurious correlations not ruled out.
  - The role of the transformer is not isolated (no comparison to simpler architectures for modeling interactions).
  - Systematic analysis of compositional generalization is lacking: extrapolation vs. interpolation on number of objects, generalization to novel object shapes (e.g., cuboid vs. cube), and performance degradation with very high or low object counts.
  - The types of entity interactions (dynamics only vs. dynamics plus reward) are not explicitly discussed; no analysis of attention patterns or how interaction density/frequency affects policy learning.
- **Design choices need justification/clarification**:
  - Why not use a matching step after OCR? The permutation-invariant design is meant to avoid it, but the benefits over pluggable matching (as in slot attention) are unclear – especially handling one-to-many mappings across views/time.
  - Why off-policy TD3? Under what conditions is it preferred over other algorithms?
  - Why is RL needed to train the DLP module? If pretrained without goals, goal-conditioned training may constrain learned representations.
  - The goal definition uses the encoder, which may prevent encoding non-predefined goals (e.g., “move red objects to the left”).
  - The sample-efficiency advantage of object-centric RL (learn on one object, transfer to others) is discarded; the method learns each task from scratch. Why not combine per-object policies? (SMORL is more sample efficient in this regard.)
- **Methodological concerns**:
  - The number of objects is assumed known (per review); handling unknown or variable numbers is not addressed.
  - The robot arm is filtered from the particle representations (supervision hidden in appendix). This assumption (white robot on white background) may not transfer to other robots or backgrounds.
  - For DLP, when the number of latent particles exceeds the number of entities, the behavior is not analyzed.
  - Identity preservation: permutation invariance may lose object identity needed for object-permanence in sequential/dynamic tasks.
  - It is unclear whether the system learns true object-object interactions or only arm-object interactions; the “adjacent goals require interactions” claim may be solvable reactively.
- **Baseline comparisons are limited**:
  - SMORL is compared only with state access (not pixels) in the presented results – reducing the fairness of comparison.
  - The comparison against unstructured RL (pixels + MLP) is considered too naïve; many previous works already show such baselines fail.
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Key details are relegated to the appendix: reward definition (Generalized Density Aware Chamfer), filtering of non-object particles, explanation of Chamfer distance equation. At least a written summary should be in the main text.
  - Theorem 2 and its consequences are not well explained in the main paper.
  - Figure 5 font is too small (right subplot).
  - Minor: open quotes, “self-attention” hyphenation, typo in Fig 6 caption (“mathcing”).
  - Appendix A or E should be moved to the main paper to better utilize space.

*Note: One reviewer (originally score 5, later upgraded to 8 after rebuttal) raised several of the above concerns; these are included as they were part of the original review. Another reviewer (score 6) provided a more critical evaluation, emphasizing limitations in object generality, interaction modeling, and baseline comparisons; that minority perspective is preserved.*
