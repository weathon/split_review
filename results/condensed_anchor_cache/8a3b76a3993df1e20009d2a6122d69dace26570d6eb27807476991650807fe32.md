## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes several enhancements for hyperbolic deep learning in computer vision: a principled method for learning curvature as a parameter (updating parameters when curvature changes), Riemannian AdamW, a maximum distance rescaling function for numerical stability, and an efficient hyperbolic convolutional layer. Experiments cover hierarchical metric learning, image classification, and VAE-based image generation.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a variety of novel techniques, including Riemannian AdamW, curvature learning with parameter rescaling, a max‑rescaling scheme, and efficient hyperbolic convolutions (Reviewer 1).
- The curvature‑update issue (previous methods ignore parameter adjustment when curvature changes) is important and the proposed fix is straightforward and effective (Reviewer 4).
- The centroid trick for adapting AdamW to the Riemannian setting is clever (Reviewer 5).
- The empirical evaluation is extensive, comparing against many baselines and considering computational challenges and modern hardware efficiency (Reviewers 3, 5).
- One reviewer found the improvements fairly significant (Reviewer 4), while others found them marginal (Reviewers 1, 5) – this contrast is noted.

### Weaknesses
- **Riemannian AdamW not well‑supported.** It is only used in the hierarchical metric learning experiment; there is no ablation comparing it to Riemannian Adam or SGD, and theoretical justification is lacking (Reviewers 1, 5). The adaptation (centroid‑based momentum/variance) deviates from standard parallel‑transport approaches, raising convergence and stability concerns (Reviewer 5). The use of exp/log maps and Levi‑Civita transport instead of retractions/vector transport is not justified (Reviewer 2). The paper also omits comparison with the trivialized AdamW from Kim et al. (2023), which allows direct Euclidean optimizers (Reviewer 2).
- **Curvature optimization method is confusing.** Lines 170‑185: it is unclear why data must be transported after updating curvature rather than before, or why it cannot remain unchanged. Theoretical analysis of which geometric properties are preserved is missing (Reviewer 2). The updating via a single tangent space appears expedient but not principled (Reviewer 2).
- **Maximum distance rescaling motivation is unclear.** It is not explained why projection onto the hyperboloid (as done in geoopt) is insufficient for numerical stability, nor why the specific scaling term is needed. The limit derivation is not shown step‑by‑step; a visualization would help (Reviewer 4). The authors do not compare against simpler alternatives like gradient clipping or norm bounding (Reviewer 5).
- **Lorentz convolutional layer lacks motivation and clarity.** Geometric properties (e.g., equivariance) are not discussed. The contributions relative to prior work are unclear, and the layer is not clearly compatible with existing 2D convolution implementations. Lorentz transformations (boosts, rotations, Cayley transform) are not explained in the background; the use of Stiefel manifolds is introduced without justification (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Lorentz‑Core Bottleneck Block** is not clearly used in any experiment and lacks an ablation study (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental improvements are marginal.** Gains compared to baselines in Tables 1, 2, 3 are often small, and some numbers labeled as bolded in Table 1 are not actually better (Reviewer 1). Self‑ablations show only incremental improvements, making it difficult to assess the individual contribution of each component (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited to small datasets.** All experiments are on small‑scale datasets (e.g., not ImageNet‑100/1000), reducing generality and raising questions about computational costs preventing larger experiments (Reviewers 2, 3). Implementation details (hyperparameters) are insufficient (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing theoretical insights.** There is no rigorous theoretical justification for any of the proposed modifications (e.g., bounding method, convergence of RAdamW) (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Notation and presentation are inconsistent and unclear.** Examples: the definition of $\mathcal{L}^n$ has the squared norm not equal to $-K$; Equation 4 missing brackets; $D(x,\mathbf{\overline{0}})_{max}^K$ notation is ambiguous; $k$ and $K$ are confused; the form of the centroid $(\sqrt{K},0)$ is unexplained (Reviewer 4). Algorithm 1 is cluttered; the meaning of $v_i$ in the centroid (should be in simplex) and $x_t$ in L129 are unclear (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Missing ablations and comparisons.** The effect of per‑layer curvature vs. shared curvature, the cost of the parameter‑moving step in Algorithm 1, the effect of clipping parameters instead of rescaling, and visualizations of training curves are all absent (Reviewer 4). A comparison of the bounding method to simpler norm‑bounding or gradient clipping is missing (Reviewer 5). The acronyms HECNN and HCNN are not defined; the fixed‑curvature baseline is not specified (Reviewer 4).
