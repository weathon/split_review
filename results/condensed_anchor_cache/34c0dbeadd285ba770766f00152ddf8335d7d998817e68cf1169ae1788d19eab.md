## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a cross-domain knowledge distillation method for person ReID, aligning an image-only pretrained student model with CLIP textual features via an adapter and PCA to handle dimension mismatches, optimized with KL-divergence and standard ReID losses. Reviewers’ scores split (3,5,3,5), with Reviewers 2 and 4 rating higher (5) but still raising significant concerns, while Reviewers 1 and 3 rated lower (3). Common weaknesses across all reviewers: unclear motivation for distilling CLIP-ReID into a unimodal student instead of using CLIP-ReID directly, limited innovation (widely used techniques), missing experimental details (L_rkd formula, reference for FKD, “This is a dog” citation, symbol inconsistencies), and inadequate analysis of why improvements occur (e.g., whether from textual knowledge or stronger visual encoder). Strengths noted include the adapter/PCA strategy for dimension alignment and comprehensive backbone evaluations.

### Strengths
- **Flexible dimension alignment strategy**: The use of a trainable adapter and PCA to handle mismatched feature dimensions between teacher and student is a practical solution for diverse architectures (Reviewer 1).  
- **Broad backbone compatibility**: The method demonstrates effectiveness across multiple backbones (TinyViT, OSNet, Solider), showing versatility (Reviewer 1).  
- **Clear paper structure**: The paper is generally well-organized and logical (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- **Comprehensive experimental design**: Multiple datasets and backbones are tested, with ablation studies analyzing the role of different loss functions (Reviewer 2).  
- **Novel perspective on cross-domain distillation**: Adapting CLIP-ReID to guide lightweight student models is a relatively new attempt in ReID (Reviewer 2).  

**Disagreement on innovation**: Reviewer 1 considers the adapter and PCA strategy a strength for flexibility; however, Reviewers 2 and 3 explicitly state the techniques are widely used (PCA, adapter, distillation) and the improvements are routine, lacking a truly novel network architecture or method. Reviewer 2 adds that the main work is essentially aligning text and image features, which is incremental.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation**: All reviewers question why knowledge from CLIP-ReID should be distilled to a unimodal student rather than using CLIP-ReID directly. Reviewers 1 and 4 specifically ask: what practical benefits does this transfer bring? For a smaller student, what advantages does the teacher provide beyond a stronger visual encoder? For a larger student, is the transfer necessary? The paper fails to justify why this distillation is preferred over direct use of CLIP-ReID (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
- **Inadequate justification of textual knowledge role**: When the student (e.g., TinyViT) improves significantly, it is unclear whether the gain comes from cross-domain textual knowledge transfer or simply from the teacher having a more powerful visual encoder. An ablation controlling for the visual encoder strength is needed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing formula and explanation for L_rkd**: The loss term L_rkd is not defined; it is unclear where it is applied (before or after adapter) and how it is computed (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Missing references and citations**:  
  - “This is a dog” (Section 3.3.1, line 260) lacks a reference (Reviewer 1).  
  - “FKD” mentioned in line 268 does not have a citation (Reviewer 1).  
  - “L_{ckl}” and “L_{rkl}” in Section 4.3 are not defined or referenced (Reviewer 4).  
- **Writing quality issues**:  
  - Bad expressions exist (e.g., page 1, lines 46–48) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Inconsistent symbol definitions: feature dimensions for student and teacher differ between lines 221–223 and line 184 (Reviewer 2).  
  - Incorrect citations throughout (Reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 1 is confusing: meaning of dotted line, why “image feature memory bank” becomes “reduced textual feature memory bank” after PCA, and overall layout and aesthetics are unsatisfactory (Reviewer 4). Many “?” marks appear in Figure 1 (Reviewer 2).  
  - Tables and images are not well presented; Table 1 and Table 3 seem unnecessary (Reviewer 3).  
- **Confusion in method description**:  
  - Section 3.2: The process when d > d' vs. d ≤ d' is unclear—does PCA always precede the adapter only when d > d'? (Reviewer 1).  
  - Inference details missing: how is the teacher image encoder used during inference? (Reviewer 1).  
- **Incomplete experimentation and analysis**:  
  - Comparative experiments (Table 2) lack references to the most recent articles, making it hard to assess competitiveness (Reviewer 3).  
  - Table 4 requires deeper explanation of why each component is effective (Reviewer 3).  
  - Results are not deeply analyzed: only performance numbers are listed but root causes of advantages/disadvantages over other methods are not explored (Reviewer 2).  
  - When the student backbone shares the same structure as the CLIP-ReID visual encoder, the effect of transferring textual knowledge vs. not transferring it is not reported (Reviewer 4).  
  - Figure 3 shows TinyViT-KD highlights body regions, yet its retrieval performance is inferior to CLIP-ReID—this discrepancy is not explained (Reviewer 4).  
- **Choice of student models not justified**: The reasons for selecting TinyViT (11M), OSNet, and Solider (Tiny, Small, Base) as students are not explained (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limitations of dimension handling**: The proposed adapter/PCA approach may have limitations for different dimension relationships not fully explored (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient content richness**: The paper’s typesetting seems unreasonable and the content is not sufficiently rich (Reviewer 2).
