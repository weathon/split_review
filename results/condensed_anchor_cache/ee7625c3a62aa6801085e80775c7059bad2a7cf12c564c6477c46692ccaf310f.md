## Merged Review

### Summary

This work proposes an optimization algorithm called CASPR, derived from the Shampoo optimizer by using a Kronecker-sum approximation to combine axes preconditioners. The authors provide theoretical convergence guarantees showing a tighter bound than Shampoo, and empirically demonstrate improvements over Shampoo and Adam(W) on several large models, including transformers and graph neural networks (OGBG-molpcba, universal dependencies dataset, C4 language modeling).

### Strengths

- Strong and clear development of the method, with a well-explained motivation for the Kronecker-sum precondition, the differences between CASPR and Shampoo updates, and a comparison with diagonal Adagrad statistics (Figure 1).
- Theoretical convergence guarantees are provided and are tighter than those of Shampoo in an online learning setting; the theoretical development of Shampoo is extended to CASPR.
- Experiments are performed on challenging tasks (transformers and graph neural networks) against Shampoo and Adam(W), which are the main competitors in the literature. The experiments suggest CASPR improves over Shampoo and potentially also other baselines.
- Comprehensive appendix is included.
- Overall, a solid piece of work with strong theoretical and empirical results.

### Weaknesses

- The abstract and introduction could be written more clearly: they state what is achieved but not how (e.g., the technique used to obtain the faster convergence guarantee).
- The empirical evaluation is limited: only compared to Shampoo and Adam(W); a broader comparison to other optimizers would strengthen validation. Moreover, no error bars are reported – figures appear to come from a single run, and the absence of multiple seeds makes it difficult to assess statistical significance and consistency of improvements. It is also unclear what target was used for hyperparameter tuning (validation accuracy?) and how many hyperparameters were optimized per method.
- The applicability of CASPR to layers that are not fully-connected (e.g., convolutional or recurrent) is not elaborated upon; the paper primarily focuses on fully-connected layers.
- The time and space complexity of the coupled Newton algorithm used for computing the matrix inverse p-root should be discussed.
- Numerical stability needs explicit discussion: (1) how to choose the damping term $\epsilon$, and the coupled Newton algorithm’s sensitivity to it; (2) whether the method works well in low numerical precision (float32 or bfloat16).
- There is no evaluation on computer vision tasks such as ResNets or Vision Transformers (ViTs), which are important benchmarks in the literature.
- The impact of introducing momentum for $L$ and $R$ matrices (for fair comparison with other optimizers) is not analyzed; experiments without momentum should be reported.
- A reviewer notes that it is unclear whether the per-row second-moments compute a neuron-level conditioner (see Fig. 1 of https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04684.pdf for “unit-wise” preconditioning).
- Minor writing issues: (1) The sentence on the first page “…to be at one end of this sequence” is confusing. (2) Typos: Lemma 3.1 Frobenius norm is missing the square (the 2nd power is missing); on page 5, when rewriting the preconditioner $X_t^{caspr}(1)$ in terms of $X_t^L$ and $X_t^R$, the matrices $L$ and $R$ should have tildes based on the definitions four lines above.
