- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 5, 8, 8, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the adaptive (parallel) complexity of sampling from log-concave distributions \(e^{-f}\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with zeroth-order oracle access to \(f\). For unconstrained sampling with log-smooth or log-Lipschitz and log-strongly or non-strongly concave \(f\), it shows a lower bound of \(\tilde{\Omega}(d)\) sequential rounds to achieve exponentially small accuracy in total variation distance. For box-constrained sampling on the unit cube, it shows an \(\tilde{\Omega}(d)\) lower bound for inverse polynomial/super-polynomial accuracy. These are the first lower bounds for adaptive sampling in these settings, leveraging techniques from adaptive complexity in optimization and information-theoretic arguments. The lower bounds are within a polynomial of known upper bounds (e.g., Anari et al.'s \(O(\log^2(d/\epsilon))\) parallel algorithm).

### Strengths
- The paper is the first to provide lower bounds for adaptive (parallel) sampling applicable to a wide range of distributions (log-concave, log-smooth, log-Lipschitz) in both unconstrained and box-constrained settings. It makes solid progress toward understanding the optimal parallel query complexity in the high-accuracy regime. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The lower bound construction is a nice blend of techniques from adaptive complexity in submodular optimization and the information-theoretic approach of Chewi et al. (Reviewer 4)
- The lower bounds are of interest to both theoreticians and practitioners, particularly given recent interest in parallel sampling methods for diffusion models. (Reviewer 2)
- The comparison to previous upper bounds is clearly presented (e.g., a table), and the results improve over prior lower bounds. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper obtains a linear-in-\(d\) lower bound even in the relatively low-accuracy regime (\(1/d^{\omega(1)}\) error) for box-constrained sampling, which is notable. (Reviewer 4)
- Reviewer 2 found the paper generally well-written and easy to understand, and recommended acceptance. (contrast to weaknesses on writing)

### Weaknesses
Several reviewers identified weaknesses in writing clarity, completeness of comparisons, and the scope/conceptual depth of the results. The scores range from 5 to 8; Reviewer 1 was less positive, emphasizing that the paper only contains negative results (lower bounds) that are not tight for any parameter regime and that the writing needs improvement.

**Writing and presentation issues:**
- The paper contains typos/grammar errors (e.g., “minimal” → “minimum”, “sup-polynomially” → “sub-polynomially”, “small accuracy” → “small error”, “total variance” → “total variation”, unclear phrasing “small accuracy” meaning high accuracy). (Reviewer 1)
- Section 3 incorrectly describes the reduction direction (states reduction from sampling to hypothesis testing instead of the correct direction from hypothesis testing to sampling) at lines 225-226, 235, 269, 274. (Reviewer 1)
- The comparison to previous lower bounds is less clear than the upper-bound comparison; a side-by-side table of lower bounds across different regimes (accuracy, assumptions) would be helpful. (Reviewer 3)
- The difference between adaptive complexity and query complexity is not sufficiently explained; it would be useful to clarify which concept the improvements are over. (Reviewer 3)
- Several notations and terminology in the proofs are unclear: Lemma 4.3's “additive error \(O(t/2)\)” is ambiguous (later clarified as per-coordinate error; also why big-O with factor 1/2?); the definition of \(X_i(\mathbf{x}')\) seems mis-indexed; “concentration of conditioned Bernoullis” is confusing (better: “concentration of linear functions over the Boolean slice”); when introducing “partition \(\mathcal{P} = (P_1,\ldots,P_{r+1})\) over the ground set”, it is not immediately clear that the ground set refers to coordinates. (Reviewer 4)

**Missing discussions and comparisons:**
- The paper does not discuss or cite recent works on parallel sampling for diffusion models, which would increase its appeal to practitioners. (Reviewer 2)
- There is no thorough description of the barriers to extending the techniques to the low-accuracy regime, nor any conjectures toward such results. (Reviewer 2)
- It is unclear in what specific settings/regimes the lower bounds improve over prior lower bounds (accuracy levels, assumptions, by how much). Also unclear whether the improvement is for first-order oracles or only for zeroth-order oracles. (Reviewer 3)

**Conceptual depth and limitations:**
- The paper only provides negative results (lower bounds), and these bounds are not tight for any parameter regime (the upper bounds are higher-degree polynomials). (Reviewer 1)
- The near-linear lower bound in the unconstrained setting may be an artifact of the high-accuracy regime (exponentially small error) rather than a fundamental dimension dependence; the true round complexity might be \(\tilde{\Theta}(\log(1/\epsilon))\). The box-constrained argument is almost verbatim the same as the unconstrained one, suggesting the linear lower bound there is a quirk of constrained setting rather than a deep insight into dimension dependence. (Reviewer 4)
- Reviewer 1 also asked about the possibility to close the gap between upper and lower bounds in some parameter regime; this overlaps with the concern that bounds are not tight. (Reviewer 1)
