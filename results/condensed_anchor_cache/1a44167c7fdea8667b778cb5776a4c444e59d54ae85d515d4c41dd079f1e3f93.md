## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a pre-trained time series model with adaptive segmentation for cross-domain tasks. The approach uses a Transformer encoder with dataset-specific segmentation modules to produce variable-length patches, trained via masked reconstruction. Reviews are mixed: two reviewers (scores 5) found the idea promising and the empirical results decent, while three reviewers (scores 3) raised substantial concerns about novelty, missing baselines, unclear notation, incomplete experimental reporting, and the validity of the cross-domain evaluation. The overall assessment is that the paper has merit but requires major improvements.

### Strengths
- **Originality and motivation**: The attempt to pre-train a single time series model on diverse domains is highly original and timely, potentially enabling knowledge transfer similar to NLP/vision. Two reviewers specifically highlighted this as a first-of-its-kind effort. (R1, R2)
- **Adaptive segmentation idea**: The variable-length segment extraction (patch scoring and pruning) is a creative and technically feasible contribution, and ablation studies confirm its effectiveness. (R1, R3, R5)
- **Clarity and structure** (partial agreement): One reviewer found the paper clearly structured and the technical details well-articulated. However, other reviewers strongly disagreed (see Weaknesses). (R1 vs. R2, R3, R4, R5)
- **Empirical scope**: The method is evaluated on both forecasting and classification tasks, across multiple datasets, and compared against some domain-specific baselines. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Reproducibility intent**: The code is publicly available. (R1, R2; but noted as incomplete by R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty** (raised by three reviewers): The core design is seen as a straightforward extension of PatchTST with an added patch scoring function (Eq. 2). Prior work like SimMTM already explores masked patch reconstruction. The architecture is a simple Transformer encoder without sophisticated temporal modeling (e.g., recurrent layers, attention with temporal bias). (R1, R5)

2. **Missing crucial baselines**: Several state-of-the-art methods are absent, making it hard to position the paper. Missing forecasting baselines: PatchTST, TimesNet, FEDformer, AutoARIMA, AutoTHETA, AutoETS, Naive, N-HITS, N-BEATS, CoST. Missing classification baselines: ROCKET, COTE variants. Missing baselines for specific datasets: STGNN, StemGNN, GraphWaveNet (for traffic datasets), and more. (R1, R2, R5)

3. **Ill-defined experimental settings / cross-domain validity**: The evaluation datasets substantially overlap with the pre-training source domains, undermining the claim of cross-domain adaptation. The authors do not evaluate on entirely unseen domains from different sources (e.g., healthcare, industrial). The “multi-domain” claim is weakened by using a separate segmentation module for each dataset, limiting generalization. (R1, R5)

4. **Notation and writing errors**: Many variables are undefined or used before definition (e.g., \(g(i,j)\) in Eq. 4; \(h(i)\) in Fig. 1). Taking the log of the SSL loss in Eq. 4 may cause unstable gradients if the loss approaches zero. The meaning of “how good” in the segment scoring function is never defined. The abstract should be a single paragraph. (R2, R3, R4, R5)

5. **Incomplete reproducibility details** (three reviewers): Critical hyperparameters are missing – the masking proportion \(\gamma\), the number/length/ranges/channels of pre-training and evaluation time series, normalization scheme, and compute infrastructure (GPU/CPU type, wall-clock vs. CPU time). The model size (layers, heads, embedding dimension) is not stated. The provided code lacks training/prediction scripts and has an empty README. (R2, R4, R5)

6. **Claims insufficiently justified**:
   - The claim that individual timestamps lack semantic meaning is unsupported and the analogy to NLP is not well-considered. (R5)
   - Variable-length segmentation is compared only against timestep-level tokens, not against fixed-length patches (PatchTST style), so the contribution over uniform segmentation is unproven. (R2)
   - Discussion of masking strategies (random vs. last-token) is superficial, with no deeper analysis of their impact on representations. (R5)

7. **Limited analysis of the method**:
   - No visualization or statistical analysis of learned segment boundaries (lengths, variance) across datasets. (R1)
   - Ablation studies only remove components; no addition of other SSL tasks (contrastive learning, predictive coding). (R1)
   - Hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., segment score thresholds) is not explored. (R1)
   - No theoretical justification for why pre-training or adaptive segmentation helps. (R1)
   - The aggregation function in Section 3.2 is not specified. (R3)
   - The combinatorial optimization problem of optimal segmentation is mentioned but not elaborated or assessed. (R3)

8. **Zero-shot and generalization limitations**: The dataset-specific segmentation module prevents zero-shot inference on new domains, and no zero-shot forecasting/classification results are provided. For small datasets, a large pre-trained model might underfit compared to a smaller model trained from scratch – no model size ablation is done. (R2, R4)

9. **Narrow evaluation scope**:
   - Tested only on forecasting and classification; no experiments on anomaly detection, imputation, or other tasks. (R1)
   - Only a few evaluation metrics reported (e.g., MAE, sMAPE missing). (R4)
   - Comparisons to domain-specific models using additional covariates (weather, economics) are absent. (R1)
   - Handling of out-of-distribution samples from unseen domains during pre-training is not addressed; the scoring function is trained on specific domains. (R5)

10. **Minor issues**: Societal impacts or limitations not discussed. Capitalization and citation formatting errors (e.g., disease names, \citep vs. \citet). (R1, R2)
