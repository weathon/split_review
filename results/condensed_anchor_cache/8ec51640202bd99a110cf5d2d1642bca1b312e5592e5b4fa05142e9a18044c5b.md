## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes fine-tuning ProteinMPNN with two enhancements to Direct Preference Optimization (online diversity regularization and domain-specific priors) to improve sequence diversity and structural consistency in peptide inverse folding. Experiments on CATH4.3 and an OpenFold-based benchmark claim state-of-the-art TM-Score improvements (>8%) and up to 20% higher diversity over standard DPO.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- It identifies that popular inverse folding models perform poorly on peptide design (Reviewer 1).
- Applying DPO to inverse folding and fine-tuning graph-based models is an interesting and innovative concept (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Leveraging ColabFold and the OpenFold database to address the scarcity of high-quality peptide structures is promising (Reviewer 3).
- The online diversity formulation and the inclusion of differential entropy are inspiring improvements over standard diversity-regularized objectives (Reviewer 3).
- Integrating structural information into training via a reward that maximises TM-Score and the design of the reward/loss function are interesting approximations (Reviewer 2).
- The experiment is comprehensive with respect to analysis metrics (Reviewer 4).
- The motivation is clear and reasonable, and the challenges addressed are attractive (Reviewer 4).
- The paper applies DPO with the stated goal of improving diversity (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of the DPO modifications.** The use of diversity regularization and scalar weightings in DPO has been extensively studied in prior work, e.g., ProteinDPO fine-tuned ESM-IF with similar ideas. The proposed differences (online optimization, ad-hoc scalar weighting) are incremental and need clearer separation from existing methods (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Insufficient baselines and missing recent comparisons.**  
  - No comparison with ProteinDPO (Reviewer 1).  
  - Missing fixed-backbone models specific to peptide design such as PepFlow (Reviewer 3).  
  - Missing general inverse folding methods that often outperform ProteinMPNN on recovery/self-consistency TM-Score, e.g., PiFold, ESM-IF, LM-Design, VFN, InstructPLM (Reviewer 4).  
  - No comparison with newer approaches from ProteinInvBench that are readily available (Reviewer 2).  
  - The paper only compares variations of ProteinMPNN, not methods with different architectures (Reviewer 4).  
- **Unfair/insufficient baseline training and evaluation.**  
  - GVP-GNN was not trained on the OpenFold benchmark, so comparing test-set scores is unfair (Reviewer 3).  
  - Sampling settings are unusual: only 4 samples at T=0 (deterministic), whereas ProteinMPNN defaults to T=0.1. This may underestimate diversity and fairness (Reviewer 3).  
  - It is unclear whether baselines are used as pre-trained models or fine-tuned, affecting comparability (Reviewer 3).  
- **Marginal improvements in key metrics.**  
  - In Table 1, the DPO fine-tuned model does not achieve significantly better diversity than vanilla ProteinMPNN (Reviewer 1). The reported diversity increase is smaller than or equal to 1% except TM-Score (6%); recovery and diversity improvements are not convincing (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
  - Even after fine-tuning, ProteinMPNN+DPO is worse than ESM-IF in TM-Score (Table 2), suggesting the method may not be state-of-the-art (Reviewer 1).  
- **Incomplete evaluation metrics.** Self-consistency RMSD is missing; TM-Score alone does not capture all aspects of structural similarity, especially local deviations (Reviewer 4).  
- **Bias from TM-score-based training.** Using TM-Score from a single static structure predictor may introduce bias; the paper should discuss this and ideally average TM-Scores from multiple prediction tools (Reviewer 2).  
- **Dataset filtering inconsistency and lack of clarity.**  
  - The filtering process for the OpenFold dataset (n=50) is not described clearly; criteria differ from CATH4.3 (n=173), making cross-benchmark comparisons questionable (Reviewer 4).  
  - No statistical analysis of the training dataset is provided (Reviewer 2).  
- **Unclear or missing ablation studies.** The ablation does not isolate the effect of each DPO component (e.g., online diversity vs. scalar weighting vs. domain priors). Including the original diversity-regularised DPO and scalar-weighted DPO as baselines is recommended (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Entropy-based diversity regularization is introduced in Section 4.4 but not presented in the main results.** It should be evaluated and compared to the pairwise-distance based regularizer (Reviewer 3).  
- **Theoretical analysis in Section 4.4 is confusing and appears out of place** (Reviewer 3).  
- **Approach appears target-specific and does not generalise well to co-optimisation** (Reviewer 2). The paper should test on general proteins to demonstrate applicability, since the same challenges exist there (Reviewer 4).  
- **Questions requiring clarification or additional experiments:**  
  - Why does ESM-IF show zero diversity on both datasets? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Why are different filtering processes used for the two benchmark datasets? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Can the connection between log-probabilities and diversity in Section 4.4 be supported by a mathematical proof? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Should ESM-IF be fine-tuned with the same DPO algorithm to see if benefits transfer? (Reviewer 1)  
- **Reviewer disagreement.** Reviewer 2 was more positive (score 6), while Reviewer 4 was significantly more negative (score 3), reflecting a split on whether the improvements and novelty are sufficient.
