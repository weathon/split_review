## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DEFD-PSGD, a decentralized learning algorithm that applies compressed model updates with an error-feedback mechanism to handle both high gradient compression and data heterogeneity. The authors claim a convergence upper bound under relaxed assumptions and present experiments showing advantages over DCD-PSGD and CHOCO-PSGD.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of design (R1)**: DEFD-PSGD synchronizes model parameters via compressed model updates, unlike prior works [Koloskova et al., 2019] which consider compressed model gossip. The core idea of introducing controllable residual error feedback to balance compression and heterogeneity effects is new.
- **Key challenge identified (R3)**: The paper identifies a genuine gap: existing algorithms struggle to simultaneously manage a high degree of gradient compression and significant data heterogeneity.
- **Algorithm simplicity (R2)**: The algorithm is simple and clear.
- **Theoretical rate (R2)**: The theoretical convergence rate matches D-PSGD.
- **Empirical improvement (R1, R2)**: In the provided experiments, DEFD-PSGD performs better than the baselines DCD-PSGD and CHOCO-PSGD, especially under high compression and heterogeneity.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption of both unbiased and contractive compressors (R2, R3)**: The analysis requires compressors to be simultaneously unbiased *and* contractive with bounded compression error \(\beta < 1\). Very few practical compressors satisfy both. E.g., random sparsification is unbiased but \(\beta = d/k - 1 \gg 1\); random quantization is unbiased with \(\beta > 2\); natural compression has \(\beta = 9/8 > 1\) (R3). Standard practice in the literature (e.g., [C2]) assumes *either* unbiasedness *or* contractiveness, but not both. This assumption severely limits practical applicability and contradicts the paper’s goal of supporting high compression (R2, R3).
- **Restricted range of \(\beta\) (R1, R3)**: The convergence guarantee holds only for a restricted range of \(\beta\) (the relative error of the contractive compressor). It is unclear why the analysis cannot be extended to the full range \(\beta \in (0,1)\) (R1). The coefficient \(\beta\) is a fixed constant determined by the compressor (e.g., random sparsification yields \(\beta > 1\)); the paper’s assumption that \(\beta\) can be made arbitrarily small is unjustified (R3). This restriction weakens the theoretical contribution compared to existing methods that cover all \(\beta\) (R1).
- **Bounded gradient divergence contradicts data heterogeneity goal (R2)**: The analysis assumes bounded gradient divergence, which effectively requires local gradients to be similar. This contradicts the aim of handling high data heterogeneity. Error-feedback is typically used to mitigate compression, not to enforce small heterogeneity; this assumption limits the algorithm to settings it was purportedly designed to overcome.
- **Outdated and insufficient baselines (R2, R3)**: Comparisons are made only with DCD-PSGD and CHOCO-PSGD, both from 2019 or earlier. More advanced decentralized compressed algorithms such as CEDAS (2023), BEER (Zhao et al. 2022, [1]), DoCoM (Yau & Wai 2022, [2]), and algorithms listed in Table 1 of [C3] already outperform CHOCO-PSGD by a large margin. Without comparisons to these, claiming “outperforms other state-of-the-art” is unsupported (R2). R1 also requests comparison with BEER and DoCoM on heterogeneous data.
- **Ambiguous comparison with CHOCO-SGD/CHOCO-PSGD (R1)**: 
  - The paper refers to “CHOCO-PSGD” but does not formally prove its equivalence to the known CHOCO-SGD algorithm (Koloskova et al. 2020, [3]); a proof of equivalence is required (R1).
  - The claim that “CHOCO-PSGD may diverge under high data heterogeneity” (line 389) lacks theoretical support – the CHOCO-SGD analysis in [3] only assumes bounded stochastic gradients and does not impose any data heterogeneity assumption, so it should converge for any degree of heterogeneity (R1).
  - The paper’s Figure 1 shows CHOCO-SGD diverging, but Figure 2 in [2] shows only slow convergence on heterogeneous MNIST, not divergence. More experiments under the same setup as [2] with proper parameter tuning are needed to substantiate the divergence claim (R1).
- **Convergence rate lacks clarity on network topology and compression effects (R3)**: The presented convergence rate (Eq. 10, Corollaries) does not explicitly characterize how the network topology (e.g., spectral gap) and the compression factor \(\beta\) influence the rate. In contrast, works like [C3] make these dependencies clear. Without such characterization, it is hard to see advantages over existing rates (R3). R1 also asks for a consensus error bound to compare with e.g., Theorem 4 of Lian et al. 2017.
- **High memory requirement makes algorithm impractical (R3)**: Algorithm 1 requires each node to store the model weights \(\{x^{i,j}\}_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i}\) for all its neighbors (see lines 4 and 7). For large models (e.g., 175B parameters >350 GB), storing multiple copies is prohibitively memory-intensive. Most existing compressed decentralized algorithms (DCD-PSGD, Choco-SGD, [C1–C3]) avoid this requirement. This is a major practical limitation.
- **Weak experimental validation (R1, R3)**:
  - The compressors used in experiments (top‑k, random quantization) do **not** satisfy the assumed unbiasedness and contractive conditions, undermining the link between theory and experiments (R3).
  - CIFAR‑10 results show test accuracy below 70%, which is unusually low; typical accuracy with similar architectures is above 90% (R3).
  - R1 suggests presenting results with varying levels of data heterogeneity (different Dirichlet parameters \(\alpha\)) on the same plot to demonstrate how each algorithm reacts, and to provide insights on how DEFD‑PSGD’s parameter \(\epsilon\) affects the heterogeneity error in the bound.
- **Minor issues (R1)**:
  - In equations (2), (6), (10), \(\nabla f(\frac{X_t \mathbf{1}_n}{n})\) should be \(\|\nabla f(\frac{X_t \mathbf{1}_n}{n})\|^2\).
  - Constants \(a,b,c,\mu\) are not defined before or in Theorem 1.
  - Typo “neightbors” → “neighbors” in line 243.
  - The nonconvex convergence analysis of CHOCO-SGD in [Koloskova et al., 2019] only covers convex objectives; the correct reference for nonconvex CHOCO-SGD is Koloskova et al. 2020 [3] (R1).
- **Missing formal proof of equivalence for comparison (R1)**: The paper’s “Comparison between DEFD-PSGD and CHOCO-PSGD” is confusing without a formal proof that Algorithm A.1 is equivalent to CHOCO-SGD. R1 notes that a similar comparison of error‑feedback schemes appears in Richtárik et al. 2021 [4], but the equivalence must be established explicitly.

**Minority reviewer (R1)** is more positive than R2 and R3 (score 3 vs. 1,1) but still lists important weaknesses. R1 acknowledges the novelty and experiments but questions the theoretical restrictions, missing comparisons, and incomplete justification.
