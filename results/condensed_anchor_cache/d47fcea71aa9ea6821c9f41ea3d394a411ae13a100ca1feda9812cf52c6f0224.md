## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Physics-aligned Schrödinger Bridge (PalSB), a method for reconstructing physical fields from sparse measurements using a diffusion bridge mechanism. The approach has two training stages: first, a Schrödinger bridge (following the I²SB approach) is trained on local patches to map sparse to high-resolution data; second, physics-aligned finetuning with physically informed and regression losses improves compliance with governing equations. Boundary-aware sampling pads inputs to enforce boundary conditions, and early-stop sampling reduces steps. The method is tested on three 2D complex systems: cylinder flow (PIV experiments), 2D turbulence, and a reaction-diffusion system. Results show higher accuracy and better physical constraint compliance than baselines.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge of reconstructing physical fields from sparse measurements, with potential high impact in applied sciences.
- Effectively combines a diffusion Schrödinger bridge (I²SB) with a finetuning step inspired by RLHF for diffusion models.
- Convincingly demonstrated across three datasets, each with two tasks, and compared against several reasonable baselines (interpolation, end-to-end models, PIDM).
- Ablation study confirms the value of the design choices (physics-aligned finetuning, boundary-aware sampling).
- Early-stop sampling is a potentially useful technique beyond physical field reconstruction.
- Method is well-chosen for the problem and clearly presented (only minor clarifications needed).
- PalSB significantly reduces physical inconsistencies and improves compliance with spatial patterns in both Fourier and real space interpolation problems.
- Integrating physical constraints (boundary conditions and equations) at the finetuning stage minimizes mismatches with real physical laws.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited methodological novelty** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not present major new methodological contributions; some techniques (e.g., early-stop sampling) feel ad hoc and are not interesting methodologically as such. *Note: Reviewers 1 and 3 considered the method well-chosen and effective, but Reviewer 2 found the contributions insufficient.*
- **Experimental validation scope** (Reviewer 2, partially echoed by Reviewer 3): Only 2D problems with fairly limited datasets are tested. A more extensive validation (e.g., 3D problems, larger datasets) is needed to demonstrate power given the limited methodological contributions. Reviewer 3 additionally recommends testing on electrostatic equations (not just Kolmogorov and reaction-diffusion).
- **Metrics choice** (Reviewer 3): Only normalized error is reported. Reviewer 3 recommends additionally using MSE and L1 error, plus a residual error metric that directly measures how well the predicted field satisfies the governing equations.
- **Missing recommended comparison** (Reviewer 3): The paper should consider combining with or comparing to the approach in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14404.
- **Derivation clarity** (Reviewer 1): The derivation of Eq. (6) from Eq. (4) is not clear; it appears to yield \( f_\theta + x_0 \) instead. The relationship between variables \( m, n, d \) (line 083-085) is ambiguously defined.
- **Backpropagation truncation & step dependency** (Reviewer 1): In the finetuning stage, backpropagation is truncated for all steps \( i < N \) (as per Fig. 1 and Alg. 2). Does this affect adherence to physical constraints in a time-step-dependent manner? How does this interact with early-stop sampling? (Appendix C1 alludes to this but needs clarification.)
- **Early-stop sampling and finetuning interaction** (Reviewer 1): Does the finetuning step itself use early-stop sampling?
- **Padding details** (Reviewer 1): The amount of padding used for boundary-aware sampling and its influence on performance are not quantified. Does the padding amount interact with the number of sampling steps?
- **Algorithm errors** (Reviewer 1): Algorithm 2 (line 7): “while \( j = T \)” should be “while \( j < T \)”. Algorithm 3 (line 11): \( t_j \) is not defined; line 12: \( t \) should be \( t_j \).
- **Minor presentation issues** (Reviewer 1): Typographical errors: line 065 “such diffusion bridge” → “such a diffusion bridge”; missing year in Fan et al. reference; line 329 wording about resolution vs. acquisition impossibility; line 883 loss equation reference should be Eq. (6) not (4); line 1063 “observation mentioned above” is vague; captions of Fig. 10+11 say “noisy” should be “noise-free”.
