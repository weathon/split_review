## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Incremental Aggregated Asynchronous SGD (IA$^2$SGD), an asynchronous distributed optimization algorithm that uses a server-side buffer of stale gradients from all workers to neutralize data heterogeneity. The authors provide convergence guarantees for smooth nonconvex objectives without assuming bounded data dissimilarity, and present numerical experiments on CIFAR-10 with CNN training under Dirichlet-based heterogeneity. The reviews are mixed: two reviewers (R1=6, R4=5) acknowledge the theoretical contribution but raise concerns about step-size restrictions and experimental results; one reviewer (R2=5) argues the algorithm is essentially SAGA with delay and novelty is limited; the most critical reviewer (R3=3) finds the theory impractical, the delay assumption restrictive, and missing key comparisons with methods that relax bounded delays.

### Strengths
- IA$^2$SGD achieves a convergence rate for smooth nonconvex problems that does **not** rely on a bounded data heterogeneity assumption – a clear departure from many prior asynchronous SGD analyses (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The convergence rate enjoys a speedup in the number of workers $n$ (R1, R4).
- The algorithm is novel in its use of stale historical gradients and a server-side buffer to mitigate heterogeneity; the problem of heterogeneous distributed learning is practically relevant (R2, R3).
- The paper is well-written with clear visuals, and the theoretical result’s independence of heterogeneity is highlighted as an important strength (R3).
- Numerical experiments on CIFAR-10 with Dirichlet-based data allocation demonstrate the algorithm’s performance in a realistic setting (R2, R4).
- The approach borrows ideas from Incremental Gradient Descent (IGD) and maintains per-iteration cost on par with standard asynchronous SGD (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Step-size analysis is overly conservative.** The stepsize $\eta = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{n (F(w^0)-F^*)}{L \tau_{\max} T}}$ depends on unknown quantities ($F(w^0)-F^*$, $L$, $\tau_{\max}$) and is inversely proportional to $\tau_{\max}$. In a deterministic setting this would force a very small step size compared to conventional distributed optimization, raising concerns about practical convergence speed. The paper does **not** compare its step-size choice with works like Freya PAGE that achieve larger steps in that regime (R1, R3, R4). Specifically, if the maximum delay $\tau_{\max} \sim T$ (i.e., a worker responds only once during training), the theory shows no convergence, while some prior algorithms still converge (R4).
- **Theorem 1 has a restrictive lower bound on $T$.** The bound $T \geq 1024 L (F(w^0)-F^*) n \tau_{\max}$ is required regardless of desired accuracy, scales linearly with $n$ and $\tau_{\max}$, and depends on the initial suboptimality – making the theory appear weak for realistic setups (R3, R4). The lower bound on $T$ from Theorem 1 is not needed for the rate derivation but still appears as a condition (R4).
- **Algorithm novelty is limited; IA$^2$SGD is very similar to SAGA.** The core update mechanism (correction term based on past gradients) is essentially the same as SAGA, with the only differences being that the participating worker is chosen with a bounded delay ($\tau_{\max}$) and gradients themselves may be delayed. Converting SAGA’s analysis to this delayed setting does not introduce fundamentally new theoretical challenges (R2). The proof technique is also described as resembling FedAvg’s, with $\tau_{\max}$ playing a role analogous to the number of local updates (R1).
- **Experiments do not show clear improvement over baselines.** In all cases of Figure 3, IA$^2$SGD performs similarly to FedBuff and vanilla ASGD, even under high heterogeneity; in some cases it matches vanilla ASGD. The lack of a consistent empirical advantage, despite the added algorithm complexity, weakens the paper’s contribution (R2, R4). Moreover, the accuracy is low (barely above 50% on CIFAR-10), and the empirical results indicate that IA$^2$SGD’s performance still degrades with increasing heterogeneity (accuracy decreases when heterogeneity increases), contradicting the theoretical claim of heterogeneity independence (R4).
- **Missing comparisons with related work that also avoids heterogeneity assumptions.** The paper fails to compare IA$^2$SGD with Malenia SGD (referred to as [1]) which achieves optimal time complexity under smoothness and bounded variance without requiring data similarity, and with methods like [2,3] that relax the bounded delay assumption (R3, R4). A thorough theoretical and empirical comparison with Malenia SGD is essential (R3, R4). The trade-off between assuming bounded delays (this paper) versus bounded data dissimilarity (some prior works) is not discussed – neither assumption is inherently superior, and the paper should address this (R3).
- **Memory overhead for $d$-dimensional vectors at both server and each worker** may limit applicability for memory-constrained clients, especially with high-dimensional models. The paper does not discuss server-side memory strategies to mitigate this (R3).
- **Several technical details need clarification:** (i) The origin of $\sigma$ in the denominator of the second term in Table 1’s convergence rate is unclear; it does not appear in Theorem 1 (R3, R4). (ii) The “dual-delayedness” concept seems misleading because workers process fresh data – sample delays already exist in standard ASGD (R3). (iii) Lines 108–109 appear to imply single-pass data usage; is that the case? (R3). (iv) If without delays $\tau_i(t)=1$, that would require restarting client computations, contradicting async operation – please explain (R3). (v) The need for two sequences $\tau_i(t)$ and $\rho_i(t)$ is not justified; isn’t there a bijection? (R4). (vi) In Figure 4, some variance bands (e.g., red shaded area) look incorrect – the dashed line sometimes falls outside the shaded area (R4). (vii) Uniform/shuffled ASGD performs worse than vanilla ASGD, which is surprising given that vanilla ASGD converges only to a neighborhood while uniform/shuffled should converge exactly (R4).
- **Minor concerns:** The analysis of IA$^2$SGD cannot recover a good deterministic rate (R1). The lower bound on $T$ makes the theory seem impractical even if not strictly required (R4).
