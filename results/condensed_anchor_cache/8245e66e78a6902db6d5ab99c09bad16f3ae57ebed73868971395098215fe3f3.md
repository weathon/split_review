## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Visual and Textual Intervention (VTI), a test-time method that precomputes shifting vectors from perturbed images and hallucinated/non-hallucinated captions and adds them to latent representations to reduce hallucinations. Reviewers agree the problem is significant, the method is intuitive and training-free, and experiments cover multiple benchmarks. However, concerns are raised about the explanation of representation stability via variance reduction, missing ablations on PCA vs. averaging, inconsistent decoding settings across comparisons, lack of generalization tests beyond hallucination, and insufficient detail on data sources. One reviewer (score 6, soundness 2) is more critical of the method’s validity and experimental fairness, while the other two (score 8, soundness 3) are more positive.

### Strengths
- The problem of hallucination in LVLMs is significant; VTI is intuitive, effective, and uses ideas from data augmentation applied at inference without retraining.
- Analysis is thorough: includes good ablation and discussion of impact on attention.
- Motivation and method are smoothly explained and easy to understand.
- Experiments cover different hallucination benchmarks and LVLMs with different vision encoders.
- As a task-agnostic test-time intervention, VTI outperforms previous baseline methods.
- Novel attempt to mitigate hallucinations by perturbing representation space in a training-free approach.
- Writing is exceptionally clear; motivation is thoroughly argued; experiments are comprehensive.

### Weaknesses
- **Explanation of representation stability:** Using variance change is one-sided; PC1 is the direction of largest variance, so variance decrease after VTI is inevitable. A more fundamental analysis of distribution stability under different noises/masks (e.g., KL divergence) is needed. One reviewer (R3, score 6) has lower confidence in this aspect (soundness 2).
- **Linear offset cannot change variance:** If embedding variance is caused by input perturbation, a linear offset cannot change that variance, so the claim of improving “feature stability” via this offset is questionable.
- **Missing analysis of embedding changes:** What do the actual embedding changes look like? How much explained variance does the first PC capture?
- **Comparison method settings not specified:** OPERA uses beam decoding, VCD uses nucleus sampling. The paper does not specify which decoding method is used for vanilla and VTI, making comparisons unfair. For fair comparison, VTI should be tested with both beam search and nucleus sampling separately (as in PAI paper).
- **Randomness in nucleus sampling:** For CHAIR and POPE, results should report average and variance over five runs, following VCD’s experimental design.
- **Unexplained decoding method switch:** MMHAL-BENCH uses beam search but this is not explained; the paper should justify and state the decoding method used in each experiment.
- **Missing ablation on PCA vs. averaging:** What is the effect of using average instead of PCA to compute the shifting vectors \(d_{l,t}\)?
- **Mask ratio 0.99 is unanalyzed:** This unusually high ratio leaves almost no image information; comparison with different mask ratios and with using fully masked images as intervention direction is needed.
- **Generalization concern:** Only hallucination benchmarks are tested; it is unclear whether VTI damages performance on other tasks when image or task domains change.
- **Method description too general (Section 3):** How many LVLMs were tested? Which one(s)? Are test images from COCO? Are observations generalized to different LVLMs?
- **Relation to VCD not discussed:** VCD (CVPR2024) also adds Gaussian noise and states “visual uncertainty amplifies hallucination” (their Figure 2). The paper should clarify differences between its exploration and VCD’s.
- **“Without additional cost” is misleading:** VTI requires additional data and tuning to compute shifting vectors.
- **Source of 50 paired examples not stated:** Lines 285-286 mention 50 examples with images, hallucinated and non-hallucinated captions; likely from COCO but not confirmed.
- **Effect of using more PCA dimensions:** Not explored.
- **Figure 2 issues:** Caption should describe CHAIRs; the three subplots are not well connected—left/middle plots (sigma and ratio) are not clearly aligned with the third figure.
- **Equation (1) and surrounding text confusing:** Sentence structure issues (missing main clause in lines 214, 278) and missing comma in Equation (1).
- **Minor typos:** Duplicate “(LLMs)” in abstract; unnecessary abbreviation emphasis in line 151-152.
