- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method to estimate the fairness-accuracy trade-off curve for a dataset and model class using the You-Only-Train-Once (YOTO) framework with confidence intervals from a held-out calibration set. Two reviewers (scores 3,3) found significant weaknesses in motivation, novelty, evaluation, and core assumptions; one reviewer (score 6) valued the practical problem and efficiency but noted limitations. The majority argued the method over-relies on YOTO without verifying it approximates the true Pareto frontier, the confidence intervals lack ground truth, and experiments are insufficient.

### Strengths
- The fairness-accuracy trade-off problem is fundamental and important in algorithmic fairness (R1; R2 also noted the need in the domain; R3: widely used).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with effective graphics (R1, R2; R3 presentation "good").
- Estimating a range of achievable fairness violations for a given accuracy is a practical problem; the proposed method offers an efficient solution with two-sided confidence intervals (R3; R1 also said confidence intervals with statistical guarantee make sense; R2 noted the need but with reservations).
- One reviewer (R3, score 6) emphasized the practical utility and efficiency of the method, while the other two were more critical of these aspects.

### Weaknesses
1. **Technical Novelty and Reliance on YOTO**: Efficiency directly adopts the existing YOTO framework; confidence interval estimation involves only trivial bounds (R1). The cost and limitations of YOTO are not discussed (R3). The method is highly based on an existing method, reducing novelty (R1, R3). Two reviewers (R1, R2) argued the assumption that YOTO achieves the ground-truth Pareto frontier is unsupported and likely wrong; the paper over-claims by presenting the YOTO-based curve as the achievable trade-off (R1, R2). The paper acknowledges this assumption only in passing (“under the assumption of large enough model capacity”, Page 4) without formal justification or empirical verification beyond visual inspection (R2). After rebuttal, the explanation that the confidence intervals depend on the gap between YOTO and true trade-off still assumes YOTO is good enough, which is not grounded (R2). A synthetic dataset with known ground-truth trade-off would be needed to validate the method (R1).

2. **Framing and Conditioning on Accuracy**: The central claim – “for a given dataset, model class, and accuracy, the permissible range of fairness violation is x to y” – is problematic because accuracy and fairness are inherently connected and cannot be arbitrarily conditioned; the achievable fairness is fundamentally limited by the chosen accuracy, and vice versa (R2). The paper should frame it as estimating the Pareto frontier, not a range conditioned on fixed accuracy (R2). The motivation for using a “universal fairness requirement across datasets” is unclear and the advantages of such an approach are not elaborated (R1). The choice of optimizing fairness with accuracy (Eq. (2)) over optimizing accuracy with fairness constraint is not justified, despite the authors highlighting the difference in Section 2.1 paragraph 1 (R1).

3. **Definition and Implementation of $\mathcal{L}_{fair}$**: The fairness loss term $\mathcal{L}_{fair}$ in Eq. (2) is not defined (R2, R3). The paper should present the smooth relaxation of demographic parity; the specific form and hyperparameters can significantly impact the trade-off curve and reproducibility (R2). This lack of definition makes assessment difficult (R2, R3).

4. **Experimental Evaluation Weaknesses**:
   - The evaluation of confidence intervals is vague; there is no ground truth for the optimal trade-off curve, so the intervals can only be visually compared but not rigorously assessed (R1, R2). The method assumes the YOTO curve is the ground truth, which is unreasonable (R2). The paper needs to justify why YOTO should be considered a reliable proxy (R2). It is also unclear which confidence interval estimation method is better (R1).
   - The claim that $\lambda$ in Eq. (2) offers little control over accuracy is counter-intuitive and requires experimental support (R1).
   - It is unclear how confidence intervals for both accuracy and fairness are plotted simultaneously in Figures 3 and 4 (R1).
   - The used tabular datasets (Adult and COMPAS) are either super-imbalanced (Adult) or small (COMPAS) (R2). More robust evaluation on larger datasets like Folktables (https://github.com/socialfoundations/folktables) is needed (R2). After rebuttal, the evaluation remains unconvincing on small tabular data without new results (R2).
   - Figure 4 shows that the estimated curve has more errors when using some particular algorithmic fairness methods (R3). Since practitioners often use algorithmic fairness methods, the practical utility of the estimated curve is concerned (R3). A possible extension would be to make the curve algorithm-sensitive (R3).

5. **Domain Limitation**: The method requires an in-distribution calibration set and only applies to in-distribution tests (R3). This limits practical applicability.

6. **Ethical Considerations**: The ability to change fairness-accuracy trade-offs using a single model may incur ethical issues, such as generating biased outcomes for certain groups; the paper requires further ethical review (R2).

7. **Title**: “Dataset Fairness” is too broad and does not accurately reflect the paper’s focus on estimating the fairness-accuracy trade-off curve; a more precise title would better communicate the contribution (R2).

8. **Contrast**: One reviewer (R3, score 6) acknowledged the practical importance and efficiency of the method but also raised concerns about YOTO costs and algorithm-agnosticism. The other two reviewers (R1, R2, scores 3,3) were more critical, particularly regarding the lack of novelty, the unsupported assumption about YOTO, insufficient experiments, and framing issues. The overall consensus was that the paper has significant gaps that prevent acceptance in its current form.
