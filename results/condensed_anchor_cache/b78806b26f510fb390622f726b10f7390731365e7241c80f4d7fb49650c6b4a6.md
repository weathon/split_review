## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ProMoT, a two-stage fine-tuning method to reduce “format specialization” (overfitting to the format of a fine-tuned task). It first freezes the model and tunes a soft prompt (prompt tuning), then freezes that prompt and fine-tunes the model itself. Evaluations on T5 and (in the appendix) PaLM 8B across a diverse set of tasks show that ProMoT achieves comparable performance on the fine-tuned task while better preserving in-context learning generalization on out-of-domain tasks. The paper provides gradient-similarity analysis to explain format specialization and includes single-task and multi-task experiments.

### Strengths
- **Interesting and simple idea** to separate format learning from task learning; the method furthers understanding of param-efficient fine-tuning by demonstrating and leveraging a unique property of prompt tuning.  
- **Well-written and easy to follow** with clear technical details and experimental setup.  
- **Comprehensive evaluation** across diverse task types (NLI, closed-book QA, translation, summarization) with multiple runs in few-shot settings, covering both single-task and multi-task fine-tuning.  
- **Effective at preserving generalization** while causing only a slight reduction in specialization; the cause of format specialization is revealed by analyzing gradient similarity between original and randomized datasets.  
- **Ablation experiments** confirm the necessity of design details.  

### Weaknesses
- **Limited fine-tuning tasks.** The method is only fine-tuned on two datasets (NLI: RTE; translation: WMT14 En-Fr). Reviewers question why other tasks (e.g., QA, summarization) were not used, and whether format specialization does not occur there. The multi-task experiments also use only two tasks and show mixed results compared to 1-shot baselines, missing 4-shot comparisons.  
- **Model generality.** The primary experiments are on a single encoder-decoder model (T5). Additional results on PaLM 8B (decoder-only) are relegated to the appendix and are less promising — the approach sometimes shows worse out-of-task performance than the pretrained model. No results are reported on other decoder-only models. One reviewer notes that only one model architecture is tested.  
- **Missing baselines.** The paper does not include a prefix-tuning column, nor a prefix + parameter tuning baseline where the prefix is not used for other tasks (in both single-task and multi-task settings).  
- **Lack of empirical verification of the format-learning hypothesis.** It is assumed that the prompt-tuning stage learns the format, but no experiment verifies that the soft prompt produces the source‑task format on other tasks.  
- **Practical utility questioned.** One reviewer (score 5) is not convinced of the setting’s practical value; the weak multi-task results and missing comparisons (e.g., 4‑shot in multi-task, and 4‑shot on PaLM 8B in the appendix) weaken the case.  
- **Minor presentation issue.** In Tables 3 and 4, the most relevant datasets (e.g., CB & WiC for RTE; WMT16 en>de & en>ro for translation) should be highlighted more clearly.  
- **Disagreement among reviewers.** One reviewer is notably more critical (rating 5, confidence 3), while the other three give ratings 6, 8, 8 (confidence 4). The critical reviewer considers the contribution limited due to missing baselines, weak multi-task results, and unverified hypothesis, whereas the others find the method effective and well-evaluated.
