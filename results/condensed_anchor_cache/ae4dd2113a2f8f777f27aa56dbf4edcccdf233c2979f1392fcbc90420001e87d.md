## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Random Ensemble Defense (RED) and its parameter-saving variant PS-RED, which enhance ensemble robustness against adversarial attacks via random sub-model sampling during inference, gradient similarity and Lipschitz regularizers for diversity, and hypernetwork-based weight prediction for storage efficiency. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and TinyImageNet show improvements in robust accuracy (e.g., ~15% increase on CIFAR-10) and parameter reduction (~90% for PS-RED). Reviewer scores range from 3 to 6, with the majority (three reviewers) rating 3 and two reviewers (4 and 5) being more positive, though all raise concerns about novelty, evaluation rigor, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The idea of using random sampling during inference to reduce latency and complicate attacks is considered innovative (Reviewers 4, 5). Reviewer 4 notes that this is “crucial for real-time applications.”
- The application of hypernetworks to predict ensemble model weights for storage efficiency is novel in this context (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5). Reviewer 5 finds PS-RED’s design “interesting.”
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with intuitive visualizations (Reviewers 1, 5). Reviewer 5 highlights the “intuitive visualization to motivate the proposed methods.”
- Results are promising: a ResNet-18 achieves 65.32% accuracy under AutoAttack on CIFAR-10 (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 3 agrees that “results appear to be promising.” Reviewer 4 states that experiments on CIFAR-10 and TinyImageNet demonstrate “significant improvements over existing methods.”
- RED is simple and straightforward to implement (Reviewer 5). The use of gradient similarity to diversify vulnerabilities and Lipschitz regularizers to strengthen individual models is reasonable (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of adaptive attack evaluation (major concern):** The attacks tested are not tailored for RED’s random model selection. An attacker could query multiple times and maintain a set of PGD iterations (one per sub-model), reducing the defense to gradient masking and inflating robustness estimates (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 adds that the poor performance of AutoAttack relative to weaker attacks (PGD, C&W, BIM, MIM, FGSM) suggests either improper tuning or gradient masking. Reviewer 3 also notes the absence of stronger ensemble-specific attacks such as MORA (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2022). Reviewer 5 does not raise this concern.
- **Similarity-based regularization overlaps with prior work:** The gradient similarity regularizer (Eq. 3) closely resembles that of GAL (minimizing cosine similarity of gradients), and the Lipschitz regularizer (Eq. 4) is similar to DVERGE. The core technical novelty over these methods is unclear (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 notes that “the ideas of Lipschitz regularization and diversity promotion have long existed.” Reviewers 4 and 5 also note the simplicity of these components.
- **Effectiveness of random sampling inference (RSI) is not convincingly shown:** RSI appears to rely on randomness as a form of gradient masking, and no ablation study is provided to quantify its contribution to robustness (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 is less critical of RSI itself.
- **Hypernetwork limitations and vulnerabilities:** The hypernetwork itself could be directly attacked, but this is not studied (Reviewer 2). Its application is restricted to certain architectures (e.g., ResNet-18 with 3×3 Conv layers); generalizing to VGG, MobileNet, or Transformers is unverified (Reviewers 2, 4). Training the hypernetwork requires additional data, loss terms, and computational cost that are not quantified (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 adds that for real-time devices, runtime performance (not just parameter efficiency) must be reported.
- **Missing comparisons and evaluations:** The paper does not compare against the latest ensemble defense methods, e.g., those from NeurIPS 2023 (Reviewer 4). Only the ResNet-18 architecture is tested (Reviewers 2, 4). Whether RED is compatible with advanced adversarial training (e.g., RobustBench) is unknown (Reviewer 5). The clean accuracy of hypernetwork-predicted weights is not reported (Reviewer 2). Black-box transferability attacks are not evaluated (Reviewer 3).
- **Loss landscape visualization is uninformative:** Figure 1 shows little difference with/without the gradient similarity regularizer, and the axes and right-most plots are not clearly explained (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Potential scalability and ensemble size effects:** Reviewer 1 questions whether baseline methods with pairwise regularizers can scale efficiently to N=8 (the setting used in the paper). Reviewer 3 notes that for adversarially trained ensembles like DVERGE, larger ensembles can *degrade* robustness; whether RED avoids this is not discussed.
- **Minor issues:** Citation format could be improved (use \citet{}); discussion of limitations and broader societal impacts is missing (Reviewer 1). Reviewers 4 and 5 did not raise these minor issues.

**Minority positive contrast:** Reviewers 4 (score 5) and 5 (score 6) are notably more positive. Reviewer 4 considers the random sampling innovation and storage reduction as important contributions, despite weaknesses. Reviewer 5 finds the empirical results very promising (“if this result is correct, the effectiveness of RED is really promising”) and is less concerned about the lack of adaptive attacks and novelty relative to prior work. However, Reviewer 5 still notes the technical components are “fairly simple” and questions compatibility with adversarial training.
