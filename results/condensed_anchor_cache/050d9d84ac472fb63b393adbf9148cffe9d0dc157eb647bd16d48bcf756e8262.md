## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally agree that the paper provides a comprehensive theoretical analysis of h-SVGD, including existence of the hybrid Stein PDE solution, a Wasserstein gradient flow interpretation, a descent lemma, and a large particle limit. The extension of SVGD theory is seen as interesting and rigorous. However, concerns are raised about limited experimental justification (advantage may not stem from variance mitigation on some datasets), the restrictive focus on the \(k_2 = c k_1\) special case, insufficient intuition/clarity in assumptions and exposition, and the practical implications of the theoretical bias (non-convergence to the target). One reviewer (score 8) is very positive but notes limited familiarity; the other three are more mixed (scores 5,6,6), with the lowest expressing the most concern about empirical support and discrepancy between theory and observed performance.

### Strengths
- The theoretical foundation of h-SVGD is comprehensive and rigorous, providing new results: existence of a solution to the hybrid Stein PDE, a descent lemma, a Wasserstein gradient flow interpretation for the \(k_2 = c k_1\) special case, and a large particle limit. Some results are proved under relaxed/more practical assumptions than in prior work.
- The paper clearly explains the relationships and distinctions between the theoretical results for h-SVGD and those for standard SVGD.
- Empirical evaluation uses diverse datasets and metrics (including DAMV, RMSE, test LL), and the experiments demonstrate the practical interest of h-SVGD, particularly its ability to alleviate variance collapse.
- The paper quantifies that h-SVGD does not incur additional computational cost compared to SVG D.
- The presentation is well-structured and figures are clear.

### Weaknesses
- **Title misleading:** The title suggests h-SVGD is a novel algorithm introduced in this paper, but h-SVGD was previously proposed. The title should reflect the paper’s focus on theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation.
- **Assumption clarity and correctness:**  
  - Assumption (A1) appears incorrectly stated: requiring \(\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=+\infty\) would be more realistic for \(\pi\) to be a probability measure; this may be a writing error.  
  - Assumptions (A2), (A3), (A4) lack interpretation. Why is the first part of (A2) stated separately when (A1) already implies \(V\) is bounded? What does the second part of (A2) mean? What is the intuition behind (A3)? Examples of potentials satisfying all assumptions would help.  
  - The remark at line 246 seems inconsistent because (A1) implies \(V\) is bounded. At line 329, the normal density violates (A1) since \(V\) is quadratic.  
  - The constant \(L\) at line 250 is said to depend on \(k_1, k_2\) and \(p\), but the variable \(p\) is unclear.  
  - At line 226, “symmetric function” needs a precise definition.
- **Lack of intuition and commentary:** The paper is hard to read; it would benefit from detailed discussion of assumptions, a reminder on Wasserstein gradient flow, and comments on Proposition 3.6 (which is currently left unmentioned).
- **Typos and notational inconsistencies:**  
  - Equation (6): should be \((x,\cdot)\) instead of \((\cdot,x)\).  
  - Line 198: should use \(\mathcal{H}\) instead of \(\mathcal{H}_1\).  
  - The use of “S-SVGD” and “SSVGD” interchangeably is confusing.
- **Limited experimental support for variance collapse explanation:** On the Protein and Kin8nm datasets, SVGD does not appear to suffer from variance underestimation, yet h-SVGD still outperforms SVGD. This suggests that the advantage of h-SVGD in BNN tasks may not primarily stem from mitigating variance underestimation, undermining the central claim.
- **Theoretical bias not reflected in reported metrics:** Corollary 3.4 shows that h-SVGD does not converge to the target distribution in the mean field limit, yet commonly used metrics (test RMSE, test LL) do not capture this limitation. The paper does not adequately interpret why h-SVGD can be advantageous despite this bias. Furthermore, the limiting distribution (annealed posterior) is a suboptimal MSE estimator but performs better in practice for BNNs – this discrepancy requires further investigation.
- **Overly focused on a special kernel case:** The main theoretical analysis is restricted to \(k_2 = c k_1\). While two RBF kernels with different bandwidths are explored empirically, the paper does not delve into the implications of using kernels from entirely different families. This limits insight into the behavior of h-SVGD in more general settings.
- **RMSE and LL gains not convincing:** The RMSE and LL results in Appendix B show no clear benefit over SVGD. The paper should provide a more detailed discussion in the main text about why DAMV is the appropriate metric for evaluating h-SVGD’s performance on variance collapse. The current justification is not sufficiently clear.
- **Missing experiments and analyses requested:**  
  - Are there ways to mitigate the bias (non-convergence to target)?  
  - Have the authors tried two completely different kernels (beyond RBF with different bandwidths)?  
  - The paper references h-SVGD’s promising results on image classification; why were these experiments not included or discussed in this work?  
  - From an applications perspective, why is avoiding variance collapse important? This motivation should be explicitly stated.
