## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates many-shot in-context learning (ICL) capabilities in multimodal foundation models, benchmarking GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3.2-Vision, and InternLM-XComposer2.5 across 14 datasets spanning natural, medical, remote sensing, and molecular imagery for tasks including image classification, VQA, and object localization. A consistent trend is that closed-source models, especially Gemini 1.5 Pro, show substantial gains from many-shot ICL (up to ~2000 examples), while open-weights models do not benefit. The authors also explore batching multiple queries in a single API call, which reduces per-query cost and latency and sometimes boosts performance. One reviewer (R3, rating 3) was notably more critical of the depth and novelty of the analysis, while the other three reviewers (R1, R2, R4, each rating 5) were more positive overall.

### Strengths
- **Well-organized and easy to follow** (R1).
- **First benchmarking of many-shot ICL for multimodal foundation models**; the idea is promising and interesting (R1).
- **Extensive evaluation across 14 diverse datasets** covering multiple domains and tasks, with rigorous methodology (R1, R2, R4).
- **Dataset construction procedure is well designed**, including class-balanced sampling (R1).
- **Tests on less common domains** (medical, remote sensing) for multimodal LLMs (R3).
- **Addresses inference efficiency** – batching reduces latency and cost, and is analyzed from domain and class calibration perspectives (R2, R3, R4).
- **Error bars are presented in most figures** (R3).
- **Reveals performance trends** of LMMs with increasing shots, highlighting the gap between open and closed models (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited number of models benchmarked** (only 2 open-source and 2 closed-source), making it hard to draw general conclusions (R1).
- **Ablation experiments conducted only on Gemini 1.5 Pro**; phenomena may differ across models (R1).
- **Lack of in-depth analysis for why open-weights models (Llama 3.2-Vision, InternLM-XComposer2.5) do not benefit** from many-shot ICL. This could be due to model size (not explored) or other factors – deeper analysis needed (R1, R3).
- **Concerns about data leakage** for proprietary models (e.g., pretrained on test datasets), which would explain the open vs. closed gap. Suggested to test only on open-source models with transparent training data (R3).
- **Benchmark tasks are relatively simple** – many image classification datasets (EuroSAT, UC Merced) already achieve 95-100% accuracy with classic CV models. Improving task diversity would strengthen claims (R3). Minor: Oxford Pets is conventional CV (R3).
- **No reference to Figure 5** in the paper text (R2, R3).
- **Lack of analysis on choice of sampling strategy** – only class-balanced sampling is used; impact of random or other class-wise sampling not addressed (R2).
- **Query batching only within same dataset** – cross-dataset batching not explored (R2).
- **Quantity vs. quality of ICL examples not addressed** – beyond scaling, are better-selected examples more effective? More theoretical/convincing discussion expected (R2).
- **Introduction is simple** and does not adequately distinguish this work from prior many-shot ICL studies in LLMs (R4).
- **Open-weights model results are ambiguous** – one reviewer (R4) states the work “reveals that open-weighted models can achieve better gains than closed LMMs”, which directly contradicts the paper’s conclusion that they do not benefit. This suggests confusion or insufficient clarity in the presentation; no explanation is given for the observed (or misperceived) behavior (R4).
- **No explanations for why closed models perform poorly on certain datasets** (e.g., CheXpert, EuroSAT) – mere reporting of results without analysis (R4; also R3 asks for hypothesis on these scaling failures).
- **Inference hyperparameters (e.g., temperature) not reported** (R3).
- **Image classification negative choice construction details missing** (R3, question from Appendix A.1).
- **One reviewer questions whether this study is sufficiently novel for ICLR** or would be more suitable as a technical report (R2).
