## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces CRLQAS, a curriculum-based reinforcement learning algorithm for quantum architecture search (QAS) under hardware noise. It employs a 3-D tensor encoding of circuits, illegal-action pruning, a random halting scheme to find shorter circuits, an Adam-SPSA optimizer, and Pauli-transfer-matrix (PTM) simulation with JAX acceleration. Experiments on VQE for molecules (H₂, LiH, H₂O, etc.) show that CRLQAS outperforms prior QAS methods in both noiseless and noisy settings, achieving chemical accuracy in some cases. However, reviewers note missing comparisons with noise-adaptive methods, limited evaluation scope, lack of real-hardware tests, unclear ablations, writing issues, and missing source code (disputed by one reviewer). The algorithm is novel and well-motivated, but the evidence is not fully convincing of advantage in realistic NISQ scenarios.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: First RL-based QAS to incorporate real-device noise models (R1, R2). Pioneering curriculum RL that specifically addresses noise in circuit architecture design (R1, R2, R5).
- **Technical contributions** (R1, R2, R5): Tensor-based 3-D encoding is compact and efficient; illegal-action pruning narrows the search space; random halting reduces episode length (encouraging shorter circuits); Adam-SPSA variant accelerates convergence; PTM formalism + JAX provides significant speedup for noisy-circuit simulation and allows high-fidelity noise simulation (R2).
- **Quality and effectiveness**: Techniques are straightforward and easy to implement (R1, R3). Detailed error-rate analysis (R4). The algorithm is tested under multiple noise models inspired by real IBM devices and shows robustness (R5).
- **Significance**: Addresses a crucial gap (noise in QAS) for NISQ deployment (R1, R2). Demonstrates chemical accuracy in some tasks and outperforms prior QAS methods (R1, R5). Results provide a useful guideline for future research (R3). Experimental setup and source code description are comprehensive (R5; but see Weaknesses on code availability).
- **Presentation**: Main ideas are mostly clear and well motivated (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and related work**:
  - No comparison with other RL-based QAS methods (e.g., Kuo et al. 2021, Chen 2023, Sun et al. 2023) (R5).
  - Only one QAS method (likely evolutionary or gradient-based) is compared; many noise-aware QAS works are omitted, including: QuantumNAS (Wang et al. HPCA 2022) (R1, R4, R5); QCAS (Du et al. 2022) tested under depolarizing noise; QuantumDARTS (Wu et al. 2023) for readout errors; Rattew et al. 2019, Layer VQE (Liu et al. 2022), TopGen (Cheng et al. 2022) (R4); Predictor-based QAS (He et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2021) (R5); and SMT-solver-based circuit optimization (Tan & Cong, ICCAD 2020) (R2).
  - Lack of comparison with noise-adaptive methods makes it unclear whether improvements come from noise-specific design or general RL enhancements (R1, R5).
- **Experiments and evaluation scope**:
  - Evaluation is weak for drawing strong conclusions (R3). Only VQE chemistry tasks are tested; claims of general VQA applicability are overstatements (R1).
  - Only one seed/run used? (R4). No results on real quantum hardware—only noisy simulators (R4). Reviewers request experiments on actual devices, especially since the proposed method yields a final circuit that can be executed directly (R4, R5).
  - For molecules like LiH-4, only shot noise and 1-qubit depolarizing noise are considered, which do not capture crosstalk, coherent errors, or correlated noise; this undermines the claim of realism (R1).
  - Computational cost is a concern: RL is inherently expensive; the noise model depends on calibration data that can shift over days/weeks, potentially making results unreliable if retraining is needed (R4). The paper does not discuss adaptation cost or efficiency for different noise models (R1, R4).
  - Chemical accuracy claim may be overstated if the spin-orbital basis uses STO-3G (R4). The basis function is not specified.
- **Lack of ablation and insight**:
  - It is unclear which component (tensor encoding, illegal actions, random halting, Adam-SPSA) principally improves results, especially under noise—the paper does not separate noise-specific adaptations from general improvements (R1, R3).
  - No isolated study on the impact of illegal-action pruning (R3). Table 4 shows CRLQAS(wo-RH) outperforms CRLQAS, raising questions about the purpose of random halting; meaning of two rows for CRLQAS(RH) is not marked (R5).
  - No explanation why noise-motivated adaptations also outperform state-of-the-art in the noiseless case (R3).
- **Writing, clarity, and presentation issues**:
  - Many grammar and notation errors (R1, R3): e.g., incomplete sentence p1, “ansatz”/”ansätze” inconsistency, “T_{s^e}” vs “T_s^e”, wrong citation format (Wang et al. 2021), “it’s” for “it is”, “the Appendix” appears mid-sentence. Section 3 organization is scattered; unclear descriptions (e.g., “n_s is number of successes”, eq2 parameters insufficiently explained) (R1, R3).
  - Tables unclear: Table 1 bolding inconsistent (should bold ‘7.21*10^-8’ and ‘2.9*10^-4’ if best) (R5); Table 4 does not specify which molecule task (R4) and has unlabeled rows (R5). Figures appear too late in the document (R3).
  - Terminology “architecture” is ambiguous and could be confused with low-level hardware design in computer architecture; “circuit design” or “circuit configuration” would be more precise (R2).
- **Source code availability** (disagreement): Reviewer 2 (high confidence) states that the promised source code is not provided, and it is essential for replicability. Reviewer 5 asserts that the paper provides source code for reproducibility. This contradiction should be resolved.
- **Other concerns**:
  - No discussion of limitations or future work (R1). Does not address qubit connectivity constraints explicitly (R5).
  - Some techniques (feedback-driven curriculum learning, Adam-SPSA) are direct adaptations of existing methods, limiting originality (R1).
