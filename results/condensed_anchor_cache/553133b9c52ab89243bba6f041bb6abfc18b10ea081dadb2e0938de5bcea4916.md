## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates fine-tuning strategies for small LLMs (3B–7B parameters) on large-scale instruction-tuning datasets, using Granite 3B, Granite 7B, and Mistral 7B. It compares stacked versus phased training, varying batch sizes, learning rates, and training configurations, evaluating on MMLU and MTBench. Key findings: larger batch sizes with lower learning rates improve performance; early training dynamics (gradient norms, loss) predict final performance; warmup can be skipped; stacked training outperforms phased training. The paper challenges common practices from TULU and LAB.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated investigation of an important and growing field: customizing small LLMs for domain-specific tasks while balancing memorization and generalization.
- Comprehensive experimental design covering many training configurations (batch size, learning rate schedules, training methods) across three models and three datasets.
- Provides practical, actionable guidance for practitioners fine-tuning small LLMs, especially the finding that stacked training saves time and resources over phased training.
- Directly challenges widely recognized practices (e.g., TULU hyperparameter recommendations), which is a valuable contribution.
- Writing is clear and easy to follow; related work section is comprehensive.

### Weaknesses
**Limited model generalizability.** Experiments only use Granite and Mistral model families. The findings may not transfer to other architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Phi, Qwen) with different pre-training methods, attention mechanisms, or normalization layers. Suggested additions: Llama 3.2 (1B, 3B), Llama 3.1-8B, Phi series. The absence of such models makes it unclear whether trends are universal or model-specific.

**Narrow evaluation benchmarks.** The study relies primarily on MMLU and MTBench, which may not capture diverse capabilities. Missing benchmarks for mathematical reasoning (GSM8K), logical inference (ARC), code generation, and complex reasoning. Also, the evaluation lacks domain-specific benchmarks (e.g., legal, medical, customer service, financial) that relate to the paper’s motivation of customizing LLMs for specialized domains.

**Lack of baselines and significance clarity.** Baseline performance of the pretrained Granite and Mistral models on MMLU and MTBench is not reported. Many reported differences are very small (e.g., 6.77 vs 6.76 on MTBench in Figure 1a, 0.5251 vs 0.5242 in Figure 3a, 0.59 vs 0.6 vs 0.61 in Figure 4a) and it is unclear if these are statistically or practically significant.

**Experimental setup justification missing.** The order of experiments and whether a full cross-product sweep was performed is unclear. Also unexplained: why these specific models were chosen, how the fine-tuning datasets were curated (Section 2.1 and A.2 lack details), why MMLU and MTBench are the right evaluation datasets, and why TULU vs LAB were the primary hyperparameter comparisons.

**Presentation issues.** Individual training curves are noisy; multiple seeds and smoother results would help. Most results should be presented in tables for easier comparison across all three models, rather than only plots.

**Findings are incremental or expected.** Several key findings (larger batch size improves performance, performance-efficiency trade-off) are already well-established in the literature. The paper does not offer a substantially novel perspective on these phenomena.

**Missing discussion of optimization techniques.** No coverage of widely-used methods like LoRA or approximate optimization algorithms (e.g., GaLore) that are common when fine-tuning small LLMs.

**Unclear definition of “skills”.** The term “skill” is used loosely; it is unclear whether it refers to a specific domain (e.g., math) or a reasoning method (e.g., chain-of-thought). Also, Phase 10 (“complex skill development”) uses tasks like poetry writing – the paper does not clarify whether these are in instruction-answer format, and how different data styles (e.g., QA vs. book knowledge) are handled.

**Minor issues.** Repetition of the same citation (Lab: Large-scale alignment for chatbots) occurs. L37 lacks a needed citation. There is an apparent contradiction: L38 argues small models suit limited-resource practitioners, but L82 states that training a small model well requires large batch sizes and specified resources.

**Reviewer questions that overlap with weaknesses.**  
- How well do these training strategies work with Phi, Llama variants?  
- How might results vary depending on whether the fine-tuning data is more general or more specific (telecommunications example)?  
- Is the contradiction between L38 and L82 (limited resources vs. requirement for large batch sizes) resolved?
