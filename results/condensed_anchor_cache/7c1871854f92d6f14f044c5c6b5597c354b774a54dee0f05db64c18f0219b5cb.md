## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a channel-wise quantization autoencoder for image tokenization, replacing standard spatial tokenization by quantizing features along the channel dimension. Tokens thus capture global image structure rather than local patches. A masked-prediction generative model (like MaskGIT) is built on top. Experiments are conducted on class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 and text-to-image generation on COCO, achieving FID 1.87 and 100% codebook usage.

### Strengths
- The idea of channel-wise quantization for image generation is novel and interesting. (R1, R3)
- The paper is well-structured, clearly written, and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Reconstruction ability (rFID, SSIM) is greatly improved compared to spatial tokenizers. (R3) However, reviewer 1 notes a discrepancy: rFID improves while PSNR worsens in Table 5. (R1)
- The method yields a tokenizer with 100% codebook usage across different codebook sizes. (R3, R4)
- Comprehensive experiments and comparisons against many relevant models are provided. (R1, R4)
- The approach is simple yet effective, achieving superior or comparable performance on image generation tasks. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Fairness of evaluation and compression ratios**: Comparisons in Table 5 and elsewhere are not apples-to-apples because the token count differs dramatically (e.g., VQAN uses 4×256 tokens vs. proposed 256×256 tokens) leading to different compression ratios. A fixed compression budget comparison is needed. (R1) Table 5 also shows inconsistent metric trade-offs (rFID improves while PSNR worsens, though SSIM improves) that require explanation. (R1)
- **Lack of comparison to standard baselines**: Stable Diffusion series (SD1.5, SDXL, SD2.1) is missing. (R4) Moreover, marginal performance gain over VAR: under similar parameters and inference steps, improvement is slight or even a drop in some cases. (R4)
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only evaluated on ImageNet 256×256 and MS-COCO 256×256; no high-resolution generation (e.g., 1024×1024). (R4) The method is only tested on one specific resolution. (R2, R4) No text-image alignment metric (e.g., CLIP score) for text-to-image. (R4) Downstream tasks (e.g., classification) are not evaluated. (R4)
- **Theoretical justification and motivation**: The motivation for channel-wise quantization is not strongly established. (R1, R3) It is unclear why issues like low codebook usage and limited embedding dimensions in spatial tokenizers lead to channel-wise design. (R3) Reviewer 2 considers the motivation fundamentally flawed, arguing that channel-wise quantization lacks intuitive rationality because it loses spatial awareness and causal structure, making generation of coherent images difficult. (R2, minority opinion)
- **Novelty concern**: Channel-wise quantization has been used in classification, LLM compression, and super-resolution; its adaptation to image generation is incremental without sufficient justification. (R4)
- **Codebook usage attribution**: The 100% codebook usage may be partly due to entropy regularization (known to increase usage) rather than channel-wise quantization per se. The compared method LlamaGen did not use such regularization. An ablation without entropy regularization is needed. (R3)
- **Tokenizer limitations**: The tokenizer is incompatible with different image resolutions, requiring retraining. (R2, R4) As the model scales, the fixed number of channels limits token count growth, potentially making learning more difficult and leading to training instability. (R2) Tokens in channel space capture global information, implying high correlation and potential memorization (each image is learned as a whole); token reuse across images may be low, contradicting the claim of strong representational capacity. (R1) This issue requires further analysis. (R1)
- **Missing analysis and visualizations**: No direct experiments support claims that channel-wise tokens capture both global structures and local details. Visualizations of learned channel tokens are requested. (R3) Claims about low similarity between channel tokens, efficient modeling, and strong capacity lack clear definition and verification. (R1)
- **Incomplete ablation**: Ablation on token dimensions only covers a limited range; experiments from 8 to 256 channels are needed to understand scaling behavior. (R1)
- **Minor presentation issues**: In Table 2, the best results under the same setting should be bolded. Figure 2 is blurry and appears to contain screenshots. (R4)
