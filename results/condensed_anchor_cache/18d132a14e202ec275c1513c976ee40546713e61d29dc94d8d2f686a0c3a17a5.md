## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Locally Constrained Policy Optimization (LCPO), a policy gradient algorithm for online RL in non-stationary environments where a time-varying exogenous context process (observable) affects dynamics. LCPO combats catastrophic forgetting by constraining policy updates to preserve behavior on past contexts while optimizing return on current experiences. It uses two replay buffers (past contexts and current context) and an OOD threshold-based detector to trigger regularization updates that anchor the policy on samples from outside the current context distribution. Evaluations on MuJoCo, classic control, and computer systems environments with synthetic and real context traces show LCPO outperforms state-of-the-art on-policy and off-policy methods and achieves on-par performance with an oracle.

### Strengths
- Well-written, easy to read, with thorough introduction, exhaustive related works, clear definitions and derivations, and well-structured methodology. The specific non-stationarity problem (exogenous observed context) is well-described and the solution fits it naturally, paralleling TRPO. Illustrative examples provide intuitive understanding of complex ideas.
- The main algorithmic idea – sampling states from the history buffer that differ from current environment samples and using them to constrain policy updates – is simple and, based on experimental results, very effective. It is an almost straightforward modification of TRPO for a completely different problem, obvious only in hindsight.
- Extensive evaluation against numerous baselines on multiple tasks (MuJoCo, classic control, computer systems) provides substantial confidence in the results and conclusions. Ablation studies (e.g., buffer size sensitivity, OOD detector threshold) offer further insights.
- Addresses an important problem (handling non-stationarity in online RL); method appears original. Figure 1 is particularly liked and suggested to be moved earlier.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of non-stationarity**: The paper considers only exogenous contextual non-stationarity. It is unclear whether the approach works for other types (e.g., changes in agent embodiment, reward function itself). The paper does not discuss how to extend to scenarios where context is unobserved (latent), e.g., combining with methods for inferring context from agent history.
- **OOD detector dependencies**: The OOD detector is critical; its threshold is environment-dependent and unknown beforehand. No sensitivity analysis or practical strategy for threshold selection is provided. Additionally, the paper could discuss which OOD detectors are beneficial for which RL environments, and whether learning the OOD proxy (e.g., weighted norm with tunable parameters) or comparing with change point detection (CPD) has been tried.
- **Approximation and stability concerns**: The constrained optimization is approximately solved without a TRPO-style local constraint (natural gradient property is lost). It is unclear how this affects convergence, whether it could cause oscillations or divergence, and what new properties emerge. The line search attempts to enforce the constraint but the approximation’s convergence properties are not analyzed.
- **Potential overfitting to early samples**: Deep neural networks are known to overfit to early samples (primacy bias); regularizing with past data might exacerbate this issue.
- **Clarity and structure issues (minority reviewer)**: One reviewer (score 5) found the paper hard to follow, lacking a clear structure; specifically, Section 4 "Locally-Constrained Policy Optimization" and the "Methodology" section should be combined with better sub-sections. Figures 3 and 4 are not thoroughly explained: the y-axis (presumably cumulative distribution function) is not stated, interpretation of steepness and intersecting curves is missing, and confidence intervals are shown for Fig 3a but not for 3b or 4. This makes claims about buffer size impact (e.g., n_b < 500 drop) questionable without confidence intervals.
- **Citation and formatting issues**: Incorrect use of textual vs parenthetical citations throughout; references use improper notation (§ instead of Sec.). The paper exceeds the 10-page limit (formatting violation). "Mujoco" should be "MuJoCo". Line 191 describes a stochastic policy but line 112 a deterministic mapping – contradiction.
- **Correction to numbers**: In Section 4, episodic returns are -4 and -6, not -3 and -5 as stated.
- **Missing discussions**: Related work on contextual MDPs could be relevant. Also, the connection to Distral (Teh et al., 2017) and Anand & Precup (2024) – which use KL constraint to a global policy or global value function – should be discussed as they induce similar regularization on past contexts.
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**: 
  - How is context information different from a task ID?
  - Does the A2C baseline use both context and state?
  - Can the proposed objective be approximated in PPO style to avoid second-order information?
  - What if reverse KL is used in Eq. 1's constraint?
  - What is the warm-up period and why is it needed?
  - Have you tried comparing LCPO with CPD (change point detection) as the detection mechanism?
  - Does the bolding in tables represent statistical significance (e.g., t-test)? The caption should clarify.
