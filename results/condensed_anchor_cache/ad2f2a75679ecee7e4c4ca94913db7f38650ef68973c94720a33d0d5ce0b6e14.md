## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper theoretically and empirically compares the expressive power of Graph Transformers (specifically GPS) and Message-Passing GNNs with Virtual Nodes (MPGNN+VNs) in both non-uniform (different network per graph size) and uniform (single network for all sizes) settings. In the non-uniform setting, with injective positional encodings, both models are universal approximators, but Reviewer 4 notes this is trivial given MLP universality and PE discrimination. The main contribution is in the uniform setting: neither model is a universal approximator, and they do not subsume each other. GTs cannot uniformly approximate unbounded summation (e.g., |V|²), while MPGNN+VNs cannot uniformly approximate functions that exploit the asymmetric weighting of softmax attention. Synthetic experiments verify the learnability of these distinguishing functions, and real-world benchmarks show mixed results with no clear ranking. The paper is well-organized (Reviewers 1, 4), though some reviewers found the introduction and proof paths unclear (Reviewer 2) and the real-data results partially redundant with prior work (Reviewer 2).

### Strengths

- **Novel comparison and first analysis of uniform setting:** Provides a novel and insightful comparison between GT and MPGNN+VNs in both non-uniform and uniform setups. The uniform setup is explored for the first time with this theoretical perspective, revealing that the two model classes do not subsume each other (Reviewer 1).
- **Crucial theoretical insights for graph learning:** The theoretical results help explain phenomena such as the superiority of GTs in some competitions, the surprising usefulness of the virtual node trick, and the existence of datasets where MPGNNs remain state-of-the-art (Reviewer 4).
- **Non‑trivial proofs of distinguishing functions:** The proofs identifying the unique functions (unbounded summation for VN, asymmetric neighbor weighting for SA) are non-trivial and provide insight into the core operations of these architectures (Reviewer 3).
- **Complementary synthetic experiments and code:** Numerical experiments on synthetic data complement and verify the theoretical findings; the code is attached (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Real‑world benchmark experiments:** On real-world datasets, MPGNN+VNs can be competitive with GTs in some cases, though this was already known in the literature (Reviewer 3).
- **Clear writing for some reviewers:** The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4); the main points are easily graspable (Reviewer 4).
- **Convincing experimental differences:** The differences between the two architectures are remarkable and convincingly demonstrated (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

**Writing and Presentation:**
- Many acronyms (“MP,” “VN,” “SA,” “PH,” “FF”) are used without explanation in Figure 1’s caption; the meanings of “l” and “r” in Figure 2(b) should be clarified. A table of all acronyms in the appendix is recommended (Reviewer 1).
- The notation “lr” in subsection 4.3 is confusing (can be misread as learning rate); it should instead be written as “l multiplied by r” or use a different symbol (Reviewer 1).
- Some values are missing from Table 1; the reason for blanks should be stated explicitly in the caption (Reviewer 1).
- Minor typos present, e.g., “Based on the the positional encoding LapPE” (Reviewer 1).
- The introduction is not easy to understand (Reviewer 2). The proof path is unclear; for example, the proof of Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4 in Section 4.2 is not understandable (Reviewer 2).

**Theoretical Assumptions and Scope:**
- The theoretical analysis focuses only on one variant of GTs (GPS) and MPGNN+VNs. Results could vary for other architectures within these families (e.g., linear attention, sparse attention) or with different positional encodings; these variations are not explored in depth (Reviewer 3).
- There is insufficient justification for why the scenario requiring injective positional encodings is important or realistic; a clear context is needed to ensure the results have practical value (Reviewer 2).
- The assumptions and limitations of the theory are unclear regarding task type: does the theory apply to node-level, edge-level, or solely graph-level tasks? The paper should explicitly state the intended scope (Reviewer 1).
- The strong assumption of the uniform setting may not align with practical cases (Reviewer 2). (Reviewer 4 also raises a related question: the synthetic experiment uses o.o.d. extrapolation (training on graphs ≤50 nodes, testing on >50) rather than i.i.d. generalization; the rationale behind this design choice and what it reveals about inductive bias needs clarification.)

**Experimental Results and Connection to Practice:**
- The message of Section 5.2 is vague and disconnected from the theory. Table 1 shows that the best performance appears somewhat random, making it unconvincing how theoretical insights can guide model selection in practice. A more thorough investigation linking theory to practice is needed—for example, analyzing datasets where properties align with theoretical strengths of either model and demonstrating improved performance (Reviewer 1). Relatedly, the differences and respective advantages should be connected to practical tasks on molecular graphs, where many public benchmarks exist (Reviewer 4).
- On some real-world datasets, MPGNN+VNs do not fully close the performance gap compared to GTs. It is unclear whether this limitation is fundamental or whether deeper MPGNN+VNs could match GTs. Alternative explanations (optimization, architecture choices, dataset nature) are not fully explored (Reviewer 3).
- The experimental results on real data are partly redundant with prior works (Tönshoff et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2023). The paper should either provide a novel perspective or build significantly upon these existing results (Reviewer 2).

**Triviality of Non-Uniform Results:**
- The non-uniform universality results are relatively trivial, as they follow straightforwardly from the combination of MLP’s universality and PE’s discrimination capacity (Reviewer 4). (This is a minority view; other reviewers did not highlight this.)

**Reviewer Questions Incorporated as Weaknesses:**
- Does the theory also cover different graph learning tasks (node, edge, graph)? (Reviewer 1, Q2)
- How can the theory be used to understand the results of Section 5.2? (Reviewer 1, Q3)
- What is the rationale behind the synthetic experiment design (o.o.d. extrapolation from graph size ≤50 training to >50 test)? Why is this setting chosen and what does it reveal about inductive bias? (Reviewer 4, Q)

**Contrasting Views:**
- While Reviewers 1 and 4 praised the clarity of writing, Reviewer 2 found the introduction and proof flow unclear.
- Reviewer 4 considered the non-uniform results trivial, whereas Reviewers 1 and 3 treated the uniform results as the main novel contribution.
- Reviewer 3 mentions that real-world results showing MPGNN+VN competitiveness are known, but still considers the theoretical contribution important. Reviewer 2 explicitly notes redundancy with prior works.
