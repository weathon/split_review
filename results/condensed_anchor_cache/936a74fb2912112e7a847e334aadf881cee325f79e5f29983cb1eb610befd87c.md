## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of how long-tailed data skews feature representations under contrastive learning (SCL loss), showing that tail class centers shrink and overlap. Based on this, it proposes FeatRecon, which generates synthetic features for tail classes within estimated "confidence supports" derived from head class statistics, and uses an iterative approach to reconstruct a symmetric, linearly separable feature space. Experiments on CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist 2018 show improvements over baselines like KCL, TSC, and BCL. The paper also introduces a class-wise temperature schedule.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical framework**: Multiple reviewers highlight the comprehensive theoretical analysis of long-tailed data's impact on feature geometry under contrastive learning (SCL loss), including conditions for tail class center shrinkage/collapse. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Method innovation**: FeatRecon is considered a novel method that reconstructs the feature space via synthetic feature generation and confidence support regularization, effectively addressing the identified issues. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Experimental validation**: Strong empirical results on multiple long-tailed benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100-LT, ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018) with detailed ablation studies and comparisons. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Presentation**: The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and includes clear illustrations (e.g., Figure 4). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Solid theoretical foundation for the method**: The method is motivated by a sound theoretical analysis, which adds rigor. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical assumptions and connection to method**:
  - The theoretical analysis relies on a simplified one-vs-all assumption with fixed head class sizes, not accounting for multiple simultaneous imbalances or varying head class distributions as in real long-tailed data. (Reviewer 1)
  - The link between the theoretical insights (feature center shrinkage/overlap) and the specific design of FeatRecon (synthetic feature generation within confidence supports) is not explicitly or strongly established. It is unclear how the size/shape of the confidence support is derived from theory to directly counteract the collapse. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
  - The notion of "optimal" representation is defined with respect to training loss, but its connection to test-time accuracy under long-tailed distribution (where classes are weighted equally) is not discussed; a reviewer asks for discussion of distribution shift in the theoretical part. (Reviewer 3, Q1)
  - The method's use of neighboring head class statistics to regularize tail classes lacks a clear intuition. (Reviewer 3, W1)

- **Experimental comparisons and settings**:
  - Missing comparisons with recent state-of-the-art methods, including GPaCo (TPAMI 2023), especially on large-scale datasets (ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018). Also missing comparisons with disentangled representation learning and meta-learning approaches. (Reviewers 1, 4)
  - Related work is incomplete; important decoupling methods (BBN, Bilateral-Branch, feature fusion), logit adjustment methods (Balanced Meta-Softmax, Gaussian clouded logit adjustment), and transfer learning (Heavy Fine-Tuning Hurts, ICML 2024) are not cited or discussed. Additionally, Menon et al. (ICLR 2021) is incorrectly cited as ICLR 2020. (Reviewer 2)
  - Experimental settings (backbone networks, training epochs, data augmentation) vary across datasets without clear justification, making it difficult to isolate the method's contribution from architectural or procedural differences. (Reviewer 1)

- **Novelty and relation to prior work**:
  - Synthesizing features for tail classes is not a novel idea; the paper needs to discuss differences and advantages over prior augmentation-based methods like ReMix and Feature Space Augmentation for Long-Tailed Data (ECCV 2020). (Reviewer 4)

- **Specific technical issues and clarifications**:
  - Feature visualization is insufficient: Figure 4 appears to be a diagram; actual t-SNE visualizations of training outcomes are needed to confirm that the feature space reconstruction aligns with expectations. (Reviewer 2, weakness 1)
  - In Figure 4, it is unclear why the distribution changes if empirical centers are fixed; the concept of "Real Distribution" and its alignment with confidence supports is not clearly explained. If training samples are already separated as in Fig 4(a), the final classifier might not change, contradicting the claimed improvement. (Reviewer 2, weakness 2)
  - There is an apparent contradiction: the claim that tail classes benefit from a smaller temperature τ, yet Eq. (11) assigns a larger temperature to tail classes. (Reviewer 4, weakness 4)
  - Minor typos: Eq. (11) has "N_min - N_min" should be "N_max - N_min"; Table 5 has inconsistent baseline references (row 3 loss relative to row 2, but rows 4-5 gains relative to row 1). (Reviewer 3, W2; Reviewer 4, weakness 5)
  - Sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., top-q head classes) is not properly analyzed. (Reviewer 3, Q3)
  - A reviewer asks whether better sampling strategies (e.g., MoCHi-style interpolation) could improve over uniform sampling in the confidence support. (Reviewer 3, Q2)

- **Minority contrast**: One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the theoretical contributions and experimental validation, while others (scores 6) raised significant concerns about the theoretical-method connection, novelty of feature augmentation, and missing comparisons. This divergence indicates that the paper's contributions are not uniformly convincing.
