## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces and analyzes a large class of stochastic component-wise soft-clipping schemes. Under standard assumptions (Lipschitz gradients, bounded variance) plus a non-standard third-moment bound, the authors prove convergence in expectation with rates for convex and non-convex cases, and almost sure convergence in the non-convex case. Experiments compare instantiations of the proposed class against Adam, SGD+Momentum, and Clipped-SGD on neural network training tasks. (Scores: 3,5,6,3,3; reviewer opinions vary.)

### Strengths
- **Novelty**: The class of methods combining soft-clipping with element-wise gradient updates is novel and the analysis covers a very general range of clipping schemes, making the proof useful to a broader literature (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). Reviewer 3 is more positive than the majority on originality.
- **Quality of theory**: The theoretical setting is well-posed, assumptions are clearly stated and not restrictive (Reviewer 1). Proofs look correct and of good quality (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5).
- **Clarity**: The paper is well written and easy to follow; key messages, context, and assumptions are clearly described (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5).
- **Significance**: The problem is of interest to the community because soft-clipping schemes are used in practice but have lacked a rigorous general analysis (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Experiments**: The numerical experiments are well done and show that soft-clipping algorithms can perform similarly to state-of-the-art methods, and may offer regularization benefits in overfitting scenarios (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 is more positive about experiments than others.
- **Convergence rates**: Proofs of convergence in expectation with rates for both convex and non-convex cases are provided (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and theoretical contribution**: The theoretical results are very similar to those already in Zhang et al. (2020a) for hard clipping, and that paper already explains how soft clipping generalizes; the additional value of more general clipping functions or element-wise clipping is not demonstrated (Reviewer 1). The comparison with existing literature (especially Zhang et al. 2020a, Eisenman & Stillfjord 2022) is insufficiently structured and does not clearly highlight what new advantages the proofs bring (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). 
- **Weakness of derived rates**: The non-convex convergence rate is only O(1/log K), much slower than standard SGD/hard clipping rates; Corollary 1 could be improved to O(1/√K) with constant step size (Reviewers 4, 5). When full gradients are used, Theorems 1 and 2 do not recover deterministic rates because B_i > 0 even then, raising concerns about tightness (Reviewer 4). The analysis under the given assumptions does not show any benefit over existing results (Reviewer 4).
- **Restrictive and unverified assumptions**: Assumption 6 (bounded third moment of iterates) is non-standard and strong; it is unclear whether it holds a priori, e.g., for a quadratic with linear noise having bounded variance but unbounded third moment (Reviewer 4). The authors rely on Eisenmann & Stillfjord (2022) which assumed bounded fourth moment, not a relaxation. The assumption effectively replaces step-size restrictions with a bound on iterates, not a real relaxation (Reviewer 4). Assumption 1 does not cover standard gradient clipping (second part fails) (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of experimental validation of theory**: No experiments verify the theoretical results (Theorem 1, Corollary 1, Theorem 2); a simple quadratic test to check gradient norm decay against bounds is suggested (Reviewer 1). Experiments do not show the proposed methods outperforming standard ones (Reviewer 4). There is no comparison of each component-wise method with its non-component-wise counterpart (Reviewers 1, 5). The curves in Figures 1 and 2 lack confidence bars and would benefit from log-scale (Reviewer 1). Colors across figures should be consistent (Reviewer 1). 
- **Missing motivation and intuition**: The paper does not justify why or when soft-clipping (or component-wise soft-clipping) is better than hard clipping or vanilla SGD (Reviewers 4, 5). The differentiability argument for favoring soft clipping over hard clipping is not substantiated – hard clipping is non-differentiable but widely effective (Reviewer 5). The motivation for the new special clipping schemes in Appendix A (Examples 2–4) is not provided; they appear to give similar curves and no experimental advantage is shown (Reviewer 3). There is no intuitive explanation of the theoretical results or the meaning of the term w_k(w) in Corollary 2 (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons and ablation requests** (questions that overlap with criticisms): 
  - Compare each component-wise scheme with its non-component-wise version (Reviewers 1, 5).
  - Systematically compare the method and results with Eisenman & Stillfjord (2022) and Zhang et al. (2020a) in a table (assumptions, convergence rates, special cases) (Reviewer 3).
  - Verify theoretical bounds empirically on a simple quadratic (Reviewer 1).
  - Test whether the method retrieves the strongly-convex results of Eisenman & Stillfjord (2022) (Reviewer 3).
  - Provide intuition on why ∑α_k^2 < ∞ and ∑α_k = ∞ are assumed (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues**: The paper needs restructuring – add an Introduction and Related Work section; move Section 1 (Setting) and non-key lemmas to appendix; move experimental technical details to appendix; typos listed (Reviewer 1).
