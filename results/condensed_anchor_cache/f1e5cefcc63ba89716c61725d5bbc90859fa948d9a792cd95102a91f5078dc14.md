## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Temporal Representation Alignment (TRA), a self-supervised contrastive loss that augments goal-conditioned behavioral cloning to improve compositional generalization. TRA aligns temporal representations of states, goals, and language instructions, enabling agents to execute novel multi-step tasks without explicit planning or reinforcement learning. Evaluated on BridgeData tabletop manipulation tasks with real-world robot experiments, TRA shows improvements on compositionally novel tasks specified via goal images or language instructions compared to baselines like AWR, LCBC, and GRIF.

### Strengths

*   **Innovative representation learning for compositionality:** TRA introduces a creative approach that structures temporal alignment of representations to achieve compositional generalization without requiring explicit planning or RL (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). It is a simple extension to existing policy frameworks (Reviewer 2).
*   **Zero-shot compositionality:** The method generalizes to unseen task combinations without retraining, a notable achievement for scaling real-world applications (Reviewer 1).
*   **Strong real-world robot results:** TRA demonstrates substantial performance gains on multiple real-world manipulation tasks (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 notes the large effort behind these experiments.
*   **Comprehensive empirical evaluation across task types:** The paper evaluates TRA on multiple task settings (e.g., compositional long-horizon, semantic generalization, multi-instruction, object-level instruction following) and compares against several baselines (Reviewer 3).
*   **Clear ablation studies:** Reviewer 3 finds the ablations validate the importance of the temporal alignment component. (Note: Reviewer 1 disagrees, see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses

*   **Limited task complexity and environmental variation:** Tasks are largely simple manipulations (mostly pick-and-place) in a highly controlled environment. Generalization to more complex, multi-agent, or highly varied real-world scenarios is not demonstrated (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
*   **Insufficient experimental validation:**
    *   Evaluation is restricted to a single real-world kitchen dataset (BridgeData) with no testing in reproducible simulation benchmarks (e.g., CALVIN, SIMPLER) that would enable fair comparison with future work (Reviewer 2). Given the large reported performance gains (e.g., +60% over second-best baselines in some settings), independent verification is needed (Reviewer 2).
    *   Table 1 reports trajectory prediction metrics, which can be misleading and do not clearly correlate with actual rollout success rates (Reviewer 2). Full rollout results are necessary.
    *   Missing comparison with recent LLM/VLM-based task decomposition methods such as RT-H (Reviewer 3).
    *   Missing comparison with approaches that combine VLM/LLM-based task decomposition with foundation models like Octo or OpenVLA for sub-task execution (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). No quantitative comparisons or concrete examples of TRA’s advantages over these methods are provided (Reviewer 3).
    *   No ablation study distinguishing object-level vs. task-level instruction following (e.g., “move the bell pepper to the bottom right of the table” vs. “move the bell pepper to the bottom left of the table”). The policy may overfit to action sequences in the replay buffer rather than learning true instruction following (Reviewer 1).
    *   No ablation on the discount factor γ in the temporal alignment objective, leaving sensitivity to this hyperparameter unclear (Reviewer 3).
    *   The reported performance for GRIF on “put the spoon on the towel” (0.2) differs significantly from the original GRIF paper (0.9); the discrepancy is not explained (Reviewer 2).
*   **Missing theoretical foundation:** No theoretical justification is provided for why temporal alignment of states should enable compositional generalization. The claim relies on a single dataset’s empirical evidence (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
*   **Dependence on goal representation quality:** Success heavily depends on the quality and specificity of goal representations, which may require fine-tuning for certain task types (Reviewer 1).
*   **Writing and clarity issues:**
    *   Incomplete sentence in Chapter 3 disrupts flow (Reviewer 2).
    *   Complex, run-on sentences throughout make technical content difficult to understand (Reviewer 2).
    *   The loss function lacks clear explanation and intuitive justification (Reviewer 2).
*   **Comparison omissions:**
    *   Reviewer 1 considers the baselines poor, specifically questioning why diffusion policies for imitation learning were not included.
    *   The alignment method is very similar to VIP (Value Implicit Pre-training) but the paper provides no description or comparison, only a citation (Reviewer 1).
*   **Missing analysis of computational efficiency:** The computation overhead of the proposed method compared to baselines or LLM-based approaches is not reported (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
*   **Minority reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 3) finds the ablation studies missing and the baselines poor, while Reviewer 3 (score 6) considers the evaluation comprehensive and ablations clear. Reviewer 2 (score 5) echoes concerns about insufficient experimental validation and theoretical grounding. The paper does not address the risk of the policy replaying buffer sequences rather than learning compositional generalization (Reviewer 1), a point not discussed by others.
