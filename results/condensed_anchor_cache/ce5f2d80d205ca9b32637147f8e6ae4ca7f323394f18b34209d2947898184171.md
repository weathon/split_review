## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the strong dependence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) on accurate Structure-from-Motion (SfM) initialization. The authors identify “limited Gaussian transportability” as the cause of poor optimization from random or noisy point clouds, and propose RAIN-GS with three modifications: (1) initializing Gaussians with a small number of large-variance Gaussians (SLV, N=100K), (2) a progressive low-pass filter during training, and (3) an adaptive bound-expanding split (ABE-Split) heuristic. Experiments on Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, RealEstate 10K, and Deep Blending show that RAIN-GS with random initialization performs on par with or better than standard 3DGS with SfM initialization. The method is simple to implement and yields consistent improvements, especially on tanks-and-temples and Mip-NeRF360.

### Strengths
- **Relevance and impact**: Relaxing the initialization constraint for 3DGS is a critical and meaningful problem. RAIN-GS demonstrates impressive performance without requiring SfM point clouds, providing an efficient solution (Reviewer 4, strong positive).
- **Performance improvement**: The method yields clear quantitative and qualitative improvements over original 3DGS on multiple datasets, especially on Tanks & Temples and Mip-NeRF360. In some cases it even surpasses Colmap+3DGS (Reviewers 2, 3, strong positive; Reviewer 1 notes “small but consistent improvements”).
- **Simplicity and ease of integration**: The proposed modifications are straightforward to add to existing 3DGS pipelines (Reviewer 2).
- **Sound motivation and analysis**: The paper provides a well-organized analysis linking the problem to limited Gaussian transportability, and the design encourages Gaussians to first learn global structure before details (Reviewers 1, 3). The analysis is clearly written (Reviewer 3).
- **Exhaustive experiments**: The authors provide extensive quantitative and qualitative comparisons and an ablation study measuring the benefit of each component in isolation (Reviewers 1, 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaimed novelty and insufficient insight**: The claim of “first in-depth analysis” is overstated; prior work already investigated local minima in 3DGS optimization. The analysis in Section 4.2 is an empirical study on Mip-NeRF360 without deeper insight extending beyond existing literature (Reviewer 1, negative, strong). One reviewer (Reviewer 1) rates the contribution low (2) and soundness low (2), contrasting with others.
- **Lack of theoretical novelty and guarantees**: The design is largely derived from experimental observations. No formal guarantee of escaping local minima is provided, and key claims should be toned down. Many hyperparameters require tuning, and detailed ablation on those hyperparameters is missing (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing comparisons with recent 3DGS variants**: As an ICLR submission, it is not reasonable to compare only with 3DGS and Mip-Splatting. Comparisons with ScaffoldGS (CVPR24) or other improvements at ECCV24/NIPS24 are needed, especially when those methods are also randomly initialized (Reviewer 4, strong negative). Additionally, the method’s effectiveness on other 3DGS variants (e.g., scaffold-gs) is unexplored (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear source of improvement and insufficient validation of motivation**: The paper identifies limited Gaussian transportability as the cause, but does not clearly show where RAIN-GS’s improvement comes from. The authors should compare RAIN-GS with standard 3DGS in both Table 2 and Figure 2 to validate the motivation and effectiveness (Reviewer 4). The claim that photometric loss is the main cause is not supported – the same loss is used without modification (Reviewer 1).
- **Assumption of accurate camera poses**: The method assumes accurate poses (obtained from sensors or SfM), yet the primary scenario for random initialization (no SfM points) does not eliminate the need for pose estimation. The paper does not analyze how pose errors affect reconstruction or whether the method can handle them. If poses come from sensors, how much error can be tolerated? Should poses be optimized to improve robustness? (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing experiments and ablations**:
  - No evaluation on texture-less areas (Reviewer 2).  
  - The progressive low-pass filter may be equivalent to a coarse-to-fine training strategy; a fair comparison with that baseline is missing (Reviewer 2).  
  - In Table 5, combining SLV, low-pass filter, and ABE-Split is shown, but an experiment combining low-pass filter, N=100K, and ABE-Split is missing (Reviewer 4).  
  - The source of improvement (edge/distant regions) in Figures 1 and 3 is not explained (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited technical depth and minor issues**: The paper lacks sufficient technical detail, e.g., on the ABE-Split heuristic – it is a variation of existing splitting strategies without justification of why this specific criterion is used (Reviewer 1). A typo exists: “were propose” → “we propose” (Reviewer 1).
- **Need for additional validation**: The method should be validated in scenarios where multi-view images come from generative models (text-to-3D) (Reviewer 3). The relation to recent monocular reconstruction (Dust3R, DepthPro) is not discussed (Reviewer 3). It is also unclear whether the suboptimal results are due to batch size 1 optimization; a parallel training paradigm like GrendelGS might resolve the issue (Reviewer 4, question).
