## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CAD-Editor, a text-based CAD editing approach that uses LLMs (LLaMA3-8b-instruct) for sequence-to-sequence generation. It employs a two-stage fine-tuning: first on a large-scale synthetic dataset generated by using design variation models (SkexGen, Hnc-CAD) on DeepCAD and GPT-4o for captions, then on a selective dataset filtered via D-CLIP or human evaluation. Experiments on DeepCAD show CAD-Editor outperforms baselines including SkexGen, Hnc-CAD, and GPT-4o on multiple metrics.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (all reviewers).
- The problem of text-based CAD editing is underexplored and industrially valuable (R1, R3).
- Innovatively transforms CAD editing into a sequence-to-sequence problem, leveraging LLMs (R2).
- The multi-stage training strategy (synthetic then selective data) effectively improves performance (R2, R3).
- The proposed dataset for instruction-based CAD editing is novel and useful (R3).
- The model-based data selection method (D-CLIP/human) is innovative and enhances dataset quality (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Generalization is a major concern: the model is only optimized on the DeepCAD dataset; the synthetic data also comes from models trained on DeepCAD, raising risk of overfitting to this specific domain (e.g., automotive) and poor performance on other industries (e.g., aerospace, construction) (R2).
- Reliance on GPT-4o for data generation: token costs are not discussed, and the quality depends on GPT-4o accurately capturing CAD operations, which may introduce biases or inaccuracies (R2). The method for automatically excluding instructions with more than three edits (line 259) is not explained (R1).
- D-CLIP evaluation and data selection are insufficiently validated: it is unclear whether D-CLIP was pretrained or fine-tuned on the proposed dataset; if so, there is a risk of overfitting and evaluation that does not generalize (R3). The authors should provide examples of D-CLIP scores with corresponding text/image pairs to demonstrate metric effectiveness (R1).
- Missing comparison: the authors did not try fine-tuning baseline text-to-CAD models (e.g., SkexGen, Hnc-CAD) instead of LLaMA3-8b-instruct for the editing task (R1).
- The model's performance under vague, ambiguous, or erroneous instructions is not evaluated; no mechanism to identify or correct such inputs is discussed (R2).
- A minor point: the claim "without control over the final appearance" (line 69) requires clarification (R1).

Note: One reviewer (R3, score 6) was more positive overall, especially regarding the dataset and data selection, while other reviewers (R1, R2, scores 5) had stronger concerns about generalization, GPT-4o dependency, and D-CLIP validation.
