## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of CATCH’s frequency patching and Channel Fusion Module for multivariate time series anomaly detection, with generally good presentation and open-source code. However, they identify several weaknesses: unclear module interactions and insufficient motivation for design choices (instance normalization, CFM masking, TFRM objectives), lack of comparisons with relevant frequency-based baselines (SR-CNN, PFT, TFAD, Dual-TF) and classic datasets (ASD, SWaT), incomplete parameter guidance, unvalidated claims about detecting frequency-specific anomalies, and editorial issues. Scores range from 5 to 8 (mean 6.25); one reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive about the innovation and experimental results, while the others (scores 5–6) are more critical of positioning, clarity, and evaluation rigor.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and clearly organized, with good presentation quality (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Figures are of high quality, effectively communicating complex concepts and results; some figures (e.g., architecture diagrams) aid understanding (reviewers 1, 2).
- The idea of frequency patching for fine-grained frequency characteristics is novel in the context of multivariate time series anomaly detection, and the Channel Fusion Module (CFM) with patch-wise masking and bi-level optimization represents a creative combination of existing ideas and new methodologies (reviewers 2, 4; reviewer 3 notes it addresses limitations).
- CATCH is verified by extensive experiments on multiple real-world and synthetic datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance with commendable results (reviewers 1, 4).
- Open-source code and datasets on GitHub ensure good reproducibility (reviewers 1, 2).
- The integration of frequency and time domain analysis holds promise for impactful applications (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear module interactions and architecture flow:** The roles and relationships of the Mask Generator, Channel-Masked Transformer Layer, and Time-Frequency Reconstruction Module (TFRM) are not cohesively explained; the flow of data and transformations between these modules is unclear, and the overall system functionality is hard to follow (reviewer 3). The left side of Figure 2 is disorganized, obscuring input data flow (reviewer 4). There is no clear explanation of how the outputs of one module serve as inputs to the next (reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient motivation for key operations:** The necessity of instance normalization before frequency patching, the criteria for mask generation in CFM (how masks are generated, which channels are masked, and why this improves channel correlation discovery), and the reason for using both time-domain and frequency-domain reconstruction losses in TFRM are not adequately justified (reviewer 3). Furthermore, the anomaly scores (freq-score and time-score in Figure 3) lack corresponding textual or formulaic explanation in the relevant sections (reviewer 4). The paper states that frequency characteristics follow a long-tailed distribution with most information in low-frequency bands, but the rationale for focusing on high-frequency details is not convincing, especially since Figure 1(a) shows that low-frequency bands already exhibit significant differences between normal and anomalous data (reviewer 4).
- **Missing and insufficient experimental comparisons:** The evaluation omits comparison with several relevant frequency-based methods (SR-CNN, PFT, TFAD, Dual-TF) despite mentioning them; including these baselines would better demonstrate CATCH’s contributions (reviewer 2). Classic datasets such as ASD and SWaT are not tested (reviewer 4). Additionally, the tested datasets are not the most common ones used by the baselines, raising questions about fair comparison and generalizability (reviewer 1). The multi-metric results in Table 2 only illustrate performance on three datasets; all results should be included in the appendix for completeness (reviewer 4).
- **Central claim about superior detection of frequency-specific anomalies is not rigorously validated:** There is no dedicated experimental design (e.g., synthetic datasets with known frequency-specific anomalies or performance analysis across frequency bands) to verify this capability (reviewer 2).
- **Lack of parameter settings and guidance:** The paper does not provide sufficient parameter settings or guidance on choosing hyperparameters, weakening reproducibility and practical applicability (reviewer 1).
- **Overclaim from Table 1:** Table 1 shows CATCH outperforming all baselines on all datasets, which raises the question of whether the method can win on any dataset/application and whether the test setup is unbiased (reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation protocol ambiguity:** It is unclear whether point adjustment was used when assessing algorithm performance (reviewer 4). The hierarchical classification of anomalies (contextual/global considered point anomalies? subsequence types?) is not clarified; the relationships between anomaly categories need further explanation (reviewer 3, line 369).
- **Unclear definitions and context:** The term “heterogeneous subsequence anomalies” (line 53) is not specifically defined (reviewer 3). Sections 2.2 and 2.3 could benefit from definitional discussions of channels and frequency domain information in the MTSAD problem (reviewer 3). The paper does not adequately contrast how the frequency patching approach differs from existing frequency-domain analysis methods (reviewer 2).
- **Editorial and formatting issues:** There are typos (e.g., “results” in the abstract; “Moudles” instead of “modules” in line 161), incomplete references (e.g., parentheses missing in line 185, some citations are preprints rather than published versions), and alignment problems between text and images (line 320) (reviewers 2, 3). The mathematical exposition in lines 230–245 is overwhelming due to an abundance of symbols without proper first-use definitions (reviewer 3).
- **Complexity of CFM:** While innovative, the CFM is complex and would benefit from more intuitive explanations or diagrams to help readers understand how it operates (reviewer 4). Moreover, the paper does not sufficiently highlight the innovation behind the CFM, particularly the necessity of the masking mechanism and its impact on detection performance (reviewer 3). The adaptive nature of the CFM is not well-justified (reviewer 3). The combination of temporal and frequency scores in Figure 5 is not justified; examples where one compensates for the other would better illustrate its effectiveness (reviewer 3).
