## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Flow Distillation Sampling (FDS) to improve geometric accuracy and rendering quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) in sparse-view settings. FDS samples unobserved views near input views, then uses optical flow from a pre-trained matching model (Prior Flow) to supervise the flow analytically derived from the 3DGS geometry (Radiance Flow). The authors claim this matching prior provides absolute scale, unlike monocular priors, and mitigates overfitting. Experiments are conducted on the SceneNet, MuSHRoom, ScanNet, and Replica datasets, with ablations and visualizations of mutual flow refinement.

### Strengths

1. The core idea is intuitive and simple: incorporating a matching prior (optical flow) provides absolute scale information, in contrast to monocular priors that suffer from scale ambiguity. This offers a promising direction for sparse-view reconstruction. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; Reviewer 2 rates idea "interesting")

2. The method is well-structured, with clear explanations of the FDS technique, adaptive camera sampling, and loss functions. Figures 1 and 2 are well done and make the approach easy to understand. (Reviewers 1, 2)

3. Comprehensive experimental evaluations are conducted across multiple indoor datasets (SceneNet, MuSHRoom, ScanNet, Replica), demonstrating effectiveness and robustness. (Reviewer 1)

4. Interpretive experiments illustrate the mutual refinement process between Prior Flow and Radiance Flow, enhancing understanding of the method's capability. (Reviewers 1, 3)

5. Ablation studies clearly show the benefit of the matching prior over monocular depth priors and even multi-view depth priors (Table 3), and confirm that FDS avoids scale ambiguity. A clear visualization of the mutual refinement is provided. (Reviewer 3)

6. FDS leverages the matching prior to mitigate the overfitting problem and enhance geometry. The related works section covers all relevant geometry-reconstruction-based 3DGS works. (Reviewers 2, 4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Reliance on prior flow quality and stability** – The results are constrained by the initial quality of the Prior Flow. The method does not explicitly address scenarios where optical flow estimation is inaccurate due to large occlusions or significant viewpoint changes between input and sampled views. Both Prior Flow and Radiance Flow suffer from inaccuracies (line 252), raising concerns about the stability of the benefits – the loss could introduce artifacts or incorrect guidance. It is unclear why FDS significantly outperforms multi-view depth supervision, which does not suffer from an inaccurate prior flow. More explanation and analysis are needed. (Reviewers 1, 3; Reviewer 1 high confidence)

2. **Limited evaluation diversity** – Experiments are limited to indoor scenes (MuSHRoom, ScanNet, Replica, SceneNet). The method lacks evaluation on widely used geometry and novel view synthesis benchmarks such as DTU, Tanks and Temples, and Mip-NeRF360. This raises concerns about robustness and generalizability to outdoor scenes or complex geometry/lighting. Without such benchmarks, the claim of state-of-the-art performance is less convincing. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3; Reviewer 1 and 2 both note this)

3. **Missing comparisons with neural field baselines** – The evaluation focuses solely on 3DGS-based methods. Comparisons to neural field based methods such as Geo-NeuS or NeuralAngelo would significantly strengthen the claim of state-of-the-art performance. (Reviewer 2)

4. **Insufficient ablation studies** – Several hyperparameters and design choices need more systematic investigation:
   * Depth-adaptive radius uses a hyperparameter σ (Eq. 8): How is σ determined? Is FDS robust to different σ values? (Reviewer 4)
   * How is the weight λ_fds for the FDS loss determined? (Reviewer 4)
   * How is the start iteration for applying FDS (e.g., 15,000) determined? (Reviewer 4)
   * Is FDS must require the normal consistency loss L_n? In Table 3, what is the performance of “2DGS+FDS” alone (without the normal prior)? (Reviewers 3, 4)
   * The depth distortion loss in 2DGS degraded results, but no evidence or explanation is provided for why this happened or how it influenced the quantitative comparison to 2DGS. (Reviewer 2)

5. **Inconsistent results and missing details** – The metrics in Table 2 and Table 3 for ScanNet appear inconsistent regarding 2DGS+FDS: does the ScanNet result in Table 2 use the normal prior or not? Why do both monodepth and multi-view depth supervision seem to worsen results in Table 3? (Reviewer 3) Visual comparison on the ScanNet dataset is lacking. (Reviewer 4) The Replica dataset is mentioned but experimental results are not presented. (Reviewer 1)

6. **Occlusion handling** – Radiance Flow maps pixels from source view to target view. For pixels in the target view containing splatted Gaussians that are occluded in the source view, how is the Radiance Flow computed? (Reviewer 2)

7. **Computational complexity not reported** – Training and inference times are not reported or compared against other methods, making it difficult to assess practical applicability, especially in resource-constrained environments. Additionally, given that RAFT is computed at every time step, how does the training time of 2DGS+FDS compare to 2DGS? (Reviewers 1, 3)

8. **Sensitivity to the optical flow model** – The paper does not explore sensitivity to different optical flow models or potential domain mismatch between the training data of the optical flow model and target scenes. This reliance on a specific pre-trained model limits applicability. (Reviewer 1)

9. **Presentation issues** – The conclusion is unspecific and contains general claims. There are inconsistent capitalizations of 'Gaussian'. Equation 2 does not explain \hat{\alpha}. The related work section on prior regulation for rendering contains grammatical errors. Algorithm 1 has notation errors. These issues need careful review and correction. (Reviewers 1, 2)

10. **Detaching the optical flow** – In line 267, how does detaching the optical flow influence overall performance? (Reviewer 2)
