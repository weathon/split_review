## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a method for warping Gaussian noise while preserving its Gaussian distributional properties, addressing the problem of generating temporally correlated noise for video applications using image-based diffusion models. The authors formulate a noise representation called ∫-noise (integral noise), which reinterprets pixel values as integrals of an underlying infinite-resolution noise field. They derive a noise transport equation and propose a method involving conditional upscaling, warping, and downscaling to advect noise across frames while preserving correlations and noise statistics. The method is evaluated on tasks including video restoration, surrogate rendering (SDEdit), pose-to-person, and conditional video generation, demonstrating improved temporal coherence.

### Strengths

- The problem is well motivated and of significant practical impact for video editing and generation using image diffusion models (R1, R2, R3).
- The explanation of why conventional warp operations fail is clear, and the proposed solution is mathematically grounded and technically sound (R1, R3). The theoretical analysis is deep and comprehensive, including the Brownian sheet model and conditional Gaussian upscaling (R3, R4).
- The method is well illustrated, particularly in Figure 2 (R4), and the paper is well written (R2).
- Video results in the supplementary material show reduced flickering and texture-sticking artifacts compared to baselines (R2, R3).
- Evaluation covers multiple tasks (rerendering, pose-to-person, video restoration) with good video illustrations (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Assumption of diffeomorphic warping**: The method relies on the warping field being a diffeomorphism, but optical flow estimated by off-the-shelf methods is rarely invertible, potentially causing over- or under-sampled regions and artifacts. The paper does not explicitly address handling non-invertible mappings, disocclusions, or significant motion (R3).
- **Sensitivity to optical flow errors**: Accurate optical flow is required; errors lead to temporal misalignment and artifacts. The experiments (e.g., super-resolution and JPEG restoration) use degraded video for flow estimation, which may not represent complex real-world scenarios with occlusions or large motions. A more thorough analysis of sensitivity to flow errors is needed (R1).
- **Limited impact on latent diffusion models**: As stated in Appendix E.3, the method’s impact is negatively correlated with the amount of conditioning and is limited for latent diffusion models. This is a significant limitation since many powerful generative models are latent diffusion models, and the method’s scope is narrower as a result (R3). Reviewer 4 also questions applicability to other pixel-level models like DeepFloyd (R4).
- **Trade-off between temporal coherence and frame-wise quality**: According to Table 1, the proposed method improves temporal coherence but worsens frame-wise perceptual metrics such as LPIPS and FID. This trade-off indicates possible sacrifice of individual frame visual fidelity for temporal stability (R3).
- **Missing comparison with alternative methods for correlated noise**: The paper does not provide a detailed comparison or visual results against DDIM inversion (which can generate correlated noise maps deterministically). It is unclear whether ∫-noise offers a significant advantage over simpler deterministic methods, especially considering computational overhead (R1). Additionally, a comparison with Control-A-Video (which also uses input video) is only shown for the fluid dynamics task; a comprehensive comparison on more tasks is needed (R3).
- **Missing comparison with dedicated video models**: The method is applied zero-shot with image models. It is unclear how it compares against models trained specifically for video (e.g., PYoCo, Zeroscope Text-to-Video, Dreampose). One reviewer questions whether image model + ∫-noise can close the gap with these video models (R4).
- **Computational inefficiency**: The method is computationally less efficient than other noise handling techniques. Quantitative comparisons of computational cost are not provided, which would be helpful for future research (R2, R3).
- **Clarity of Figure 5**: The illustration in Figure 5 is difficult to interpret. The explanation that Random Noise creates incoherent details and Fixed Noise has sticking artifacts while ∫-noise moves fluid more smoothly is not easily mapped to the shown results. The authors should highlight specific image content to support these conclusions (R2).
- **Missing experiments on training video models**: Most experiments are zero-shot. It remains to be seen whether ∫-noise helps when training a video model like PYoCo (R4).
- **Derivation details**: Some implementation details are unclear:
  - How are the 9 triangulation points warped in Figure 1a, and how is the `WARP_\infty` operation (Algorithm 2) implemented given optical flow at image resolution? (R3)
  - The transition from continuous Eq. 24 to discrete Eq. 5 and the justification for first-order approximation could use more explanation (R3).
  - An explicit proof that Z is a Gaussian vector could improve clarity (R3). 
  - One reviewer suggests exploring a small modification of bilinear interpolation with square-root weighting to preserve variance, noting that Eq. 5 uses similar variance-preserving coefficients; this baseline was not examined (R3).

Note: One reviewer (R4) is more cautious, rating the paper lower (6) due to concerns about zero-shot nature, limited impact on latent diffusion, and missing comparisons with video models. This contrast is reflected in the weaknesses above, particularly regarding the method’s practical scope and the trade-offs. All three other reviewers rated it highly (8), acknowledging the problem’s importance and technical soundness.
