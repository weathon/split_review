## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper evaluates the adversarial robustness of seven self-supervised learning (SSL) models—Barlow Twins, BYOL, DINO, MoCoV3, SimCLR, SwAV, and VICReg—alongside a supervised ResNet50 baseline across ImageNet classification, transfer learning (linear evaluation), segmentation, and detection. It investigates the impact of architectural choices (ResNet50 with limited ViT usage), training duration, and attack types (instance adversarial and universal adversarial perturbations). The authors find SSL models generally more robust than supervised on ImageNet, with this advantage less pronounced in segmentation and detection, and show that contrastive SSL (e.g., MoCoV3) can be as robust as non-contrastive methods. However, multiple reviewers raise concerns about experimental design, missing methods, limited depth, and insufficient analysis.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive model and task coverage**: The paper evaluates seven SSL models plus a supervised baseline across classification, transfer learning, segmentation, and detection, providing a broad view of SSL robustness across vision tasks.
- **Diverse attack types**: Authors employ both instance-level adversarial attacks and universal adversarial perturbations, helping to identify which SSL methods are robust under different attack conditions.
- **Task-specific robustness insights**: The study highlights that SSL robustness does not carry over equally to all tasks (e.g., segmentation and detection), informing users about where SSL may be less reliable.
- **Contrastive vs. non-contrastive nuance**: The finding that contrastive SSL (e.g., MoCoV3) can be as robust as non-contrastive methods in some cases challenges the common belief that non-contrastive methods are always better, offering a balanced view for model selection.
- **Investigation of architecture and training**: Exploration of architectural choices and training durations adds depth, showing that longer training may offer slight improvements without compromising robustness.
- **Exquisite visualizations** (reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and writing quality** are good (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Freezing backbone for detection/segmentation is problematic** (Reviewer 1): Standard practice is end-to-end training, not freezing the backbone. Since end-to-end training alters weights significantly, any observed robustness cannot be attributed solely to the pretraining algorithm. This undermines the claims on segmentation and detection tasks.
2. **Limited architectural exploration** (Reviewers 1, 2): The study focuses almost exclusively on ResNet50, with only limited ViT usage. Reviewer 2 asks: why are only ResNet50 results provided if the impact of architecture on robustness is discussed? Testing more architectures and sizes is needed.
3. **Only white-box attacks** (Reviewer 1): Excluding black-box and grey-box attacks limits the comprehensiveness of the robustness evaluation.
4. **Transfer learning only uses linear evaluation** (Reviewer 1): Real-world scenarios often involve fine-tuning, so the absence of fine-tuning results limits broader applicability.
5. **Missing key SSL methods** (Reviewers 1, 2): MAE and DINOv2 are not included. Reviewer 2 specifically notes that only joint-embedding methods are studied; mask modeling (e.g., MAE) and generative models are missing, which is a significant gap given the paper’s scope.
6. **Lack of statistical significance** (Reviewer 3): The statistical significance of robustness differences—especially where SSL and supervised models show minimal variation—is not consistently addressed.
7. **Limited depth in segmentation/detection setups** (Reviewers 1, 3): Reviewer 1 notes missing COCO dataset (the standard benchmark for detection), limited details on attack objectives (e.g., object vanishing, fabrication, mislabeling), and no exploration of poisoning/backdoor attacks. Reviewer 3 adds that limited exploration of specific setups restricts generalizability and yields shallow insights.
8. **Augmentation not deeply explored** (Reviewer 1): The authors mention augmentation could play a role but do not test different strategies.
9. **Novelty concern** (Reviewer 1): The finding that SSL models are generally more robust than supervised ones has been explored in prior work (Goldblum et al., NeurIPS 2024).
10. **Insufficient experimental data for a review article** (Reviewer 2): The paper presents itself as covering many methods but the data is still insufficient; lacks in-depth analysis and could provide at least some visualizations of inner workings.
11. **Missing discussion on natural corruptions** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not address robustness to distribution shifts and corruptions (referencing Chhipa et al., ICCVW 2023; Zhong et al., arXiv 2022).
12. **Source code not provided** (Reviewer 1), hampering reproducibility.
13. **Figure 2 abbreviation** (Reviewer 2): “MoCo v3” should be more precisely written as “MoCo v3”.

**Questions that overlap with criticisms (treated as weaknesses):**
- **Attack objectives for detection/segmentation** (Reviewer 1): Which objectives were used (e.g., object vanishing, fabrication, mislabeling)? Different objectives may affect robustness.
- **Epsilon and step size for PGD** (Reviewer 1): In Table 2, is epsilon 1/255 or 1/1? What step size was used?
- **Hyperparameter impact** (Reviewer 1): Are there insights on how learning rate, batch size, etc. affect SSL robustness in this study?
- **Root cause analysis** (Reviewer 3): For insights 2, 3, and 4 in the introduction, can the authors provide more in-depth root cause analysis? Otherwise the paper is a pure measurement technical report.
- **Difference from other review articles** (Reviewer 2): Can the authors discuss how this article differs from similar reviews on SSL robustness?

**Contrast between reviewers**: Reviewer 2 (score 3, low confidence) is substantially more critical, stating the experimental data is insufficient and the paper lacks depth, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 5) are more positive but still raise significant methodological and coverage concerns. All three agree that the paper has weaknesses in experimental design and analysis depth.
