## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces ELFS (Effective Label-Free Coreset Selection), a method that uses pseudo-labels from deep clustering to approximate training dynamics and estimate data difficulty scores without ground truth labels. A double-end pruning technique mitigates distribution shift caused by pseudo-label noise. ELFS is evaluated on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and ImageNet-1K, consistently outperforming existing label-free coreset selection baselines (e.g., outperforming D2 by up to 10.2% on ImageNet-1K when using SwAV) and approaching the accuracy of supervised selection methods. The motivation is solid and the idea of estimating difficulty through clustering is elegant.

### Strengths

- Solid motivation that aligns with the conference; clear writing that effectively conveys the idea (Reviewer 4).
- Elegant and effective idea to estimate data difficulty scores via deep clustering, handling the challenge of measuring prediction uncertainty without labels (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Double-end pruning improves selection of informative samples and yields consistent performance gains over baselines, including in challenging scenarios (Reviewer 1).
- ELFS significantly outperforms existing label-free baselines across multiple vision benchmarks (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, ImageNet-1K) at various pruning rates (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Notably, many baselines even fail to beat random sampling (Tab. 1) (Reviewer 4).
- Extensive ablation studies justify each module of the design (Reviewers 2, 4). The method shows that including harder examples improves model performance, and ELFS effectively prioritizes such examples via double-end pruning (Reviewer 1).
- One reviewer notes strong robustness and transferability across datasets and model architectures (Reviewer 3), though this point is contested by another reviewer (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Hyperparameter search and computational cost**: The hard pruning rate and the hyperparameter β are optimized via grid search. The optimal values differ across datasets and sampling ratios (Fig. 5), making grid search inefficient for large datasets (e.g., millions of images). The paper lacks analysis of how β interacts with pseudo-label quality and how the optimal pruning rate relates to the distribution of easy/hard examples. A more principled or efficient setting is needed. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Dependence on pretrained encoders and domain shift**: The method relies on feature extractors like SwAV and DINO for clustering. It is unclear if more advanced encoders (e.g., CLIP) would further improve pseudo-labels or stability. More critically, the evaluated datasets are too easy for these pretrained encoders (many images may be included during pretraining). Evaluation on out-of-distribution benchmarks (e.g., medical image datasets) is necessary to test whether the method works when visual features are not already well-aligned. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Transferability and architecture bias**: Table 7 shows that ResNet50 does not outperform ResNet18 on the selected subset (on CIFAR10). This raises concern that the coreset may be biased toward the architecture used for selection. The authors should investigate transferability to different architectures and more complex datasets (e.g., ImageNet) where the advantage of coreset selection over active learning can be better demonstrated. (Reviewer 2) *Note: Reviewer 3 claims good transferability, but this weakness highlights a potential limitation.*
- **Need for broader evaluation**: The method should be tested on larger, more complex datasets with long-tail or uneven distributions, and on non-vision domains (text, audio, structured data) to establish generalizability. (Reviewer 3) Additionally, evaluation on datasets from different distributions (e.g., medical images) is required to verify robustness when the pretrained encoder is not a good match. (Reviewer 4)
- **Novelty concerns**: The method builds on familiar techniques (pseudo-labeling, coreset selection), which may reduce its perceived novelty for some readers. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing explanations and analyses**:
  - The paper does not explain fully why double-end pruning helps performance; a more detailed justification is requested. (Reviewer 4)
  - Clarify whether the model is fine-tuned or trained from scratch on the coreset. (Reviewer 4)
  - It would be informative to compare the scenario where the coreset is used as a labeled set and the rest as an unlabeled set with a semi-supervised learning method (e.g., SemiReward). If random sampling also works well under semi-supervision, the benefit of ELFS coreset selection diminishes. (Reviewer 4)
  - A visual representation (e.g., graph) of hard vs. easy examples as discussed in Sec. 4.4.2 would improve understanding. (Reviewer 3)
  - Further analysis of how the data distribution of the coreset selected by ELFS differs from random selection (Table 1) is needed to clarify its selection criteria. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor presentation issues**: Some sentences are redundant (Reviewer 4); Sec. 4.1 (problem formulation) should be moved to Sec. 3 for clarity. (Reviewers 2, 4)
