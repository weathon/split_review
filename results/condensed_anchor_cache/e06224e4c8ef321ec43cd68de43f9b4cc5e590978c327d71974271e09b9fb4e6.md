## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies annealed Langevin Monte Carlo (LMC) for sampling from non-log-concave, possibly multimodal densities. It provides non-asymptotic convergence bounds (in KL divergence) under a gradient Lipschitz (β-smoothness) assumption, bypassing log-concavity or log-Sobolev inequalities. The analysis uses Girsanov’s theorem and the action of a curve of interpolating measures, rather than isoperimetric constants. The paper considers a specific annealing schedule and an exponential integrator discretization. This is, to our knowledge, the first thorough non-asymptotic analysis of annealed LMC.

### Strengths
- The problem is interesting and actively researched; the approach is original – using the action of a curve instead of isoperimetric inequalities.
- First thorough non-asymptotic analysis of annealed LMC (Reviewer 4).
- Bounds are generic: require only β-smoothness of the potential, not log-concavity or a log-Sobolev inequality (Reviewer 2).
- Convergence bounds in KL divergence under “weak” conditions.
- Explanations are clear and well-illustrated, mixing intuition-building and commentary for a general audience (Reviewer 2, 4).
- The methods introduced are interesting contributions (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Annealing schedule impact**: The paper does not clarify how the annealing schedule affects the overall complexity or the action parameter \(\mathcal{A}\). The choice \(\lambda(\theta) \propto (1-\theta)^\gamma\) is used in the mixture-of-Gaussians example, but how \(\gamma\) influences \(\mathcal{A}\) and whether an optimal \(\gamma\) exists is not analyzed (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Assumptions not well discussed**:
  - Assumption 1 (the curve \(\pi_\theta\) is absolutely continuous) is not verifiable beforehand for a given target and schedule; no guidance is given on how to check it (Reviewer 3).
  - Assumption 2 is gradient Lipschitz continuity, which is stronger than classic smoothness; this should be explicitly stated (Reviewer 3).
  - The strong convexity constant for the initial distribution may be negative unless \(\lambda_0\) is large enough – the justification is insufficient (Reviewer 3).
- **Proof clarity issues**:
  - Sketch of Theorem 2 is not clear; notation in the appendix is inconsistent (e.g., \(\nu^{\text{ALMC}}\) for a different process) (Reviewer 1).
  - Theorem 2 uses Lemma 1 (Girsanov) to bound KL between SDE (6) and the “true” SDE (4), but (6) has a drift depending on a past state, which the lemma does not cover; the reasoning is not explained (Reviewer 1).
  - Lemma 2: the meaning of “reachable” equality is unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - Existence of a velocity field \(v_t\) such that \(X_t \sim \tilde{\pi}_t\) and satisfies the Fokker–Planck equation is assumed but not proven (Reviewer 3: “Is there always such \(v_t\)?”).
- **Mathematical errors in Example 2 (mixture of Gaussians)**:
  - The equation after line 1077 is incorrect: the density of a sum of independent random vectors is the convolution, not the product (Reviewer 3).
  - After recentering Gaussians, the \(\propto\) sign erases terms \(\exp(-\frac{2\lambda\beta}{\lambda+\beta}\|y_i\|^2)\), so the resulting mixture weights differ from \(p_i\); this can be fixed by replacing \(p_i\) with some \(q_i\) (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation for discretization and schedule**:
  - It is not explained why the exponential integrator scheme is used instead of an Euler scheme; the Euler scheme is dismissed as “non-optimal” with no supporting reasoning (line 373-374) (Reviewer 1).
  - It is not completely clear why Section 2.4 (action of curve) is needed; a reader would benefit from motivation and an idea of how it is used later (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of experiments**: No numerical simulations are provided, even simple ones (e.g., sampling from a mixture of Gaussians to show the dimension dependence or comparison with other algorithms) (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Comparison table unclear**: Table 1 lists results for various algorithms denoted by acronyms, but differences between the algorithms are not explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Notation**: The notation for \(\pi\) has too many indices and is used incoherently, making reading harder (Reviewer 3). In Example 1, the notation for \(\rho_0\) and \(\rho_1\) is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Claim of non-asymptotic analysis**: The abstract calls the analysis non-asymptotic, but the main results are given with \(\widetilde{O}\) notation (Reviewer 3). [Note: \(\widetilde{O}\) still denotes finite-sample non-asymptotic bounds, but the reviewer’s criticism is preserved.]
- **Intuitive explanations**: Lines 318-323 give obvious intuitions that are not clarified by the results; this may confuse readers (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions that point to missing justification or potential flaws**:
  - Are the LSI and PI constants in Example 1 exponentially dependent on \(d\) for all \(t\)? (Reviewer 1)
  - What are estimates of the action \(\mathcal{A}\) for more general target distributions beyond the mixture example? (Reviewer 3)
  - Can the action- based technique be applied to standard LMC to gain something, or is it only available for annealing? (Reviewer 3)
- **Oracle inequality weakness**: The bounds reassure convergence under assumptions (β-smoothness) that are hard to verify for a particular problem; e.g., what does β-smoothness imply about the posterior density? (Reviewer 2)
- **Non-expert comment**: The reviewer notes taking some steps of the proof on faith (Reviewer 2).
- **Typo**: line 956: “cure” → “curve” (Reviewer 3).
