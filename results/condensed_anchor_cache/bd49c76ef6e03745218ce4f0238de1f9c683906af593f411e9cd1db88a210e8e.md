## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a unified framework for single-image-to-3D generation that integrates diffusion-based multi-view image generation and 3D reconstruction into a recursive diffusion process. The key idea is to jointly train both modules via a self-conditioning mechanism, where at each denoising step the multi-view diffusion model receives 3D-aware maps (RGB and canonical coordinate maps, CCM) rendered by the reconstruction module from the previous timestep. This joint training aims to alleviate the data bias between separately trained stages. The approach builds on the LGM framework and uses 3D Gaussian Splatting as the 3D representation. Experiments compare against methods like SyncDreamer, SV3D, VideoMV, TripSR, LGM, and InstantMesh, reporting quantitative improvements and demonstrating multi-view consistency and visual quality.

### Strengths

- The general motivation of jointly training the diffusion and reconstruction models to fill the domain gap between separately trained stages is well-founded and reasonable.
- The technical design of providing RGB and CCM feedback from the reconstructed 3D model to each denoising step is an effective way to enforce multi-view consistency and iterative refinement; the accompanying video effectively demonstrates this recursive process.
- The paper is well-written, clearly structured, and easy to follow.
- State-of-the-art results with incremental improvements over prior work are presented; the framework achieves enhanced visual realism and consistent multi-view generation.
- The idea of incorporating 3D geometry feedback into the denoising loop is a sensible direction, and the performance is good.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns.** Several reviewers noted that the overall pipeline is very similar to existing works such as VideoMV (integrating a large Gaussian reconstruction model into the denoising process of video diffusion) and SyncDreamer (using 3D feedback conditions, but with depth maps instead of RGB/CCM). The main claimed technical difference—using CCM and RGB feedback rather than depth/normal maps—is considered an incremental change that may not rise to a major contribution, especially without a substantial improvement in results. Reviewers 3 and 4 explicitly state the novelty is limited.
- **Missing ablation for joint training.** The ablation study (Table 2) only shows: (a) without joint training and 3D reconstruction, (b) with RGB only, (c) with CCM only, (d) full model. There is no direct pair of experiments with/without joint training (keeping the 3D feedback), so the individual effect of joint training cannot be isolated. Moreover, the baseline without CCM and RGB feedback still outperforms many methods in Table 1; the source of these improvements (e.g., other design choices, data, training details) is not explained.
- **Qualitative comparisons are insufficient.** The qualitative enhancements over LGM and CLAY are not clearly apparent; side-by-side visualizations at the same viewpoint and step-by-step comparisons during denoising would better highlight the claimed improvements. The provided Figure 8 does not compare with a baseline at the same timesteps, limiting its usefulness.
- **Practicality of 3D representation.** The method uses 3D Gaussian Splatting, which may be less suitable for downstream applications compared to meshes.
- **Questionable robustness to noisy inputs.** The reconstruction model is expected to reconstruct a 3D Gaussian Splatting from both slightly and highly noisy multi-view images. The paper does not explain how the model maintains such robustness across varying noise levels.
- **Efficiency concerns.** Training and inference costs are higher than baselines; the training stage suffers a significant efficiency drop.
- **Missing discussion of limitations.** The paper does not discuss any limitations of the proposed approach.
- **Results still exhibit flaws.** The showcased 360° demos (e.g., the stuffed toy, the statuette with pony tails, the cute dinosaur in the supplementary) show flat side views and a bas-relief ambiguity reminiscent of single-view reconstruction bias, where the model only looks plausible at frontal views.
- **Lack of generalizability evaluation.** The authors claim the framework can accommodate various multi-view generation and reconstruction networks, but no experiments with different backbones (e.g., the baselines’ components) are provided to demonstrate this flexibility.
- **Effectiveness of additional geometric features unclear.** The method can extract geometric features beyond rendered color images (e.g., CCM), but the reliability and contribution of these features as guidance are not thoroughly validated (e.g., comparing with depth/normal conditioning).
- **Related work coverage.** The technical novelty relative to prior iterative/recursive approaches (including those using depth or other 2.5D proxies) is not sufficiently delineated; a more detailed comparison of differences and advantages over works like SyncDreamer and VideoMV is needed.
- **Improvements over SOTA are incremental.** While results are state-of-the-art, the gains are modest and the visual quality still falls within the class of prior methods; no substantial breakthrough is demonstrated that would justify the claimed contribution.
