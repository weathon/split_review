## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PANet, a physics-guided parametric augmentation network to generate realistic hazy-clean training pairs for improving real-world image dehazing. PANet consists of a Haze-to-Parameter Mapper (HPM) and a Parameter-to-Haze Mapper (PHM) that use a cyclic mapping through a parametric haze space, leveraging a physical scattering model and a Data-driven Haze Refiner (DHR). Experiments show that augmenting existing dehazing methods (DW-GAN, Dehamer, FocalNet) with PANet-generated data improves performance on real-world datasets (NH-Haze20, RTTS) and a few synthetic benchmarks.

### Strengths
1. Addresses a practical and meaningful problem: bridging the domain gap between synthetic and real-world haze data by generating realistic hazy-clean pairs.
2. The hybrid approach combining physics-guided modeling (scattering model) with data-driven components (HPM, PHM, DHR) is physically sensible and practically effective for generating diverse haze patterns.
3. The cyclic unsupervised learning of intermediate haze parameters (HPM+PHM) works well and is data-efficient: can be trained on as few as 50 image pairs.
4. Applying the proposed augmentation to existing dehazing models (DW-GAN, Dehamer, FocalNet) yields notable improvements in dehazing quality on real images and some synthetic test images, with fewer artifacts than GAN-based augmentation.
5. The method allows generating an arbitrary number of new patches with varying haze densities (pixel-wise resampling of parameters).
6. The paper is well-prepared, clearly organized, and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty**: The core idea of cyclic mapping between haze parameters and images is derived from CycleGAN and other established cyclic image-to-image methods. The claimed distinctions (GAN structure, global vs. pixel-wise haze adjustment) are largely engineering refinements rather than scientific advancements. The use of ResBlocks and a neural network for parameter estimation is standard practice. (Minority: one reviewer considers this a major limitation; others rate contribution 2-3.)
2. **Method does not specifically address thick/dense haze**: The main challenge of dehazing is thick haze/fog, but PANet has no specific mechanism or treatment for it. The proposed method does not show clear superiority over existing methods on thick haze, and qualitative results (Fig. 1, 6) still suffer from color shift and residual haze when haze is thick. Reviewer 2 argues the results could be achieved by existing non-deep-learning methods with comparable quality.
3. **Reliance on a pre-trained depth estimator (RA-Depth)**: The depth estimator may not generalize to unseen images, and the Depth Refinement Module (DRM) is trained on a specific dataset. The accuracy of generated haze is directly tied to depth quality. Reviewer 1 notes the entire approach fails if DHR is excluded (Table 3). No analysis of sensitivity to depth quality is provided.
4. **Dataset limitations and lack of diversity**: The method is validated on NH-Haze20, which has only 50 training pairs and 5 testing pairs, generated using a fog machine. This creates domain discrepancies with natural real-world haze (particle size, density, environmental/lighting conditions). The lack of diverse large-scale datasets limits generalizability and risks overfitting. Augmented data is based on this small initial set. (Multiple reviewers raise this.)
5. **Insufficient comparisons with real-world dehazing and augmentation methods**: The three baselines (DW-GAN, Dehamer, FocalNet) are primarily trained on synthetic data (SOTS). Comparisons with real-world-oriented methods like RIDCP, DAD, PTTD are missing. The method is not compared with existing data augmentation techniques for dehazing on datasets beyond NH-Haze20 and RTTS. This makes it hard to assess the true advantage of PANet. (Reviewers 3, 5.)
6. **Narrow experimental evaluation**: Only one real-world dataset (RTTS) is used for evaluation, with only NIQE and PIQE metrics. Additional no-reference metrics (FADE, BRISQUE, NIMA, US) are needed for fuller comparison. Evaluation on popular benchmarks (SOTS-Outdoor, SOTS-Indoor) is missing. No experiments on downstream tasks like object detection on RTTS (which is designed for detection). (Reviewers 1, 5.)
7. **Parametric augmentation not sufficiently explained**: It is unclear if the parameter α is continuous or discrete, what its range is, and what specific values were used in experiments. The method's handling of non-uniform or color-dependent haze is not discussed. (Reviewer 3.)
8. **Idealized physical scattering model assumptions**: The model assumes homogeneous atmosphere and neglects complex scattering effects, causing mismatch with real-world haze. The fog machine generates specific particle sizes, not representative of diverse atmospheric conditions. (Reviewer 4.)
9. **Strong dependency on DHR and depth estimation**: The approach fails without DHR (Table 3). No analysis of depth estimator outputs or DRM performance is given, and visualizations of depth maps, beta(z), or transmission maps are missing. (Reviewer 1.)
10. **Qualitative results clarity and artifacts**: It is unclear which dehazing model was used to generate qualitative results (Figs. 6, 7). The results contain visible artifacts, especially in complex textures. Full images (not zoomed) for real-world natural haze are requested. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4.)
11. **Questionable augmentation choices**: Augmentations like "reverse its haze location" seem unrealistic (haze increases with distance). The effect of excluding such augmentations is not analyzed. (Reviewer 1.)
12. **Limited performance on indoor images**: Improvement on outdoor images is higher than indoor; this discrepancy is not discussed or analyzed. (Reviewer 1.)
13. **Missing convergence analysis**: Table 5 shows improvement with more augmented pairs, but testing stops at 600. Convergence of dehazing performance vs. number of augmentations is not evaluated. (Reviewer 5.)
14. **Writing quality issues**: Equation reference error on line 238 (cites "Eq. ??"), typos like "pixel-wisely" (line 307), and Figures 2 and 3 are nearly identical. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5.)
15. **Missing citation**: [1] Structure Representation Network and Uncertainty Feedback Learning for Dense Non-Uniform Fog Removal is referenced but not provided. (Reviewer 2.)
16. **Risk of overfitting**: Not analyzed, and color shift (especially in sky, white objects, road) is observed but not explained. (Reviewers 1, 2.)
