## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes Average Precision (AP) and proposes DAP-loss, a differentiable approximation of AP for direct optimization, applied to classification and localization jointly without task-balancing hyperparameters. It designs a cost function for the Hungarian matching algorithm in DETR-based detectors. Experiments on COCO with DETR and Deformable DETR (plus RT-DETR) show improvements when DAP-loss replaces the original loss in later training stages.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, straightforward, and easy to follow.
- Joint optimization of classification and localization in end-to-end models without needing task-balancing hyperparameters is an interesting and worthwhile direction.
- Adapting mAP as a loss function is a promising approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Formulation and derivation flaws (Reviewer 3, high confidence):**  
  - Transition from Eq 3 to Eq 4 is unclear and appears flawed: Eq 3 uses P values (0–1) and ΔT (counts of TPs), while Eq 4 multiplies localization score (0–1) with H() values (0–1), giving different value ranges.  
  - Eq 4 claims “localization task pertains to function L(·) and is independent of H(·)” – this is incorrect because the equation includes H().  
  - Gradient derivation in Section 3.2.2 (Appendix 3) is flawed: (i) ignores dependence of P(x) on x and s_t, (ii) skips critical steps from Eq 3 to Eq 20, (iii) includes an unexpected term dR(x)/dx.  
  - Line 304: momentum update error – “P^i_a = m P^i_a + (1-m) P^i_a” is self-referential (should use previous estimate).  
  - Line 311: “computing Piα within ˆL is challenging, so it is replaced by Piα” – this replacement is flawed.  
  - Missing definitions: b_score, L(), f_b, and inconsistent use of symbols (e.g., G denotes both ground truths and another value).

- **Missing key prior work (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3):**  
  - No comparison with other methods designed to optimize AP loss for object detection:  
    - Chen et al., “Searching parameterized AP loss for object detection” (NeurIPS 2021)  
    - Chen et al., “Towards accurate one-stage object detection with AP-loss” (CVPR 2019)  
    - Xu et al., “Revisiting AP loss for dense object detection: Adaptive ranking pair selection” (CVPR 2022)  
    - Oksuz et al., “A ranking-based, balanced loss function unifying classification and localisation in object detection” (NeurIPS 2020) – this has the same goal and overall motivation as DAP-loss.  
  - Specifically for DETR: Yavuz et al., “Bucketed Ranking-based Losses for Efficient Training of Object Detectors” (ECCV 2024) applies a ranking-based loss (combining classification and localization) into DETR-based detectors.  
  - Works from the group of Georg Martius on ranking losses are also missing.  
  - Related literature references are incomplete (e.g., missing [1] per Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient experimental validation (all reviewers):**  
  - **Dataset:** Only COCO; generalizability limited – PASCAL-VOC should be considered (Reviewer 1).  
  - **Models tested:** Only DETR and RT-DETR (Reviewer 1, 3); missing recent strong models like DN-DETR, Rank-DETR, and Co-DETR (Reviewer 1, 3).  
  - **Backbones:** No experiments with large backbones (e.g., Swin-L) (Reviewer 2).  
  - **No non-DETR detectors tested** (Reviewer 3).  
  - **Missing comparisons** with other ranking-based loss functions (Reviewer 2, 3).  
  - **Results on newer DETR detectors** show no gains (Reviewer 3, Table 1); the comparison may be unfair because DAP-loss is applied as fine-tuning (starting from a pre-trained model) while baselines are trained from scratch (Reviewer 3).

- **Training from scratch limitation (Reviewer 2, 3):**  
  DAP-loss is only applied in later stages of training (fine-tuning), not from scratch. Similar methods train from scratch with significant gains. This limits the technical contribution and practical utility.

- **Hungarian matching cost function (Reviewer 1):**  
  How does the proposed cost function address the one-to-one matching constraint of the Hungarian algorithm? The paper claims a specially designed cost function but does not clarify how it handles strict matching and alignment.

- **Questionable contributions (Reviewer 3):**  
  - “Comprehensive analysis of the AP metric” is far from comprehensive.  
  - Differentiable AP loss is not novel given the missing prior work.  
  - Theoretical convergence proof is flawed.

- **Methodology issues (Reviewer 3):**  
  - Not explained why the cleaner AP Loss formulation by Chen et al. was not adopted for multiple IoU thresholds.  
  - Unclear what the “initial training phase” (Line 304) consists of before DAP-loss fine-tuning.  
  - Eq 2: choice of IoU thresholds (0.5, 0.55, …, 0.95) is not justified.  
  - Figure 2 text is small; typo: “location score” → “Localization score”.  
  - Line 228: “fb(·)” is not clearly defined – box has both location and class distributions.  
  - Line 229–230: interpreting H() as “the cumulative distribution function of the impulse function” does not make sense.  
  - Line 242: symbol inconsistency – previously used T for number of TPs, now tp(x).  
  - Formatting: missing spaces before parentheses (e.g., “AP-loss(Chen et al., 2019)”).  
  - Line 371: “Modles” → “Models”.

- **Reviewer disagreement note:** Reviewer 3 rates the paper very low (1, confidence 5) and identifies severe theoretical flaws and missing prior work, while Reviewer 2 rates it higher (5) and focuses mainly on insufficient experimentation. Reviewer 1 rates 3 and raises concerns about dataset/model breadth and the Hungarian cost function. All three agree that the experimental evaluation is too narrow.
