## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the problem of text comprehension in visual instruction tuning by enhancing LLaVA with an OCR-focused data collection pipeline. The authors collect 422K noisy pretraining images from LAION using OCR tools and generate 16K high-quality GPT-4 instruction-following conversations from OCR outputs and captions. The resulting model LLaVAR is evaluated on text-based VQA datasets and GPT-4-based instruction-following evaluations, showing improvement over LLaVA. Reviewers broadly acknowledge the practical value of the dataset and the experimental validation, but express concerns about methodological novelty (extension of LLaVA), limited evaluation (few benchmarks, only recall metric), and lack of clear advantage over existing models (e.g., mPLUG-Owl) at the same resolution.

### Strengths
- **Pioneering focus on text-rich images in visual instruction tuning:** The paper is one of the first to systematically explore improving OCR ability for multimodal LLMs like LLaVA (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). The problem is important (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical and large-scale data collection pipeline:** A straightforward method to collect a large (over 400K) dataset of text-rich images from LAION using off-the-shelf OCR tools, with data to be released publicly – of clear benefit to the community (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). The generation of instruction-following data via GPT-4 is reasonable and effective (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental validation of the dataset’s effectiveness:** The paper demonstrates that the collected data improves LLaVA’s performance on text-based VQA benchmarks, particularly relative to the LLaVA baseline (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The two-stage training design (OCR pretraining then instruction tuning) is clearly explained (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Model capable of handling high-resolution images:** The architecture integrates extra visual encoders and cross-attention modules, enabling higher-resolution processing (Reviewer 1). (One reviewer [Reviewer 4] later notes this is still the LLaVA backbone without explicit design for text-rich images, representing a disagreement.)
- **Well-organized and easy to follow:** The paper is clearly written, with detailed description of the data collection pipeline and experimental analysis (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental methodological novelty:** The core approach (instruction tuning, data generation via GPT-4, two-stage training) follows LLaVA closely, with the main contribution being the data collection rather than a new architecture or training paradigm. Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 consider the contribution limited or incremental. A minority (Reviewer 1) finds it “pioneering,” but this is contradicted by the majority view. Compared with other work explicit on text reading (PreSTU, Pix2Struct), the novelty and advantage are not clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Comparable or not clearly superior to existing models at same resolution:** In Table 2, LLaVAR does not show a significant performance advantage over mPLUG-Owl (2023) when both are evaluated at 224×224 resolution (Reviewer 1). This raises the question whether the OCR-specific data is necessary or whether the scale is insufficient (Reviewer 3). The paper also lacks detailed comparison with SOTA models specifically designed for text-rich images (e.g., information extraction, document classification) (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited evaluation:** The evaluation primarily relies on four OCR-based VQA datasets, and the authors acknowledge (Fig. 4/5) that the adopted metric (only recall) may be unreliable (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Additional metrics (e.g., image captioning metrics) and more diverse benchmarks (e.g., LLaVA benchmark, information extraction, document classification) are expected, especially in ablation studies (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). The “GPT-4-based instruction-following evaluation” is not clearly explained: how a text-only GPT-4 can generate ground truth for image tasks and how scores are computed (Reviewer 2).
- **Weak qualitative analysis:** Section 5.3 provides only a single case study, making conclusions from it not convincing (Reviewer 1).
- **Data quality and choice issues:**
    - OCR errors are inevitable (e.g., “Boynton” vs. “Byington”), and their impact on downstream tasks is not analyzed (Reviewer 1).
    - The decision to collect from LAION rather than a well-annotated OCR dataset is not justified (Reviewer 3).
    - Fragmented OCR results (few words) that are filtered out may be representative of real-world text-rich images (posters, tables); removing them may hurt real-world distribution alignment (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues:** Figure 7 has unclear notations and fails to visually reflect implementation details (Reviewer 1). The term “temperature” in Section 5, first paragraph, is not defined (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing cross-model experiments:** It would be informative to finetune other open-source models (e.g., mPLUG-Owl) on the collected dataset and compare performance (Reviewer 4).
