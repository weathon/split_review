## Merged Review

### Summary
UniDetox proposes a universal detoxification method for LLMs via dataset distillation with contrastive decoding. It creates a toxic model, distills detoxifying text from a small model (GPT-2), and fine-tunes any target LLM (OPT, Falcon, LLaMA-2) on that data without model-specific hyperparameter tuning. The authors provide a theoretical analysis (Eq. 5, Taylor approximation) aligning fine-tuning with the opposite direction of a "toxic vector." Experiments show toxicity reduction and reduced political bias, but the evaluation is critiqued for limited baselines, weak metrics, and insufficient ablations.

### Strengths
- **Novel universal approach**: Combines dataset distillation with contrastive decoding to produce detoxifying text that can be applied to any LLM without model-specific tuning, addressing limitations of prior model-specific methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Theoretical grounding**: Provides a derivation (Sec. 2.2, Eq. 5) showing fine-tuning moves weights opposite to a "toxic vector" via Taylor approximation, though Reviewer 3 requested more steps in the derivation.
- **Practical efficiency**: Distilled text from a small model (GPT-2) successfully detoxifies larger models (OPT, Falcon, LLaMA-2), and a single hyperparameter configuration works across different models, reducing tuning costs (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Interesting combination of ideas**: Effectively applies task arithmetic and dataset distillation principles to toxicity mitigation, and the baseline includes straightforward prompt variants as a useful comparison point (Reviewer 2).
- **Thorough baseline coverage according to one reviewer**: Reviewer 4 considered the evaluation complete with significant results on PT and EMT metrics across benchmarks, and praised the well-structured paper and rigorous reasoning. (Other reviewers, however, found the baselines and metrics insufficient – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparisons with modern methods**: The paper lacks comparisons with recent detoxification and alignment techniques, including DPO, task arithmetic baselines (e.g., [1][2] from R1), parameter-efficient contrastive decoding (R2), LM Steering (Han et al., R2), and newer methods such as Suau et al. (ICML 2024) “Whispering Experts” and Pozzobon et al. (EMNLP 2023) “Goodtriever” (R3). The related work section is also criticized for missing coverage of these methods (R2, R3).
- **Omission of Perspective API**: Most detoxification work uses the Perspective API, but it is omitted without explanation (R2). The metrics used (ppl, n-gram) are considered too weak for current LLM abilities; zero/few-shot evaluations (e.g., MMLU) to gauge model capability retention are missing (R3).
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters and lack of ablations**: Although UniDetox avoids model-specific tuning, results are sensitive to learning rate and α (Sec. 3.4, R1), and the key hyperparameter λ lacks an ablation study (R3). The amount of distilled data required for effectiveness is not discussed, and no qualitative examples of the distilled sentences or generated outputs are provided (R1, R2, R3). The authors are asked to show what the detoxifying text looks like (e.g., is it just non-toxic or has other lexical properties?) (R2).
- **Counterintuitive perplexity behavior and universality concerns**: The perplexity of GPT-2 drops from 17.28 to 12.23 after fine-tuning on distilled data, which is surprising since intervention typically increases perplexity; the authors do not adequately justify this (R3). In Table 2, UniDetox reduces perplexity for OPT and Falcon but increases it for LLaMA-2 at the default learning rate, while at lr=1e-5 the opposite occurs, hinting that the fine-tuning parameters are not fully universal but rather well-suited for certain model/parameter combinations (R3).
- **Evaluation scope is limited**: The method is only tested on a small number of models (GPT-2, OPT, Falcon, LLaMA-2) with non-comprehensive metrics; no evaluation on instruction-tuned models (e.g., gemma-2-2b-it) for safety preprompt comparison (R3). OOD toxicity reduction is stronger than ID reduction, which is unexpected for a fine-tuning method and not explained (R3). Diversity metrics drop compared to other methods, and broader evaluation on other LM tasks is needed (R1).
- **Theoretical derivation needs clarification**: The steps from Eq. 1 to Eq. 5 (Taylor approximation) are not fully detailed; the authors should provide them (R3).
- **Computational cost and scalability concerns**: UniDetox still requires fine-tuning the target model, which is computationally intensive (R4). As model size scales (e.g., 14B to 405B), the small amount of detoxifying text may become insufficient relative to the parameter space, and no preventive techniques are proposed (R4). The method is limited to text-only models; applicability to multimodal models is unclear (R4). Questions about computational resources, time cost, and impact on larger models are raised (R2, R4).
- **Minority positive contrast**: Reviewer 4 gave a higher score (8) and found the evaluation complete and results significant, while other reviewers (5, 6, 6) considered the evaluation insufficient and baselines outdated, particularly regarding comparative methods and metric comprehensiveness. This disagreement highlights the need for stronger validation.
