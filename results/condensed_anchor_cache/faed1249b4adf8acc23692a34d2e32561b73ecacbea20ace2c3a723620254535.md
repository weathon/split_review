## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Neural Predictive Ensemble Sampling (NeuralPES) for non-stationary contextual bandits, combining deep neural networks with ensemble sampling and future reward prediction via sequence modeling. The algorithm is presented as a neural implementation of Linear Predictive Sampling (LinPS), which has regret guarantees for non-stationary environments. Empirical evaluations are conducted on synthetic data and two real-world recommendation datasets (one-week and two-month periods). The paper includes theoretical results comparing LinPS to Thompson sampling and ablation studies. Three reviewers rated the paper 3, citing weaknesses in exploration, computational cost, theoretical limitations, and reproducibility; one reviewer rated it 6, praising its clarity and comprehensive analysis.

### Strengths
- Extending neural bandits to the non-stationary setting is novel and necessary; first work to do so. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- The algorithm uses a sliding window to handle changing reward mappings and prioritizes information with lasting value. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- The problem is practically significant and underexplored. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- The neural architecture handles high-dimensional, non-linear features while addressing non-stationarity via sequence-based reward prediction. (Reviewer 3)
- Combines NN-based sequence modeling (predicting future rewards from past weight sequences) with ensemble sampling (when posterior is intractable). (Reviewer 3)
- Experiments demonstrate superior performance over other neural bandit algorithms and their sliding-window variants. (Reviewer 3)
- One reviewer (Rating 6, Reviewer 4) found the paper well written and clear, with comprehensive theoretical analysis and ablation studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Exploration mechanism:** Ensemble sampling randomly draws neural models without considering per-network variance or confidence intervals. This lacks a theoretical tie to regret minimization and may cause inefficient exploration in high-dimensional spaces. (Reviewer 1)
- **Computational cost and scalability:** Training an ensemble of neural networks each round is prohibitive for online deployment. The paper provides no complexity analysis, memory requirements, or execution time. Given use of an A100 40GB GPU, cost is a practical concern. The ensemble size M is not reported or analyzed. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3 points 5,6,9)
- **Theoretical analysis limited to linear models:** The regret analysis addresses LinPS (linear) vs TS, not the neural algorithm. This avoids the central challenge of analyzing neural networks and appears nearly identical to Liu et al. (2023), without highlighting novel contributions or differences in assumptions. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 point 4)
- **Ambiguous regret claims:** The statement "regret of LinPS is zero" is unclear—it does not specify cumulative vs. average regret, and it is not obvious how zero regret is achievable when the benchmark is the best arm at each time step under frequent changes. Also, terms like \(I(\theta_2;\theta_1)\) and the entropy term in Corollary 1 are undefined. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 points 7,8)
- **Vague definition of non-stationarity:** The paper claims to handle any type of non-stationarity, but the theory only covers abrupt changes and AR(1). It is unclear whether the algorithm generalizes. (Reviewer 2)
- **Confusing algorithm presentation:** Five algorithms (NeuralENS, LinPS, NeuralPES, TrainNN, etc.) are described without clarifying their relationships, the role of TrainNN, or the differences between Neural Ensemble Sampling and NeuralPES. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 point 1)
- **Missing comparison with PS (Liu et al. 2023):** The paper does not clearly compare PS and NeuralPES in terms of computational cost, memory, assumptions, or types of non-stationarity handled. It is also unclear whether NeuralPES approximates the infinite reward sequence of PS with a finite horizon and how that horizon is chosen. (Reviewer 3 points 1,2,3)
- **Reproducibility concerns:** Code is not provided; the ensemble size M is not stated for experiments; data preprocessing for the long-term dataset (grouping 12-hour sessions) may ignore order within sessions and introduce bias. (Reviewer 3 points 10,11)
- **Narrow evaluation scope:** Experiments only cover recommendation datasets; evaluation on other domains (e.g., dynamic pricing) is absent. (Reviewer 4)
- **Hard-to-read figures:** Figure 3 is not clearly plotted. (Reviewer 4)
- **Undescribed limitations:** The paper does not discuss the challenges or failure modes of the approach. (Reviewer 4)

Note: Reviewer 4 (Rating 6) rated the paper significantly higher than the other three reviewers (Rating 3), leading to disagreement on the comprehensiveness of the theory and clarity of presentation. The weaknesses listed above are raised primarily by Reviewers 1–3, with Reviewer 4 adding only minor points.
