## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes integrating LLMs with ASR to improve transcription accuracy in low-resource healthcare settings, specifically by fine-tuning Whisper-Large via LoRA on a custom dataset (Pharma-Speak) and applying LLaMA 3 for second-pass rescoring of N-best hypotheses. While the idea is relevant and could be practically useful, most reviewers found the submission severely incomplete and lacking substance.

### Strengths
- The use case (medication-related conversations in low-resource healthcare) and the general approach (LLM-based rescoring for ASR) are relevant and potentially viable. One reviewer highlighted that the concept is a simple, efficient way to build a domain-specific system and could lower the cost/barrier for ASR in healthcare.
- The description of adapting medical entity names via LoRA fine-tuning of the LLM to correct ASR errors was noted as a clear explanation of the mechanism (though the breadth of evaluation was criticized).
- One reviewer stated that the experiments are “described clearly,” but this view is contradicted by others who found critical details missing.

### Weaknesses
- **Paper is largely incomplete.** Multiple sections (especially 4 and 5) contain only placeholder text or insufficient detail. Two reviewers noted that the paper appears to be an unfinished draft (e.g., missing dataset description, baseline, results analysis, and conclusion with lessons learned).
- **No baseline comparison.** The work does not compare against a baseline system (e.g., raw Whisper, other rescoring methods, or prior domain-adapted ASR). Without such comparison, the claimed improvements in WER cannot be assessed.
- **Dataset description is critically missing.** No information is provided on:
  - Total size (hours, number of utterances)
  - Number of speakers, gender distribution, named entities
  - How data was collected (public/private, recording conditions, speaker overlap across splits)
  - Train/validation/test splits and whether test speakers overlapped with training speakers
- **Experimental details are absent or insufficient.** The paper does not specify the amount of training data used for LoRA fine-tuning, the hyperparameters (e.g., rank, number of layers adapted, learning rate), the evaluation metrics beyond WER, nor any ablation studies. One reviewer explicitly asked for ablation of LoRA weight/layer choices.
- **No novelty in approach.** All reviewers agree that the submission simply applies existing models (Whisper, LLaMA 3, LoRA) to a custom dataset without introducing technical novelty. One reviewer (score 5) noted the domain is under-explored but still found no visibly novel aspect beyond that.
- **Lack of related work / comparative study.** The paper does not discuss or compare against other works that use LLMs for ASR N-best rescoring (e.g., Li et al. Interspeech 2024, arXiv 2406.18972, as referenced by one reviewer). No survey of alternative techniques (e.g., direct LLM summarization, rephrasing, other fine-tuning methods beyond LoRA) is provided.
- **Claims not backed.** The paper states that accuracy improves but does not demonstrate generalizability (e.g., across multiple languages, different drug names, or realistic noise conditions). One reviewer noted the dataset is not holistic – many drugs are omitted and chemical names are used instead of English terms.
- **Reproducibility is low.** The missing details on data, parameters, and splits prevent reproduction or verification.
- **Typographical errors.** Examples include “medical settings1” (line 044) and “low-rank decomposition matrices 1” (line 123), where numeric superscripts appear without explanation (likely leftover from referencing).
- **Minority review contrast:** One reviewer (score 5) acknowledged the paper describes a plausible mechanism and shows some WER reduction, but still found the paper meager in details and lacking benchmarking. That reviewer also requested comparisons with other LLMs and holistic benchmark tests.
