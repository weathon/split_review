## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OTGM, an Optimal Transport Graph Matching model that reformulates graph matching as a transportation plan minimizing Wasserstein distance between node/edge distributions, and introduces a self-supervised graph denoising module to filter noisy edges. It provides theoretical guarantees on generalization ability and reports state-of-the-art performance on three real-world datasets. Reviewers are split: two rate 5, two rate 6.

### Strengths
- By targeting semantic-level correspondences via distributional alignment, the approach is more robust to occlusions and transformations than traditional topological/geometric matching (Reviewer 1).
- The combination of optimal transport and self-supervised denoising improves accuracy and reliability compared to fixed matching strategies (Reviewer 1).
- The graph denoising module is a significant contribution; it effectively filters noisy edges via bootstrapping without external knowledge, making the model adaptable to imperfect real-world data (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The paper provides theoretical guarantees on the method’s generalization ability (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Experimental results on three real-world datasets show effectiveness; the ablation study is reasonable (Reviewer 3).
- Supplementary materials and code are provided (Reviewer 3).
- Clear representation and good illustration (Reviewer 2; note: Reviewer 1 strongly disagrees on clarity—see Weaknesses).
- Sufficient experimental evaluation on all graph matching benchmarks (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Using optimal transport for graph matching is not novel; previous works have exploited similar ideas (e.g., [1] Graph matching via optimal transport, [2] Gromov-Wasserstein learning for graph matching, [3] DHOT-GM, [4] Subgraph matching via partial optimal transport). The role of OT for semantic-level alignment needs further proof (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 considers distributional alignment from OT theory a strength, highlighting disagreement.
- **Writing and presentation issues** (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 found writing clear—minority contrast):  
  * Too many variable names hinder legibility.  
  * Inconsistent definitions: V_A, V_B in L215 vs. L268.  
  * In L287, y_j should not appear in the equation.  
  * Function c defined as c(x_i, z_j) in L185—does c(;) in Eq. 6 have the same meaning?  
  * Missing definitions: T'_{i',j'} in Eq. 6, c_{ij} and π_{ij} in Eq. 5 (coordinates or something else), L_Y in L240.  
- **Weak connection between the two innovations**: The distributional alignment formulation and the denoising module do not seem strongly related, undermining the paper’s cohesion (Reviewer 3).  
- **Insufficient robustness experiments**:  
  * No comparative experiments specifically designed for noisy correspondence; improving accuracy alone does not demonstrate robustness (Reviewer 2).  
  * Figure 3 shows qualitative visualization but does not depict noisy matching scenarios (Reviewer 2).  
  * The paper aims to mitigate viewpoints and noisy annotations, but the experiments do not make this easily discernible; more experiments like those on noisy correspondences in [1] (Lin et al. 2023, ICCV) and additional visual examples are needed (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing analysis and ablations**:  
  * Computation complexity of the model is not provided (Reviewer 2).  
  * Scalability to large graphs is not addressed; the complexity of OT computations combined with denoising could be a bottleneck for real-time/large-scale applications (Reviewer 4).  
  * The denoising module adds considerable complexity (binary sampling mechanism increases training time), and gains are incremental on some benchmarks given the added complexity (Reviewer 4).  
  * No ablation on the λ hyperparameter (line 293): how is λ determined, what are the results for different λ, and what if λ=0 or 1 (only one loss)? (Reviewer 3).  
  * Only noise in edges is considered; denoising of keypoints (e.g., LightGlue [1]) was not attempted (Reviewer 3).  
- **Unclear terminology and Figures**:  
  * The name "feature-invariant graph matching" is unclear—does invariance refer to fixed matched keypoints? (Reviewer 1, question).  
  * Figure 2 step 1 shows no change in the graph before and after denoising—mistake or intentional? (Reviewer 3).  
  * Line 080-081 claim about scenarios with noisy/inaccurate input data lacks direct experimental evidence; Table 4 in appendix is a combined result (Reviewer 3).  
  * Typo: "brunch" should be "branch" (lines 106, 107, 111) (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited scope**: The work focuses only on keypoint matching and does not explore other graph matching applications (e.g., scene graph generation, 3D pose estimation), limiting generalization claims (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing efficiency comparisons**: No efficiency comparison or simplification discussion is provided (Reviewer 4, question).
