## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a single-image-to-3D pipeline (Fourier123) that uses a hybrid score distillation loss (hy-FSD). It leverages novel-view diffusion (Zero-1-to-3) in the spatial domain for geometric consistency and a generic image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) in the Fourier amplitude domain for finer texture details. The approach is applied on DreamGaussian and DreamFusion. Reviewers are divided: one (score 5) finds the perspective new and results promising among score‑distillation methods; the other three (score 3) deem the experimental evidence insufficient, the qualitative results underwhelming, and the central assumption outdated.

### Strengths
- The idea of using Fourier‑domain amplitude in score distillation is novel and a creative attempt to extract structural information from predicted noise without spatial alignment. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The motivation is clear and reasonable: novel‑view models (Zero‑1‑to‑3) produce consistent but blurry results, while image generation models (SD) give detailed but inconsistent views. (R1, R4)
- The exposition is clear and the paper is comfortable to read. (R1, R3)
- The framework design (hybrid spatial/frequency supervision) is natural given the insight. (R1)
- The method is validated on two backbones (DreamGaussian and DreamFusion) with consistent improvements in CLIP‑Sim. (R1, R2)
- The proposed loss is a better alternative for SDS when combined with Zero123 SDS. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Outdated central assumption**: The claim that novel‑view models produce over‑smooth results is based only on Zero‑1‑to‑3. More recent models (Zero123++, CAT3D, video models, some open‑source) can generate detailed and consistent novel views. The observation may be specific to that model, not a general attribute. (R3, R4)
- **Weak qualitative results**: The rendered 3D models look noisy, blurry, and cartoonish. In Fig. 5, Magic123 often shows better texture details despite weaker global consistency. Results of InstantMesh and direct Zero123 distillation are not convincing; reviewers suspect baseline implementation issues (e.g., not using threestudio’s implementation, which is standard). (R2, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient quantitative evaluation**: 
  - No 3D metrics (Chamfer Distance, F‑Score, Volumetric IoU). Only CLIP‑similarity is reported, which reflects overall semantic alignment, not texture detail. (R2, R3)
  - Evaluation is limited to 100 objects. For an optimization‑based method, at least a few hundred objects on GSO, Omni3D, etc., should be used. (R2)
  - User studies lack an aspect for texture details, so the central claim about improving visual quality is not adequately supported. (R3)
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: 
  - No comparison with Wonder3D or Unique3D, both publicly available. (R1)
  - Not discussed how hy‑FSD compares to multiview diffusion models (e.g., MVDream), which dominate recent 3D generation. The method does not show potential to outperform them. (R1, R4)
- **Speed disadvantage**: As an optimization‑based method, it is much slower than feed‑forward methods (InstantMesh, Stable Fast 3D), which take seconds and may have better geometry and appearance. (R2)
- **Possible error in Eq. 9**: If \(z\) is the denoising output (L231), a Jacobian term backpropagating from amplitudes to the spatial domain is missing. (R3)
- **Uncertain gradient quality**: DFT amplitudes of a single image (or noise \(\epsilon\)) are very noisy; it is unclear whether such gradients fundamentally improve score distillation quality. (R3)
- **Missing ablation**: The paper does not consider applying a high‑pass filter over the Fourier domain (only amplitude), which could be a relevant variant. (R1)
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) finds the results promising and the contribution novel, but the other three (scores 3, confidence 5/4) judge the experimental support as weak and the visual quality poor. (All)
