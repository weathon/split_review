## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Cur-MGAE, a curriculum-based masked graph autoencoder that incorporates a difficulty measurer and self-paced scheduler to mask and reconstruct edges in easy-to-hard order, aiming to address the overlooked variation in difficulty of edge recovery in existing masked graph autoencoders. The paper provides theoretical analysis of the alternating optimization’s convergence and reports experiments on node classification and link prediction. Reviewer scores range from 3 to 6, indicating mixed opinions: one reviewer (R1, score 6) finds the approach thoughtful with solid theory, while another (R4, score 3) raises fundamental concerns about motivation and the scientific definition of edge complexity.

### Strengths

- The paper introduces complexity-guided curriculum masking and self-paced mask scheduling as thoughtful strategies for integrating curriculum learning into graph masked autoencoders, providing a clear framework for applying curriculum learning principles effectively. (R1)
- Detailed theoretical analysis of the convergence properties for the alternating optimization algorithm used in Cur-MGAE. (R1)
- The curriculum-based strategy for masked graph autoencoders is novel and interesting, and may provide insights to other researchers. (R2)
- Considering varying difficulty levels of masking edges is an important improvement over traditional approaches that ignore difficulty. (R2)
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow. (R3)
- The motivation of considering the difficulties of pretext tasks is reasonable. (R3)
- Visualization of the curriculum learning process is provided. (R3)
- Considering the complexity of edges to optimize graph structures and learn high-quality node representations. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Baselines are outdated**: Reviewers find the chosen GNN baselines too old (R1); missing comparisons with GAT and leaderboard models from OGBN-Arxiv like GLEM, GIANT, and SimTeg (R2); and missing discussion of recent works GraphMAE2, AUG-MAE, and Bandana, notably AUG-MAE which also proposes an easy-to-hard training scheduler (R3).  
- **Scalability and efficiency concerns**: The difficulty measurer and self-paced scheduler may introduce extra computational overhead, especially for large graphs; R1 asks for a discussion of computational efficiency, R2 raises scalability concerns.  
- **Theoretical foundation for difficulty levels**:  
  - R2 finds a lack of robust theoretical justification for how difficulty levels are defined, casting doubt on the validity of the curriculum approach.  
  - R4 questions the scientific basis of the definition (subtracting initial structure from learned structure) and its physical significance, as well as its generalizability; asks whether a similar definition could apply to node complexity.  
  - R3 identifies a technical gap: using residual errors of a graph autoencoder (GAE) to measure confidence for a masked graph autoencoder (MaskGAE) is problematic due to their different SSL principles.  
- **Insufficient motivation**: Although R3 finds the motivation reasonable, R4 considers it insufficient, questioning whether edge complexity is important in practice, what specific issues exist in current Mask-GAE, and why edge complexity is considered. (R4)  
- **Complexity of implementation**: The curriculum-based approach may complicate model architecture and training, hindering reproducibility and practical deployment. (R2)  
- **Missing complexity analysis and model size comparison**: The edge embeddings in Equation 2 appear relatively high-dimensional; the paper should provide complexity analysis and compare model parameters with baselines. (R3)  
- **Minor typos**: Missing reference for PubMed in lines 359 and 363; “citepseer” should be “CiteSeer”. (R1)
