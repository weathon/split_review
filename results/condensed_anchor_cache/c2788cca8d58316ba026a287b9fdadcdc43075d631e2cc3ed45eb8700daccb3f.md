## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Talagrand's (2014) chaining method to derive bounds for Gaussian Process Regression prediction intervals, focusing on RBF and Matérn kernels. It claims tighter, practical bounds and improved robustness over scaling posterior standard deviations. Three reviewers scored 6, 6, and 3. The lower-scoring reviewer (R3) finds the empirical presentation insufficient, while the higher-scoring reviewers (R1, R2) see novelty but raise concerns about clarity, scalability, and experimental depth.

### Strengths
- The problem of uncertainty quantification in GPR is important (R1, R2, R3).
- The chaining-based approach is novel and the application to GPR bounds is interesting (R1, R2, R3).
- The bound is clean with a neat proof and has practical value (R2).
- The review of SOTA methods and chaining literature is enlightening (R1).
- Numerical results are presented clearly (R2); comparison with three recent methods shows on-par or better performance (R1).
- The method is tested on real-world datasets with suitable baselines (R3).
- The paper is well written and clearly structured (R3; also echoed by R1 for the early parts).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and exposition gaps:**
  - After Theorem 1, a short discussion connecting it to the GPR bound problem is missing; readers may not see how it applies (R2).
  - The variable \( t_0 \) remains unexplained until the algorithm; its role (e.g., training vs. test point) should be clarified earlier, after Theorem 1 or 2 (R2).
  - The re-definition of RBF and Matérn kernels is trivial and breaks the flow; the presentation of the bounds lacks context and clarity (R1).
  - Proofs are included in the main text but should be moved to the appendix (R1).
  - The statement at L84 about grouping similar variables lacks theoretical justification (R1, Q1).
- **Empirical evaluation concerns:**
  - The performance is called "stellar" (L481), which overstates the results (R1).
  - The wave-style behavior in Figure 1c is unexplained; it may be due to pre-training GP hyperparameters (R1).
  - All examples are for extrapolation (forecasting); the method should either be explained as extrapolation-only or an interpolation (infill) example should be provided (R2).
  - The confidence level \((1-\alpha)\) is never specified, and no results for different \(\alpha\) values are shown (R3 recommends at least two values, possibly in appendix).
  - The PICP metric is marked with an up arrow, implying higher is always better, but PICP should be close to the nominal level, not exceed it; the arrow should be removed and text clarified (e.g., "one test point remains uncovered" is not by itself indicative of good/bad performance) (R3).
  - All metrics in Table 1 lack uncertainty estimates; the reader cannot assess statistical significance (R3).
- **Scalability and computational cost:**
  - The maximum number of observations for Algorithm 1 is not discussed (R1).
  - Computational cost and scalability with data size \(N\) are not considered; no runtime comparisons are provided (R1, R3).
- **Theoretical extensions and missing comparisons:**
  - The bound for RBF and Matérn 3/2 should be extended to generic Matérn smoothness \(\nu\) (at least \(\nu=1/2,5/2\)), or the challenges should be discussed (R2).
  - The impact of high-dimensional domains (e.g., dimension 100) on the bound is not addressed (R2).
  - Mentioning related probabilistic bounds such as Adaptive Cholesky Gaussian Processes (Bartels et al., AISTATS 2023) would add context (R1, Q3).
  - The claim in L97 that for sufficiently large \(n\), \(\pi_n(t)=t\) and the approximation stops needs clarification (R1, Q2).
- **Minor issues:**
  - L133 typo: “ecently” instead of “recently” (R1).
  - In Section 5.2, the font for metric names like PICP should be \(\mathrm{PICP}\) (R2).
- **Title similarity:** The title is very close to Fiedler et al. (2021) “Practical and Rigorous Uncertainty Bounds for Gaussian Process Regression,” which may confuse readers (R3).
