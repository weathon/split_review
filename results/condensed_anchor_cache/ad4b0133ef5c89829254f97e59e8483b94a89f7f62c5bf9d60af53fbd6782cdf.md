## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles visual imitation from observations (V-IfO), where the agent learns from expert videos without access to expert actions and must handle partial observability from pixels. A theoretical upper bound is established, showing that the learner’s suboptimality can be bounded by the TV divergence between the expert’s and agent’s latent state‑transition distributions. Motivated by this bound, the authors propose Latent Adversarial Imitation from Observations (LAIfO), which combines an off‑policy adversarial imitation loss (with gradient penalties from DAC) with the stacking and data‑augmentation strategy of DrQv2, and learns latent representations by backpropagating the Q‑function loss (from DDPG) through the encoder. The method is evaluated on DeepMind Control tasks and compared against PatchAIL, VMAIL, and LAIL. Experiments also incorporate demonstrations into vision‑based RL (vs. DreamerV2 and DrQv2). Reviewers are split: one found the work sound and novel (score 8), another considered it limited in novelty and evaluation (score 3), and a third noted modest empirical gains (score 5).

### Strengths
- Sound theoretical analysis (Theorems 1 and 2 on the upper bound of suboptimality in POMDPs) – reviewed as correct, with assumptions clearly communicated.
- The problem setting (visual imitation without actions) is relevant and important.
- The method is a novel combination of existing components (DAC’s adversarial imitation with gradient penalty, DrQv2’s frame stacking and data augmentation, and latent representation learning via Q‑function gradients).
- Presentation is clear: notation is well defined, assumptions are stated explicitly, and the algorithm is straightforward to reproduce.
- The paper is well written and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Statistics and significance**: Table 2 results have overlapping confidence intervals; highlighting the best value in bold is misleading (Reviewer 1).
- **Unfair RLfD comparison**: In the reinforcement‑learning‑from‑demonstrations setting, none of the baselines (DreamerV2, DrQv2) use expert demonstrations. Reviewer 1 calls this unfair; Reviewer 2 also requests results where DrQv2/DreamerV2 have expert demos added to their replay buffer.
- **Limited novelty and missing related work**: The method is a straightforward combination of DAC and DrQv2; Section 5 should explicitly attribute these components (Reviewer 2). The theoretical bounds are straightforward variations of previous TV‑distance bounds in IL/RL (Reviewer 1). Important related work is missed: defining imitation rewards with Sinkhorn distances (beyond the GAIL framework) (Reviewer 3). The claim that all prior observational‑IL works assume full observability is inaccurate – some works (e.g., [3] as cited, and [4,5]) deal with visual observations (Reviewer 2).
- **Narrow evaluation scope**: Only 6 tasks from 3 DMC environments for imitation; only 3 tasks from one environment for RLfD. Reviewer 2 suggests including more complex environments (quadruped, Jaco arm) and other benchmarks (car racing, claw rotate from VMAIL). The ablation is limited – missing studies of spectral norm vs. gradient penalty, number of stacked frames, type of data augmentation, and a simple behavior‑cloning baseline with the same architecture/data augmentation/optimization.
- **Baseline performance inconsistency**: The reported performance of DrQv2 and DreamerV2 is considerably lower than in their original papers; the gains from expert demonstrations appear marginal. The authors should clarify this discrepancy (Reviewer 2).
- **Misleading claims**: The statement “we solve these tasks” for the humanoid tasks is inaccurate because performance on walk and run is still very low (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experiments and details**:
  - How is adding a reward objective to the imitation loss justified? Can detrimental effects be bounded? (Reviewer 1)
  - Were alternative representation‑learning approaches considered (e.g., contrastive losses, maximizing predictive information)? (Reviewer 1)
  - Details on expert data collection protocol are not in the main text (Reviewer 2).
  - Learning curves for more steps on the humanoid tasks, and full 3e7‑step DrQv2 curves for reference in Figure 4, are missing (Reviewer 2).
  - Does the latent representation require pretraining, or is it learned end‑to‑end? (Reviewer 3)
- **Modest empirical improvement**: The proposed approach does not show much superior sample efficiency or final return compared to baselines (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8) found the method sound, well presented, and novel, while another (score 3) considered the novelty limited and the evaluation insufficient, and a third (score 5) noted only modest performance gains. This contrast persists throughout the reviews (e.g., the positive view of the theory vs. the criticism of limited insight).
