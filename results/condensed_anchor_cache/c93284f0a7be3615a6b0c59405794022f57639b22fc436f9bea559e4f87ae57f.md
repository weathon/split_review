## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a unified probabilistic formulation for diffusion-based image editing, where a latent variable is edited task-specifically and defines a corresponding SDE or ODE for the reverse process. The authors prove that the KL divergence between the marginal distributions of two SDEs decreases as time approaches zero, while that for ODEs remains constant, arguing for the advantage of SDEs. They provide SDE counterparts for existing ODE baselines in inpainting, image-to-image translation, and propose SDE-Drag for point-based dragging. A new benchmark, DragBench, is introduced. Experiments across all three tasks consistently show SDE outperforming ODE baselines. Reviewers agree on the clarity of presentation and the solidness of experimental evidence; however, there are disagreements on theoretical novelty, depth of justification, and completeness of comparisons with state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths

1.  **Clear writing and contribution statements** – the argumentation is limpid and well structured (all reviewers).
2.  **Comprehensive theoretical analysis** – proof that SDE marginals converge in KL while ODE marginals stay constant, reinforced with an illustrative Gaussian toy example (Reviewers 1, 3; Reviewer 3 calls it “comprehensive”).
3.  **DragBench benchmark** – a new open-set dataset for point‑based dragging, with a user study showing SDE-Drag significantly outperforms the ODE baseline, existing diffusion methods, and DragGAN (Reviewers 1, 2).
4.  **Extensive experiments across three tasks** – inpainting, image‑to‑image translation, and dragging – consistently demonstrating SDE’s superiority over ODE baselines (all reviewers; Reviewer 1 notes the experimental study is “impressive”).
5.  **SDE-Drag algorithm** – a simple yet effective method for point‑based content dragging (Reviewer 3).
6.  **Code provided** – ensures reproducibility (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

1.  **Theoretical justification questioned** – Reviewer 1 doubts that minimizing KL(\(p_0,\tilde{p}_0\)) is desirable for editing because the score is imperfect and \(p_0\) differs from the true data distribution \(q_0\); the paper’s problem statement (end of Section 3.1) is never clearly answered. The relevance of the log‑Sobolev inequality and the “fixed noise” variant of CycleSDE are also left unclear (Reviewer 1, major). (Reviewer 3, in contrast, finds the theoretical analysis “comprehensive”.)
2.  **Insufficient novelty** – the core idea (SDE beats ODE in editing) is similar to CycleDiffusion [6]; this paper mainly generalizes the phenomenon to multiple tasks without a significant new insight (Reviewer 2).
3.  **Missing comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art methods** – for inpainting and I2I, the paper does not compare against RePaint [1], DDNM [2], DPS [3], T2I‑Adapter [4], ControlNet [5], or more recent contextual‑attention/transformer‑based inpainting models and GAN‑/diffusion‑based I2I methods. This makes it hard to assess the true practical impact (Reviewers 2, 3).
4.  **Missing experimental details** – the specific diffusion model and training dataset used are not reported (Reviewer 1).
5.  **Missing runtime information** – cost time is provided only for the dragging task; runtime for inpainting and I2I is omitted, hindering evaluation of practical applicability and comparison with reported timings of existing methods (Reviewer 3).
6.  **Minor exposition issues**:
    *   Section 2 “Samples” paragraph incorrectly uses “equivalent” for sampling vs. discretization; discretizing (4) or (5) does not sample from \(q_0\) if the score is imperfect (Reviewer 1).
    *   The ODE inversion process and the relation between deterministic inversion and adding Gaussian noise need clarification (Reviewer 1).
    *   The “mild assumption” should be detailed, at least in the appendix (Reviewer 1).
