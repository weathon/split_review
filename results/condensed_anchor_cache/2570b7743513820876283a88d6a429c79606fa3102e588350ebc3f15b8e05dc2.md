## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides approximation and estimation theory for convolutional residual networks (ConvResNeXts) trained with weight decay, focusing on nonparametric classification of smooth target functions supported on a low-dimensional manifold. The authors prove that these overparameterized networks can adapt to the function’s smoothness and low-dimensional structure, avoiding the curse of dimensionality. The analysis extends to infinite building blocks and shows that weight decay implicitly enforces sparsity. The theoretical results cover both approximation and generalization bounds for functions in a Besov class.

### Strengths
- The paper performs strong, rigorous analysis and develops interesting theoretical guarantees for ConvResNeXts, a well-known architecture with high practical performance. (Reviewer 1)
- The ideas of proof are novel and interesting. (Reviewer 1)
- The problem addressed—understanding overparameterized ConvResNets through the lens of weight decay and nonparametric classification—is important to the community. (Reviewer 2)
- Central results are clear and easy to locate; Section 4 is extremely useful for understanding the proofs. (Reviewer 4) [Reviewer 4 is overall more negative, but still acknowledges this strength.]
- The paper makes a good combination of several ideas on neural network construction and generalization estimation; most claims are well-supported. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison with prior work, especially feedforward networks, and unclear novelty.** Multiple reviewers (1, 3, 4) note that the paper does not provide a direct comparison with prior research on different NN architectures and function spaces. It is not clear how the analysis of ConvResNeXts differs from analyses of standard feedforward networks or standard ResNets, especially regarding metric entropy bounds and the impact of overparameterization. Reviewers 3 and 4 point out that adaptivity to smooth functions on a manifold is a well-known property of fully connected networks, even in the kernel regime. The paper should state which results also apply to feedforward networks, which are novel, and where there is an actual benefit for using convolutional networks. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4) [Reviewer 4 is particularly critical, while Reviewer 2 considers the problem important; thus a contrast exists on this point.]
- **Assumption of global optimum in Theorem 4.** The analysis depends on finding the global optimizer of the regularized empirical risk, which is a strong assumption given the non-convex nature of neural network training. While weight decay encourages sparsity, it does not guarantee finding the global optimum. The theoretical results would be more compelling if tied to practical optimization algorithms such as SGD, as the gap between the theoretical global optimum and practical solutions raises questions about practical relevance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** Several reviewers note problems:
  - Unclear language and an unfinished sentence (page 7, line 4). (Reviewer 1)
  - The definition of ConvResNeXts (end of second paragraph) is too brief and insufficient. (Reviewer 4)
  - The definition of Besov spaces is too dense and difficult to understand; examples and a more detailed account of how they generalize Sobolev and Holder spaces are needed. (Reviewer 4) The paragraph explaining Besov spaces in the conclusions also needs clarification and concrete support for claims like “capturing edges.” (Reviewer 4)
  - Typos: Definition 6 has an unclosed bracket; “hyperparameters parameters” on bottom of page 5 should be “hyperparameters”; “our This” at top of page 7. (Reviewer 3)
  - Some constants in the approximation and generalization bounds have exponential dependence on the intrinsic dimension of the manifold; this should be shown explicitly. (Reviewer 3)
  - [Disagreement: Reviewer 2 found the paper well-written.]
- **No numerical experiments.** The paper does not conduct any experiments to support the theoretical findings. The absence of empirical validation makes it hard to assess practical relevance; experiments demonstrating the performance of ConvResNeXts, the impact of weight decay, and behavior on low-dimensional manifolds are needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Restriction to binary classification with logistic loss.** The theoretical guarantees are limited to binary classification with the empirical logistic loss; it is unclear whether they extend to other commonly used loss functions such as cross-entropy or mean squared error. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing clarifications on several key claims and concepts.** (Reviewer 1)
  - Remark 1: Why is there only a small number of non-trivial blocks, and why does weight decay play a crucial role?
  - Why does the curse of dimensionality not occur? Is it due to the ConvResNeXt structure, the Besov function space, or other assumptions?
  - The claim that ConvResNeXts “efficiently learn” the function: does this mean sample efficiency or computational efficiency?
  - What is the intuition behind the claim that ConvResNeXts learn Besov functions with a convergence rate close to minimax and significantly faster than NTK methods?
