## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for joint audio-visual generation of talking faces from a static image, a voice profile, and a target text, using a multi-entangled latent space to synchronize audio and video modalities. The model comprises three phases: encoding, entangled latent space formation, and decoding. It is tested on VoxCeleb, CelebV-HQ, and HDTF and claims favorable scores in FVD, FID, FAD, and MCD over recent baselines. Reviewers are divided: some praise the meaningful setting and quantitative gains (scores 6, 5, 5, 6), while one reviewer (score 3) considers the technical contribution minimal due to combining existing architectures (Hallo, XTTS, etc.) and lacking sufficient ablation.

### Strengths
- Synthesizing audio and video together from a text prompt, image, and audio profile addresses a meaningful and realistic setting for talking face generation. (R1)
- The multi-entangled latent space for integrating multiple modalities is a novel concept that targets limitations in synchronous audio–video generation. (R2, R5)
- The framework design appears reasonable, employing attention mechanisms for cross-modal fusion. The three-phase pipeline (encoding, latent space, decoding) is clearly described. (R1, R2, R3)
- Quantitative results show notable improvements over recent approaches, with favorable scores in metrics like FVD, FID, and FAD for video quality and MCD for audio quality. (R1, R2, R5)
- The ablation study indicates that key techniques are effective (R1); however, some reviewers find the ablation incomplete (see Weaknesses).
- The model is tested on multiple datasets (VoxCeleb, CelebV-HQ, HDTF), suggesting robustness and generalization. (R2, R5)
- Code is provided to facilitate reproducibility (R3); though one reviewer could not locate the claimed model checkpoints and dataset on GitHub (see Weaknesses).
- The approach has potential applications in real-world scenarios. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing evaluation of audio–visual synchronization**: Although the paper identifies lip synchronization as a target problem, there is no dedicated metric or direct analysis of temporal alignment between generated audio and lip movements. Lip-sync accuracy and expressiveness are not thoroughly evaluated quantitatively or qualitatively. (R1, R5)
- **Lack of qualitative results**: Only two examples are available on GitHub. Without playable video/audio samples, it is difficult to assess coherence, smoothness, synchronization, realisticness, and expressiveness across diverse subjects, lighting, head poses, and speech styles. (R1)
- **Insufficient ablation studies**: The multi-entangled latent space and cross-modal attention mechanisms lack analysis of their individual contributions. The only ablation provided removes both encoders or shares encoders. Clarification is needed on which modality interactions are essential for each generation task (e.g., impact of removing vision tokens on audio generation; role of vision tokens in audio generation). (R2, R4)
- **Computational cost and generation time not discussed**: No information on parameter size, FLOPs, training cost, or inference time is given. The multi-stage framework with transformers and diffusion models may be expensive, and practical applicability (e.g., real-time adaptation) is unclear. (R2, R5)
- **Dependence on clean audio profile and lack of noise robustness evaluation**: Robustness to various noise types (background noise, reverberation, speech overlap) and different SNR levels is not discussed. (R2)
- **Missing technical details**: Hyperparameters, training iterations, training time, and loss function balance are not reported. (R3)
- **Poor figure quality**: Figures 1, 3, and 4 are unclear, which detracts from readability. (R3)
- **Missing resources**: Model checkpoints and the proposed dataset claimed to be on GitHub were not found by one reviewer. (R3)
- **Minimal technical contribution (minority view)**: One reviewer (score 3) argues that the task primarily combines existing architectures (Hallo, XTTS, etc.) and could have been approached as zero-shot, questioning the novelty. (R4)
- **Missing important prior work**: FaceChain‑ImagineID (Xu et al., CVPR 2024) is not cited. (R5)
- **Unaddressed ethical considerations**: Potential misuse (privacy risks, unauthorized identity mimicry, deepfakes) is not discussed; safeguards are not mentioned. (R5)
- **Unanswered conceptual aspects**: The practical execution of the multi-entangled latent space for managing spatiotemporal alignment across modalities needs clarification. (R2, R5)
- **Additional unclear details**: Which components use pretrained weights versus being trained? How were frames sampled in the figures? Why were some commonly used benchmark datasets not employed for direct comparisons, and which values in Tables 1–2 were re-evaluated versus taken from original papers? Does training with reference audio cause biases linking visual features to specific audio profiles (e.g., child’s voice with adult male image) that affect inference? How does the model handle diverse accents and languages? (R1, R2, R4, R5)
