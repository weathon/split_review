## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a semi-supervised continual learning setup (open-world CL) and a method, OpenACL, that uses proxy-based contrastive learning to leverage unlabeled data containing novel classes. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet show improvements over baselines. The reviewers acknowledge the practical motivation but have significant concerns about novelty, comparisons, experimental limitations, and the realism of the open-world assumption.

### Strengths
- Addresses the practical limitation of label scarcity in online continual learning (OCL), making it more applicable to real-world scenarios (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- The proposed setup (semi-supervised CL with open-world unlabeled data) is more realistic and challenging than standard CL (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 2).
- The method is conceptually simple and outperforms prior arts on several benchmarks (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- Innovative use of contrastive learning and proxies to effectively utilize unlabeled data for continual learning (Reviewer 3).
- Paper is easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- Evaluated in both task-incremental and class-incremental scenarios, demonstrating broad applicability (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Technical novelty and related work**: The method is a straightforward application of contrastive learning to semi-supervised CL (Reviewer 2). The problem setup has been proposed before (Boschini et al., *Pattern Recognition Letters*, 2022) (Reviewer 2). Related work omits existing semi-supervised CL methods (e.g., Kang et al., ICCV 2023) and generalized category discovery (GCD) methods using proxy learning (e.g., Kim et al., ICCV 2023) (Reviewer 1). The "open-world continual learning" problem definition should clarify if it is first proposed or by others (Reviewer 3). It also differs from but is not compared to "Continual Category Discovery" (Cendra et al., Ma et al.); the relation and relative challenge should be discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical gains and baselines**: Gains are marginal (often within standard deviation) compared to prior arts (Reviewer 2). Table 7 shows proposed components bring little gain (Reviewer 2). Baseline comparisons are incomplete: why not include Kang et al. (even in online setting)? Adding LUMP + kNN for labeled data would be informative (Reviewer 1). For class-incremental learning (Table 2), results are poor; comparisons should include recent transformer-based CL methods (L2P, DualPrompt) (Reviewer 4). The claim that unlabeled data helps catastrophic forgetting (L22-23) is not backed by sufficient ablation on label ratio: only 20%, 50%, and 10% (Appendix) are tested; ratios like 0.5%, 1%, 5% from prior SSL-CL works (Fini et al., Madaan et al.) are missing (Reviewer 1). Stanford Cars results (Table 12) are very low and hard to compare (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental scope**: Datasets are too small (CIFAR, Tiny-ImageNet); ImageNet-1K is needed to validate scalability (Reviewer 2). Only one network architecture used (Reviewer 1). Realism of open-world assumption: using partial data from the same dataset is a strong assumption; true open-world data may come from different domains (Reviewer 3). Also, the method appears to use all unlabeled data each task, which is impractical if unlabeled data is enormous (Reviewer 3). The combination of SSL+CL may trivially solve the setup (e.g., SimCLR+CL near SOTA in Tab.1); the unique obstacles of combining SSL and CL in open-world should be clarified (Reviewer 4).
- **Hyperparameter and sensitivity analysis**: No sensitivity study for λ (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). What is the k in k-means initialization? (Reviewer 1)
- **Representation analysis**: Need analysis of similarity between labeled data and proxy clusters, and whether proxy clusters contain examples of the same class (Reviewer 4).
- **Limitation of proxy count**: Number of proxies must equal number of classes per task, restricting flexibility (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and notation**: Issue with consistency (eq4: \(x_j \neq x_i\) vs. index check; eq5 introduces \(A(i)\) differently) (Reviewer 1). Eq.5 and 6 should use \(\mathrm{sim}\) instead of "sim" (Reviewer 4). L323 typo: "overwriting" → "overriding" (Reviewer 2). Missing autoref for equations and appendix (Reviewer 4). Introduction only mentions forgetting, not the combined challenge of forgetting and adaptability (L201-202) (Reviewer 2).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive about the innovation and realism, while the other three (scores 3,3,5) are more critical of novelty, marginal gains, and weak comparisons.
