## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ARTE (Aligning TeacheR with StudenT PreferencEs), a framework that aligns a teacher LLM with student preferences via DPO to generate tailored instruction-tuning datasets for lightweight student models on reasoning tasks. The pipeline consists of initial question–rationale generation, preference collection using student ICL performance as a proxy, teacher alignment, and final data generation. Experiments on Big-Bench-Hard and other reasoning benchmarks show performance gains over existing instruction-tuning datasets.

### Strengths
*   **Novel paradigm and promising results.** The core idea of aligning teacher data generation with student preferences is new (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Main results show large-margin improvements over several baselines (Reviewer 1). The aligned teacher can generate useful data even for tasks and student models not seen during alignment (Reviewer 3).
*   **Thorough empirical analysis.** Extensive experiments across multiple reasoning tasks and student models provide a solid evaluation (Reviewers 2, 4). Insightful findings on rationale length (longer not always better) and consistency are reported (Reviewers 3, 4).
*   **Clear and well-structured presentation.** The paper is easy to follow and the content is sufficient (Reviewers 2, 3). Some reviewers rated the paper 5, highlighting its novelty and potential (Reviewers 2, 4), while others rated it 3, citing methodological concerns (Reviewers 1, 3); this contrast reflects disagreement on overall rigor.

### Weaknesses
*   **Unfair baseline comparisons and missing related work.** Most baselines (e.g., OpenOrca, UltraChat, GPT-4-LLM, Tulu-v2, WizardLM) are built for general instruction-following, not reasoning tasks, creating a distribution shift in Table 3 and 4 comparisons (Reviewers 2, 3). The paper should compare *methods* (e.g., using the same seed dataset) rather than directly using existing datasets (Reviewer 3 question 1). Additionally, several closely related works are missing: One-Shot Learning as Instruction Data Prospector (ACL24), Selective Reflection-Tuning (ACL24 findings), and LLM2LLM (ACL24 findings) all use student feedback or data selection (Reviewer 2). The paper should also compare against other KD or alignment methods (Reviewer 1).
*   **Limited model diversity and feasibility concerns.** Experiments only use one teacher (Llama3-70B) and one student size (2B). It is unclear whether the method extends to larger students (e.g., 7B) or smaller teachers (Reviewers 1, 3). Was PEFT used for DPO on the 80B teacher? Full fine-tuning of LLaMA3-80B with DPO on 8 A100-80GB GPUs would be infeasible; this detail is missing (Reviewer 4 question 2).
*   **Weak justification of ICL score as a preference proxy.** The use of one-shot ICL performance to evaluate question–rationale pairs is not well-justified. A low ICL score may simply indicate an incorrect rationale, not a preference misalignment. How does a single score separately evaluate question quality and rationale quality? (Reviewer 4 weakness 1, question 1). The claim that longer rationales do not improve performance (Insight 1) lacks experimental support or case studies that directly attribute the effect to the lost-in-the-middle or repetition problems (Reviewer 3 question 3). It may also be that the evaluation metric is unsuitable for long responses (Reviewer 2). Moreover, these insights may only hold for sub-task fine-tuning, not general instruction-following (Reviewer 2; citing ICLR24 on evaluating instruction following).
*   **Lack of theoretical depth.** The pedagogical motivation of "responsive teaching" is not backed by strong theoretical justification or on-policy analysis (Reviewer 1). The paper lacks a formal theoretical framework connecting the method to its effectiveness (Reviewer 4).
*   **Missing ablations and efficiency analysis.** Training overhead and time efficiency are not discussed (Reviewer 1). No ablation on the size of the teacher’s DPO training dataset (Reviewer 3 question 4). The potential benefit of iterative multi-round generation (e.g., as in Self-Play Fine-Tuning and Self-Instruct) is not explored (Reviewer 3 question 5). The reduction in gain as the number of shots increases (delta) should be tested beyond 3 shots (e.g., 5); the method may be most beneficial in low-shot settings (Reviewer 4 question 3).
*   **Reproducibility and writing issues.** Code and datasets are not released (Reviewer 4). The methodology section interleaves experimental details and is not written generically as a framework (Reviewer 4). Typos include: line 162 "trained" → "train"; line 209 "to" → "on"; line 229 "Since We" → "We"; line 512 "have also inspired" → "have also been inspired" (Reviewer 1).
