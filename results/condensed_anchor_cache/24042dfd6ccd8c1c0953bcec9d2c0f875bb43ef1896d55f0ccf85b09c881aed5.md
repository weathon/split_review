## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the steerable EquivarLayer, a generalization of InvarLayer (Li et al., 2024) that extends affine-invariant layers to steerable equivariant layers supporting arbitrary input and output group representations. It builds on the method of moving frames and differential invariants, and introduces a Det-Pooling module to enable canonicalization of pre-trained models for the affine group (and its subgroups) beyond the Euclidean group. Experiments on transformed MNIST image classification under affine, rotation-scaling, and scaling transformations demonstrate the effectiveness of EquivarLayer as a canonicalization function compared to data augmentation.

### Strengths
- Novel pooling layer (Det-Pooling) that preserves equivariance to affine groups (Reviewer 1).
- Clear presentation with well-introduced mathematical objects; addresses a larger class of group representations than previously seen for this framework (Reviewer 2).
- The problem of constructing equivariant/invariant layers for larger transformation groups (e.g., affine, 6 DOF) is generally underexplored and potentially useful (Reviewer 2).
- Intertwining canonicalization with traditional equivariant layer construction via moving frames is a useful avenue (Reviewer 2).
- Novel and efficient method for extending affine-invariant layers to affine steerable equivariant layers; the compact model size (0.4M parameters for ResNet-32) may enable very deep equivariant networks (Reviewer 3).
- Technically sound and original in the context of steerable networks: linearly combining equivariants produced by moving frames using type-0 (scalar) features as weights (Reviewer 4).
- The considered application for canonicalization using global invariant pooling is technically sound and properly shows utility, since canonicalization only using type-0 features is not straightforward (Reviewer 4).
- Experiments show canonicalization of a non-equivariant backbone for invariant MNIST classification under affine transformations, which was previously unavailable in the literature (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison with fully equivariant networks**: The paper does not compare against fully equivariant baselines such as InvarPDEs-Net, InvarLayer, or scale-equivariant steerable networks. This includes accuracy, number of parameters, and computational cost (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 notes the scale range used (0.8–1.2/1.6) differs from standard scale-equivariant papers (e.g., [0.3,1]) and related works (e.g., Sosnovik et al.) are not cited (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing baseline: moving frame used directly for canonicalization**: The moving frame (Definition 8) or equivariant matrix (Theorem 10) could be used directly for canonicalization without multiplication by type-0 features (Eq. 10), which coincides with frame averaging (Puny et al., 2021). Adding this baseline would ablate trainability from canonicalization (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Unclear connection between moving frame definition and equivariant construction**: The logical steps from Definition 8/Theorem 9 to the construction of equivariants using relative invariants in Section 2.3 are not clearly explained. Notation after Eq. (16) (e.g., \(u_x, u_y\)) is undefined; the derivation of \(\alpha\) in Eq. (14) and how the equivariant matrix is obtained (Eq. 17) lacks explanation. Reviewers question why only first- and second-order gradients are used and whether gradient order relates to information preservation (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Limited demonstration of the framework**: The experiments only apply the proposed layer as the last layer of a canonicalization network (extracting 2-channel type-1 features from type-0 features). Missing: (1) feature maps of type >1, (2) layers taking non-scalar features as input, (3) layers mapping between non-scalar features, and (4) deeper networks with multiple non-scalar layers. A straightforward extension would be to modify InvarPDEs-Net/InvarLayer to have non-scalar hidden features (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Novelty claim overblown (according to Reviewer 1)**: Many concepts up to Section 2.3 (moving frames, prolongation, relative invariants) are already present in Olver’s work and in Sangalli et al. (applied to image data). The paper should clarify how it differs from [6] regarding relative invariants and note alternative treatments for zero-division errors ([5],[7]) (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of concrete examples beyond image data**: The paper would benefit from application on other modalities (e.g., [9]) and from presenting more concrete realizations of the operators beyond theory (Reviewer 1).
- **Experiment design and reporting**: The demonstration of canonicalization via invariant classification is indirect; the downstream ResNet-50 obscures the strengths/limitations of EquivarLayer. Train/test split is not reported; time/space complexity analysis is absent; standard 3-channel image data (e.g., CIFAR-10/100) is not included (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing experiments/comparisons** (from reviewer questions, included as weaknesses):
  - Results on additional datasets (CIFAR-10/100) with comparison to Steerable CNNs (Reviewer 1).
  - Comparison with the simple canonicalization method of Kaba et al. (Reviewer 1).
  - Evaluation on roto-translation and scale transformations separately (Reviewer 1).
  - Scaling analysis with data size, dimensions of invariants, and group size (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical clarifications needed** (reviewer questions that reflect gaps in clarity or missing analysis):
  - Are there restrictions on activation functions for non-scalar fields (as in steerable methods)? Must norm non-linearities be used? (Reviewer 2).
  - How does Det-Pooling ensure a parametrization on GL(2,R) (mapping to \(\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}\) does not guarantee a group element)? (Reviewer 2).
  - What is the connection between this work and Olver’s relative invariants? (Reviewer 2). Does it extend to reflections (\(\det(A)<0\))? (Reviewer 3).
  - Is it necessary that equivariant matrices are square (Definition 6)? Should the number of columns be flexible? (Reviewer 4).
  - The paper’s limitation about sampling from GL(n)-invariant measure on SPD matrices seems unclear; such sampling is possible (e.g., Said et al., 2015) (Reviewer 2).
