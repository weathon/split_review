## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SlimHead, a two-stage slim-and-fat head network that downsamples then upsamples feature pyramid maps to reduce computational cost in dense object detectors. Reviewers agree the method is simple, clearly presented, and tested across multiple tasks (object detection, pedestrian detection, instance segmentation) and datasets (COCO, PASCAL VOC, DOTA, CrowdHuman). A clear split exists: two reviewers (2, 3) rate it 5, praising the ablation study and breadth of experiments; two reviewers (1, 4) rate it 3, calling it an engineering trick with marginal gains, outdated baselines, and available simpler alternatives. All note that speed improvements are small (< 1 FPS) and novelty is limited.

### Strengths
- Clear writing, good figures, easy to understand (reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Extensive experiments across multiple dense perception tasks (object detection, oriented object detection, pedestrian detection, instance segmentation) and datasets (COCO, Pascal VOC, DOTA, CrowdHuman) (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Ablation study sufficiently demonstrates effectiveness of each component (reviewer 3).
- Proposes an efficient head module aiming for better speed-accuracy trade-off (reviewer 2).
- Reviewers 2 and 3 find the method a credible efficiency improvement; reviewers 1 and 4 consider these strengths insufficient given the limited novelty and marginal gains.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty** – Repeatedly described as an engineering trick: only applying an interpolator and inverse interpolator to change feature map size. Two reviewers (1, 4) find the contribution insufficient for ICLR; reviewers 2 and 3 also note the simplicity but are more open to its practical value. (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
2. **Minimal speed improvement** – For all models shown, FPS gain is < 1 FPS (e.g., Table 5). With larger backbones like Swin‑L, improvement becomes tiny (Table 9: 6.6 → 6.7 FPS). Reviewers 1 and 2 question the practical impact. (reviewers 1, 2)
3. **Outdated comparisons** – Table 5 uses old competitive methods; the absolute COCO AP is 43.2, whereas SOTA detectors now exceed 65.9. Results are “comparable to research from a few years ago.” (reviewers 3, 4)
4. **Problematic baseline reporting** – In Table 5, GFocalV2 achieves 41.1 AP on COCO 2017 val, but the official GFocalV2 with ResNet‑50 reports 44.3 on test set – a discrepancy not explained by dataset difference alone. Similarly, TOOD is reported as 42.3 vs. the original 42.5. (reviewers 2, 3, 4)
5. **Alternative simpler methods achieve similar results** – Reviewer 1 shows that simply halving the channels at P3/P4 (Figure 8) gives AP 39.3 vs. 39.4 and FPS 39.9 vs. 40.2, adding only 2M parameters. Why not adopt this? Another alternative: apply DCN only on high-level features (Table 4) – more cost‑effective. (reviewer 1)
6. **Insufficient support for “generic” claim** – Experiments only use ResNet‑50 backbone for object detection; Table 9 shows Swin‑L yields marginal gains even with 2× schedule. Missing experiments with ResNet‑101, RetinaNet, etc. (reviewer 2)
7. **Non‑uniform configuration engineering** – SlimHead uses DCN in the first two layers for detection but in all layers for segmentation; reviewer 2 questions how to choose configurations, whether it requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, and if NAS could automate it. (reviewer 2)
8. **Lack of comparison with efficient backbones and other efficiency techniques** – Reviewer 2 asks for comparisons with EfficientNetV2, EfficientViT, as well as pruning (Molchanov ICLR 17, He ICCV 17) and quantization methods that do not require retraining. (reviewer 2)
9. **Missing ablation on channel/spatial reduction and residual connections** – Reviewer 2 asks: have the authors considered reducing both spatial and channel dimensions simultaneously, or adding residual connections in SlimHead? (reviewer 2)
10. **Trade‑off across feature scales** – Performance improvement mainly on medium/large objects, yet the method modifies shallow features (targeting small objects). Reviewer 2 asks whether there is a trade‑off between feature scales. (reviewer 2)
11. **Question about finetuning only head layers** – Reviewer 2 asks whether SlimHead works when finetuning only the head from a pretrained model on a subset of data, which would broaden applicability. This missing experiment is listed here as a weakness. (reviewer 2)
12. **Minor writing issues** – Sub‑figures labelled “figure3Right” (should be “Figure 3, right”); typo “dimention” (L214). (reviewer 2)

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Reviewers 2 and 3 (both rating 5) viewed SlimHead as a reasonable efficiency module with thorough experiments; they acknowledged some weaknesses but were more optimistic. Reviewers 1 and 4 (both rating 3) were sharply critical of the novelty, marginal speed gains, and outdated/incorrect baselines, concluding that many simpler engineering alternatives already offer comparable results.
