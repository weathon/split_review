## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LanGoal, a model-based RL method that combines large language model (LLM) guidance with intrinsic exploration rewards. It uses a hierarchical policy where a high-level policy proposes goals based on LLM guidance and a low-level policy attempts to achieve them, together with a world model. Experiments are conducted solely on the Crafter environment. Some reviewers found the results impressive; however, two reviewers raised serious plagiarism concerns regarding strong similarity to the arXiv paper “World Models with Hints of Large Language Models for Goal Achievement” (DLLM, arXiv:2406.07381). Other reviewers noted limited novelty, poor writing, and insufficient evaluation.

### Strengths
- Some reviewers (R3, R4) found the approach interesting and the results impressive (Crafter is a hard task). R3 specifically highlighted:
  - A novel perspective using LLMs to guide exploration goals for sparse reward RL.
  - A well-structured, adaptable hierarchical policy with language-based interpretability.
  - Clear mathematical formulations and thorough experiments (success rate, goal achievement, ablation studies) on Crafter.
  - Potential applicability to robotics, autonomous navigation, and open-world gaming.
- R4 noted the performance improvements on Crafter as impressive.
- **Disagreement:** Reviewers R1 and R2 identified no strengths, citing plagiarism and lack of originality.

### Weaknesses
- **Plagiarism concerns (R1, R2 with high confidence):**
  - The equations in Section 4.2 of LanGoal are identical or nearly identical to those in Section 4.3 of the DLLM paper (arXiv:2406.07381), including the unconventional abbreviations “catxent” and “binxent.”
  - The reward design section (Section 4.3 of LanGoal) differs from DLLM only by changing the threshold hyperparameter from 0.5 to 0.6.
  - Appendix D merely replicates part of Section 4.3 from DLLM without any additional information or changes.
  - The description of high-level policy generating a hidden state and low-level policy producing actions mirrors the DreamerV3 encoder concept (the backbone of DLLM) with minimal difference.
  - Section 4.4 claims to implement classifier-free guidance (CFG) policy but provides no details on implementation or hyperparameters; RSSM and diffusion are fundamentally different, making this appear as an awkward attempt to broaden scope.
  - Baseline experiment results match those in DLLM without proper citation.
  - The paper’s structure (Figure 1, prompt details in appendix, Table 1) closely resembles DLLM.
- **Limited novelty (R3, R4):**
  - The hierarchical high/low-level policy structure is a common RL technique; the extension with LLM guidance is incremental on existing methods (e.g., ELLM, OMNI) rather than a fundamentally new mechanism.
  - The combination of model-based RL (Dreamer-like) and LLM-guided exploration appears to be a simple combination without clear new problem formulations.
- **Poor writing and presentation (R3, R4):**
  - Many grammatical errors and language issues that impede comprehension (R4 suggests using language tools/native speaker help).
  - Excess blank spaces between figures/equations (R3).
  - Unclear definitions: “semantic guidance” is not defined (R4); the fourth paragraph of the introduction is confusing (R4).
  - The second contribution is vague (R4).
- **Limited environment evaluation (R3, R4):**
  - Only tested on Crafter; not representative of diverse sparse-reward/open-world environments. Missing experiments on navigation, Minecraft, or 3D environments.
- **Insufficient related work and motivation (R4):**
  - No mention of OMNI (arXiv:2306.01711) which also uses LLMs for exploration in Crafter; only ELLM is cited as a baseline but not in related work/introduction.
  - Missing discussion of goal-based exploration as a broader field (e.g., Oudeyer and Kaplan’s competence-based intrinsic motivation; review arXiv:2012.09830).
  - No clear paragraph listing similarities/differences to Dreamer, OMNI, and ELLM.
  - Not explained why HRL or world models help exploration.
- **Method clarity issues (R4):**
  - Many variables undefined: h_t (assumed RSSM memory state), o_t vs x_t in figure mismatch.
  - The world model is made goal-dependent via auxiliary loss of predicting goal v_t; motivation unclear (model-based RL is typically goal-agnostic).
  - The multi-modal decoder description is unclear (two networks with same weights?).
  - Algorithm 1 notation is inconsistent (e.g., v_t = txt_enc(v_t | LLM(g_t | o_l))).
  - The classifier-free guidance section is unexplained and does not improve performance (test-time technique gives no significant boost: biggest difference 0.7 with std 3.6); should be removed or moved to appendix.
- **Goal encoder ambiguity (R4):**
  - “we use an autoencoder to transform the goal state into a discrete action space with lower dimension” – unclear why a semantic goal embedding would be compressed into an action; the following sentence refers to RSSM inner state s_t, not the goal.
  - Equation refers to z_t as latent code and s_t as input; later equation 5 replaces z_t with u_t. The implementation is confusing.
- **Dependency on predefined LLM goals (R3):**
  - Relies on LLM-generated language goals that may not align with agent state in dynamic environments; no feedback mechanism to adjust subsequent goal generation based on exploration results.
- **Scalability concerns (R3):**
  - Frequent LLM queries could cause computational bottlenecks in larger or real-time settings; no exploration of lightweight alternatives (distilled models, reduced query frequency).
- **Limited failure analysis (R3):**
  - Only positive findings presented; no discussion of failure cases where LLM guidance contradicts optimal exploration.
- **Hierarchical policy training dynamics (R3):**
  - Interaction between high-level and low-level policies when objectives conflict (exploration vs. goal-reaching) is unclear; no ablation on hierarchical structure itself.
