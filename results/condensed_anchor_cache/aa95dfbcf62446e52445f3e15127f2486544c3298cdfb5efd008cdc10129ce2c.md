## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes LLIT, a continual reinforcement learning method that uses a pretrained LLM to infer task tips and content from observation/action specs. These are used to train an auxiliary reward model and a prompt pool that captures task correlations, enabling selective sub-network reuse. Experiments on Continual World benchmarks show LLIT outperforming existing CRL methods. Reviewers found the idea novel and the results promising, but the paper received low scores (3,3,1) because it is severely incomplete, lacks implementation details, and is not reproducible. Two reviewers gave score 3 (moderate confidence), while a third reviewer (confidence 5, absolutely certain) gave score 1, stating the paper should be withdrawn. There is strong agreement on major flaws but a split on the severity: the minority reviewer finds the paper fundamentally unacceptable in its current state.

### Strengths

- Novel application of large language models to continual RL: using LLM-inferred task descriptions to guide reward shaping and task-embedding organization. (All reviewers)
- Results on the Continual World benchmark are strong and appear to outperform several baselines and prior SoTA CRL methods. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Experimental evaluation includes ablation studies that help demonstrate the contribution of LLIT’s components. (Reviewer 1)
- The research topic is interesting and important; the idea of grounding human prior knowledge via LLMs is a promising direction for CRL. (Reviewer 1, 3)

### Weaknesses

- **Paper is incomplete and not reproducible.** Many typos, missing figures (e.g., hyperparameter effects, Fig.3), no appendix, and few architectural details. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **No details on key components**:
  - How the auxiliary reward model is trained (what data, target, loss), and why a similarity-based model (f_ARM) can provide a reward signal. (Reviewers 1, 2)
  - How the auxiliary reward and similarity reward are exactly connected to the policy, and whether they vary per time-step or are constant per task. (Reviewer 2)
  - The full architecture, hyperparameters (especially alpha, lambda, D), and prompt design (what prompts are used, why split tips vs. content, example outputs). (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - No parameter count or time complexity; the method likely requires significant additional compute. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear presentation of several sections**:
  - The Grounding Tips to Policy section does not justify why tips must be mapped to observation specs; no alternative methods tested. (Reviewer 2)
  - Section 3.3 (Modular Pool Model) uses notation inconsistent with Figure 1, and the training process (including the embedding e_tn) is not described. (Reviewer 2)
  - The experimental setup (Section 4) assumes full knowledge of the Continual World benchmark; no explanation of what CW provides or what the authors modified. (Reviewer 2)
- **Figures and results are insufficient**:
  - Figure 2 shows most baselines and models do not converge, raising concerns about the evaluation. (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper mentions Mujoco and Classical Control tasks in the setup but provides no experiments on them. (Reviewer 3)
- **Core motivation unclear**. The auxiliary reward model measures semantic similarity; it is unclear how it can act as a reward signal or effectively guide policy learning. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing references**: e.g., "Fig.??" placeholders remain in the text. (Reviewer 3)
