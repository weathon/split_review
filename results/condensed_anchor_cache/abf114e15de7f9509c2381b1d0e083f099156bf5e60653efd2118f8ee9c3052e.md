## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper empirically investigates the intrinsic dimension (ID) of the BLIP vision-language pre-training model on MS-COCO, exploring relationships among ID, modality, and prunability. It finds that visual representations have higher and differently shaped IDs than language representations, that cross-modal attention does not embed modalities into the same low-dimensional manifold, and that visual representations are more sensitive to pruning while language representations are robust (90% language weights pruned with only 3.8 CIDEr drop). An ID-based importance metric is proposed for multimodal pruning. (Reviewer 2 adds that the paper tries to do too much and overclaims on several fronts; there is disagreement, with Reviewers 1 and 3 rating the work higher (5) and Reviewers 2 and 4 rating it lower (3).)

### Strengths
- Pioneering empirical study of intrinsic dimension (ID) in a large-scale multimodal model (BLIP), offering potential theoretical insights into representation structure.
- Interesting findings: higher ID for visual compared to language modality; cross-modal attention struggles to align low-dimensional language representations with high-dimensional visual ones; visual modality is more sensitive to pruning than language.
- Proposes an ID-based importance metric for multimodal pruning and provides experimental support (Tables 1, 2).
- The problem of better understanding multimodal models is important, and the ID perspective has potential for analyzing and improving such models (Reviewer 2).
- Detailed experiments appear to support the main claims (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited generality and insufficient comparisons.** The study is restricted to one model (BLIP). It remains unclear whether findings hold for other architectures (e.g., CLIP, LLaVA, OpenFlamingo, ImageBind) or different training objectives and attention mechanisms (Reviewers 1, 4). For pruning, only one baseline (Sens Zhang 2022) is compared; more established pruning methods and a full performance‑vs‑parameters plot are needed to demonstrate superiority (Reviewer 2).

2. **Overclaiming and lack of substantiation.** The paper claims multiple contributions—empirical ID study, modality contribution analysis, performance prediction, and a superior pruning method—but does not fully support them (Reviewer 2):
   - ID computation relies on the TwoNN method without evaluating the accuracy or robustness of the estimated IDs.
   - The “modality contribution” analysis is only anecdotal (Section 3).
   - The claim that ID can predict model performance is not shown in any experiment; the argument that “maximum ID is more critical” is contradicted by Figure 4, where the last latent layer ID also correlates with performance (Reviewers 2, 3).
   - The claim of “superior pruning” lacks adequate baselines (only one comparison), and the paper would benefit from focusing on one or two well‑supported contributions (Reviewer 2).

3. **Poor presentation and unclear definitions.** Writing and organization are weak: key terms (e.g., TwoNN, Magnitude/Sensitivity pruning, BLIP) are introduced without proper explanation, figures and symbols are unclear (Figures 1, 2, 3: “im”, “op” undefined), and inconsistent citation styles appear (Reviewer 4). Sections such as the comparison of IDs between Transformers and CNNs (Section 3.1) seem out of scope, and the overall narrative lacks a cohesive message (Reviewers 2, 4). The motivation for interpreting cross‑modal attention via IDs (Section 3.3) is not justified, nor are insights validated against existing interpretation methods (e.g., via human user studies; Reviewer 2).

4. **Missing implications and unanswered questions.**
   - The paper does not discuss how the observed ID differences could guide training algorithms, architectural changes, or model selection/hyperparameter tuning in multimodal settings (Reviewer 4).
   - Specific experimental details are unclear: (a) why the hunchback‑shaped ID profiles are not significant in Figure 2 (Reviewer 3); (b) whether “w/o retrain” in Figure 4 means no fine‑tuning, and why not simply state “w/o fine‑tuning” (Reviewer 3); (c) why multiplying IDs in Figure 5 causes a decrease in the ID of pure language representations (Reviewer 3); (d) the statement “ID values have a positive correlation with model performance but not in direct proportion” requires further elaboration (Reviewer 4).
