## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates in-context learning (ICL) in LLMs through the lens of surface repetitions, proposing the concept of *token co-occurrence reinforcement*: tokens that co-occur in the context become more likely to be predicted. Experiments on MMLU and GSM8K demonstrate that this reinforcement can both help (by constraining the output space and formatting output, e.g., forcing “Let’s think step by step”) and harm (when non-informative or incorrect labels are repeated, hurting accuracy). The study also shows that self-reinforcement occurs with random word sequences, that longer repeated stretches strengthen the effect (Figure 3), and that even discontinuous repeated subsequences produce it. Section 4 examines the impact on ICL, finding, for instance, that in GSM8K replacing questions and CoT answers with random tokens reduces performance far less than replacing the separator (Figure 7).

### Strengths

- Novel perspective and framework: introducing token co-occurrence reinforcement as a lens to understand ICL success and failure.
- Systematic, comprehensive experiments using an openly available LLM family (reviewers note the thoroughness of the empirical analysis).
- Meaningful explanations for ICL failures, including the role of spurious correlations induced by token repetition.
- Interesting specific results, e.g., Figure 7 shows that in GSM8K CoT prompting, replacing questions and CoT answers with random tokens hurts performance much less than replacing the separator—highlighting the importance of surface pattern.
- The work explicitly demonstrates how token reinforcement can cause spurious correlations, constraining output space yet also leading to incorrect results.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper does not explain *why* token co-occurrence reinforcement arises. Two reviewers (R1, R2) consider this a fundamental gap. R1 notes that the information-theoretic perspective of Hahn & Goyal (2023) analyses repetition structures via PCFG complexity and tests it experimentally. R2 calls for an analysis of the interaction between prompts and the self-attention mechanism. This criticism is echoed by R4, who finds the paper’s claims about illuminating “inner workings” unclear because the mechanism behind the co-occurrence effect is not explained.
- **Limited generality**: Experiments rely on a single LLM family (R2). To reach common conclusions, more LLM architectures should be tested.
- **Novelty concerns**: The findings are partly derivative of known distributional bias and spurious correlation effects in out-of-distribution generalization literature (R2). The paper needs to offer more insights beyond the observation that such bias exists and should discuss how to mitigate the inevitable distributional bias in demonstrations.
- **Unclear motivation and framing** (R3): The rationale for studying token repetition is not well-articulated; the abstract does not capture the core idea, and the gap the work aims to fill is unclear. The paper must explain why token repetition is a relevant factor to study beyond its mere existence.
- **Clarity and operational definitions** (R3, R4): Key terms—*surface pattern*, *self-reinforcement effect*, *token reinforcement*—are not operationally defined or measured. The mechanisms by which these effects operate are not explained. Experimental details are ambiguous: in Section 3.1, “sentence” examples are described as “randomly generated,” but it is unspecified whether tokens are sampled i.i.d. from the vocabulary with equal probability, and the same question applies to how masked subsequences are resampled (R4). In Section 4.1, “Learning to follow patterns,” it is unclear whether masking the Question means the demonstrations’ questions are replaced with random tokens while the final query question remains intact (R4).
- **Limited real-world relevance** (R3): The experiments are overly specific and not connected to practical applications of ICL; the paper should demonstrate how findings on repetition patterns translate to realistic use cases.
- **Overarching contribution ambiguity** (R4): While the paper aims to explain both limitations and inner workings of ICL, it remains unclear whether co-occurrence reinforcement explains the LM’s ability to pick up input-label mappings or what internal mechanisms cause the effect. The paper hints at connections to prompt-like structures but does not make them explicit.
