## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers found the paper to present a novel method (MOREC) that achieves strong empirical gains—4.6% on D4RL and 25.9% on NeoRL, and is the first to exceed 95% online performance in 6/12 D4RL and 3/9 NeoRL tasks. The concept of a “dynamics reward” and its use as a transition filter is seen as interesting and easy to combine with existing offline MBRL methods. However, several reviewers raised significant concerns: the dynamics reward is not well-justified (appears similar to a density model, no closed-form solution), the choice of GAIL over GAN is not explained, theoretical results (Proposition 1) are limited to the tabular setting and do not clearly explain OOD generalization, and there is contradictory evidence of weak OOD robustness on halfcheetah. Ablation studies are limited, baseline tuning fairness is questioned, and the evaluation is restricted to locomotion tasks. One reviewer (score 8) viewed the contribution as solid, while another (score 6) rated soundness as fair (2) and questioned the core claims.

### Strengths

- Novel concept of “dynamics reward” that is used to filter model rollouts, addressing compounding error in offline MBRL (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Strong empirical results: SOTA improvement of 4.6% on D4RL tasks and 25.9% on NeoRL tasks; first method to exceed 95% of online RL performance in 6/12 D4RL tasks and 3/9 NeoRL tasks (R1, R3).
- Method can be seamlessly integrated with existing offline MBRL methods (R3, R4).
- Helpful analyses: supporting toy experiment, joint distribution analysis, ablation on the filtering mechanism (R2, R4).
- Clear writing and easy to understand (R1).
- A credible and novel perspective on a known problem (R3). One reviewer (score 8) considers it a solid contribution to offline model-based RL.

### Weaknesses

- **Conceptual clarity of dynamics reward**: What the dynamics reward represents is not clear; no closed-form solution or intuitive interpretation is provided. The reward may simply act as a density model over (s,a,s’) transitions, raising the question: why not learn a density model directly? (R1, R2)
- **Unnecessary complexity**: Framing the dynamics model as an agent and introducing a reward adds confusion. A simpler adversarial formulation (e.g., using the discriminator score directly) would be easier to understand (R2).
- **Choice of GAIL over GAN not justified**: The offline dataset is fixed, so one could treat the dynamics model as a generator and the dynamics reward as a discriminator; the paper does not explain why the IRL/GAIL formulation is preferred over a standard GAN (R4).
- **Theoretical result limited to tabular setting**: Proposition 1 only holds for tabular dynamics. It does not explain how a discriminator trained on in-distribution transitions generalizes to OOD samples under function approximation. The halfcheetah experiments (especially medium-expert and medium) show the dynamics reward is less robust on OOD samples, contradicting the claim of strong generalization (R4).
- **Rate of convergence and practical choice of T**: Proposition 1 gives a rate of 1/√T, implying T must cover a large portion of the state-action space. No practical guidance is given; the paper uses T=5000 (Table 4) without justification (R4).
- **Limited ablation studies**: The paper does not isolate why MOREC works so well; it is unclear which components are responsible for the gain and under what conditions the method helps (R3).
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison to a simple filtering technique like selecting the transition with the lowest Bellman error (R3).
- **Filtering choice not motivated**: Why construct a softmax distribution over transitions instead of directly taking the argmax? (R3)
- **Relation to energy-based models not discussed**: The dynamics reward resembles an energy function on (s,a,s’) (R3).
- **Hyperparameter and tuning concerns**: Multiple hyperparameters (temperature, penalty, threshold) and extensive search are used; it is unclear whether baselines received similar tuning resources, which is important for fair comparison (R2).
- **Narrow task coverage**: Experiments focus on locomotion tasks; D4RL and NeoRL share similar environments. More diverse domains (e.g., with complex dynamics or higher dimensionality) are needed to demonstrate general applicability (R4).
- **Figure 2 is hard to parse** and does not clearly support the claim that dynamics reward is superior to the dynamics model (R2).
- **Implementation complexity** may limit the method's adoption (R2).
- **Minor issues**: Questions about the explanation on page 2 (why task reward is known, “static capabilities”), a potentially confusing line about behavior policy (R2, R4), and typos (page 1).
