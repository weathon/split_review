## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes using Chinese characters’ pictophonetic structure, via a Six-Writings Pictophonetic Coding (SWPC) framework, to improve natural language processing in humanoid robots. It uses the Chinese periodic table as a case study and mentions integrating SWPC with Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) and machine learning for multimodal recognition. All three reviewers (ratings: 1,1,1; confidence: 4,3,5) agree that the work lacks experimental evaluation, a clear connection to robotics, and rigorous justification. The paper reads more like a proposal or introduction than a complete study.

### Strengths

- **Novelty of the idea**: Reviewers acknowledge that leveraging the rich structure of Chinese characters for NLP is a good idea (R1) and possibly a new way to model Chinese character information (R2). The application of SWPC and SIFT to Chinese NLP tasks (character identification, word generation) is seen as original (R3).
- **Clarity of explanation**: The paper provides concrete examples (e.g., Chinese periodic table characters) and explanatory figures that demonstrate why SWPC can be a compact representation (R3).
- **Potential significance**: The proposed methods could improve certain aspects of Chinese NLP tasks, which is a worthwhile direction (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **No connection to humanoid robotics**: The paper repeatedly claims the work aids humanoid robots, but no concrete proposal, implementation, or discussion of how the methods integrate with robotic systems is provided (R1, R2, R3). Reviewers consistently note this disconnect as a fundamental flaw.
- **Lack of experimental evaluation**: None of the reviewers found any quantitative experimental results. Only one experiment is mentioned (Section 4.3 on SWPC dataset and image libraries), but no accuracy, precision, recall, or other metrics are reported (R1, R2, R3). There is no evaluation of how well the method performs, nor any comparison to existing approaches (R3). R3 specifically states that even a qualitative or interpretability argument requires quantitative evidence on benchmark tasks.
- **Narrow and unrepresentative case study**: The paper only uses the Chinese periodic table as a demonstration, with unclear motivation (R1). Reviewers question why this single case is chosen and whether the approach generalizes to other domains (R1, R3 – from R3’s question 3).
- **Missing implementation details**: No description of how SWPC or the SIFT-based system is implemented is given, making reproducibility impossible (R3).
- **Lack of comparison with existing methods**: The paper mentions state-of-the-art methods (MA-CRNN, MaskOCR) in passing, stating that while they may achieve higher accuracy, SIFT offers better interpretability and compatibility with SWPC. However, no scenario-specific analysis, quantitative comparison, or evidence for those claims is provided (R3). R1 also notes the absence of comparison.
- **Inconsistent logical progression**: The motivation does not clearly lead to the proposed solution, and the scope is confusing: the only presented experiment is a computer vision task (character/word recognition), despite the paper’s focus on natural language understanding for robots (R2, R3).
- **Low-resolution figures**: Figures 1 and 2 are noted as being low resolution, harming clarity (R3).
- **Overlapping reviewer questions (converted to weaknesses)**:
  - *Evaluation and comparison*: How would one evaluate performance? How does SWPC/SIFT compare to other Chinese character encoding/recognition methods (R1, R3)? Why are SIFT and SWPC chosen over deep learning approaches, and in what scenarios do they outperform or not (R3)?
  - *Generalizability*: Why only the periodic table? Could other case studies exemplify the method (R1)? What experiments support interpretability and compatibility claims (R3)?
  - *Integration with ML*: How does SWPC/SIFT integrate with machine learning models for language processing (R3)?
  - *Quantitative results request*: Can the authors provide quantitative experimental results for effectiveness, computational efficiency, and accuracy compared to traditional and deep learning methods (R3 reviewer questions 1, 2)?
