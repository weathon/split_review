## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes two FedAvg-type algorithms for federated compositional optimization (FedDRO and DS-FedDRO) and provides convergence analysis and experiments on large-scale DRO problems. Reviewer opinions vary: one reviewer (rating 6, confidence 2) is positive, emphasizing clear methodology and the two-sided learning rate’s benefits; another (rating 5, confidence 3) finds the paper clear but notes several missing discussions and limited experimental scope; a third (rating 3, confidence 4) criticizes multiple technical errors in Table 1, incorrect claims about linear speedup and communication complexity, incremental novelty, and insufficient comparison to existing federated compositional and bilevel methods.

### Strengths
- **Clear methodological progression**: The paper first demonstrates that vanilla FedAvg fails for compositional optimization (CO) due to bias from data heterogeneity, then uses this insight to motivate the proposed algorithms (R1, R3). The improvement process is clearly explained (R1).
- **Strong motivation and problem formulation**: The introduction clearly states the challenges (C1–C3) and unique difficulties of CO in federated learning. The problem formulation is rigorous and accessible, with DRO examples grounding the theory (R2).
- **Well-presented algorithms and analysis**: Algorithm 2 (DS-FedDRO) is well-detailed and easy to follow. The assumptions, lemmas, and theorems are clearly stated and technically precise (R2). Theorem 4.1’s proof is interesting (R3).
- **Comparative summary**: Table 1 provides a clear overview of theoretical results, helping readers quickly see the advantages of the proposed methods relative to existing approaches (R2).
- **Key advantage of DS-FedDRO**: It achieves optimal \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})\) communication complexity (matching best-known for standard FL) and linear speedup, while avoiding large batch gradients; its update rules require sharing fewer sequences compared to bilevel optimization algorithms (R1).
- **Experimental validation**: Experiments on ResNet20 with CIFAR-10/100 add empirical support (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Table 1 errors** (R3): Multiple entries are incorrect.
  - ComFedL and FedNest communicate every iteration, so their communication complexity is \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})\), not as listed.
  - Local-SCGDM’s communication complexity is \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1.5})\), not \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})\).
  - FedBiO’s computation complexity is \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1.5})\), not \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2.5})\); its communication complexity is \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})\).
  - FedNest should account for the inner loop length.
- **Incorrect claims about linear speedup** (R3): The paper states this is the first work ensuring linear speedup in federated CO. However, existing federated bilevel (e.g., FedBiO, FedMBO in Table 1) and federated compositional algorithms (e.g., Wu et al. 2024, Gao 2024) already achieve linear speedup.
- **Communication complexity of Algorithm 2 may be misstated** (R3): Algorithm 2 seems to require communicating \(y\) in every iteration, so its communication complexity should equal the number of iterations, contradicting Corollary 4.4’s claim.
- **Incremental novelty** (R3): Communicating \(y\) is already done in existing methods (e.g., Local-SCGDM for compositional; FedBiO for bilevel). The convergence analysis does not appear to introduce new challenges compared to those existing works. The paper should explain why existing designs are insufficient for the FedDRO problem.
- **Missing discussion of related methods** (R2): The literature review does not cover important works on two-sided (server-side and client-side) step sizes that control heterogeneity (Malinovsky et al. 2023, Charles & Konečný 2020, Mai et al. 2023). Also missing: comparison to client-drift reduction methods like SCAFFOLD, ProxSkip, FedLin (Karimireddy et al. 2020, Mishchenko et al. 2022, Mitra et al. 2021). These directly relate to the paper’s core idea of using two-sided learning rates to mitigate client-server divergence.
- **Unclear difference between Assumptions 3.4 and 5.2** (R2, R3): Both relate to bounded heterogeneity, yet they have distinct roles; they should be given distinct names and their purposes clarified. Furthermore, the paper states DS-FedDRO requires stronger assumptions than FedDRO, but Assumptions 5.1 and 3.4 appear identical. The reason for DS-FedDRO’s better communication complexity under seemingly the same assumptions needs detailed explanation (R3).
- **Missing explanation for different \(g\) estimators** (R3): DS-FedDRO and FedDRO estimate \(g\) differently; the paper does not justify this design choice.
- **Complexity analysis incomplete** (R2): The Big-O bounds omit constants (e.g., Lipschitz constants), which are important for a complete theoretical picture.
- **Limited generality** (R2): Results are given only for the general non-convex case; no analysis for strongly convex, convex, or PL-condition cases, which could yield improved bounds. The authors should comment on the difficulty of extending to these settings.
- **Narrow experimental scope** (R2, R1): Only CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet are tested. A broader range of datasets and models is needed.
- **Missing controlled experiment** (R2): No experiments in a setting where theoretical assumptions (e.g., smoothness, bounded heterogeneity) are explicitly satisfied (e.g., logistic regression with non-convex regularization) to directly validate the theory.
- **Only accuracy plots** (R2): The optimization theory focuses on convergence, but the experiments show only accuracy; adding loss convergence plots would better illustrate optimization performance.
- **Baseline missing** (R1): Table 1’s experiment (imbalanced classification as DRO) does not include the baseline the paper compares to; the reviewer asks how classification with an imbalanced dataset is formulated as a DRO problem.
- **Abstract is somewhat lengthy** (R2): Could be more concise for quick understanding.
