## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Temporal-Difference Variational Continual Learning (TD-VCL) to mitigate error accumulation in Variational Continual Learning (VCL). The authors argue that standard VCL suffers from compounding approximation errors because its objective depends only on the posterior of the immediate previous task. They introduce two variants: (1) n-Step KL-VCL, which regularizes using the posteriors of the last \(n\) tasks and incorporates a replay buffer of their data, and (2) TD(\(\lambda\))-VCL, which weights these terms using a decay parameter \(\lambda\), drawing a connection to temporal-difference methods in reinforcement learning. The methods are evaluated on PermutedMNIST, SplitMNIST, and SplitNotMNIST, showing improved performance over standard VCL and non-variational baselines.

### Strengths
1. **Addresses a gap in Bayesian continual learning**: The paper correctly identifies that using only the immediate previous posterior in VCL can cause error accumulation. Using multiple past posterior estimates (n-step) helps dilute individual errors and reduce catastrophic forgetting. (Reviewers 1, 3)
2. **Enhanced learning objectives**: The n-Step KL regularization leverages broader context from previous tasks, leading to clear performance improvements over standard VCL. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
3. **Single-step optimization**: Unlike some existing methods that require complex two-step optimization or explicit replay mechanisms, the proposed approach naturally incorporates replay into the learning objective. (Reviewer 3)
4. **Principled connection to Temporal-Difference methods**: Drawing on reinforcement learning to formulate a variational objective with replay for the last \(n\) tasks is a significant conceptual contribution. The theoretical propositions and proofs strengthen the work. (Reviewer 4)
5. **Clear presentation and thorough analysis**: The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow. It includes hyperparameter sensitivity analysis and a detailed discussion of the method. (Reviewers 2, 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Small-scale, simple benchmarks**: Experiments are limited to PermutedMNIST, SplitMNIST, and SplitNotMNIST – datasets that are too simple to reflect real-world continual learning challenges. All four reviewers note that the method should be evaluated on more complex datasets such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet using larger architectures (e.g., CNNs, ResNets, ViTs). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
2. **Missing comparisons with strong recent baselines**: The paper compares only with standard VCL and a few non-variational baselines. It omits many recent, competitive methods:
   - UCL and UCB (Ahn et al., 2019; Ebrahimi et al., 2020) – achieve much better accuracy on PermutedMNIST/SplitMNIST without requiring large memory. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - ALTA, DER, L2P (Reviewer 2)
   - Bayesian Structural Adaptation (Kumar et al., 2021) (Reviewer 3)
   - Experience Replay (ER) in class-incremental setting (Reviewer 4)
   These omissions raise questions about the practical relevance and competitiveness of the proposed method. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
3. **Memory and computational overhead**: Storing both a memory buffer of past \(n\) tasks and the posterior distributions for all \(n\) steps increases memory usage significantly. Standard VCL variants (UCL, UCB) store only the previous task’s posterior. Moreover, computational complexity may be high; training and inference times are not reported. (Reviewers 1, 3)
4. **Task-incremental learning only**: The experiments are restricted to the task-incremental setting (task identity provided at test time). The more challenging class-incremental setting (no task ID) is not explored. Reviewer 4 considers this a major weakness; Reviewer 3 asks explicitly which strategy (TIL or CIL) was used. (Reviewers 3, 4)
5. **Disconnect from recent continual learning advances**: The work builds on an earlier VCL framework, but the field has moved toward larger-scale benchmarks and more stable, scalable methods. Reviewer 2 states the work would have been more impactful six years ago and that variational models are now less relevant due to scalability and stability issues. (Reviewer 2)
6. **Overall accuracy low on simple benchmarks**: On PermutedMNIST and SplitMNIST, the reported accuracy is too low compared to methods like UCL and UCB, which achieve much higher performance without requiring large replay buffers. (Reviewer 1)
7. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and bias**: Performance depends heavily on hyperparameter choices (e.g., \(n\), \(\lambda\)). Also, if earlier posterior estimates are significantly biased, they can still adversely affect the learning target. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Unrealistic task assumption**: The framework assumes tasks are IID, which may not hold in many real-world scenarios. (Reviewer 3)
9. **Lack of empirical analysis for the RL connection**: Although the paper draws a strong conceptual link to temporal-difference learning, no experiments are conducted in reinforcement learning settings. (Reviewer 3)
10. **Missing ablations and analysis**: Several important questions remain unanswered:
    - For SplitMNIST/SplitNotMNIST, is the setting Task-Incremental or Class-Incremental? (Reviewer 3)
    - What is the intuition behind re-weighting the posteriors with KL-divergence to mitigate error accumulation? What are the implications when \(n = t\)? (Reviewer 3)
    - How does the framework perform in an exemplar-free setting? (Reviewer 3)
    - How does it compare against replay-based methods in class-incremental learning (e.g., ER)? (Reviewer 4)
    These missing ablations weaken the empirical validation. (Reviewers 3, 4)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 3 (score 6, confidence 5) views the work as a valuable contribution to the literature despite its limitations, whereas Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 3) are more critical, finding the contribution weak and disconnected from current progress. Reviewer 4 (score 5) sees the theoretical contribution as significant but demands stronger experimental validation. The weaknesses above reflect all perspectives.
