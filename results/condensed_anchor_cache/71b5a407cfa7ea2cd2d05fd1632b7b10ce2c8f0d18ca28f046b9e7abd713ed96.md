## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AdAutoMixup, an adversarial automatic mixup augmentation method that builds on AutoMix. It alternates between training a mixed sample generator and a target classifier: the generator aims to produce hard mixed examples, while the classifier learns robust features from them. To prevent collapse, an EMA teacher and cosine similarity are introduced for end-to-end learning. The method is evaluated on several image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, fine-grained datasets) and compared to existing mixup methods, including on Transformer architectures. Two reviewers score the paper 8, one scores 6, with the latter expressing skepticism about the actual impact and pointing out several weaknesses.

### Strengths
- Provides an interesting view of improving mixed sample qualities through adversarial training in a closed-loop mixup framework (R1).
- Idea of augmenting with hard examples and iterating between augmentation and classifier is interesting (R3).
- Well-motivated and overall easy to follow (R1).
- Results are consistently better than AutoMixup (R2, R3) and the evaluation is thorough, including comparisons on multiple mixup benchmarks and Transformer architectures (R1, R2).
- Strong empirical results across datasets (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing fine-grained analysis:** No ablation isolating the impact of the adversarial loss (e.g., compare with/without adversarial training, while keeping all other components constant). No sensitivity analysis for adversarial training parameters (learning rate, perturbation strength) or hyper‑parameters (mixing ratio λ, mixed sample number N, β in Eq. 12) (R1, Q1).
- **Limited scale of experiments:** Evaluation only on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and fine-grained datasets. Lacks experiments on ImageNet‑1K or other large‑scale datasets (R1).
- **Insufficient robustness evaluation:** No experiments with adversarial attacks, noise, occlusions, or transfer learning to downstream tasks (e.g., object detection, segmentation) (R1).
- **Missing comparison to adversarial data augmentation approaches** (R2 recommends citing [1, 2, 3, 4] and adding related work).
- **Evaluation criteria not standard / justified:** The paper reports the median of top‑1 test accuracy in the last 10 epochs, but does not use a validation set for checkpoint selection. Adversarial methods may have unstable training dynamics; mean accuracy could fluctuate, so this metric needs justification (R2, Q).
- **Calibration error analysis incomplete:** Section 4.2 claims the proposed method has the lowest calibration error, but no ECE values or table compares baselines – only Fig. 4 shows ECE for the proposed method (R2).
- **Notation and writing issues (R1, R2):**
  - Term “cross attention module” (CAM) can be confused with “class activation mapping” (CAM) from saliency methods.
  - Confusing notation for EMA encoder update: “the encoder weight is updated with an EMA of the weights of the classifier – $\hat{\phi} = \xi \hat{\phi} + (1-\xi) W$” – unclear whether the encoder belongs to the generator or the classifier. Later ψ is referred to as the target classifier with weights W.
  - Equations 7 and 8: using the same value of y is inconsistent; the form of the cross‑entropy loss should be kept consistent with $y_{mix} = \sum_i y_i \lambda_i$.
  - Equations 10–15 are badly formatted and hard to read; the individual contribution of the four cross‑entropy terms and the choice of α and β are unclear.
  - Typos: “to facility representation”, “Mxiup”, bad formatting in eq 5, unclear scalar multiplication in eq 6‑7, “inherent meaning of images” sounds unscientific and needs more explanation (R2).
- **Coherence and motivation (minority reviewer R2):** The paper feels like it has too many elements scattered around; it is unclear where the improvement actually comes from. R2 initially recommended reject and after rebuttal remains skeptical about the method’s impact, noting that performance numbers seem to be at the point of diminishing returns. (R1 and R3 are more positive, but this contrast is preserved.)
- **Resource cost:** The method is harder to implement and requires more resources than standard augmentation techniques, though this is expected in the augmentation timeline (R3).
