## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces InstaSHAP, a method that computes Shapley values in a single forward pass by training a purified Generalized Additive Model (GAM). It establishes a variational framework linking GAMs and SHAP via functional ANOVA, enabling automatic GAM purification through a novel loss function with output masking. Experiments on synthetic, tabular, and high-dimensional (CUB bird) datasets show improved efficiency and accuracy over FastSHAP and FaithSHAP, with theoretical results characterizing the representation limits of SHAP as identical to those of GAMs.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: Uniquely connects Shapley values and GAMs through functional ANOVA, addressing correlated features where SHAP traditionally struggles. The variational perspective unifies recent SHAP variants (FastSHAP, FaithSHAP).
- **Technical quality**: Rigorous proofs linking SHAP and GAMs, including conditions for Shapley explanations. The loss function for automatic GAM purification is well-designed. Experiments convincingly show advantages over FastSHAP in representing complex feature interactions on synthetic data.
- **Significance**: Enables real-time Shapley computation, highly relevant for high-stakes domains (healthcare, finance). Theoretical contributions provide a foundation for future work on GAMs and functional ANOVA in model interpretation.
- **Clarity**: Complex concepts are made accessible with formal mathematical definitions and visual aids; the paper guides readers from theory to practice.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited scope of empirical evaluation**:
   - The main high-dimensional benchmark is limited to the CUB bird dataset. Missing evaluations on popular CV datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) and NLP datasets (SST-2, IMDB) that would better demonstrate scalability to complex interactions and correlations.
   - Real-world datasets with inherently correlated feature spaces (e.g., finance, healthcare, time-series with lagged variables, patient data with interconnected measurements) are absent. The current evaluation lacks datasets where correlations are not merely a byproduct of high dimensionality.
   - Several reviewers request broader comparisons beyond SHAP-based methods: include non-SHAP explainability methods such as LIME, Integrated Gradients, or counterfactual explanations to contextualize InstaSHAP’s relative performance.

2. **Scalability and performance analysis is insufficient**:
   - No quantitative runtime or latency benchmarks comparing InstaSHAP to FastSHAP (or other methods) on large datasets. The paper claims real-time applicability but does not provide empirical evidence (e.g., training times, inference speed).
   - Scalability to higher-order interactions and complex dependencies (spatial/sequential data) is acknowledged as a limitation (e.g., bird classification experiment), but no analysis of when GAM underperforms or how to address scaling issues. Some reviewers suggest exploring hierarchical or multi-scale architectural modifications.

3. **Theoretical clarity and accessibility**:
   - Dense mathematical sections (variational equations, functional ANOVA) lack intuitive explanations, diagrams, or high-level summaries, making them difficult for practitioners.
   - Figure captions and annotations need more detail for clarity.

4. **Assumptions about feature correlations and causal interpretation**:
   - Insufficient analysis of how InstaSHAP performs when feature correlations differ from its assumptions. The purification process may misattribute spurious correlations as meaningful interactions, especially in the presence of confounding. A more nuanced discussion of association-based vs. causal explanations is needed (e.g., sensitivity to observed confounders).
   - The impact of GAM architecture choice on explanations is not discussed; different GAM structures may capture interactions differently.

5. **Trade-offs and approximation errors**:
   - Unclear when and why InstaSHAP might produce Shapley values that differ from traditional methods (e.g., KernelSHAP, TreeSHAP). No analysis of approximation error introduced by GAM purification, especially for highly non-additive models.
   - The fidelity of explanations in the presence of higher-order interactions is not thoroughly evaluated; the paper notes scaling difficulties but does not quantify the degradation.

6. **Missing comparisons with other popular Shapley-based methods**: The paper mainly compares with FastSHAP; reviewers request comparisons with KernelSHAP and TreeSHAP, especially when the underlying model is not a neural network (e.g., tree-based models for TreeSHAP).

7. **Practical guidelines and real-world use cases**: No discussion of ideal use cases, dataset types, or feature characteristics that align with InstaSHAP’s strengths. Practitioners lack guidance on when InstaSHAP is preferred and when it may fail (e.g., non-GAM-friendly tasks).
