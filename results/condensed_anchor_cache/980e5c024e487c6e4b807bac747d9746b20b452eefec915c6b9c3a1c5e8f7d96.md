## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a computationally efficient algorithm for machine unlearning in image-to-image (I2I) generative models, supported by theoretical analysis. The method minimizes the l2 distance between forget-set embeddings and a normal distribution while preserving retain-set embeddings, and does not require retain samples. Experiments on ImageNet-1K and Places-365 with VQ-GAN, Diffusion Models, and Masked Autoencoders show strong retain-set performance and deteriorated forget-set performance. Reviewers acknowledge the timeliness and importance of unlearning for generative models, and the paper’s clear structure and theoretical foundations. However, concerns are raised about the lack of comparison to a retrained gold-standard model, incomplete evaluation (missing test-set generalization), limited novelty, overclaimed scope, hyperparameter setting, potential information leakage, and missing comparisons to related I2I unlearning work. Reviewer 4 is notably more positive (score 8) than others (scores 5,5,6), praising the theoretical analysis and experimental breadth, but still questions the claim of unexplored territory and potential decoder-based memorization.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a timely and important problem: machine unlearning for generative models, directly relevant to current regulations and lawsuits (Reviewers 1,2).
- The work presents the first systematic approach for unlearning in image-to-image generative models, with a clean, simple framework (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Strong theoretical underpinning, well-written and easy to follow, with rigorous analysis (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Thorough experiments across multiple model architectures (VQ-GAN, Diffusion, MAE) and two large-scale datasets (ImageNet-1K, Places-365), using relevant metrics (FID, Inception Score, CLIP Distance) (Reviewers 2,4).
- The algorithm does not require retain samples, aligning with data retention policies (Reviewers 1,3).
- The use of an encoder E_0 to generate L_F and L_R is a smart way to avoid estimating mutual information (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing gold-standard comparison**: The paper does not compare to a model trained only on the retain set, which is the canonical baseline for unlearning. The proposed method produces Gaussian noise on forget samples, which may signal prior training and contradict privacy goals. It is unclear whether the unlearned model’s forget-set performance is better or worse than the gold standard, and whether “worst” forget-set performance is actually desirable. (Reviewers 1,2 – high concern)
- **Incomplete evaluation**: Table 1 only presents retain and forget dataset results; a test dataset measuring generalization is missing. Also, the work lacks a comparison to the related I2I unlearning method in [9]. (Reviewer 1)
- **Scope overclaimed**: The paper focuses on image inpainting but titles itself as “I2I generative models.” This risks overgeneralization. The authors should either expand the tasks or explicitly limit the scope. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited novelty**: The approach is a straightforward extension of student-teacher or continual learning frameworks; similar ideas have appeared in classification unlearning literature [1,2]. (Reviewer 2)
- **Hyperparameter σ issues**: The theoretical assumption about σ may be idealistic and not hold in practice. Lemma 1 suggests σ should be estimated from the forget set, but Appendix C.4 sets σ=I without justification. (Reviewer 3)
- **Potential information leakage**: The noise generated from forget samples may retain high-level correlations with the original image. Reviewer 4 suggests training a secondary autoencoder to check if the original cropped image can be reconstructed from the noise. Additionally, Theorem 1 uses the same decoder pre-unlearning; this may allow information extraction or memorization of forget samples via the decoder. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear experimental setup**: The selection of retain samples (800 random classes from the 1000) and whether the original model is trained on all 1000 or only 200 classes is not clearly explained. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing related work**: Reviewer 4 questions the statement that generative model unlearning is “relatively unexplored,” and suggests a more thorough literature review is needed.
- **Technical clarification**: Equation 3 uses α but gives no explanation for why α is not bounded between 0 and 1 (i.e., (1-α) term0 – α term1). (Reviewer 4)
