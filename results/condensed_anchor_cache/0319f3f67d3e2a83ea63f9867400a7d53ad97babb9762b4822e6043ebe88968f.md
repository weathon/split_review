## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the problem of predicting binding affinity from DNA-encoded library (DEL) screening data by proposing a framework (DEL-Ranking) that combines a new ranking loss, iterative activity-referenced correction, and consistency losses. The authors aim to handle distribution noise and systematic shifts between read counts and true affinities. They evaluate on multiple carbonic anhydrase datasets and release new datasets. However, significant presentation and clarity issues prevent two of three reviewers from properly assessing the technical contributions. While one reviewer (R1) found the field description, analysis of noise sources, number of datasets, and ablation experiments helpful, the other two (R2, R3) could not gauge strengths due to incomprehensible problem formulation, undefined terms, and missing intuition. All three reviewers raised major concerns about the clarity of equations, justification of design choices, hyperparameter selection, and robustness of the method.

### Strengths
- Helpful description of the field and deep dive into biochemical/technical sources of variation (R1).
- Impressive number of datasets used for evaluation and ablation experiments, which are clearly a lot of work (R1).
- Authors are well versed in the field and problem domain (R1).
- The paper addresses relevant challenges to an important problem of predicting binding affinity from DEL data (R3).

*Note: R2 was unable to gauge strengths due to presentation issues.*

### Weaknesses
- **Critical presentation and clarity issues**: Two reviewers (R2, R3) found the paper extremely difficult to follow. Equations are poorly defined, variables are reused inconsistently (e.g., \(y_i\) used for both activity label and predicted read count; \(s_i\) introduced but not defined). Several equations (Eq. 2, 4, 5, 6) have unclear indices and scope (e.g., \(L_{PSR}\) sum does not depend on \(j\), \(L_{GSR}\) mixing terms without double sum). This prevents proper evaluation of the method.
- **Undefined terms and acronyms**: ZIP loss is referenced (line 104) but never defined. Acronyms ARDC, AGR, CDEC are used without intuition, making it hard to follow model components. The authors should use simpler, descriptive names (e.g., “Reference Correction” for ARDC) (R1, R3). In Fig. 1, the middle panel incorrectly depicts DNA barcodes binding to the target protein (should show “building blocks”) (R2).
- **Missing motivation and justification**: Individual loss terms (e.g., Eq. 8) are presented without explanation of why they are preferred over simpler alternatives (e.g., \(\|A_{true} - A_{pred}\|_2\)) (R3). The claim of learning “causal features” (line 147) is too strong – no mechanism to disentangle correlation from causation is provided (R1).
- **Hyperparameter concerns**: The method introduces at least six new hyperparameters (R3). There is no description of how they were chosen, no demonstration that they can be robustly tuned using a validation set, and no ablation showing sensitivity. The paper reads as a “bag-of-tricks” without evidence that the increased complexity yields a more useful method beyond slight Spearman correlation improvements (R3).
- **Activity label definition and source unclear**: In Section 3.1, the activity label \(y_i \in \{0,1\}\) is mentioned but its derivation is not explained. In Section 3.3, “ground-truth activity” is used – it is unclear if this is truly known or just a biochemical proxy (R1). Similarly, \(A_{pred}\) vs \(A_{true}\) – is “true” actually observed? (R1).
- **Zero-shot experiments are not properly zero-shot**: The “zero-shot” predictions are on carbonic anhydrase (same target as training). True zero-shot should be on an entirely different target (R1).
- **Molecular docking poses potentially problematic**: Docked poses (Section 4) may be inaccurate and add noise. The paper does not validate that these poses correspond to actual binding modes or correlate with read counts (R1, R3). The necessity of the pose encoder is questioned – it appears superfluous and adds complexity (R1).
- **Missing experimental rigor**: No analysis of whether the identified functional groups (e.g., pyrimidine sulfonamide) are discovered by simpler baseline methods (R1). The iterative refinement algorithm (ARC) is not adequately explained (R3).
- **Specific equation errors / variable misuse** (R2, R3):
  - \(M_i\) and \(R_i\) are defined in \(\mathbb{R}^+\), but read counts can be zero.
  - In Eq. (2), why does each compound \(i\) have a different \(\pi_i\)? Why not separate \(\pi\) for control and target as in Shmilovich et al. 2023?
  - In Eq. (4), \(L_{PSR}\) depends on \(y_i\) and \(y_j\) but should likely depend on predicted values \(\hat{y}_i, \hat{y}_j\). What is \(G_i = \text{softplus}(y_i)\)? Overloading \(y\).
  - In Eq. (5), \(K\) is not defined.
  - In Eq. (6), \(s_i\) is undefined; likely should be \(r_i\).
- **Vague statement**: Line 147 (“existing methods… face challenges in interpretability”) needs elaboration (R2).
