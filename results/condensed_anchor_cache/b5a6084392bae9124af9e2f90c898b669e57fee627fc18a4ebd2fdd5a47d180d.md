## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TEASER, a method for 3D facial reconstruction from a single in-the-wild image. It combines explicit FLAME parameters with a multi-scale appearance token that conditions a neural renderer (UNet+AdaIN+ControlNet). A token cycle loss and pose-dependent landmark loss are used for self-supervised training. Experiments on LRS3 and HDTF show improved expression reconstruction, temporal stability, and photorealism over SMIRK and 3DDFA-V3.

### Strengths
+ Well-written and clearly presented methodology; supplementary provides useful implementation details and video results demonstrating temporal stability.
+ The multi-scale appearance token is novel; the combination of AdaIN and ControlNet-based feature conditioning is well motivated.
+ The intuition of using a more powerful renderer to improve geometry reconstruction is sound.
+ Quantitative and qualitative results show improvements over prior methods (SMIRK, 3DDFA-V3) in expression detail and temporal consistency.
+ Detailed ablation study comparing six pipeline variants confirms all components contribute.

### Weaknesses
- Novelty over SMIRK (Retsinas et al. 2024) is limited: the only crucial addition is the appearance token, and its impact on reconstruction quality appears small. The method is heavily based on SMIRK’s architecture.
- The pose-dependent landmark masking (Eq. 13) is suboptimal: it is a hard mask that introduces discontinuities at yaw angles near ±ε; a smooth confidence score would be better. It also ignores pitch angle, which realistically occludes landmarks.
- The experimental protocol using LRS3 is questionable: the same individuals appear in both training and test sets (even if different frames), making comparisons potentially unfair.
- Quantitative and qualitative comparisons are insufficient: only SMIRK and 3DDFA-V3 are used; many other SOTA methods (e.g., DECA, EMOCA, Deep3DFace, FOCUS) are omitted. Downstream tasks (identity swapping, face animation) are shown without any SOTA comparison.
- No evaluation of ground-truth reconstruction accuracy: the paper relies solely on 2D image metrics. The NoW dataset (two-view geometry) is suggested to verify that the renderer does not simply copy input pixels. A direct 3D geometry metric is needed.
- The rendered portraits still suffer from over-smoothed textures, lack of specular highlights, and fail to faithfully model highly reflective surfaces – issues that the paper claims to address.
- Lack of analysis of the multi-scale token: which scales correspond to semantics vs. texture details? What is the definition of ‘semantics’? How does changing only certain scales affect the output? The interpretability of the deep neural renderer is reduced compared to explicit renderers.
- Missing methodological details: how are multi-scale feature maps combined (simple concatenation vs. learned weighting)? What is the parameter count compared to the SMIRK renderer?
- The token cycle loss is designed to prevent token leakage of view/expression information, but its sensitivity/robustness is not analyzed. It is unclear how invariance to expression is achieved and what trade-offs exist.
- Training details: are the shape and head-pose encoders frozen during training while only the expression encoder is updated? If so, the geometry improvement may come entirely from the neural renderer, not the geometry estimator.
- Questions raised about tuning of ε in Eq. 13, inference time (efficiency vs. MobileNetV3), and how geometry-rendering matching is measured (since no explicit texture is obtained).
