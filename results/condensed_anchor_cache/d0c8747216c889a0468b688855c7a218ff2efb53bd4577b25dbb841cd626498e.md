## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces SWIRL, a framework that extends IRL by incorporating time-varying, history-dependent reward functions to model animal behaviors. SWIRL models long behavioral sequences as transitions between short-term decision-making processes (hidden modes), each with a unique reward function, and includes history dependency at both the decision level and action level. The method is evaluated on simulated data and two real-world animal behavior datasets, claiming quantitative and qualitative improvements over baselines. Reviews are mixed: one reviewer is positive (score 6, confidence 3) but with substantial methodological, scalability, and biological-plausibility concerns; two reviewers are critical (scores 3,3; confidences 2 and 4) citing limited novelty, insufficient comparison with prior work (particularly Nguyen et al. 2015), unclear scalability, experimental issues, and poor mathematical presentation. All reviewers note potential but require major revisions.

### Strengths

- **Novelty/Originality**: First IRL model to integrate both decision-level and action-level history dependency in a switching-reward framework; addresses a gap in modeling complex, naturalistic animal behaviors with shifting motivations. (R1)
- **Empirical Validation**: Thorough mathematical formulation; tested on both simulated (gridworld) and real-world datasets; demonstrates improvements over baseline methods. (R1) The method produces interpretable results on real-world data (rat maze). (R2)
- **Clarity & Motivation**: Well-written paper with clear motivation; well contextualized within existing literature (e.g., connection to autoregressive dynamics models). (R1, R2, R3)
- **Reproducibility**: Experiments are (in theory) reproducible using public datasets and publicly available code. (R1)
- **Significance**: Promises more accurate models of animal decision-making, with potential applications in neuroscience and behavioral science. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited Novelty and Comparison with Nguyen et al. (2015)**: Insufficient discussion distinguishing SWIRL from Nguyen, Low, and Jaillet (2015) on "inverse reinforcement learning with locally consistent reward functions." Both frameworks use multiple locally consistent reward functions; Nguyen’s algorithm allows the transition kernel of reward functions to be dominated by external inputs. It is unclear whether SWIRL is a special case (with external input defined as (s1,…,s_L)). Detailed algorithmic comparison and experimental performance comparison are missing. (R3)
- **Missing Related Work / Alternative Formulations**: The problem could be interpreted as a POMDP or Hierarchical RL (option setting) with existing IRL methods (e.g., [1], [2] cited by reviewer). The authors do not discuss or compare against these alternative formulations. (R2)
- **Scalability and Computational Complexity**: The method appears limited to small discrete state/action spaces (e.g., 5×5 gridworld in simulation). No discussion on scalability to larger environments (e.g., 50×50), higher-dimensional tasks, or continuous spaces. No runtime comparisons with baselines; no analysis of how computational time scales with state/action cardinality or hidden-mode space. All three reviewers raise this concern.
- **Convergence and Theoretical Analysis**: No convergence guarantees for the EM algorithm; no discussion of optimality conditions or theoretical properties. (R1, R3)
- **Hyperparameter Selection**: Lack of transparency on how hyperparameters are chosen:
    - History length L: Not experimentally explored beyond 1 and 2; no guidelines for choosing L in practice. (R1)
    - Number of hidden modes: Varies across experiments (2, 3, 5) but criterion for selection not stated; unclear whether it is learned automatically. (R1, R3)
    - Temperature parameter α in soft-Q iteration: No details on tuning or cross-validation; sensitivity to initial parameters not assessed. (R1)
- **Experimental Issues**:
    - Figure 3: The best-performing baseline is labeled "I-1" while poorly performing baselines have full names, which is highly misleading and could be confused with the author's contribution. (R2)
    - Figure 4: Missing MaxEnt baseline; unclear what shaded areas represent. (R2)
    - Box plots (Figure 3E): Not clear how many runs or how outliers are selected; Figure 3E appears to eliminate the best result for baseline I-1. (R2)
    - Figure 3: Colors for "water" and "explore" modes are very hard to distinguish in print (especially Figure 3F); similar issue in Figure 4 though less severe. (R3)
    - Section 4.3 discussion of reward maps (lines 492–505) and Figure 4A/C are very hard to follow; not clearly supporting the major claim about SWIRL. (R3)
- **Robustness**: No assessment of robustness to noisy or incomplete data, which is common in real-world animal behavior datasets. (R1)
- **Biological Plausibility**: Claims of biologically plausible history dependency lack supporting evidence or discussion on the validity of the specific mechanisms. (R1)
- **Mathematical Writing**: Several issues impair readability:
    - Equation numbering is sloppy: some equations labeled but never referenced; e.g., lines 660–671 labeled "(8)", then lines 685–698 each line given different labels "(10)" to "(15)" without clear meaning. (R3)
    - Missing punctuation in display equations. (R3)
    - Notation inconsistency: line 136 uses both "R" and "r ∈ ℝ" with unclear distinction; subsequent usage treats r as a function over states/actions, not a scalar. (R3)
    - Symbol S^L (line 170) used without definition (presumably Cartesian product of L state spaces). (R3)
    - Line 231: "z ∈ Z" should be "z ∈ 𝒵". (R3)
    - Appendix: only section A.1.1 appears; no further sections; subsubsection label should be removed if no other sections. (R3)
    - Summation ranges inconsistent: e.g., "∑_n" vs. "∑_{n=1}^N" without clarification. (R3)
    - Line 701–703: Notation Q(θ, θ^k) is undefined and likely typo for G(θ, θ^k). (R3)
- **Missing Details**: Reference to an appendix at line 453 appears to be missing. (R2) Symbol ξ (line 202) never defined. (R2)
- **Limitations**: The paper does not discuss situations where history dependency might fail, very long-term dependencies beyond L, or whether the framework works in both discrete and continuous spaces. (R1)

**Reviewer Disagreement**: One reviewer (R1, score 6) is substantially more positive, citing strong innovation and clarity, though still raising concerns. Two reviewers (R2, R3, scores 3) are more critical, emphasizing insufficient novelty, experimental flaws, and poor presentation. R2 states they would increase score if issues are corrected. R3 notes the paper can be improved but keeps score unchanged post-rebuttal.
