## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HAMSTER, a hierarchical vision-language-action model for robot manipulation. A high-level VLM is fine-tuned on cheap “off-domain” data (videos, simulation, sketches) to predict 2D gripper trajectories (with color-gradient time encoding) and gripper apertures. These 2D paths are overlaid on the input image and serve as conditioning for a low-level imitation-learning policy that outputs 6DoF end-effector actions. The goal is to leverage abundant off-domain data for semantic/visual generalization while limiting the need for expensive on-robot data. Experiments are conducted in simulation (RLBench/Colleseum, comparing to 3D-DA) and in the real world (comparing to RVT-2 and other baselines), including a human evaluation of the VLM’s trajectory predictions.

### Strengths
- The hierarchical structure (high-level VLM → 2D path → low-level policy) is clear, neat, and sensible, separating semantic from geometric reasoning and enabling integration of varied data sources.
- The focus on using cheap, abundant “off-domain” data (videos, sketches, simulation) to train the VLM is relevant and interesting; it addresses a key scalability challenge in robotics.
- The paper demonstrates that a VLM can be trained offline on cheap data to predict 2D gripper trajectories for tasks not seen during training, and that this hierarchical approach significantly outperforms non-hierarchical baselines in real-world generalization experiments (e.g., different language prompts, spatial rearrangements, objects).
- Human evaluation shows that HAMSTER’s VLM trajectory predictions are ranked better than baselines.
- Inference is efficient: the VLM is queried once, then only the lightweight low-level policy runs.
- The idea of distilling task knowledge into a visual path representation is inspiring and leverages advantages of imitation learning and VLA models.
- Both simulation and real-robot experiments are included, indicating workload and applicability.
- Baseline comparisons are detailed and include human evaluation (reviewer 2 found them fair and detailed; reviewer 3 agrees the experiments verify improved generalization).
- One reviewer (score 8) was more positive, praising the fair baselines and efficient design, while others (scores 5 and 6) had more critical concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient 3D spatial reasoning**: The model relies only on 2D trajectories from single-view images, which does not account for occlusions, viewpoint changes, or object geometry. Tasks requiring nuanced 3D awareness (pushing, rotating, deformable objects, precise alignments, multi-step sequences) are not evaluated, leaving generalization uncertain.
- **Limited task complexity and novelty**: The evaluation is largely confined to pick-and-place tasks; more challenging or continuous tasks are missing. The approach to VLM-based waypoint generation does not clearly improve over existing methods, and the paper lacks a detailed comparison against state-of-the-art VLM manipulation baselines to assess its true contribution.
- **Missing key ablation experiments on the hierarchy mechanism**: (a) The choice of 2D-path intermediate representation over alternatives (e.g., key-points, 3D paths) is not ablated. The paper mentions key-points as an alternative but does not test it, even though key-points could be extracted from the 2D path endpoints and other work shows accurate key-point prediction without fine-tuning. (b) Alternative ways to condition the low-level policy on the 2D path (e.g., concatenation rather than overlaying on the image) are not examined. (c) The need for a separately trained imitation-learning low-level policy is not justified; other work achieves strong performance without such a policy. (d) There is no explicit empirical evidence for the claim of drastically reducing the number of in-domain demonstrations needed – a learning-curve experiment with varying data amounts is missing.
- **Simulation evaluation is not compelling**: HAMSTER outperforms 3D-DA by only 8% on average, and the baselines are inconsistent (RVT-2 used in real-world, 3D-DA in simulation) making cross-setting comparison difficult. The simulation discussion is brief and lacks insights into why HAMSTER has lower performance and higher variance on “no variation” tasks, or why the VLM would help specifically on tasks like “man obj size” or “tab col”. Also, why was 3D-DA used instead of RVT-2 in simulation?
- **Failure modes not discussed in main paper**: The paper only refers to a supplementary website for failure modes, but this discussion (e.g., how often the VLM predicts infeasible paths, whether failures are due to VLM or low-level policy) is important for understanding limitations and should be in the main paper.
- **Human ranking metric lacks justification**: No details are given about the participants (how many, their familiarity with robot arms) or instruction; it is unclear why human ranking was used instead of a quantitative metric like MSE on expert trajectories.
- **Notational and formatting issues**: Proprioception notations (\(s\) and \(p\)) are used inconsistently (lines 234 vs 336). Figure 5 is missing labels (a), (b), (c). Missing period at line 215 and bottom of page 4; “i.e” should be “i.e.,” (line 231). The system requires human-engineered prompts for the VLM and additional efforts like the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm to simplify paths.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms / missing experiments**:
  - How would the model perform on tasks requiring intricate 3D manipulation (pushing, rotating)? (Reviewer 1)
  - Why were primarily pick-and-place tasks chosen? (Reviewer 1)
  - Can the 2D path intermediate representation limit the versatility of low-level motions, and how would performance vary with different numbers of path points? (Reviewer 3)
  - Which part of the system has 3D knowledge: does the VLM project 3D paths to images or does the policy handle it? (Reviewer 2)
  - How is time encoded in the 2D trajectory (absolute/relative scale, speed)? Why predict gripper aperture in the VLM rather than letting the low-level policy handle it? Why not train the VLM to predict gripper rotation? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why was only the `front_cam` view used in RLBench when training on multiple views could improve robustness? (Reviewer 4)
  - Are the VLM outputs normalized across datasets (e.g., pixel locations to [0,1])? What is the output length of the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm? Unify notations in problem formulation (proprioception vs. path-labeler vs. states). (Reviewer 2)
