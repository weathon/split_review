- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies scaling laws for pre-training embodied agents (behavior cloning, world modeling) using a video game dataset (Bleeding Edge) with transformer architectures and tokenized/CNN inputs. It finds power-law relationships between compute and optimal model size, with coefficients affected by tokenization and architecture. Reviewers agree the topic is important and the experiments are systematic, but raise significant concerns about: measuring training loss rather than test loss or downstream performance, relying on a single simulated environment, insufficient model scale, missing dataset-size analyses, limited hyperparameter justification, and a narrow contribution relative to prior work. Reviewer 3 is particularly critical of the train‑loss assumption, while Reviewer 1 is more negative overall; Reviewers 2 and 4 are more positive but still identify substantial missing analyses.

### Strengths
- **Exploration of visual encoder architectures and pre-training tasks** (BC and WM) showing scaling law resemblance to language modeling, with concrete exponents: WM 256‑token: \(N_{\text{opt}} \propto C^{0.49}, D_{\text{opt}} \propto C^{0.51}\); WM 540‑token: \(N_{\text{opt}} \propto C^{0.62}\). (Reviewer 1, high confidence)
- **Novel finding that tokenizer compression has a significant effect on scaling laws**: higher compression (540 tokens per image) shifts optimal model scaling toward larger models. (Reviewer 1; *but see weakness on frozen encoder limitation from Reviewer 2*)
- **Empirical validation of scaling laws for world modeling and BC** across multiple model sizes and architectures, providing credible evidence. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Systematic methodology** with frontier fit and parametric fit, enabling clear trade-off analysis. (Reviewer 4)
- **Clear presentation and effective visualizations** (Figures 3, 4). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Interesting and thorough analyses** – “a lot of interesting analyses” (Reviewer 3); “thoroughness of many results” (Reviewer 4).
- **Comparison between tokenized and CNN-based approaches in BC** reveals different scaling properties. (Reviewer 4)
- **Addresses an important and impactful problem** for the community. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Use of training loss instead of test loss**: The paper assumes training loss is a proxy for test loss (Section S3.4). Reviewer 3 calls this “baffling and completely unjustified” – the cited appendix (B.3.1) only discusses data regime, not a proper validation. This reviewer views it as a major flaw; other reviewers did not explicitly comment on this point, creating a significant **disagreement among reviewers** on the soundness of the primary metric. (Reviewer 3, high severity)
- **No evaluation of downstream task performance**: All reviewers note the paper measures only pre-training loss (BC or world modeling) rather than actual agent performance (e.g., navigation, manipulation). Reviewer 1: “crucial for embodied AI – lower pre-training loss might not translate to better generalization or real-world performance.” Reviewer 3: “most other papers (Hu et al. 2023) measure downstream performance; why should we trust this proxy?” Reviewer 4: “less emphasis on embodied AI – why is this study needed for BC or world modelling without connection to downstream tasks?” (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; severity high)
- **Single environment / dataset**: All experiments use only the video game Bleeding Edge. Reviewer 1 recommends validation on real‑world datasets like OpenX and DROID, and notes the sim‑to‑real gap is not addressed. Reviewer 3: “what if conclusions are idiosyncratic to this particular game?” Reviewer 4: “does not capture the full complexity of real‑world embodied learning scenarios.” (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Missing analysis of relationship between loss and dataset size**: Reviewer 2 notes the introduction mentions this trade‑off, but Figures 1a and 1b primarily show compute effects, not the influence of dataset size. (Reviewer 2)
- **Frozen VQGAN encoder limits tokenizer claims**: Reviewer 2: “experiments utilize a frozen VQGAN visual encoder, so claims about tokenizers are inherently limited by the encoder’s performance.” This **contrasts with Reviewer 1’s positive emphasis on tokenizer compression as a strength** – a direct reviewer disagreement. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing CNN experiments for world modeling**: Reviewer 2 questions why CNN experiments (done for BC) were not conducted for world modeling tasks. (Reviewer 2)
- **Insufficient model scale**: Most experiments max at 206 M parameters, many smaller (e.g., 27 M for Figure 5). Only Figure 7 reaches 894 M. Reviewer 3: “not sure models are large enough to fully justify all conclusions” – stated with some caution but raised as a missing analysis. (Reviewer 3)
- **Hyperparameter selection not justified**: Reviewer 4: number of tokens per image and learning rates across scales lack detailed rationale – e.g., “how were these optimized? Sensitivity analysis or empirical benchmarks needed.” Unclear whether token count was selected based on reconstruction quality or downstream performance. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited contribution relative to prior work**: Reviewer 1: “contributions limited by similarity to previous work (Tuyls et al.) and narrow focus on architectural adjustments rather than task diversity or downstream metrics.” Reviewer 3: “What is this paper doing above prior work?” (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing narrative and references for embodied AI**: Reviewer 4: paper lacks a cohesive narrative explaining the relevance of its findings to embodied intelligence; missing relevant citations. (Reviewer 4)
