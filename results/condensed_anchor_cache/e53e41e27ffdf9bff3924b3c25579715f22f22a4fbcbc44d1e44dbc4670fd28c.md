## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles continual learning (CL) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), introducing a benchmark based on image-caption datasets (COCO, Flickr30k) and evaluating cross-modal retrieval and zero-shot classification. The proposed C-CLIP combines Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to reduce forgetting and Contrastive Knowledge Consolidation (CKC) to retain old knowledge. Experiments on nine vision-language datasets show significant improvements over existing methods (7–10 points), matching or exceeding full finetuning, and retaining zero-shot performance. The ablation study (Table 4) shows CKC alone provides large gains. The approach is praised as innovative, modular, and highly relevant. However, concerns about missing comparisons (other VLMs, generative tasks, parameter-efficient methods), incomplete benchmarking details, reproducibility, hyperparameter analysis, and delivery issues are noted. One reviewer (score 8) is more positive; others (scores 6,6,6) have several reservations.

### Strengths
- **Key idea and modularity**: Using LoRA as a replacement for a memory bank and adding contrastive loss (CKC) is easy to implement, modular, and promising for enabling continual learning across a range of VLM models.
- **Strong empirical results**: Performance improvement over competitive baselines is very significant (7–10 points). C-CLIP consistently outperforms baselines on a comprehensive set of benchmarks, including seen and unseen domains.
- **Benchmark contribution**: The paper introduces a rich benchmark for vision-language continual learning, with strategies to measure zero-shot generalization degradation and class-incremental performance, increasing the challenge over typical CL benchmarks.
- **Relevance and timeliness**: Addressing multimodal CL is highly relevant given the current unimodal focus. Retaining zero-shot capabilities while adapting to new domains is an important practical need.
- **Ablation study**: Useful; CKC alone gives significant improvement (Table 4), and the theoretical analysis connecting LoRA to CL objectives is supported.
- **Clear presentation of results**: Advantages over other CL methods are clearly demonstrated through detailed figures and tables.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model and task diversity**: Experiments only use CLIP ViT-B/16. Testing with other CLIP variants (e.g., ViT-L/14) is needed to assess robustness across capacities and architectures; larger models may exhibit different forgetting patterns. Only discriminative tasks (retrieval, zero-shot classification) are evaluated; generative VL tasks like captioning (e.g., LLaVA) should be explored, as they are more sensitive to parameter changes.
- **Missing comparisons**: 
  - No comparison with well-known parameter-efficient CL methods (e.g., DualPrompt, CPE-CLIP, Learning to Prompt; also prompt-tuning methods like MaPLe). The paper should justify why LoRA is chosen over other parameter-efficient adaptation techniques (e.g., prompt tuning, prefix tuning), especially since Equation 4 holds for other approaches as well.
  - No comparison with existing rehearsal-free CL methods based on parameter-efficient tuning (prompt tuning, prefix tuning) beyond the standard methods listed.
  - CLIP baseline (Table 2) is implicitly fine-tuned (see Figure 3, “Fine-Tune”), but frozen CLIP is not added as a baseline, which would help verify if pre-training alone ensures acceptable generalization and degradation.
- **Reproducibility and rigor concerns**: Code is not provided; paper details may be insufficient for full reproduction. Important hyperparameters are not tested properly (e.g., α for LoRA integration, ratio of CKC loss and CLIP loss). The performance gaps in Table 4 are large, making it unclear at which point improvements emerge. Only a subset of tasks is reported in major figures (Figure 3, 4, 6, 7 and Table 4, 5); the full set of tasks should be shown for key setups like ablation studies.
- **Benchmark definition and explanation**: The benchmark is confused and badly explained. The role of each dataset (used in CL fashion vs. static evaluation) is unclear until Section 5.2. A clear table with dataset statistics (number of splits, classes per split) and roles (e.g., continual learning vs. zero-shot generalization) should be placed in the main text. The paper should explicitly state from the beginning that this is a class-incremental learning (CIL) scenario (line 232: “the model has no access to the task identity”).
- **Missing or insufficient related work**: Several relevant works are missed: Qian et al. (2023), Wang et al. (2021), D’Alessandro et al. (2023), Srinivasan et al. (2022) (CLIMB), and the general FSCIL literature. The introduction needs to clarify the differences between CL in multimodal vs. unimodal scenarios, especially the role of the language encoder in classification vs. cross-modal retrieval.
- **Delivery issues**: Many typos (e.g., “leran” instead of “learn”, line 255; “space is view as”, line 280; “loss is use in the”, line 294). Terms like “single-modal scenario” should be replaced with “unimodal”; “CIL” and “MTIL” are undefined (Table 1 may be unnecessary). Figure 2 does not effectively convey CKC; the y-axis range in Figure 3(c) is too wide, making local behavior hard to see. Equations 5 and 6 use variable $z$ confusingly (e.g., substituting $t$ in $\tilde{z}_i^t$ with $t-1$ is not identical to $\tilde{z}_i^{t-1}$). The paper incorrectly cites “pioneering works” as recent at L96.
- **Computational cost analysis**: While LoRA reduces trainable parameters, the computational costs of CKC during fine-tuning are not analyzed. A cost analysis would strengthen the paper.
- **CKC vs. prior distillation works**: Distillation in continual learning is not new (e.g., Contrastive Continual Learning, ICCV 2021). A theoretical or empirical comparison between CKC and such previous methods is necessary. Questions from reviewers overlap with this criticism: “Could the authors further elaborate on why CKC avoids the trade-offs associated with other regularization methods?” and “Is $h_\psi$ in L277 used elsewhere after training? Why is an identical projector used for both vision and text embeddings in equation 5?”.
- **Generalization to other VLMs**: The approach may require specific adjustments for VLMs beyond CLIP; this is not discussed.
- **Task identity**: It is unclear whether the model is given the task ID during training (e.g., “pet-classification” is known) – a key detail for the CL setup that should be stated explicitly.
- **Miscellaneous**: Equation 23 is recursive in $K$ and may be unbounded for large $L$ and $M$; the authors should discuss whether the bound is meaningful. The “Theoretical Analysis” section could be moved to appendix to free space for clarifying the benchmark.
