- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces ET-Plan-Bench, a benchmark that uses LLMs to programmatically generate and automatically evaluate embodied task planning tasks in VirtualHome and Habitat. The benchmark focuses on spatial (relation constraint, occlusion) and temporal/causal understanding. The authors evaluate GPT-4, LLAMA, and Mixtral on the generated tasks, measure success rate, action sequence length, and moving distance, and show that supervised fine-tuning a LLAMA-7B model on the generated data achieves performance comparable to GPT-4.

### Strengths

1. **Scalable automatic task generation**: Using LLMs to generate tasks (including their descriptions and completion criteria) can, in principle, yield a large number of diverse tasks. This approach is useful for procedurally generating new data. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
2. **Comprehensive evaluation**: Multiple foundation models (GPT-4, open-source LLAMA and Mixtral 8x7B) are evaluated, and insights about their planning abilities under different task types are drawn. (Reviewers 1, 3)
3. **Fine-tuning on generated data works**: A supervised fine-tuned LLAMA-7B model using the benchmark’s EQA data performs on par with GPT-4. (Reviewers 1, 3)
4. **Relevant problem**: Exploring the extent to which language models can solve embodied tasks and focusing on spatial-temporal cognition is extremely relevant. (Reviewer 4)
5. **Large-scale effort**: The work appears large and comprehensive. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty and constrained setup**: Task generation using LLMs has been explored extensively in prior work (e.g., RoboGen). The benchmark tasks are relatively simple and constrained (only basic spatial/temporal constraints like “object A is on top of object B”). The simulators (VirtualHome and Habitat) are restricted in task complexity and environmental interactions; agents lack true perception (they receive text descriptions rather than visual input). The spatial and temporal constraints are quite basic. (Reviewers 2, 3)
2. **Unclear contribution and insufficient motivation**:
   - The paper claims a focus on skill-level task decomposition rather than lower-level control, but this motivation is insufficiently justified—as better foundation models become available, skill-level decomposition may require less analysis than spatiotemporal reasoning for low-level control. (Reviewer 2)
   - It is unclear what achieving LLM-based task generation actually adds over a mechanical task generation pipeline (e.g., randomly sampling objects and compatible containers). The role of the LLM in providing common-sense reasoning over the larger task search space is not well described. (Reviewer 4)
   - The paper does not provide a principled framework for formalizing different types of embodied tasks, unlike concurrent work (e.g., “Embodied Agent Interface: Benchmarking LLMs for Embodied Decision Making”). (Reviewer 3)
3. **Risk of in-domain evaluation and lack of validation**: Because tasks and evaluations are both performed by LLMs, methods consisting of the same LLMs may obtain inflated performance evaluations (in-domain issue). This could reward behaviors that do not generalize to the real world. The paper should validate that performance in this benchmark correlates with performance on pre-existing benchmarks (e.g., DROID, OXE) or real-world tasks, and should incorporate nondeterminism (e.g., structured perturbations) in task generation to avoid test-set leakage. (Reviewer 2)
4. **Insufficient analysis and missing comparisons**:
   - Comparisons with RoboGen, which shares a motivation for automatic task generation, are missing. Also missing is a direct cross-simulator analysis that tests identical tasks across simulators in a principled way. (Reviewer 2)
   - The paper does not offer new insights about *why* foundation models struggle with spatiotemporal reasoning (e.g., through the proposed task categorization) and provides no fine-grained error analysis or discussion of failure modes. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
   - No clear framework for systematic task generation and evaluation is presented. (Reviewer 3)
5. **Lack of clarity in presentation**:
   - Writing is poor; the method, primary assumptions, and overall system are hard to understand. Important details (action space, observation space, LLM-simulator interaction, whether images are used) are buried in the appendix rather than in the main text. (Reviewers 3, 4)
   - The abstract nearly duplicates sentences in the introduction—unnecessary and should be avoided. (Reviewer 4)
   - The definition of “navigation with occlusion due to distant initial position” (top 20th percentile) seems arbitrary; a more straightforward definition could directly check occlusion from the agent’s view in simulation. (Reviewer 1)
   - Distribution of generated tasks (Figure 1) is not justified (whether it is from the generation pipeline or deliberately achieved). (Reviewer 1)
6. **Task generation quality and filtering**:
   - The generated tasks themselves could be wrong (e.g., incomplete, impossible). Details on how incorrect tasks are filtered are missing. (Reviewer 1)
   - Human evaluation of “reasonableness” is highly subjective and difficult to quantify. (Reviewer 4)
   - It is unclear whether the diversity of tasks is bottlenecked by the scenes and actions (including transition models) the simulators provide. (Reviewer 1)
7. **Missing qualitative examples**: The paper should provide both positive and negative qualitative examples. (Reviewer 1)
8. **Specific task definition issues**:
   - What distinguishes “regular navigation” from “navigation due to distant initial position” is unclear. (Reviewer 1)
   - How is the optimal path defined? (Reviewer 1)
   - What is the percentage of different types of temporal and causal constraints in the generated tasks? (Reviewer 1)
9. **Agent setup limitations**: Agents do not have true perception (only text descriptions) and the robustness of the perception modules used in the modular approach is not evaluated. The paper should clarify if LLMs take rendered images (then they are VLMs, not LLMs). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
10. **Usefulness tied to underlying LLMs**: The benchmark’s effectiveness is directly tied to the underlying LLMs’ abilities to perform task generation and evaluation flawlessly, which limits its robustness. (Reviewer 2)

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 2 is notably more negative (score 3) and emphasizes missing validation against real-world tasks, the risk of in-domain LLM evaluation, and the need for correlations with pre-existing benchmarks. Reviewer 3 originally raised strong concerns about novelty, simplicity of tasks, and clarity, but upgraded their score after clarifications, though still noting limited analysis. Reviewers 1 and 4 (both score 5) are more positive about the scale and relevance, but agree that clarity is a major issue.
