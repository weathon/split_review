## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AttBalance, an attention-driven constraint balancing framework for visual grounding. It first analyzes the correlation between transformer attention scores inside the ground-truth bounding box and the predicted IoU, then introduces three components: (1) Rho-modulated Attention Constraint (RAC) that applies a BCE loss to encourage attention values to sum to 1 inside the GT box and 0 elsewhere, (2) Momentum Rectification Constraint (MRC) that distills attention from a moving-averaged teacher to smooth training, and (3) Difficulty Adaptive Training (DAT) that dynamically weights loss terms for hard samples. Experiments on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCO-g show consistent improvements over multiple transformer-based models, and when integrated into QRNet it outperforms compared methods. However, comparisons with recent SOTA (e.g., VG-LAW) are missing, and reviewers disagree on the strength of the motivation and the robustness of the approach.

### Strengths
- Identifies an important problem: loss functions in visual grounding are not designed to model vision-language interactions, unlike object detection losses (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The design of RAC, MRC, and DAT is motivated by an analysis showing a positive Spearman correlation between the sum of attention within the GT bounding box and the predicted IoU (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). This makes the method intuitive.
- Consistently improves performance across five different transformer-based models (TransVG, QRNet, VLTVG, etc.) on multiple benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCO-g). With QRNet, AttBalance achieves new state-of-the-art results among the compared methods (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). However, one reviewer notes the improvement for VLTVG is marginal (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive ablation studies show the contribution of each component (RAC, MRC, DAT) and the necessity of their combination (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Provides qualitative attention map comparisons (with vs. without AttBalance) that help visualize the effect (Reviewer 2).
- The framework is designed to be architecture-agnostic and can be readily integrated into different transformer-based visual grounding models (Reviewer 4). (One reviewer questions whether this is a contribution distinct from the method itself – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation assumption debated:** The paper assumes that lacking explicit attention guidance causes suboptimal performance, but this is not fully convincing. Multiple reviewers note that end-to-end training could learn optimal attention patterns without explicit constraints. The Spearman correlation is weak for VLTVG-R101, and on the “unc testA” split the trend even contradicts the argument. Moreover, for small objects, attention outside the bounding box (e.g., context) may be critical, undermining the assumption that attention should be forced inside the GT box (Reviewer 2 W1, Reviewer 3 W1).
- **Missing comparisons with recent work:** The paper lacks comparison with more recent SOTA methods such as VG-LAW (Su et al., CVPR 2023) that uses focal loss and segmentation-based losses, and other works with object-text/patch-text alignment losses. It is unclear if explicit attention guidance offers an advantage over these implicit mechanisms. Additionally, the paper should compare attention maps with such methods (Reviewer 2 S1, W3). Also, comparison with Word2Pix (2021) is needed to demonstrate superiority over early transformer-based grounding (Reviewer 1 W1).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and lack of justification:** The method introduces many hyperparameters and heuristics. For example, in VLTVG, the authors “partition the attention values within the ground truth region of VLTVG’s last layer into 8 equal number parts, omitting the extreme intervals, i.e., those less than 0.1 or greater than 0.9” – introducing at least three hyperparameters without ablation. Similarly, the need for MRC to mitigate issues from RAC suggests the initial constraint may be too aggressive, and DAT further complicates training. The robustness to different datasets and architectures is unclear (Reviewer 2 W2, Reviewer 3 W3).
- **MRC alone degrades performance – insufficient explanation:** Table 2 shows that using only MRC decreases performance, while combining it with RAC yields improvements. The paper only briefly remarks that “merely smoothing attention behavior by MomModal does not bring benefits,” but a deeper explanation of how MRC rectifies RAC is needed (Reviewer 1 W3, Reviewer 3 Questions).
- **Missing experiments and details:** Additional results on ReferItGame and Flicker30K should be provided (Reviewer 4 W1). The paper should report training time compared to baselines (Reviewer 4 W2). Also see questions below.
- **Presentation issues:** Figures (e.g., Figure 1, Figure 3) and Table 2 lack explanation of legends and notations in captions; figures are not self-contained. Writing quality needs improvement (e.g., “analyzing” → “analyze” in Section 4.3, abstract clarity, “uniform gains” should be “constantly improves”, reference formatting) (Reviewer 2 W5, Reviewer 3 major and minor writing issues). Related work placement (currently in Section 5) could be moved earlier (Reviewer 1 W2).
- **Contribution claim overstated:** The third contribution (seamless integration into different transformer-based methods) is a feature of the proposed framework, not a separate contribution; it should be merged with contribution (ii) (Reviewer 2 W4).
- **Questions from reviewers that address missing analyses / alternative approaches:**
    - Why not use a learnable 2D regional weighting layer (like in Large-Scale Image Retrieval with Attentive Deep Local Features, ICCV 2017) to modulate attention instead of explicit constraints? This could serve as a baseline to validate the need for explicit guidance (Reviewer 2 Q4).
    - For VLTVG (which showed marginal improvement), does the attention map change mirror the small performance gain? Is the shift in attention more subtle than for QRNet? (Reviewer 2 Q1).
    - In Section 2 analysis, was the “IoU value” the IoU between the predicted bounding box and the GT? Were attention values normalized in any way? (Reviewer 2 Q2, Q3).
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** Two reviewers (R1, R4) rated the paper 6 and found the approach compelling and thorough, while two reviewers (R2, R3) rated it 5 and expressed stronger concerns about the motivational logic, hyperparameter complexity, and missing comparisons. The critical reviewers question whether the proposed explicit constraints are necessary or if the observed improvements could be achieved with end-to-end learning or alternative losses.
