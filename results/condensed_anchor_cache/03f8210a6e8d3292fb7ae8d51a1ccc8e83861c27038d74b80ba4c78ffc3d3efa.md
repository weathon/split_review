## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Neural Deconstruction Search (NDS), a destroy-reconstruct iterative improvement framework for vehicle routing problems. A neural policy trained with reinforcement learning selectively removes nodes from a solution, then greedy insertion rebuilds the solution. Experiments on CVRP, CVRPTW, and PCVRP show competitive performance. The paper is clearly written and presents interesting experimental breadth, but all reviewers note significant issues with novelty overselling, incomplete baselines, and methodological details that undermine the claimed contributions.

### Strengths
- The deconstruction-reconstruction framework is a novel deviation from standard autoregressive construction approaches, opening an interesting algorithmic avenue. (R1, R2)
- Experiments are thorough across three VRP variants and show promising performance relative to several baselines. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The paper is technically well-written and easy to follow, with clear visuals explaining the process. (R1, R2, R3)
- The method is simple yet achieves a certain level of effectiveness. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Overselling of results**: The abstract states “Our approach surpasses the performance of state-of-the-art operations research methods,” but this claim is not sufficiently substantiated. The numerical comparisons are skewed by a time-based stopping criterion that favors NDS, because neural deconstruction works instantaneously while OR baselines require more time to converge. In practice, performance-based stopping (e.g., number of iterations without improvement) would be more natural. Additionally, the time limit does not account for the significant training overhead of the neural network, which the benchmarks do not require. (R2) This is a major concern across multiple reviewers.
- **Novelty limited to engineering-level migration**: The NDS framework is essentially SISRs with a learning-based destruction step replacing a heuristic destruction step. The greedy insertion with simulated annealing is largely the same as SISR’s “greedy insertion with blinks.” Thus the contribution is a straightforward replacement of a heuristic with a DNN, which does not significantly advance the field. (R3, R4) Reviewer 2 acknowledges the idea is valuable but borderline in novelty; R3 and R4 are more critical.
- **Missing comparisons with key baselines**:
  - No comparison with 2-opt or other classical local search heuristics that also iteratively refine solutions. (R1)
  - No comparison with other learning-based improvement heuristics (e.g., the category from Costa, 2020). (R1)
  - For CVRP, newer learning baselines like ELG [1] and UDC [2] are missing. (R3)
  - For VRPTW and PCVRP, more baseline algorithms are needed. (R3)
- **Generalizability and problem scope**: The experiments are limited to a subset of VRP problems. No tests on TSP, which the framework could likely handle but is not shown. (R1, R3) The paper does not discuss whether the approach can be adapted to other combinatorial optimization problems like Maximum Independent Set. (R1)
- **Lack of ablation and hyperparameter analysis**: No ablation study on the random seed \(v\) (a control parameter in the deconstruction). No explanation for the choice of hyperparameters such as threshold factor and temperature. (R3) Why is greedy insertion used instead of regret insertion? (R3) No details on how the model was trained for VRPTW and PCVRP—what changes were made to handle constraints? (R3)
- **Missing details on baseline training and configuration**: LEHD and BQ (and other learning-based solvers) are reported with performance that differs from their original papers. For example, LEHD was originally trained on problems up to 100 nodes; the paper does not clarify how it was retrained or configured for larger instances. (R1) It is unclear whether benchmark algorithms were hyperparameter-tuned at all. (R2)
- **Lack of explanation of the DNN mechanism**: The paper provides no details on how the DNN is used in the decoding process, leaving the reader with little understanding. (R4) “IMPROVEMENTSTEP” is used in Algorithm 1 without specific explanation. (R4) The claim that construction-based methods are “independent of prior selections” (Section 5) is misleading—they do retain prior customer or partial path information. (R4)
- **Relation to prior work**: The paper does not adequately discuss its relationship to SISRs or to earlier papers that use DNNs for remove-and-reconstruct operations in VRP (e.g., [1–4] mentioned by R4). (R3, R4) The advantages over such prior methods need detailed clarification. (R4)
- **Figure quality**: Figure 3 should be a vector diagram, not a raster image. (R3)
- **Generalization to varying instance sizes**: The paper studies distribution shift (Section 4.4) but does not investigate generalization across different instance sizes. (R2)
