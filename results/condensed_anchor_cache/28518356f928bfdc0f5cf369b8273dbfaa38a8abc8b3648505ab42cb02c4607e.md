## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers assessed a paper proposing Plan4MC, a hierarchical method for long‑horizon Minecraft tasks that uses LLMs to build a skill‑dependency DAG and DFS for planning, plus fine‑grained basic skills (including a Finding‑skill with lidar and grid‑based exploration) trained via RL with intrinsic rewards (MineCLIP). Scores were 3,5,3,5; two reviewers were more positive, two more negative.

### Strengths
- Using LLMs to construct a skill‑dependency graph for planning is interesting and original. (R1)
- The method is sample efficient and shows promise for extracting domain‑specific knowledge from LLMs for planning. (R1)
- Learning fine‑grained basic skills, especially the Finding‑skill (navigation), appears promising for open‑world exploration. (R2)
- Plan4MC outperforms the MineAgent baseline, its own variant without Finding‑skill, and an interactive LLM‑based planner. (R2)
- The overall pipeline of skill learning + planning is straightforward and reasonable. (R4)
- The method achieves good success rates on 40 Minecraft tasks. (R4)
- The Finding‑skill offers a novel approach to task decomposition. (R3)

### Weaknesses
**Generalization and environment specificity**  
- The method seems cobbled together with many environment‑specific choices (e.g., switching between DQN for low‑level and PPO for high‑level policies without motivation). It is hard to tell what would generalize to other environments. (R1)  
- The schemes appear tailored to Minecraft; there is limited insight into broader applicability. (R2, R3, R4)  
- The Finding‑skill heavily relies on lidar information that is not used in human gameplay or in many related works. The authors’ claim that lidar can be replaced with a CV model contradicts their own motivation that Minecraft data is hard to acquire (the CV model would need large Minecraft training data). (R3)  
- The Finding‑skill assumes target items are uniformly distributed across the world surface, which may not hold in general open worlds. If the distribution is environment‑dependent, the 10×10‑grid state‑counting RL could be inefficient. (R2)  
- If the target environment is not covered by LLM training data (unlike Minecraft), it is unclear how the LLM‑based skill graph generation would be adopted. (R2)  
- The method relies on pre‑defined basic skills that require human understanding of task decomposition. (R4)  
- The LLM‑generated skill graph quality depends heavily on user‑defined prompts; completeness of the pre‑defined skill set is not ensured. (R4)  
- It is unclear that a DAG can be easily pre‑generated for more realistic settings. (R1)  

**Missing comparisons and ablations**  
- Only a few baselines are compared. It would be useful to compare with Dreamer V3 and STEVE‑1, which have reported strong results on many of the same tasks (e.g., cut tree, mine stone). (R3)  
- The atomic subtask model should be compared with STEVE‑1. (R3)  
- No comparison with symbolic planning baselines (e.g., LEAGUE, PEORL, ICAPS 2020). (R4)  
- The relationship to Voyager (which also uses LLMs for knowledge in Minecraft) is unclear and should be discussed. (R3)  
- The originality relative to prior LLM‑planning works (Do As I Can, LLM‑Planner, etc.) needs clarification. (R2)  
- It is not reported whether the Finding‑skill is included in the Interactive LLM Planner ablation or other ablations. (R1)  

**Data and sample efficiency concerns**  
- Despite claiming sample efficiency, the method uses MineCLIP intrinsic rewards, which require a large dataset of Minecraft videos paired with captions. Thus the method is not truly free of large demonstration data. (R3)  

**Presentation and clarity (disagreement among reviewers)**  
- One reviewer found the paper well‑organized and easily read (R4), but another reviewer gave presentation a score of 1, noting poor organization, hard‑to‑follow abstract/introduction, and unclear technical details until late in the paper (R1).  
- The abstract’s statement that the Finding‑skill “improves sample efficiency for training all skills” is unclear even after reading the introduction. (R1)  
- “Skill search algorithm” is repeatedly mentioned without stating it is DFS. (R1)  
- The high‑level finding policy goals (section 3.1) and the goal distribution (section 3.2) are unclear. (R1)  

**Other**  
- No discussion of related work in hierarchical RL. (R1)  
- It is unclear whether automated craft actions or the game interface was used, and the FPS is not reported. (R1)  
- Minor typo: in Table 9 (on page 23) stone sword, stone axe, iron trap door, and skill icons appear in wrong images. (R2)  
- Several relevant references are not cited (e.g., [1][2][3] from R4, [4][5][6] from R2). (R4, R2)
