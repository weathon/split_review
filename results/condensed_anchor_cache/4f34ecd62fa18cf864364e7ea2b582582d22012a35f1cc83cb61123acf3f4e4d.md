## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RePaFormer, a method to accelerate MetaFormer-structured vision transformers by applying structural reparameterization to feed-forward network (FFN) layers. It introduces a channel idle mechanism that bypasses non-linear activation for a subset of channels, allowing merging of linear projections at inference time. Experiments on ImageNet classification, self-supervised learning, and dense prediction tasks show significant throughput improvements with accuracy drops that vary by model size and task.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-organized, and easy to follow. It is motivated by a comprehensive latency analysis identifying FFN layers as a major efficiency bottleneck in Vision Transformers.
- The proposed method is interesting and technically sound. It brings actual GPU latency speedups in practice.
- Experimental results are extensive: validated on classification, downstream tasks (dense prediction), and self-supervised learning settings, highlighting generalization ability.
- The method achieves significant throughput improvements for ViT-based classification models (e.g., 67.5% for DeiT-Base, 49.7% for Swin-Base; up to 68.7% for ViT-Huge).

### Weaknesses
- **Accuracy drops, especially on smaller models.** The throughput improvement comes at substantial accuracy loss on small architectures: e.g., –7.9% on DeiT-Tiny and –6.7% on PoolFormer-s12. This suggests that simpler compression techniques like pruning might be preferable for small models. Accuracy drops are also present overall.
- **Insufficient comparison with other reparameterization strategies.** It is unclear why the proposed channel idle mechanism is preferable to RepVGG-style multi-branch reparameterization (idle channels can be seen as a special case of a dual-branch structure). Direct comparisons with previous structured reparameterization methods (e.g., FastViT’s design) are missing.
- **Lack of comparison with other model compression techniques.** The paper does not compare against pruning, distillation, or quantization. It provides no justification of the proposed method’s advantage in terms of generalizability, ease of implementation, impact on accuracy, training overhead, or hardware requirements/constraints.
- **Missing essential baseline: training the inference-time reparameterized architecture from scratch.** The paper should include a baseline where the merged structure (e.g., Figure 1(c)) is directly trained without reparameterization, to isolate the benefit of the channel idle mechanism.
- **Confounding effect of BatchNorm.** The vanilla backbone uses LayerNorm, while RePaFormer uses BatchNorm. The paper does not provide a comparison where the vanilla model’s LayerNorm is replaced with BatchNorm (keeping everything else constant), making it impossible to attribute performance changes to the reparameterization versus the normalization change.
- **Channel idle mechanism inadequately tested.** For the default case μ=1 (75% idle), the transformation is effectively a linear function that could be represented by a single C×C matrix but instead uses 6C² parameters. A baseline should be trained where the idle-channel path is replaced by a single linear layer (as in Figure (b) without BatchNorm inference reparameterization), and its throughput and accuracy should be compared. Furthermore, different sparsity patterns for the idle mechanism are not explored; their impact on performance is unknown.
- **Performance gap in dense prediction tasks.** The accuracy drop on dense prediction tasks (e.g., segmentation, detection) is non-negligible compared to classification. The paper lacks analysis of why the gap is larger and whether reparameterization affects spatial sensitivity. The latency improvement is also smaller on these tasks, likely because FFNs occupy a smaller portion of runtime for high-resolution inputs.
- **Performance gap in self-supervised learning.** The gap between RePaFormer and the vanilla baseline is larger under self-supervised learning than supervised learning. The paper does not explore the impact on learned representations or generalizatiol ability for foundation models; experiments on downstream tasks like fine-grained classification are needed to validate this.
- **Training costs and scalability claims not supported.** The paper does not report training time of the proposed method compared to the vanilla version. The abstract claims that improvements in speed *and accuracy* are expected on even larger models, but the accuracy improvements are not empirically supported (e.g., the ViT-Large and ViT-Huge results already show accuracy gains, but the statement is about expectation rather than evidence; further validation on other large models is needed).
- **Dependence on BatchNorm.** The method seems to require BatchNorm for the reparameterization. It is unclear whether there is a workaround to avoid using BatchNorm (e.g., when LayerNorm is preferred).
