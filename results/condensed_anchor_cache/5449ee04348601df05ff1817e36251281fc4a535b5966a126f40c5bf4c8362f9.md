## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper explores the transferability of targeted adversarial examples to proprietary black-box Vision-enabled Large Language Models (VLLMs) such as GPT-4o, Claude, and Gemini. It proposes two attacks (CLIP Score attack and VLLM Response attack) and two enhancement tricks (data augmentation and ensembling surrogate models), evaluating on object recognition, visual question answering, image captioning, and a custom safety-related reasoning benchmark. Reviewers are divided: two (scores 5) find the work timely, thorough, and original in demonstrating the highest transfer attack success rates seen and releasing a safety benchmark; the other two (scores 3) question novelty given prior work (Dong et al. 2023; Zhao & Yin et al.), note unclear experimental setups, lack of baseline comparisons, and excessive perturbation size, and find the paper lacks clarity and cohesion in places.

### Strengths

- **Relevance and timeliness** for deployed VLLMs; investigating attack transferability to proprietary models is significant and needed for the safety community (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- **Two proposed attack methods** – CLIP Score attack (aligning image embeddings with incorrect text embeddings) and VLLM Response attack (directly maximizing log-likelihood of wrong output) – plus two enhancement tricks (data augmentation, model ensemble) (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Thorough experiments** across two open-source surrogate VLLMs and three black-box proprietary VLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini), covering three tasks (image classification, text generation/captioning, safety reasoning) (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- **Demonstrates the highest transfer attack success rates** seen against frontier closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) (Reviewer 4).  
- **Ablation studies** showing how data augmentation and ensemble size enhance transferability, providing practical guidance (Reviewer 4).  
- **Discussion of universal perturbations** for VLLMs is compelling and underexplored (Reviewer 3).  
- **Introduces three evaluation tasks**, including a custom safety reasoning dataset/benchmark (VLLM SafeBench) that is released (Reviewers 4, 1).  
- **Well-written and easy to follow** overall, with informative figures and tables (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **Surrogate models are SOTA** open-source VLLMs (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns**: The CLIP Score attack and VLLM Response attack closely resemble prior transfer-based attacks (Dong et al. “How Robust is Google’s Bard to Adversarial Image Attacks?”; Zhao et al. “On Evaluating Adversarial Robustness of Large Vision-Language Models”; Yin et al. “Vlattack”). The paper does not adequately acknowledge this prior work nor run baseline comparisons against it. Reviewers recommend softening novelty claims (e.g., “building on prior works, we enhance transferability by…”) and directly comparing to these baselines (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Lack of baseline/comparison methods** in main results: no comparison to random perturbations, prior attack methods, or ablations isolating each attack individually. It is unclear whether the reported success is due to the proposed techniques or simply large perturbations (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- **Unclear threat model and experimental setup**: It is not specified whether attacks are white-box on surrogates / black-box on victims, nor which specific attack (CLIP Score, VLLM Response, or a mixture) is optimized for each table. Surrogate and victim models are not identified in tables; the term “transfer attack” in line 268 is ambiguous (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing ablation of individual attacks**: The method appears to use a mixture of both attacks (e.g., accumulating both losses), but no ablation shows the contribution of each attack separately (Reviewer 4).  
- **Perturbation scale**: ε = 32/255 is large and may affect stealthiness; random perturbations at that size could also cause misclassification – this baseline is missing (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **Targeted attack results** are only provided on ImageNet-1K classification; more experiments on other tasks are needed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient explanation of transferability reasons**: The paper does not explain why attacks transfer beyond the empirical results (Reviewer 1).  
- **Influence of textual prompts** in equation (2) on attack success is not analyzed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Typos and notation errors**: Confusion of \(\tilde{t}_a\) and \(\tilde{x}_a\) in line 223 and equation (5); line 335 “more less” should be “more or less”; line 392 backward quotation mark (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Definitions placed in appendix**: “transferable” and “universal” in line 186 should be in the main text (Reviewer 3).  
- **VLLM SafeBench tool** is introduced without prior context in experiments (Reviewer 3).  
- **Claims in Section 4.3** about the safety benchmark could be narrower – it evaluates only a subset of safety-critical domains, not all (Reviewer 4).  
- **Motivation for two separate attacks** is unclear (lines 218–219) and insufficiently supported; the CLIP Score attack is untargeted, the VLLM Response attack is targeted, but this distinction is not clarified (Reviewer 3).  
- **Equation (1)** is too loose: requiring only that outputs are different could be satisfied by a single token flip (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minor cohesion issues**: term “Multimodal LLMs” appears only in some section titles and table captions, not consistently (Reviewer 3).  
- **No analysis of text perturbation** effects alongside image perturbation (Reviewer 2).
