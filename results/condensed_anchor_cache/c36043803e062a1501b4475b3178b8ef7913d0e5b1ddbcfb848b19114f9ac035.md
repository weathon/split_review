## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies reward model overoptimization in RLHF and evaluates ensemble-based conservative optimization (WCO and UWO) for BoN sampling and PPO. It extends Gao et al. (2023) by adding 25% label noise. Results show that conservative optimization practically eliminates overoptimization and improves performance by up to 70% for BoN, and for PPO always reduces overoptimization relative to a single RM, especially when combined with a small KL penalty. The paper systematically investigates effects across model sizes and data sizes using open-source models.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and timely; overoptimization is a key bottleneck in RLHF and LLM safety.
- The experiments are extensive and well-executed, covering different optimization methods (BoN, PPO), label noise, and multiple model/data sizes.
- The approach is simple and elegant: training reward model ensembles from different random seeds yields significant improvements, especially for BoN.
- The paper is the first to reproduce RM overoptimization with open-source models (as noted by Reviewer 4).
- The empirical claims are well-supported by thorough experiments; the results are promising and clearly demonstrate the value of ensemble methods.
- The writing is generally clear and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3, 4 note clarity; Reviewer 1 notes some presentation issues but still rates presentation fair).
- While the technique itself (pessimistic optimization with ensembles) is not novel, its systematic evaluation for RLHF on LLMs is a new and important contribution (Reviewer 3 emphasizes this empirical contribution).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of multiple random seeds for PPO experiments.** All PPO results (Figures 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9) appear to be from a single random seed. Due to high noise in RL training, conclusions about ensemble methods’ effectiveness are not statistically robust. Running at least 3-5 seeds with error bars is needed to demonstrate consistency (Reviewers 3 and 4). The absence of variance makes it unclear whether observed improvements are significant or due to chance.
- **Interpretability of the gold reward metric.** It is hard to gauge practical impact: how much better is a chatbot with gold reward 0.5 vs 0.4? Reporting win-rates (as in AlpacaFarm) would make results more interpretable and help assess the real improvement from ensembles (Reviewer 3).
- **Diminishing gains with larger models and datasets.** Figure 8 shows that ensemble gains shrink as RM size increases (1.3B shows little benefit of ensembles over single RM). Similarly, Figure 9 suggests that with 46K training data, ensemble methods are only marginally better than a single RM. This raises the question: with more commonly used larger models and larger datasets, will the benefit of ensembles further diminish? (Reviewer 2). The paper should discuss and investigate this scaling trend.
- **Paper requires reading Gao et al. (2023) to understand key details.** KL divergence axes in Figures 2-7 are not defined (e.g., what KL is measured, why scales differ between figures). Captions and text should clarify which KL is plotted (KL between policy and SFT? Policy and reference?). Reviewers should not need to consult prior work to follow the figures (Reviewers 1 and 4).
- **Missing experimental details.** Several design choices are insufficiently justified or reported:
  - Why train reward models for 5 epochs? What is training/validation accuracy? In Figure 15, using steps as x-axis is confusing; epochs would be clearer (Reviewer 4).
  - For BoN, nmax=12,500 is used but “maximum” suggests sometimes fewer samples; clarify (Reviewer 4).
  - For PPO, “3000 steps” is given but number of episodes is not specified (Reviewer 4).
  - Dual y-axis in Figure 3: proxy RM and gold RM scales differ – how are these scales defined and aligned? (Reviewer 4).
  - Figures 6 and 7 appear contradictory: KL penalty 0 yields gold reward ~0.15 in Figure 6 but ~0.03 in Figure 7. Explanation needed (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 8 shows PPO underperforming BoN for 1.3B models, contradicting Gao et al. (2023) where 1.2B PPO outperforms 1.2B BoN; why? (Reviewer 4).
  - The sources of randomness (query dataset seed, RM seed, policy seed) should be explicitly listed; “different random seeds” may also involve different data shuffling (Reviewer 4).
  - In Figure 4, why is single RM only shown with KL=150 and not other KL coefficients? (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work omission.** The paper should cite and discuss https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07472.pdf (evaluates ensembles of LLM-based reward models for active learning, relevant to the conjecture on uncertainty estimates improving sample efficiency) (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues.** Typo at bottom of page 5: “for PPO, WCO, and UWO” should remove the second comma. Hyperlink to “Section 4.1 for details” in SFT paragraph appears broken; no details found in Section 4.1 (Reviewer 1). For PPO plots showing KL/reward over training time, intermediate values are less relevant because early stopping is not typically used; Figure 7 is a better comparison. A version of Figure 7 plotting gold reward vs. sqrt(KL) for each (objective, KL coefficient) would be more informative (Reviewer 3).
