## Summary

TOKENCOUNT proposes a training-free object counting framework built on SAM with two main components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that iteratively samples point prompts from a similarity-derived probability distribution, updating the distribution based on verification outcomes, and (2) output token-based verification that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS (Triangle Similarity-Sector Similarity) metric to distinguish positive from negative instances, rather than relying on image-embedding similarity as prior work does. The method is evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK, achieving an MAE of 16.25 on FSC-147 (best among SAM-only training-free methods) and 4.68 on CARPK (outperforming all training-based methods).

## Strengths

- **Novel use of SAM's output tokens for verification (well-motivated and empirically validated).** The core idea — repurposing SAM's decoder output tokens (which integrate semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention) as a verification space rather than using raw image embeddings — is genuinely novel and clearly motivated. Section 3.2 explains why cosine similarity and Euclidean distance are inadequate for tokens that encode both angular (semantic) and magnitude (positional) information. The claim is supported by Table 5 (output token space MAE 16.25 vs. image embedding space MAE 16.94 on FSC-147) and by the PCA visualization in Figure 5 showing better class separability in output token space.

- **Probabilistic prompt generation with adaptive distribution updates is effective.** The iterative sampling procedure (Section 3.1) is a principled approach: prompts are drawn from a multinomial distribution derived from exemplar similarity, and the distribution is updated after each iteration by suppressing probabilities at positive detections (set to 0) and decreasing them at negative detections. Table 4 demonstrates that increasing prompts-per-iteration consistently improves MAE at every iteration count (e.g., at 16 iterations: 32→64→128 prompts gives 21.74→18.13→16.25), confirming the adaptive mechanism directs prompts toward undiscovered objects.

- **Strong performance on CARPK.** Table 1 shows MAE 4.68 and RMSE 6.13 on CARPK, outperforming all listed training-based methods (e.g., BMNet+ MAE 5.76, SAFECOUNT MAE 5.33, LOCA MAE 9.97) and all SAM-only training-free methods. This demonstrates the method generalizes well to consistent-viewpoint scenarios without any training data for the specific category.

- **Competitive one-shot counting without auxiliary models.** Table 2 shows MAE 18.57 under the one-shot setting on FSC-147, outperforming several training-based methods (CFOCNet 28.60, FamNet 26.76) and all SAM-only training-free baselines (C-Anything 33.53, TFOC 22.96), while using only SAM without auxiliary encoders.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Abstract and claims are overstated relative to Table 1 results.** The abstract claims "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." On FSC-147, this is contradicted by the paper's own Table 1: TFCAC (training-free) achieves MAE 12.26, LOCA (training-based) 10.79, PseCo 13.05, BMNet+ 14.62, and SAFECOUNT 14.32 — all better than the reported 16.25. On CARPK, TFCAC (4.39) also outperforms the proposed method (4.68). The body text in Section 4.2 is more measured ("best performance among training-free SAM only methods"), but the abstract and introduction do not reflect this qualification. This disconnect between the paper's strongest advertised claim and the presented evidence undermines credibility.

- **Missing ablation to isolate the contribution of probabilistic prompt generation.** The paper's central claim is that *both* components (probabilistic prompting and output token verification) are critical. However, there is no baseline that replaces probabilistic prompt generation with a plain grid or superpixel prompting strategy while keeping the output-token verification fixed. Table 4 varies only iterations and prompts-per-iteration within the probabilistic framework; it does not compare against a non-probabilistic alternative. Without this, the reader cannot determine whether the performance gain over TFOC, C-Anything, etc., comes primarily from the novel verification space, the adaptive prompting, or their combination. This is the single most important missing experiment for substantiating the paper's contribution structure.

### Minor

- **Which SAM output token(s) are used is not specified.** SAM's decoder produces 4 output tokens (3 for mask prediction + 1 for IoU prediction). The paper discusses "output tokens" generically but never states which token(s) are fed into the TS-SS similarity computation — whether only the IoU token, one specific mask token, an aggregation, or all four. This is a reproducibility gap that prevents an independent implementation of the central verification stage.

- **No error bars, variance, or statistical significance.** The core comparison (TS-SS 16.25 vs. cosine 16.48 vs. L2 16.53 in Table 3) involves differences of 0.23–0.28 MAE (≈1.4%). While the trend across multiple metrics is consistent, the absence of any variance estimate (standard deviation, confidence intervals, or multi-run results) makes it difficult to assess whether these differences are meaningful or within the noise range of the sampling procedure and threshold choices.

- **Token verification threshold (300) is not accompanied by sensitivity analysis.** The paper states the threshold is set to 300 "based on validation experiments" but provides no analysis of how MAE varies with threshold choice. Since TS-SS is an unbounded product of two components (TS and SS in Eq. 2), the threshold's scale depends on feature magnitudes that may vary across categories. A sensitivity curve would substantially strengthen the work.

- **No inference-time comparison against baselines.** The paper claims "superior computational efficiency compared to state of the art" (Section 5) and notes being "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC, but reports only its own timing (1.69 s/image on FSC-147). Without wall-clock times for TFOC, C-Anything, and TFCounter under identical hardware, the efficiency advantage is asserted but not demonstrated.

- **High RMSE on FSC-147 (133.11) is not discussed.** While the method achieves competitive MAE, its RMSE is worse than TFOC (132.16) and far worse than TFCAC (56.33), suggesting large errors on some images. The paper focuses on MAE throughout but does not analyze these failure modes (e.g., whether the method systematically overcounts or undercounts on particular categories).

- **PCA visualization (Figure 5) shows only 2 image examples.** The claim that output tokens are more separable than image embeddings is supported by only two qualitative examples. More examples across diverse categories would strengthen the generalization argument.

### Trivial

- The paper uses degrees (360°) in Eq. 2 for the TS-SS metric, which is conventional for the document-clustering origin of this metric but unusual in vision; this does not affect the mathematics but is worth noting.

## Novel Insights

The paper's central insight — that SAM's decoder output tokens encode a joint semantic+positional representation that is *more discriminative* for object verification than the image encoder's embedding — is the review's most interesting finding. Unlike prior work that treats the image embedding as the final representation and computes similarity there, the paper recognizes that the decoder's attention mechanism has already solved a harder problem (differentiating objects at similar positions and similar objects at different positions) and that its output tokens naturally reflect this. The TS-SS metric, borrowed from document clustering, turns out to be a good fit for this dual-nature embedding space. Whether this insight generalizes beyond counting — e.g., to other SAM-based verification tasks like tracking or open-vocabulary detection — is an open question that the paper does not explore, but it is worth flagging.

## Suggestions

1. **Fix the abstract** to accurately reflect the method's standing: "best among SAM-only training-free methods" or "competitive with training-based methods" rather than "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods."
2. **Add a grid-prompting ablation** replacing probabilistic prompt generation with uniform grid prompts (keeping the same output-token verification). This is essential to disentangle the two contributions.
3. **Specify which SAM output token(s)** are used for the TS-SS similarity computation, and add pseudocode or a detailed description of the verification step in the appendix.
4. **Report variance** (std. dev. over multiple runs or bootstrapped confidence intervals) for the main results, especially Table 3 where the differences are small.
5. **Add a sensitivity curve** showing MAE vs. verification threshold on a validation split to demonstrate robustness of the chosen threshold.
6. **Include wall-clock inference times** for TFOC, C-Anything, and TFCounter on the same hardware to substantiate the computational efficiency claim.
