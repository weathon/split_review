## Summary

This paper proposes a hybrid neural physics framework for real-time fluid simulation with sketch-based control. The core ideas are: (1) a GNN-based neural physics model operating at low spatiotemporal resolution, hybridized with a classical MPM solver via a cosine-similarity fallback condition triggered when dynamics become complex; (2) a diffusion-based Fluid ControlNet trained on data generated by a novel reverse simulation strategy that solves for the force fields needed to reverse forward trajectories. Experiments span 2D/3D water, sand, and multi-material scenarios.

## Strengths

- **Hybrid fallback mechanism effectively improves the error-latency Pareto front.** Figure 7 and Figure 10 show that the hybrid solver consistently achieves a better trade-off than either pure neural physics or pure MPM across six scenarios. On Water 2D, 1000 steps run in 676.4ms (vs. 1931.1ms for full-resolution neural physics) while reducing final grid RMSE from 0.0188 to 0.0109.

- **The cosine-similarity fallback trigger is well-motivated and cheap to compute.** Section 3.1.2 introduces a fluid complexity measure (cosine similarity of particle accelerations over a 10-step window) that shows clear negative correlation with simulation error (Spearman −0.3902, Figure 5). The design choice to favor this over velocity divergence (which is more expensive to compute) is principled.

- **Reverse simulation for training-data generation is a creative contribution.** Section 3.2.2 derives a closed-form solution (Equation 3) for the external force field that reverses a forward trajectory, enabling automated synthesis of ground-truth control data without manual annotation. The method also automatically produces user sketches (arrows, ovals) from trajectories.

- **Comprehensive ablation of spatiotemporal downsampling ratios.** Figure 6(a–c) systematically explores how spatial ratio rₚ and temporal ratio rₜ affect the error-latency trade-off, providing empirical justification for the chosen configuration (rₚ=1/1.75, rₜ=2), which reduces neural physics latency by 78.8%.

- **The system works across diverse materials and dimensions** — water, sand, 2D, 3D, and multi-material (Water-Sand) — which strengthens the claim of generality.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **"Real-time" is never defined, and some scenarios fall below typical real-time thresholds.** The paper claims "real-time simulations at high frame rates" but never specifies what frame rate is targeted. The Water-Sand 2D scenario runs at 0.08s per step (≈12.5 steps/sec), which is below the 30–60 FPS typically expected for interactive applications. By contrast, Sand 3D runs at 0.90ms per step (≈1111 steps/sec). Without a definition, the reader cannot assess which scenarios meet the stated goal. This inconsistency weakens the core positioning of the paper.

2. **The fallback threshold r_c=0.8 is validated on Water 2D only and applied across all domains without re-validation.** The ablation in Figure 6(d) and Table 1 is conducted solely on Water 2D. The paper then applies this same threshold to sand, 3D, and multi-material scenarios without showing that the trade-off transfers. Since the cosine-similarity measure likely behaves differently for granular materials vs. water, and in 3D vs. 2D, this is an unsubstantiated generalization of the method's key hyperparameter.

3. **The generative control evaluation is too weak to support the claims.** The control baseline is a "spatiotemporal constant force field" — not a learned baseline. The paper cites Chu et al. (2021) and Yan et al. (2020) as prior fluid control work but does not compare against them. The metric (grid RMSE at the final time step only) does not measure whether particles follow the user sketch *during* the simulation. The improvement over baseline is modest (e.g., Water 2D: 0.0802 vs. 0.0908), and no variance or statistical significance is reported. The visualizations in Figure 11 are static snapshots and cannot convey temporal fidelity.

4. **No error bars, confidence intervals, or multiple-seed statistics are reported on any metric.** Every reported number (Figure 10 points, Table 1, Table 3) is presented as a single point. Without any measure of variance, the reader cannot assess whether observed differences are meaningful. This is a significant gap for a submission at a top-tier conference.

### Minor

- **The paper does not report the fallback frequency across scenarios.** What fraction of simulation steps trigger MPM in each domain? This directly determines the hybrid solver's latency and behavior but is never discussed.

- **Grid RMSE_̄m is used as the primary fidelity metric, but its correlation with visual quality is not validated.** The metric is inspired by Huang et al. (2021), but the paper does not show that differences in RMSE_̄m correspond to perceptible differences in simulation quality.

- **The Water-Sand 2D scenario in Figure 10(f) appears to operate at a very different time scale** (0–100ms per step) compared to the other scenarios (1–2ms per step), but this discrepancy is not discussed.

- **The control evaluation only measures final-step error**, which is a narrow operationalization of "following the sketch." A metric like Chamfer distance over the trajectory or proportion of particles entering a sketch-defined region would be more informative.

### Trivial

- In Equation (2), there is a typographical artifact: "t-t-δt:t" in the second argument of the cosine.
