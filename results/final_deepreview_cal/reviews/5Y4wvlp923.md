## Summary

The paper identifies a genuine problem in contrastive time-series representation learning — that dominant semantic components (e.g., trend) can suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality) — and proposes a diagnostic metric called SDE (Semantic Disentanglement/Separability/Decomposition Error) to quantify this imbalance, along with an Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW) mechanism that uses SDE-derived asymmetry scores to reweight contrastive losses in the CoST framework. The paper presents a well-structured experimental sequence: it first validates the imbalance exists (Table 1), shows direct SDE regularization fails (Table 2), and then evaluates the full APW method (Table 3). However, the evaluation contains several significant gaps that weaken the central claims.

## Strengths

1. **SDE diagnostic is well-motivated and empirically validated on TS2Vec.** Table 1 provides clear, systematic evidence that the SDE metric captures semantic asymmetry in TS2Vec embeddings across varying amplitude ratios (ranging from 0.1 to 10). When trend dominates (r=10), SDE_trend,period = 0.0011 while SDE_period,trend = 1.1083, cleanly demonstrating the imbalance the paper targets.

2. **The negative result in Section 4.3 strengthens the contribution.** Table 2 shows that directly adding SDE as a regularization term fails to improve forecasting (e.g., ETTm1 at horizon 288: 0.101 → 0.126, Electricity at 168: 0.449 → 0.508). This negative finding is non-obvious and supports the claim that the adaptive weighting mechanism, not the diagnostic itself, drives any improvements.

3. **Genuine improvements on several datasets.** On ETTm1 (third block of Table 3), CoST+APW beats vanilla CoST across all five horizons (e.g., at 288: 0.056 vs 0.067, at 672: 0.132 vs 0.154). On ETT2, APW wins in 4 of 5 settings. These are non-trivial gains.

4. **Systematic experimental flow.** The paper progresses logically from diagnosing the problem (Section 4.2) to testing a naive fix (Section 4.3) to evaluating the proposed solution (Section 4.4), making the narrative easy to follow.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Overclaiming "consistent gains" — the empirical results are mixed, and some degradation is substantial.** The paper claims "consistent gains" (abstract, Section 4.4.4, conclusion) but the data tells a more nuanced story. On Electricity, CoST+APW is substantially worse than vanilla CoST at horizons 168 (+33% MSE), 336 (+17.5%), and 720 (+10.9%). On Weather, APW loses to CoST at horizons 48, 336, and 720. Across 25 dataset-horizon pairs, APW beats CoST in roughly 15, loses in 9, and ties in 1. The paper never acknowledges these failures or analyzes when/why the method hurts. Claiming "consistently" is inaccurate and undermines reader trust.

2. **The SDE values for the proposed method (CoST+APW) are never reported.** Section 4.4.4 states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" and claims "consistently lower SDE values," but Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE column. Since SDE is the paper's core diagnostic and the method's stated purpose is to reduce semantic imbalance, the absence of SDE measurements on the proposed method is a critical evidential gap. The reader cannot verify whether the reweighting actually reduces the imbalance it is designed to fix.

3. **The "without architectural changes" claim (abstract) is contradicted by the MLP fusion layer.** Section 4.4.2 introduces "a small MLP g_φ that maps the concatenated embeddings [v(a)||v(b)] to a composite embedding." This is an architectural addition to CoST, not a plug-in. Moreover, the paper never ablates whether improvements come from the reweighting mechanism or from the extra representational capacity of the MLP. Without comparing (CoST + MLP without reweighting) vs (CoST + APW with reweighting), the contribution of the core mechanism cannot be isolated.

4. **Key hyperparameters γ and γ' are never specified.** Section 3.4 and 4.4.3 introduce these scaling hyperparameters but provide no default values, tuning procedure, or sensitivity analysis. Without this information the experiments cannot be reproduced and the method's robustness to hyperparameter choices is unknown.

### Minor

5. **Naming inconsistency for the SDE acronym.** The paper uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title, abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3) to refer to what appears to be the same metric. This suggests hasty assembly and reduces clarity.

6. **The linearity assumption in the SDE formula is unvalidated.** The metric SDE_{a,b} = 1 - cos(v(a+b) - v(b), v(a)) assumes v(a+b) - v(b) ≈ v(a) in the embedding space. For nonlinear encoders (convolutional/transformer-based networks used in CoST and TS2Vec), this is not guaranteed. The paper appeals to word2vec-style vector arithmetic (Section 3.2), but word2vec's vector arithmetic emerges from a linear embedding layer with a specific training objective, not from deep contrastive encoders. While Table 1 shows SDE empirically captures meaningful asymmetry, the theoretical gap remains unaddressed.

7. **No statistical significance reported.** Table 3 reports single MSE values without standard deviations or confidence intervals across multiple seeds. Given that many APW-vs-CoST differences are small (e.g., ETT1 at 168: 0.101 vs 0.107), it is unclear which improvements are reliable.

### Trivial

8. **Section 4.3 redundantly redefines SDE.** The SDE metric is already defined in Section 3.2 and then redefined in Section 4.3 under a different name ("Semantic Decomposition Error"), adding unnecessary repetition.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation comparing (CoST + MLP, no reweighting) vs (CoST + APW) to isolate the reweighting contribution.
- A plot or table of Δ over training to show the reweighting mechanism actually reduces asymmetry.
- Sensitivity analysis for γ and γ'.
- Results on at least one non-forecasting task (e.g., classification or anomaly detection) to broaden the evaluation, as the paper motivates SDE for multiple tasks.

## Removed Points

- **"SDE fails as a training objective, so why should Δ be reliable for reweighting?"** — This conflates two distinct uses of the same quantity. Using a metric as a regularizer (direct gradient update) vs. as a diagnostic signal for reweighting existing contrastive losses are fundamentally different optimization strategies. The paper explicitly hypothesizes (Section 4.3) that SDE "fails to provide constructive optimization gradients when directly used as a regularizer," which is a reasonable explanation. This criticism is weakened to a methodological observation rather than a fatal contradiction.

- **"The MLP contradicts 'pluggable' claim" — speculation that gains come from MLP capacity** — Retained as Major #3 (this is real), but the strength finder's claim about "pluggable design with minimal overhead" needs to be evaluated alongside it.

- **"CoST+APW is worse at every horizon except 24 on Weather"** — Partially inaccurate; APW also wins at horizon 168 (0.200 vs 0.213). The core concern about mixed results is retained in Major #1.

- **Strength finder generic strengths about "important problem" and "worthwhile contribution"** — Removed as superficial.

- **"Comparison on more than forecasting" from strength finder** — Downgraded to nice-to-have.

2. Add an ablation removing the MLP fusion layer to isolate the contribution of the reweighting mechanism.
