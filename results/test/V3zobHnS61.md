## Summary

This paper proposes RA-TTA (Retrieval-Augmented Test-Time Adaptation), the first TTA method for vision-language models that retrieves external knowledge from a web-scale image database (LAION-2B) on-the-fly at test time. The key innovation is the use of fine-grained text descriptions (generated by GPT-3.5 Turbo) as intermediate "semantic chunks" — descriptions relevant to the test image are selected via image-to-text search, then used to retrieve external images via text-to-image search. A description-based adaptation step, using a semantic gap measure aggregated via optimal transport with importance weighting, fuses retrieval-based predictions with the VLM's initial prediction. RA-TTA achieves best or second-best results on 16 out of 17 datasets, outperforming prior TTA methods by 2.49–8.45% on average across 13 transfer learning benchmarks and 9.18% over Ensemble on natural distribution shifts.

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated paradigm.** RA-TTA is the first approach to introduce external-knowledge retrieval into test-time adaptation for VLMs. It addresses a genuine limitation of prior TTA work (TPT, C-TPT, RLCF), which relies solely on internal VLM parameters. The analogy to RAG-style document chunking (using text descriptions as "semantic chunks" for images) is clearly articulated and grounded in a concrete failure mode of naive image-to-image retrieval.

- **Strong, consistent empirical results.** Across 17 datasets spanning standard transfer learning and natural distribution shifts, RA-TTA achieves best or second-best accuracy on 16/17 (Table 1, Table 2). The gains are particularly large on specialized/fine-grained domains where external knowledge is most needed (e.g., +8.23% on FGVC Aircraft, +8.47% on RESISC45 over the second-best method). The method demonstrably outperforms both tuning-based VLM TTA methods and prior retrieval-augmented methods (SuS-X-LC, Neural Priming).

- **Training-free and adaptive retrieval.** Unlike prior retrieval-augmented VLM methods (REACT, RA-CLIP, RECO) that require fine-tuning or additional parameters, RA-TTA requires no pre-test-time training. Unlike SuS-X and Neural Priming which use static offline-retrieved sets, RA-TTA retrieves images adaptively per test image, enabling it to handle distribution shifts that the static baselines cannot.

- **Component-level validation.** The ablation study (Table 3) confirms that each proposed component (description-based retrieval, description-based adaptation, image weighting) contributes positively. The hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (Figure 5, averaged across 13 datasets) further demonstrates robustness to key parameters.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **External database construction is under-analyzed.** The paper constructs the external database by keyword-matching on LAION-2B captions (line 188) but provides **no quantitative characterization** of the resulting database. No statistics are given on how many images are retrieved per class or per dataset, whether coverage is balanced, or whether fine-grained classes (e.g., "Boeing 737-200," "Chevrolet Impala 2012") are adequately represented. Since the method's core claim depends on having useful external images to retrieve, this gap prevents a reader from assessing the method's failure modes or generalizability to new datasets. The strong results across 17 datasets indirectly validate the approach, but the paper should explicitly characterize the database to make this argument rigorous. This is especially important because the sensitivity analysis (Figure 5c) varies \(K_S\) (how many images to retrieve) but does not vary the *availability* of relevant images — a factor that would differ substantially across classes and datasets.

- **Ablation study is limited to a single dataset.** Table 3 reports ablation results on FGVC Aircraft only — the dataset where RA-TTA's gains are largest. This makes it difficult to assess whether each component (description-based retrieval, description-based adaptation, image weighting) is universally beneficial or has dataset-specific effects. For example, description-based adaptation may matter less on datasets with simpler semantics, and image weighting may matter more on noisier retrieval sets. Extending the ablation to at least 3–4 diverse datasets (e.g., one fine-grained, one coarse-grained, one with natural distribution shift) would substantiate the claim that all three components are consistently effective.

### Minor

- **Optimal transport is not properly ablated vs. simpler alternatives.** The ablation disables "image weighting" (Var. 3) by removing the weight vectors \(\mathcal{U}\) and \(\mathcal{V}\), but OT with uniform weights is still used. This means we cannot tell whether OT itself is beneficial, or whether a simpler aggregation (e.g., weighted mean, min-pooling) would suffice. Given that OT adds computational complexity, the paper should compare OT against a simpler aggregation to justify its use.

- **Computational cost is not discussed.** RA-TTA performs per-test-sample retrieval from a large database, computes image-text similarities for \(K_D\) descriptions across \(M+1\) augmented views, and solves an OT problem per class-group. The paper does not report inference time, FLOPs, or any runtime comparison against baselines. This makes it difficult to assess the method's practicality for real-time or resource-constrained deployment.

- **No analysis of failure cases or performance variation.** The paper reports that RA-TTA wins on 12/13 transfer learning datasets but does not discuss where the improvement is smallest or why. Understanding the conditions under which the method struggles would sharpen the contribution and guide future work.

### Trivial

- **Single LLM and description template used without sensitivity analysis.** Only GPT-3.5 Turbo with CuPL-style templates is used. While this is standard practice, the paper does not analyze how RA-TTA's performance depends on description quality or LLM choice.
