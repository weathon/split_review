## Summary

This paper introduces DAMEON, a framework that formulates language model decoding as a constrained optimization problem: find the distribution q closest to the LM p_θ in reverse KL divergence while matching expected scores on a set of quality metrics to human texts. The solution yields an analytical energy-based model p_{θ,μ}(x) ∝ p_θ(x) exp[-μᵀf(x)]. The authors prove that this induced distribution improves perplexity on human texts over the original LM (Proposition 2), estimate the Lagrange multipliers μ via importance-sampling-based moment matching, and sample via Sampling-Importance-Resampling. Experiments across Wikipedia and News domains with GPT-2 XL and OPT-6.7B show consistent improvements over strong baselines on repetition, coherence, diversity, information content, MAUVE, and human evaluation.

---

## Strengths

- **Principled formulation with an analytical solution (Proposition 1).** Framing decoding as reverse-KL minimization subject to expectation-matching constraints is a novel and theoretically grounded departure from heuristic truncation or contrastive objectives. The closed-form EBM solution provides interpretability and connects to information geometry.

- **Provable perplexity improvement (Proposition 2).** The paper proves that H(p_d, q_opt) = H(p_d, p_θ) − D_KL(q_opt‖p_θ), guaranteeing lower perplexity on human texts. This is a genuine theoretical contribution — most heuristic decoding methods do not permit distribution-level evaluation at all, let alone improvement guarantees.

- **Simultaneous alignment across multiple aspects without hard trade-offs.** In Table 1, DAMEON achieves metric scores closest to human references on repetition (sr-4), coherence, diversity, and information content simultaneously. For example, on Wikipedia with GPT-2 XL: sr-4=0.42 (ref. 0.48), coh=62.5 (ref. 62.3), div=92.2 (ref. 92.5), e^ent=22.8 (ref. 23.2). Baselines either sacrifice coherence for diversity (Typical) or sacrifice diversity for coherence (CD). DAMEON is the only method achieving near-human levels on all axes.

- **Strong empirical generalizability across domains and model scales.** Consistent gains on both Wikipedia and News, and with both GPT-2 XL (1.5B) and OPT-6.7B, demonstrating robustness.

- **Human evaluation validates practical quality.** Pairwise comparisons (Table 2) show statistically significant wins (p<0.005) over CD, CS, Nucleus, and Typical on fluency, coherence, and informativeness.

- **Ablation studies are thorough and informative.** Table 3 validates each metric constraint's individual contribution; Figure 2 shows the method dominates the coherence-diversity Pareto frontier; Figure 3 shows improvements with more SIR candidates M.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
None.

### Minor

- **SIR weights are inconsistent with the temperature-modulated proposal (Minor, not Fatal).** Algorithm 2 proposes from p_θ^τ (τ<1) but uses importance weights w_i = exp(−E_μ(x)), omitting the correction factor p_θ^{1−τ}(x) that would be required for the empirical SIR distribution to converge to the exact target p_{θ,μ}(·|prefix) ∝ p_θ(·|prefix)exp(−E_μ). The paper separately states (line 291–293) that SIR with p_θ recovers the exact distribution as M→∞ and then adds that "in practice" it uses p_θ^τ — but the algorithm box implements only the temperature-modulated version without adjusting the weights. This creates a technical gap between the theoretical claim and the implemented procedure.

  This is **not fatal** for three reasons: (i) With τ∈[0.97, 0.99], the missing factor p_θ^{0.01–0.03} compresses log-probability variation across sequences by over two orders of magnitude, making the approximation error small; (ii) the ablation study at τ=1.0 (Figure 3) validates that the core approach converges correctly without temperature modulation; (iii) the strong empirical results stand independently of exact SIR correctness. The paper should either include the correction factor or acknowledge that temperature-modulated SIR is an approximation and bound its error.

- **Proof sketch for Proposition 2 is terse on one step (Trivial/Minor).** The proof claims "there exists some α'<0 such that p_α' is a probability density function" (i.e., non-negative) without justification. In the discrete text space (finite vocabulary, bounded sequence length), this existence follows because the ratio p_d(x)/q_opt(x) has a finite maximum over a finite set — so choosing |α'| small enough suffices. The reviewer's objection about "infinite support" does not apply to this setting, but the proof sketch would benefit from noting the finite-space argument.

- **Limited discussion of coefficient estimation properties (Minor).** The paper estimates μ by minimizing RMSRE between importance-sampled expectations and targets, which is a valid approach for finding Lagrange multipliers. However, the discussion of uniqueness, convergence guarantees, sensitivity of the decoding distribution to perturbations in μ, and the actual RMSRE achieved on the development set is limited. This does not invalidate the method but would strengthen the paper.

### Trivial

- The perplexity improvement (e.g., 23.1→22.0 for GPT-2 XL on Wikipedia) is modest (~5% relative). While consistent with theory and a useful sanity check, its practical significance should not be overstated.

## Removed Points

- **Reviewer's claim that the Proposition 2 proof is fundamentally unsupported / the existence of α'<0 is a structural error.** This claim is removed because it does not apply to the discrete (finite-vocabulary, bounded-length) text space assumed throughout the paper. The existence of a uniform bound follows from finiteness of the space. The proof sketch is terse but the underlying claim is correct.

- **Reviewer's claim that the coefficient estimation is "a moment-matching heuristic, not a direct consequence of the constrained optimization problem."** This is removed because the procedure is a standard approach: estimate E_{p_{θ,μ}}[f] via WIS (which is consistent because p_{θ,μ} ∝ p_θ exp(−E_μ)), then adjust μ to satisfy the constraints. The Lagrange multipliers in an exponential-family EBM *are* found by matching expectations. This is directly connected to the optimization problem.

- **Reviewer's "Other Observations" about discussing metric collinearity, identifiability, and well-behavedness of the energy function.** These are moved to Nice-to-Haves as they are reasonable suggestions for improvement but do not constitute weaknesses — the paper's scope is appropriately focused.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting point that emerges across the reviews is the tension between the clean theoretical framework (constrained reverse-KL minimization → EBM) and the practical implementation details (temperature-modulated SIR, moment-matching coefficient estimation). The paper's core contribution — the constrained optimization formulation and its analytical solution — is elegant and principled. The practical approximations (SIR temperature, WIS-based coefficient estimation) are standard techniques that work well empirically, but the paper could more clearly delineate which claims are proven (the perplexity improvement of the exact solution) versus which are empirically validated (the implemented procedure performs well). This distinction is important for future work: follow-up efforts could focus on sampling more faithfully from the exact EBM or bounding the approximation error of the temperature-modulated SIR.

## Suggestions

- **Fix the SIR weights:** Either (a) include the correction factor p_θ^{1−τ}(x) in the importance weights when using a temperature-modulated proposal, or (b) if the temperature is kept as a practical approximation, explicitly acknowledge that Algorithm 2 targets an approximate distribution and discuss the approximation error (e.g., by noting that for τ close to 1, the missing factor is near-constant across samples).
- **Strengthen the proof sketch:** Add a brief note that the existence of α'<0 follows from the finiteness of the discrete text space, making the argument rigorous.
- **Report converged RMSRE:** Include the actual RMSRE values achieved on the development set to give readers a sense of how well the constraints are satisfied.
- **Add sensitivity analysis:** Show how the decoding distribution changes when the estimated μ is perturbed.

## Score and Decision

This paper makes a novel and principled contribution to the decoding literature. The constrained-optimization formulation, the analytical EBM solution, and the theoretical perplexity guarantee are genuine advances over heuristic truncation and contrastive methods. The empirical validation is thorough, covering two domains, two model scales, nine automatic metrics, MAUVE, and human evaluation. The main technical issues (the SIR weight inconsistency and the terse proof step) are fixable and do not undermine the core claims — the empirical results are robust, and the theoretical framing remains insightful. The paper would benefit from addressing these issues in a revision, but in its current form it already represents a substantial contribution.
