## Summary

This paper introduces MetaMath, a family of LLMs fine-tuned on MetaMathQA — an augmented dataset constructed by "bootstrapping" mathematical questions from GSM8K and MATH using GPT-3.5-Turbo. The augmentation covers four types: answer augmentation (AnsAug, generating multiple reasoning paths via rejection sampling), forward rephrasing, and two backward reasoning methods (Self-Verification and FOBAR, which mask a number in the question and ask the model to recover it given the answer). MetaMath models (7B, 13B, 70B) achieve SOTA results among open-source models at the time, with MetaMath-7B reaching 66.5% on GSM8K and 19.8% on MATH, substantially outperforming prior open-source models.

## Strengths

- **Empirically strong and clean main results.** MetaMath achieves large and unambiguous improvements over prior open-source models at every scale (7B, 13B, 70B). MetaMath-7B outperforms WizardMath-7B by +11.6% on GSM8K and +9.1% on MATH. MetaMath-70B surpasses GPT-3.5-Turbo on GSM8K (82.3% vs. 80.8%). These gains are clearly presented in Table 2 and are the paper's central claim.

- **Novel application of backward reasoning as data augmentation.** Prior work (Weng et al., Jiang et al.) used backward reasoning only at inference time for verification. This paper is the first to use SV and FOBAR to *generate training data*, turning backward reasoning into a data augmentation strategy. The ablation (Table 4) shows that adding SV and FOBAR to AnsAug+Rephrasing raises GSM8K accuracy from 60.6% to 64.4% and MATH from 15.3% to 17.7% (7B model trained on GSM8K data).

- **The fixed-20K diversity experiment cleanly isolates the effect of augmentation type.** The paper shows that at a fixed data volume (20K new samples), adding AnsAug data yields only 0.1% gain, Rephrasing yields 0.4%, FOBAR yields 2.3%, and SV yields 2.6%. This directly controls for data quantity and provides evidence that backward reasoning data is more beneficial than additional forward-reasoning or rephrased data of the same size.

- **Comprehensive analysis suite.** Beyond the main results, the paper includes: ablation of each augmentation component (Table 4), a diversity gain analysis with a submodularity-based metric (Figure 6), a study of backward reasoning ability on a new GSM8K-Backward test set (Figure 7), an error analysis by question length (Figure 8), and a surprising finding that merging RFT data into MetaMathQA hurts performance (Figure 9). This is a thorough evaluation for a data-augmentation paper.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

### Major

### Minor

1. **The perplexity analysis (Figure 5 / Section 5.1) is poorly defined and unconvincing.** The paper compares perplexity on "answer-only data," "GSM8K CoT," and MetaMathQA, but neither "answer-only data" nor "GSM8K CoT" is defined concretely. The connection to TinyStories is anecdotal. The lower perplexity of MetaMathQA could simply reflect that the dataset contains many paraphrases and structurally similar questions, making conditional distributions easier to predict — an artifact of the augmentation method rather than evidence of superior "simplicity" as an activator of latent knowledge. This analysis does not meaningfully strengthen the paper and should either be carefully redesigned (e.g., with strictly controlled dataset sizes and content) or removed.

2. **The diversity-accuracy correlation (Pearson = 0.972) is reported with unwarranted precision.** This correlation is computed across only four data points (one per augmentation type: AnsAug, Rephrasing, FOBAR, SV). A Pearson coefficient with p-value on n=4 is not statistically meaningful. The qualitative trend is informative and supports the paper's claims, but reporting the precise number (0.972) overstates the evidence. This should be presented as a suggestive trend rather than a rigorous correlation.

3. **No variation of the teacher model.** Every augmented question — AnsAug, rephrasing, SV, and FOBAR — is generated by GPT-3.5-Turbo. The paper does not experiment with other teacher models (e.g., LLaMA-2-70B, GPT-4, or a different commercial model). This makes it impossible to tell whether the benefits transfer to other teacher distributions or are specific to GPT-3.5-Turbo's output characteristics. A small-scale replication with a different teacher would strengthen generality claims.

4. **The RFT merging result (Figure 9) is presented without analysis.** The observation that adding RFT data to MetaMathQA hurts performance is interesting, but the paper merely notes it "may not be beneficial" without investigating why. Possible explanations (different answer distributions, noise patterns, crowding out useful diversity) are not explored. This limits the insight that could be drawn from a non-obvious finding.

5. **No confidence intervals or statistical significance reported.** The main results and ablations are reported as point estimates. Given that GSM8K has 1,319 test samples and MATH has 5,000, differences of 1-2 percentage points may not be significant. Reporting confidence intervals (e.g., via bootstrap) would be particularly helpful for the ablation experiments in Table 4.

6. **Limited evaluation benchmarks.** The paper evaluates only on GSM8K and MATH. While these are the two standard benchmarks, evaluating on additional held-out reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AQuA, SVAMP, MultiArith) would increase confidence that the gains generalize beyond the specific distribution the model was trained on. The cross-dataset transfer experiments (GSM8K→MATH and MATH→GSM8K) partially address this, but extra benchmarks would strengthen the case.

### Trivial

- The paper does not release the exact prompts used for rephrasing, SV, and FOBAR generation. While the method is described conceptually, the specific wording of prompts can affect output quality, and omitting them limits precise replication. The authors state they will release the dataset and code; including prompts in the release would resolve this.

- The "bootstrapping" framing is slightly overstated. The paper is transparent about using GPT-3.5-Turbo for all data generation (line 366-368), but calling this "bootstrapping" could imply a more self-contained process. This is a framing preference rather than a factual error and does not affect the paper's empirical contribution.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A small-scale experiment varying the teacher model (e.g., using LLaMA-2-70B or a different API model) to test whether the augmentation pipeline's benefits transfer.
- Evaluation on additional held-out math reasoning benchmarks (AQuA, SVAMP, MultiArith) to test generalization beyond the training distribution.
- A controlled ablation where AnsAug+Rephrasing data is scaled to the same total size as the full MetaMathQA (395K) before comparing, as a complement to the already-informative fixed-20K experiment.
- An analysis of why RFT data hurts MetaMathQA performance — e.g., examining answer distribution overlap, noise patterns, or diversity saturation.
