## Summary

KOSMOS-2 extends the KOSMOS-1 multimodal large language model with grounding and referring capabilities. The key technical contribution is a "hyperlink" data format that represents text-span-to-bounding-box associations as location tokens within a causal language modeling framework, requiring no architectural changes. The authors construct GrIT, a large-scale dataset of ~91M grounded image-text pairs, by using GLIP to automatically annotate noun phrases and referring expressions from LAION-2B and COYO-700M captions. KOSMOS-2 achieves impressive zero-shot performance on phrase grounding (78.7 R@1 on Flickr30k) and referring expression comprehension (61.65 on RefCOCOg), significantly outperforming prior zero-shot methods, while maintaining competitive results on standard perception-language and language tasks.

## Strengths

- **Novel hyperlink data format for grounding in MLLMs.** The paper introduces a clean, architecture-agnostic method for representing bounding boxes as discrete location tokens and linking them to text spans via a markdown-inspired format (`<p>text</p><box><loc1><loc2></box>`). This enables grounding as a next-token prediction task without special-purpose detectors, object queries, or architectural modifications. The approach is conceptually simple and reusable.

- **Large-scale grounded image-text dataset (GrIT).** The paper constructs GrIT with ~91M images, 115M text spans, and 137M bounding boxes — orders of magnitude larger than existing grounding datasets (e.g., RefCOCOg has 27K images). Table 1 clearly demonstrates this scale difference. The automated pipeline using spaCy and GLIP is described in sufficient detail for reproduction, and a subset is publicly released.

- **Strong zero-shot grounding performance that far exceeds prior zero-shot methods.** On Flickr30k phrase grounding, KOSMOS-2 achieves 78.7 R@1 (test), versus GRILL's 18.9 — a 59.8-point gap. On RefCOCOg referring expression comprehension, it achieves 61.65 accuracy versus GRILL's 47.5. These results demonstrate that grounding can be learned as a foundation capability of an MLLM without task-specific fine-tuning.

- **Zero-shot referring expression generation exceeding a fine-tuned baseline.** On RefCOCOg, KOSMOS-2's zero-shot CIDEr (60.3) surpasses the fine-tuned SLR model (59.2), with few-shot examples further improving performance (62.3). This shows the model can generate unambiguous descriptions for image regions without dedicated training.

- **Preservation of existing MLLM capabilities.** KOSMOS-2 maintains competitive performance on perception-language tasks (Flickr30k captioning: 66.7 vs. 65.2 CIDEr for KOSMOS-1) and language understanding benchmarks, demonstrating that grounding can be integrated without degrading other capabilities.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The evaluation of zero-shot grounding is weakened by GLIP's training data overlap with the evaluation benchmarks.** GrIT is constructed using GLIP (a pretrained grounding model) to generate bounding boxes. GLIP was trained on several datasets that include Flickr30k Entities, RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg — exactly the benchmarks used for evaluation in Sections 4.1 and 4.2 (Tables 1 and 2). Consequently, the bounding boxes in GrIT are not independent of these evaluation sets; they are generated by a model that has seen those datasets during supervised training. When KOSMOS-2 is evaluated zero-shot on the same benchmarks, the results may partially reflect reproducing patterns already present in the training signal from GrIT, rather than a clean test of generalization to unseen grounding scenarios. This is most acute for referring expression comprehension (Table 2): KOSMOS-2 achieves ~52% on RefCOCO val while supervised models exceed 80%. The paper frames this as "promising zero-shot performance," but given the data provenance this characterization overstates the evidence for generalization. The paper does not acknowledge this limitation or discuss the overlap. **This does not invalidate the core contributions** (the hyperlink format, the GrIT dataset, and the training approach remain valid), but it substantially weakens the evidential support for the claim that KOSMOS-2 has acquired a *general* grounding capability. At minimum, the paper should explicitly acknowledge this limitation and either provide an out-of-distribution evaluation on a benchmark GLIP has never seen, or substantially downscale the zero-shot claims.

### Minor

- **The near-identical R@1, R@5, and R@10 on phrase grounding (77.8, 79.2, 79.3 on Flickr30k val) indicates the model almost always produces a single bounding box per prompt, capping performance at single-box accuracy.** The paper acknowledges this and frames it positively ("no need for post-processing redundant locations," lines 300–302), and this is a reasonable characterization of a design trade-off. However, it also means the model cannot express uncertainty or propose multiple plausible locations for ambiguous phrases — a capability that MDETR (R@1=83.6, R@5=93.4, R@10=95.1) demonstrates. The gap to supervised methods is larger than R@1 alone suggests because those methods gain substantially from multiple candidates. This is a limitation of the autoregressive single-hypothesis decoding strategy and is worth discussing as a direction for future work.

- **The data filtering pipeline's impact on coverage and bias is not analyzed.** The pipeline applies a GLIP confidence threshold of 0.65, discards captions with no retained boxes, and eliminates noun chunks subsumed by longer referring expressions (Section 2). These decisions could systematically filter out small objects, occluded objects, and rare categories. The paper does not analyze what fraction of noun chunks is discarded, the distribution of confidence scores, object sizes, or category frequencies in GrIT compared to standard grounding benchmarks. Without this analysis, readers cannot assess potential distribution shift between training data and evaluation scenarios.

- **The referring expression generation results show only modest improvement from zero-shot to few-shot (60.3 → 62.3 CIDEr), and the absence of a non-grounded MLLM baseline makes it difficult to isolate the contribution of grounding training.** KOSMOS-2's zero-shot CIDEr (60.3) exceeds the fine-tuned SLR (59.2), which is a strong result. However, the few-shot gain of only ~2 CIDEr points is small, and without a comparison to KOSMOS-1 or another non-grounded MLLM on the same task, it is unclear how much of the generation ability comes from the grounding training versus the underlying language model's inherent referring ability.

- **The paper does not clearly show whether instruction tuning improves grounding performance.** Instruction tuning is described (Section 3.3), but all main grounding, perception-language, and language results are reported "without instruction tuning." It would be helpful to state explicitly whether instruction tuning was applied to the grounding evaluations and, if not, whether it improves grounding performance.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting tension revealed by combining the reviews is between the paper's framing of its "zero-shot" results as evidence of a learned general grounding capability and the reality that the training data inherits biases from a model (GLIP) that was itself trained on the evaluation benchmarks. This tension is common in the era of large pretrained models used as data annotators: the distinction between "zero-shot" and "partially supervised" becomes blurry when the annotator model has seen the test data. The paper's hyperlink format and GrIT dataset remain valuable contributions independent of this concern, but the evaluative evidence for generalization is weaker than claimed. This points to a broader need in the field for out-of-distribution evaluation protocols when training data is generated by pretrained models with known training distributions.

## Suggestions

1. **Acknowledge the GLIP overlap explicitly** and either provide an out-of-distribution grounding evaluation or recalibrate the claims about zero-shot generalization. Even a small-scale human-annotated dataset with novel object compositions would significantly strengthen the paper.

3. **Clarify the instruction tuning setup**: state explicitly which results use the instruction-tuned version and whether grounding improves with instruction tuning.
4. **Discuss the single-box limitation** as a design trade-off and consider sampling-based decoding as future work.
