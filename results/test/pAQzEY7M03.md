## Summary

The paper proposes FakeShield, the first multi-modal large language model framework for explainable image forgery detection and localization (e-IFDL). It introduces a new task requiring models to output not only tamper detection results and masks but also textual explanations of the judgment basis. The framework comprises: (1) a Domain Tag Generator that predicts the tampering category to mitigate domain conflicts, (2) a DTE-FDM module that uses an M-LLM for detection and explanation, and (3) an MFLM module that leverages the textual explanation to guide SAM-based segmentation. The authors also construct MMTD-Set, a multi-modal dataset augmenting existing IFDL benchmarks with GPT-4o-generated textual descriptions. Experiments across 7+ benchmarks spanning PhotoShop, DeepFake, and AIGC-Editing tampering show strong detection performance and competitive localization.

## Strengths

1. **First framework for explainable IFDL (e-IFDL) with a clear task formulation.** The paper defines a novel task requiring joint output of authenticity, mask, and textual explanation — a meaningful step beyond black-box IFDL methods. This task formulation is a genuine contribution that opens a new direction.

2. **Construction of MMTD-Set with tamper-type-specific prompting.** Section 3.1 details how GPT-4o is guided via tailored prompts to produce "image-mask-description" triplets that capture characteristic artifacts of each tampering type (pixel-level edge artifacts for Photoshop, facial blurring for DeepFake, texture/text anomalies for AIGC-Editing). This dataset enables supervised fine-tuning of M-LLMs for forgery analysis.

3. **Domain Tag Generator effectively mitigates domain conflicts.** The ablation (Table 5/ablation\_DTG\_table) shows clear degradation when DTG is removed: detection ACC on DeepFake drops from 0.98→0.89 and on AIGC-Editing from 0.93→0.72. This provides strong evidence that domain-tag conditioning alleviates distribution differences across tampering types.

4. **Strong detection performance across diverse tampering types with fair baselines.** Table 1 shows FakeShield achieves the highest ACC/F1 on all 7 tested benchmarks. The IFDL baselines (SPAN, ManTraNet, OSN, HiFi-Net, PSCC-Net, CAT-Net, MVSS-Net) are all retrained on the same MMTD-Set training data, making these comparisons fair. Margins are substantial (e.g., AIGC-Editing ACC 0.98 vs. 0.82 for CAT-Net).

5. **Decoupled architecture design with supporting evidence.** The paper identifies that joint training of detection (language reasoning) and localization (visual segmentation) creates optimization interference. The ablation in Figure 5 (ablation2) — comparing the full framework against a variant that feeds everything into a single module — shows the decoupled design consistently outperforms across 25 training epochs.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Explanation quality comparison (Table 5) is confounded by training regime.** FakeShield is fine-tuned on MMTD-Set, while the comparison M-LLMs (GPT-4o, LLaVA-v1.6-34B, InternVL2-26B, Qwen2-VL-7B) are evaluated zero-shot without any fine-tuning. The paper explicitly acknowledges these are "pre-trained M-LLMs" (Section 4.3), but the framing ("our approach consistently achieves the best performance") implies architectural superiority when the result may simply reflect training distribution alignment. Since the ground-truth explanations were themselves generated by GPT-4o on the same data distribution, the CSS metric measures how well FakeShield mimics the training data's explanation style — not whether it produces genuinely better explanations. A fair comparison would require either (a) fine-tuning the comparison M-LLMs on MMTD-Set, or (b) evaluating all models zero-shot on a held-out explanation task. *Impact:* This weakens but does not invalidate the paper, because the core contribution (the e-IFDL framework + detection/localization) does not rest on this one comparison.

2. **Self-constructed AIGC-Editing data lacks transparency.** The paper repeatedly references "self-constructed AIGC-Editing tampered data" (Section 3.1, Section 4.1) for both training and testing, but provides no details about its construction: which generative models were used, what prompts/parameters, which source images, resolution, sample counts, or quality filtering. This is problematic because (a) FakeShield achieves 0.98 ACC / 0.99 F1 on AIGC-Editing vs. 0.82/0.81 for the second-best method CAT-Net — an unusually large gap that cannot be assessed without understanding the test data, and (b) the comparison IFDL methods were also retrained on MMTD-Set (which includes this data), yet perform much worse, raising questions about whether the self-constructed data has distributional alignment with FakeShield's architecture. *Impact:* These results are unverifiable and would be treated skeptically by the community without full documentation.

### Minor

3. **Localization results on harder datasets are modest, and the "precise localization" framing is overclaimed.** On Korus (IoU 0.17), NIST (IoU 0.32), and AIGC-Editing (IoU 0.18), FakeShield's performance is low in absolute terms. While the baselines are similarly weak (e.g., Korus best is PSCC-Net at 0.15 IoU), the paper's language — "precise localization," "accurately delineate modified regions" — overstates the capability on these datasets. The qualitative examples in Figure 3 appear cherry-picked from easier cases. The paper would benefit from an explicit limitations discussion acknowledging that the method excels on semantically meaningful forgeries but struggles with subtle or non-semantic tampering.

4. **The ablation of the LLM role conflates decoupling with model capacity removal.** The ablation (Figure 5) removes the entire 13B LLM from DTE-FDM, feeding tokens directly into the smaller TCM. The resulting performance drop could be due to the loss of 13B parameters, different training dynamics, or the TCM's limited language generation ability — not necessarily the decoupling strategy per se. A cleaner test of decoupling would keep the LLM in the detection path and add a joint training objective. As presented, this ablation demonstrates that the full architecture is better than a much smaller variant, but does not cleanly isolate the benefit of decoupling.

5. **Domain Tag Generator accuracy is not reported.** The DTG is a classifier that predicts the tampering category (PhotoShop, DeepFake, AIGC-Editing). If it makes errors, the wrong domain tag could mislead the LLM. No confusion matrix or per-class accuracy is provided, making it impossible to assess how often the model receives correct guidance.

6. **Detection threshold from text output is unspecified.** The paper states "a default threshold of 0.5 is applied" for detection (Section 4.1), but FakeShield's output is textual — the LLM generates natural language about authenticity. How is this text parsed into a binary detection decision, and how is a numerical threshold applied to a text output? This is critical for reproducibility and is never explained.

7. **No detection accuracy reported separately for authentic (untampered) images.** The paper reports ACC and F1 on test sets that mix tampered and authentic images, but does not break down false positive rates on authentic-only images. Since the task includes binary classification, this matters for practical deployment.

### Trivial

8. **Robustness study is narrow in scope.** Only tested on CASIA1+ with mild degradations (JPEG 70/80, Gaussian σ=5/10). While the results are informative, the single-dataset scope limits the strength of the "robustness" claim.

9. **No description examples of authentic images.** Section 3.1 mentions that GPT-4o generates descriptions for authentic images too, but no examples or analysis of these descriptions are provided.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface a tension between two of the paper's claimed contributions: the DTG mechanism alleviates domain conflicts *within the detection task*, while the decoupled architecture separates language-based detection from vision-based localization. However, the DTG itself could be seen as a form of soft decoupling — it provides a categorical signal that tells the model which detection strategy to adopt per domain. The ablation removing DTG hurt detection substantially more than localization, suggesting the domain tag primarily benefits the language reasoning path. This interplay between domain conditioning and task decoupling is underexplored both in the paper and the reviews, and could be a fruitful direction for future work.
