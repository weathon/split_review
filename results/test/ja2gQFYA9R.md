## Summary

This paper proposes DSO-Net, a framework for open-vocabulary text-to-motion generation that operates in two coupled stages: (1) **Textual Decomposition**, which converts raw motion text into atomic motion texts (simple body-part movements over short time periods) via a fine-grained description conversion algorithm and an LLM, and (2) **Sub-motion-space Scattering**, which learns the compositional process from atomic motions to target motions via a Text-Motion Alignment (TMA) module and a Compositional Feature Fusion (CFF) module. The core thesis is that by using atomic motions as intermediate representations and scattering the learned sub-motion-space, the model converts extrapolation of out-of-distribution motions into interpolation. The method achieves strong quantitative results on out-domain datasets (Idea400, Mixamo) while remaining competitive on in-domain HumanML3D.

## Strengths

- **Novel two-stage framework addressing the full-mapping problem directly**: The paper clearly motivates why existing paradigms (simple mapping, CLIP-based alignment, pretrain-then-finetune) are fundamentally limited to sub-text-space↔sub-motion-space mappings (Section 1, Figure 1). Introducing atomic motions as an intermediate representation and decomposing the problem into textual decomposition + sub-motion-space scattering is a principled and well-motivated departure from prior work. The design is grounded in the observation that human motion understanding naturally partitions into simple body-part movements.

- **Strong quantitative results on out-domain datasets**: The method achieves state-of-the-art or competitive results across all three test settings. The gains on out-domain datasets are particularly notable, demonstrating that the framework generalizes to open-vocabulary texts better than prior methods.

- **Fine-grained description conversion algorithm as a concrete technical contribution**: Section 3.1 details a four-step pipeline (Pose Extraction, Pose Aggregation, Clip Aggregation, Description Conversion) that converts raw 3D motion into fine-grained text descriptions using velocity, magnitude, and behavior categories. This rule-based algorithm, combined with an LLM, is designed to produce atomic texts consistent with actual motion, addressing the known issue of LLM hallucination in motion description.

- **Systematic ablation of TMA and CFF components**: Table 2 provides ablation isolating the contributions of TMA and CFF, showing that TMA nearly doubles R-Top3 and CFF adds meaningful gains on top of TMA (11% vs. 3% without TMA), supporting the claim that text-motion alignment is important for the compositional learning.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None. The paper makes a genuine contribution and the core results are not invalidated.

### Major

- **The textual decomposition step — central to the paper's contribution — is never independently evaluated.** The entire pipeline depends on the quality of atomic motion texts obtained via the fine-grained conversion algorithm and LLM, yet there is no experiment isolating the effect of decomposition itself. The ablation study (Table 2) compares conditions that *all* include decomposition (baseline, +CFF, +TMA, +TMA+CFF), so the contribution of decomposition is never separated from the rest of the pipeline. Key questions are unanswered: How often does the LLM produce atomic texts consistent with the motion? What is the LLM? What prompt template and how many in-context examples are used? Without isolating decomposition (e.g., comparing the full pipeline against a version that skips decomposition and uses only raw text features, holding all other components constant), the reader cannot assess whether the improvements come from the decomposition itself or from the additional conditioning signal provided by atomic features. This is the most critical missing analysis in the paper.

- **The "scattering" claim — that CFF converts extrapolation into interpolation by geometrically scattering the sub-motion-space — lacks direct evidence.** The paper asserts this as a structural claim about the learned representation space, but provides no analysis of that space. The ablation shows CFF improves FID and R-Precision, but this improvement is equally consistent with a simpler explanation: the atomic text features provide richer conditioning information to the generative model. The term "scattering" remains a metaphor; the paper never connects it to a measurable property of the model's behavior — e.g., by showing that motions generated by the full model are closer to convex combinations of atomic-motion clusters than those generated by baselines, or by visualizing the latent space with and without CFF. Without such evidence, the central conceptual contribution is asserted rather than demonstrated.

### Minor

- **The specific LLM used is never named.** The paper repeatedly refers to "a large language model (LLM)" without naming the model or version (GPT-4, LLaMA-2, etc.). Since LLM capabilities vary widely in following decomposition instructions, and the method's success may depend on this choice, this omission is a reproducibility issue.

- **Thresholds for the fine-grained description conversion algorithm are not reported.** Section 3.1 (Description Conversion) classifies movements as "significant" when |S_PDi| exceeds a threshold and "slowly" when V_PDi is below a threshold, but neither threshold value is given. This makes the algorithm difficult to reproduce or compare against.

- **The dataset used for TMA pretraining is not specified.** Section 3.2 states TMA is pretrained on "text-motion pair data" using InfoNCE loss, but does not state which dataset(s) were used. If it is the same HumanML3D data used for finetuning, TMA may not provide additional alignment beyond what finetuning itself would learn; if other data were used, that should be clarified.

- **Details on baseline evaluation are incomplete.** While the paper reports comparisons against several methods, it does not specify whether OMG and other baseline results were obtained from published numbers or re-implemented, or under what conditions (e.g., same pretraining data scale). The textual descriptions used as input for out-domain datasets (Idea400, Mixamo) are mentioned as "annotated textual descriptions" without further detail about their provenance.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface a deeper issue beyond the individual weaknesses: the paper makes two strong conceptual claims (decomposition as a bridge from full-text-space to full-atomic-text-space; scattering as a geometric transformation of the representation space) but only provides *system-level* evidence (end-to-end performance numbers). Neither claim is tested at the *component level* — decomposition quality is never measured, and the representation geometry is never analyzed. This mismatch between the nature of the claims (mechanistic/structural) and the evidence (performance-based) is the common thread connecting the two major weaknesses. The paper would be substantially strengthened by meeting each claim with commensurate evidence: human evaluation or a reconstruction task for decomposition, and latent-space analysis (e.g., effective rank, convex hull coverage, or nearest-neighbor interpolation analysis) for scattering.

## Suggestions

1. **Isolate the decomposition step**: Compare the full pipeline against a version that removes decomposition (using only raw text) while keeping TMA and CFF architecture intact (e.g., feeding the raw text feature in place of atomic text features). This would directly test whether decomposition provides unique value beyond richer conditioning.

2. **Provide geometric evidence for scattering**: Analyze the learned representation space. Compute whether the full model's latent representations or generated motions lie closer to convex combinations of training-set atomic-motion clusters than baseline representations. Alternatively, visualize latent spaces (e.g., via t-SNE/PCA) with and without CFF to show whether the subspace is actually "scattered."

3. **Specify the LLM, prompt template, and in-context learning setup**: Name the exact LLM model/version, provide the prompt template, and state the number and source of in-context examples used during inference.

4. **Report thresholds for the conversion algorithm**: Provide the specific threshold values used for |S_PDi| (for "significant") and V_PDi (for "slowly") classification.

5. **Specify the TMA pretraining dataset**: State clearly which dataset(s) were used for the contrastive pretraining of TMA.
