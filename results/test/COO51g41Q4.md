## Summary

This paper proposes SynergisTic pAtch pRuning (STAR), a patch-pruning method for Vision Transformers that fuses two sources of patch importance: (1) intra-layer importance obtained online from each layer's [CLS] attention map, and (2) inter-layer importance computed offline via Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP). The fusion is formulated as a KL-divergence centroid, yielding a closed-form weighted geometric mean. STAR also automates layer-wise retention rates using Average Cosine Similarity (ACS) between patches, controlled by a single hyperparameter ρ. Experiments on ImageNet across DeiT and LV-ViT variants show substantial throughput improvements (up to ~100% speedup) with minor accuracy loss, competitive with or exceeding prior methods.

## Strengths

1. **Demonstrably effective accuracy/throughput trade-off**: On DeiT-Small, STAR achieves 4,080.6 images/s (83.9% speedup, 43.5% FLOPs reduction) with only 0.4% Top-1 accuracy drop; the pruned model is 60%+ faster than the much smaller unpruned DeiT-Tiny while being 7.0% more accurate (Section 6.1, Table 1). These numbers directly support the paper's central claim.

2. **Ablative isolation of each component**: Table 2 cleanly separates intra-layer-only, inter-layer-only, fusion, and ACS-based rate selection (all before fine-tuning), attributing measurable gains to each design choice. Fusion improves Top-1 by +0.6% over intra-layer-only at the same FLOPs; ACS-based schedules outperform manual schedules by +0.3% Top-1 (Section 6.2, Table 2).

3. **Broad evaluation across models and baselines**: Experiments cover three DeiT sizes and two LV-ViT sizes, comparing against 12 prior methods (SCOP, PoWER, VTP, HVT, SAViT, IA-RED², EViT, PS, CP-ViT, VTC-LFC, ToMe). STAR consistently achieves competitive or better accuracy at similar or higher throughput (Table 1).

4. **Practical lightweight design**: The offline components (LRP-based inter-layer scores and ACS computation) are one-time costs on the training set. The online pruning adds only attention-map extraction, element-wise product fusion, and top-k selection — the throughput measurements include this overhead, yet STAR still achieves substantial speedups (Section 6.1).

### Minor

1. **Baseline throughput comparisons could be more explicit**: The paper's Table 1 uses a marker (*) to indicate results "from the corresponding articles" and states that throughputs are measured on an A100 GPU with batch size 128 in FP32 (line 118). However, the paper never explicitly states "we reran baselines without the * marker under identical conditions." A reader must infer this from the marker system. While the experimental setup is described, an unambiguous statement about which baselines were rerun and which were taken from other papers would eliminate any ambiguity in the speedup comparisons. This does not invalidate the results (the marker system is clear in principle) but leaves an unnecessary opening for doubt.

2. **ACS-based retention rate selection is a heuristic with limited validation**: The paper maps ACS to retention rates via a linear model determined by two boundary conditions (p₁=1, p_L=ρ). While the monotonicity properties are nice, no rationale is given for why the relationship should be linear rather than, e.g., exponential or threshold-based, and the paper does not compare against other automatic rate-selection approaches (e.g., sparsity-promoting regularizers as in Chen et al. 2023). The sensitivity of the rates to the 500-image sample used for ACS computation is not discussed. The method works empirically, but the design choice is undersupported.

3. **The inter-layer component is static and its marginal value over a simpler baseline is not isolated**: The inter-layer scores are computed offline on the training set and applied to all test inputs identically. The ablation (Table 2) shows that fusion (intra + inter) outperforms intra-only, but the paper does not include an ablation that replaces the LRP-based inter-layer scores with a uniform distribution (or a constant prior). This would quantify how much of the benefit comes from the specific LRP-derived values versus having any static smoothing prior at all.

4. **KL-divergence framing overstates a simple operation**: The fusion mechanism (Eq. 4) is a weighted element-wise product of normalized scores — a textbook solution for the KL-divergence centroid of two pmfs. Presenting this as a minimization problem with a closed-form solution is mathematically correct but inflates what is ultimately a straightforward combination. The paper would lose nothing and gain clarity by stating the fusion rule directly.

5. **Limited discussion of the hard distillation fine-tuning protocol relative to baselines**: STAR fine-tunes with hard distillation from the original model (line 118). It is unclear which baselines also use distillation during fine-tuning and which do not. If STAR uses distillation and some baselines do not, this could partially explain accuracy advantages independently of the pruning decisions.

6. **α hyperparameter selection has limited justification**: The paper uses α=0.15 for DeiT and α=0.3 for LV-ViT (line 158) but provides no sensitivity analysis or principled selection method for this parameter.

### Trivial
- The abstract reports DeiT-Small throughput as 4,256 images/s (abstract, line 4) while the main text reports 4,080.6 images/s (line 129) — a minor numerical discrepancy.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation replacing the LRP-based inter-layer scores with a uniform distribution to isolate their specific contribution.
- A sensitivity analysis showing how accuracy and FLOPs vary with ρ on the validation set, demonstrating the tunability of the single-parameter control.
- A breakdown of the online pruning overhead as a percentage of total inference time.
- Results for a-STAR on additional models beyond DeiT-Small (currently only shown for that model in the main text).

## Removed Points
- **"Unclear experimental comparisons — no statement about which baselines were rerun"**: The paper does state that throughputs are measured on A100 with batch 128/FP32 (line 118) and uses a marker system (*) to identify results from other papers (line 133). The reviewer's claim that "many entries lack this marker without explanation" cannot be verified from the text (the table is an image), and the marker convention is explicitly defined. The core concern about whether entries without * were rerun by the authors is reasonable and kept above (Minor #1), but the framing as a "critical flaw" that makes comparisons "unreliable" overstates the issue given the paper's transparency about the marker system and hardware conditions.

## Suggestions

1. Add an explicit sentence in Section 6: "We reran all baselines without the * marker on the same hardware (A100, batch 128, FP32) using the authors' public code where available." This would close the main ambiguity.
2. Include an ablation replacing inter-layer scores with a uniform distribution to quantify the specific contribution of LRP-based scores.
3. Present the fusion formula directly as a weighted geometric mean (Eq. 4 already is this) and reduce the KL-derivation to a footnote or reference — this would tighten the presentation.
4. Add a brief sensitivity study for the linear ACS-to-retention-rate mapping and the α parameter.

## Score and Decision

**Originality**: The combination of offline LRP-based inter-layer scores with online attention-based intra-layer scores is a reasonable synthesis of existing techniques rather than a fundamentally new idea. The ACS-based rate selection is a practical heuristic. The contribution is incremental but practically useful.

**Importance**: Efficient ViT inference is an active and important problem. A method that achieves strong accuracy/throughput trade-offs with lightweight overhead is of clear practical interest to the community.

**Claims**: The central claims (STAR achieves high throughput with minimal accuracy loss) are supported by the evidence. The marker system and hardware description provide reasonable transparency for the comparisons.

**Soundness**: The experimental methodology is generally sound. The ablations cleanly isolate components. The main weakness is the lack of an unambiguous statement about baseline reproduction, which creates minor uncertainty but does not invalidate the results.

**Clarity**: The paper is well-structured and clearly written. The KL-divergence framing is slightly over-engineered for a simple product operation.

**Value**: The method is practical and the results are competitive. The automatic retention rate selection replaces manual tuning, which is a genuine practical improvement.

The paper presents a solid empirical contribution — a well-engineered patch-pruning method with thorough ablations and competitive results. The weaknesses are minor: the baseline comparison could be more explicitly documented, the ACS heuristic could be better validated, and an additional ablation for the inter-layer component would strengthen the paper. None of these threaten the core empirical findings. The paper would benefit from the suggested clarifications but is acceptable as a useful applied contribution in its current form.
