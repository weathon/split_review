## Summary

This paper proposes Distributional Edge Layouts (DELs), a preprocessing method that runs multiple force-directed graph layout algorithms (Fruchterman–Reingold and Kamada–Kawai) from random initializations, converts the resulting node positions into per-edge distance features, and feeds these edge features into standard GNNs (GAT, Graph Transformer, GPS). The core empirical claim is that this multi-layout edge feature augmentation consistently improves performance across six TUDatasets. The method is backbone-agnostic and depends only on the adjacency matrix.

## Strengths

- **Consistent empirical improvement across multiple GNN backbones and datasets.** On MUTAG, GAT+DEL-F improves over plain GAT from 85.69% → 90.00%; GT+DEL-F from 84.02% → 91.52%; GPS(RWSE, DEL-F) reaches 93.34% vs. 91.94% for GPS(RWSE). On NCI1, GPS(RWSE, DEL-F) improves from 79.97% → 84.36%. These gains are meaningful and non-trivial. (Table 1)

- **Simple, flexible, backbone-agnostic design.** DEL is a pre-processing step that only needs the adjacency matrix; the resulting edge features can be plugged into any edge-aware GNN without architectural changes. The paper demonstrates this with three distinct backbones (GAT, Graph Transformer, GPS), all benefiting from DEL. (Sections 4.4–4.5)

- **Ablation studies provide practical design insights.** The paper systematically analyzes the effect of layout count (Table 4), layout dimensionality (Table 2), and steady-state convergence (Section 5.2). Performance generally improves with more layouts; higher dimensions are not strictly needed; convergence matters. These are useful for practitioners.

- **Conceptually motivated global edge feature.** Using force-directed layouts as edge features is a reasonable idea — it injects global structural information (derived from the full topology via energy minimization) that local message-passing alone would not capture. The use of *multiple* layouts to span the energy landscape is a sensible form of multi-view augmentation.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Theory–practice gap in the central "Boltzmann sampling via Langevin dynamics" framing.** The paper presents a formal Langevin dynamics update (Eq. 3) with noise injection (√α·ε_t) and temperature parameter α, claiming this "guarantees to sample P from ℙ(P)" (line 102–106). However, the implemented algorithms — Fruchterman–Reingold (Algorithm 1) and Kamada–Kawai — are purely deterministic force-equilibration procedures with **no injected noise, no temperature parameter, and no stochastic sampling component**. The paper acknowledges this only in passing ("an approximation of the gradient... is sufficient", line 109), but never justifies why dropping the noise term from Langevin dynamics still yields samples from a Boltzmann distribution, nor characterizes what distribution the deterministic local-minima ensemble actually follows. The abstract and conclusion (§6) continue to claim "DELs are generated by sampling topological layouts from a Boltzmann distribution" without qualification. This mismatch between the theoretical apparatus and the actual method is the paper's most significant weakness: the interesting theory (Eq. 1–3) is not realized, and the actual method (multiple deterministic local minima from random starts) is simpler than claimed. The empirical results stand on their own, but the framing is misleading.

- **Expressivity analysis is anecdotal, not systematic.** Section 4.6 supports the claim of "extra expressivity beyond 1-WL" with exactly one pair of 1-WL-indistinguishable graphs (Decalin vs. Bicyclopentyl). A single example does not constitute a systematic analysis, especially given the strong claim in the abstract that DEL "bring[s] extra expressivity on top of WL-test." The analysis uses Graph Total Weight (GTW) — a scalar summary — rather than showing that the *edge features* derived from multiple layouts actually enable a GNN to distinguish cases that 1-WL GNNs cannot. A rigorous analysis would either provide a formal separation result (e.g., identifying a class of graphs where DEL-equipped GNNs provably surpass 1-WL) or systematically test on established hard graph families (strongly regular graphs, EXP-class graphs, etc.).

### Minor

- **Experimental evaluation, while solid, falls short of fully supporting the "state-of-the-art" claim.** The baselines (GIN, GMT, GraphMAE, SEP-G, WL kernel, GPS variants) are reasonable and the consistent improvements are convincing. However, the paper does not include several competitive post-2022 methods that report results on these same TUDatasets, such as K-Clique GNN, CIN, or other expressive GNNs. The improvements over GPS(RWSE) — arguably the strongest baseline — are sometimes modest (e.g., +1.4% on MUTAG, +1.2% on PROTEINS). The SOTA claim needs more comprehensive comparison to be fully justified.

- **Cross-validation methodology is standard but could be more transparent.** The paper states it "followed the 10-fold cross-validation setting from previous work" (line 263), which is acceptable, but does not specify whether the same fold splits were used across all methods or describe how folds were generated. Several standard deviations are notably low (e.g., 0.15 on NCI1 for GPS(DEL-F), 0.12 on MUTAG for GT+DEL-K), which, while not impossible for stable runs with 10-fold CV × 5 seeds, would benefit from a brief clarification to rule out evaluation artifacts.

- **Scalability discussion is present but limited.** The paper correctly identifies O(|V|²) complexity for the repulsive-force computation in DEL-F and references a preprocessing-time table (§5.5). However, for real-world graphs with >10k nodes, quadratic cost in |V| is prohibitive. The paper mentions "advanced layout algorithms" as future work but does not discuss at what graph size the method becomes impractical, nor report actual wall-clock times for the datasets used (the preprocessing table is referenced but not present in the main text). This does not invalidate the contribution (most TUDataset graphs are small) but limits practical applicability claims.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A control experiment using random node positions (instead of random distances) would better isolate the contribution of energy-based layouts vs. simply having multiple per-edge features.
- Reporting sensitivity of downstream performance to the random seed used for layout initialization would strengthen the robustness story.
- The comparison to RWSE: since GPS(RWSE) already uses structural encodings, a clearer picture would come from ablating DEL against other edge-level encodings (e.g., shortest-path distances, edge angles) rather than only against random and MPNN baselines.

## Suggestions

1. **Realign the theoretical framing with what is actually implemented.** Either (a) implement proper Langevin dynamics with noise injection and temperature to match the Boltzmann-sampling story, or (b) remove the Langevin/Boltzmann apparatus and reframe DEL as a deterministic multi-view feature from force-directed layouts that samples multiple local minima via random initialization. Option (b) is simpler and would make the paper more honest without weakening its empirical contribution.

2. **Expand the expressivity analysis.** Demonstrate DEL's ability to distinguish additional 1-WL-indistinguishable graph families (e.g., strongly regular graphs with the same parameters, EXP-class graphs) using actual GNN performance — not just scalar GTW distributions.

3. **Scale back or qualify the "state-of-the-art" claim** unless additional strong baselines are added to the comparison. The consistent improvements are the paper's real strength; the SOTA label is unnecessary and invites valid criticism.

4. **Add wall-clock preprocessing times** for the datasets used and a brief discussion of at what graph size O(|V|²) becomes prohibitive.

5. **Clean up editorial artifacts** ("tianshu", "zxj", "DEF-F" vs. "DEL-F").

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a simple, empirically effective method with consistent improvements across multiple backbones. The core idea — using force-directed layouts from random initializations as multi-view edge features — has practical merit and is well-supported by the experiments. However, the paper significantly overclaims its theoretical framing (presenting Langevin dynamics and Boltzmann sampling that are not actually implemented) and its expressivity claims (supported by only one graph pair). These issues require substantive revisions to the presentation and positioning. On balance, the empirical contribution is real, but the overselling in its current form is a meaningful problem.
