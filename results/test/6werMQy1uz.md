## Summary

This paper introduces the Information Bazaar, an open-source simulated marketplace where LLM-powered agents buy and sell information on behalf of principals. The central mechanism to address the buyer's inspection paradox is that agents can inspect information provisionally and then "forget" it if they choose not to purchase. The paper presents microeconomic experiments characterizing LLM biases (positional bias, price-quality confusion), validates debate prompting as a debiasing technique, and demonstrates in macro-level simulations that inspection and higher budgets improve answer quality. The primary contribution is the simulation infrastructure and the behavioral findings about LLMs as economic agents.

## Strengths

1. **Novel open-source simulation environment for studying information markets with LLM agents.** The Information Bazaar is implemented in Python using the mesa library (Section 3), providing a concrete, reproducible testbed. This is a genuine infrastructure contribution for studying LLM economic behavior in a controlled setting with realistic dynamics (tenders, quotes, multi-round purchasing, recursive query trees).

2. **Systematic empirical characterization of LLM biases in economic decision-making.** Section 4.1 uncovers specific irrational behaviors: positional bias (Figure 3 shows all tested models exhibit order-dependent selection), price-quality confusion (Figure 2 shows higher pricing leads to more errors even when content is fungible), and non-linear price sensitivity in Llama 2 (Figure 4 shows a puzzling preference for mid-priced goods). These findings are concrete and reproducible.

3. **Empirical validation that inspection improves information valuation.** Table 1 shows inspection boosts gold-passage purchase rates by 18.34% (Llama 2) and 17.24% (GPT-4), and Figure 5 (right) shows that inspection leads to better answer quality per credit spent. This directly validates the paper's core mechanism within the simulation.

4. **Debate prompting as a practical debiasing technique.** Section 3.4 introduces debate prompting, and Section 4.1 shows it improves rational choice, especially for GPT-3.5 and Llama 2, which are weaker models. This is a methodologically useful finding for anyone building LLM-based agents that make economic decisions.

5. **Multi-faceted evaluation including human validation of the automated evaluator.** The paper validates GPT-4 as an evaluator against human judges on 50 samples (Figure 6b), showing human–GPT-4 agreement is comparable to human–human agreement. This methodological care strengthens trust in the automated evaluations used throughout.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Overclaiming regarding the "ability to forget" as a solution to the inspection paradox.** The abstract states agents "come equipped with the ability to forget" and this "significantly reduces the risk of unauthorized retention." The paper later writes that discarding non-essential information "ensur[es] no unauthorized retention" (line 20). These claims conflate the *simulation-enforced* clearing of context (the environment erases unpurchased content) with a property of the agent. The paper never acknowledges that in any real deployment, a buyer could modify their agent to retain inspected content, or that an LLM might memorize information even after context deletion. The simulation demonstrates the mechanism *works if enforced*, but the paper's language implies a stronger guarantee. A dedicated limitations section candidly discussing this trust/enforceability gap would substantially strengthen the paper. The experiments and behavioral findings remain valid within the simulation; the overclaim is in the framing of what has been "addressed."

### Minor

2. **No baseline comparing the marketplace against simpler alternatives.** The paper shows that inspection improves over metadata-only selection and that higher budgets improve quality, but never asks whether using the Information Bazaar produces better answers than simply querying the same LLM directly (from parametric knowledge) or using a straightforward RAG pipeline without the tender/quote/forgetting machinery. While the paper is primarily about *agent behavior in the marketplace* rather than SOTA answer quality (the authors' RQs are about feasibility and behavioral analysis), the abstract's emphasis on "enabling information flow" implicitly promises practical value. Adding this baseline would clarify whether the marketplace's value is in access control and multi-source synthesis rather than answer accuracy per se, and would not require new experiments at a different scale.

3. **Circularity in query generation and gold passage construction.** Queries are generated by passing each passage to Llama 2 and asking it to produce a query for which that passage is the answer (Section 3.4). The gold passage is therefore *by construction* the most relevant passage for the query. This setup is reasonable for testing whether LLMs can identify known-relevant content despite biases (the paper's actual goal), but it may inflate the apparent value of inspection and limit external validity. The paper would benefit from at least acknowledging this as a potential confound.

4. **Limited statistical reporting.** The inspection-quality results (Figure 5 right) show cumulative wins without error bars, counts, or confidence intervals. The model comparison (Figure 6a) is a win-rate matrix with no indication of variability. The human evaluation of GPT-4 is based on only 50 samples with no kappa statistics. While these are standard practices for exploratory simulation studies, adding bootstrap intervals or similar measures would help readers assess reliability, especially given the modest number of queries (110).

5. **Narrow dataset scope limits generalizability.** The dataset of 725 LLM papers from ArXiv (mostly 2023) is thematically narrow, and the main marketplace experiments use only Llama 2 (70B) due to budget constraints (acknowledged by the authors). The behavioral findings about LLM biases may not generalize across model families, domains, or dataset distributions. This is a standard limitation for work of this scope but should be stated explicitly in a limitations section.
