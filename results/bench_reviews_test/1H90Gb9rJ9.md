## Summary
The paper proposes a deterministic optimization pipeline for two-layer Heaviside-threshold neural network representations of Boolean networks. It characterizes σ-equivalence classes of multilinear polynomials as a polyhedron via the Sierpiński matrix (Theorem 3.1), reducing minimization to an ILP with ℓ₁ relaxation, accelerates aggregate optimization by exploiting NPN canonical forms, and introduces a depth-preserving variant `optMaintainDepth`. Experiments on four digital circuits report up to 70%/60% reductions in connections/neurons and up to 34.3×/5.9× speedups vs. naive/cached baselines.

## Strengths
- **Theorem 3.1** is a clean, non-obvious result: it converts the σ-equivalence class [p]_σ into a linear-inequality polyhedron Aq ≤ b via the Sierpiński matrix, turning a previously search-based problem into an ILP (Corollary 3.1, Eq. 8). This is the genuine technical contribution.
- **NPN-transport machinery** (Theorems 3.2–3.4, Lemma 3.2) principally extends the NPN-classification trick from EDA to the NN-MP setting; the inverse-transformation construction τ_ν ∘ τ_{π⁻¹} ∘ τ_{π(φ)} for handling input negation is correct and non-trivial.
- **`optMaintainDepth`** leverages a well-defined "leeway" notion (Definition 3.8, Lemma 3.3) with an O(V(V+E)) algorithm, giving a practical depth-preserving compression knob — reducing connections by up to 50% / neurons by 20% on layer-merged NNs without depth increase (Figure 5b).
- **Rigorous formalization** linking ℓ₀ weighted norms of MP coefficients to neuron/connection counts via c^uni and c^deg (Definition 3.1, Lemma 3.1) cleanly transforms NN compression into sparse polynomial optimization.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Comparison is only against the authors' own prior pipeline (Gavier et al. 2023, `optNone`).** Decades of mature classical logic-synthesis work (ABC, mockturtle, espresso, NPN-based resynthesis — the paper even uses Zhou et al. 2020's canonical-form algorithm from that literature) target essentially the same minimization. "70%/60% reduction vs. SOTA" measures only how much fat the immediately prior NN-mapping paper left on the table, not absolute NN size. Since the stated motivations (high-throughput simulation, neurosymbolic deployment) hinge on absolute size, this gap weakens the practical claim.
- **The "lossless" framing conflates functional equivalence with optimization optimality.** The abstract repeatedly contrasts "lossless" (ours) against "lossy" (prior), motivated by the NP-hardness of finding a minimal MP. The body (§3.1, after Corollary 3.1) explicitly concedes that the relaxed ℓ₁ solution "may differ from the actual minimum." The compression is lossless in the equivalence-preserving sense (the same property that prior NN-mapping already guarantees by staying in [p]_σ), but not in the optimization-quality sense the title and abstract imply. The relaxation gap is never quantified theoretically or empirically.
- **Applied motivations are asserted but unmeasured.** The introduction motivates this work via high-throughput circuit simulation and neurosymbolic co-placement, but no GPU/CPU throughput numbers, no end-to-end inference benchmark, and no head-to-head against simulating the original BN are reported. The applied case remains rhetorical.

### Minor
- **Headline numbers are "up to" extrema over (circuit, K, criterion).** No mean/median compression per circuit, no per-K tables in the main body, no distributional reporting. The deterministic-mapping argument for omitting variance on size is reasonable for a fixed solver, but tie-breaking in the ℓ₁ ILP (the argmin set is generally non-singleton) makes the reported number solver-dependent, and the solver, time limits, and optimality-gap tolerance are not stated.
- **Naive vs. NPN is a weak comparison.** The 34.3× headline is against `Naive` (no caching at all); against the meaningful `Cached` baseline the speedup shrinks to 5.9× and only at K=11 on `aes`. The body concedes NPN is merely "competitive" for small BN/K. Naive at K=11 on `uart` and `aes` is omitted "due to compute constraints" — the regime where the headline framing puts the most weight. Reporting Cached vs. NPN as the primary comparison would be more honest.
- **Only four circuits (2K–127K gates)** is a thin slice of the digital-design space for generalization claims, though additional circuits are deferred to appendix.
- **Depth bars in Fig. 5b are tautological** because `optMaintainDepth` preserves depth by construction; reporting depth for `optAll` alongside would be more informative.
- **The U-shape in optimization time** vs. K is described but not modeled or turned into a practitioner recommendation.

### Trivial
- No numeric tables in the main body — all results are in figures, hampering quantitative reading.
- Input-negation (τ_φ) is not preserved by the PN-invariant criteria; the recipe handles this correctly via τ_{π(φ)}, but it means speedup over Cached is bounded by the PN-orbit, not the full NPN-orbit — worth stating quantitatively.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An empirical or theoretical bound on (ℓ₁-relaxation cost) / (true MMP cost) on small instances where the ℓ₀ ILP is tractable, to make the relaxation gap concrete.
- One worked-out small example illustrating [p]_σ as a polyhedron and where the ℓ₁ minimizer lies vs. the ℓ₀ minimizer.
- Comparison to at least one classical synthesis tool (e.g., ABC `resyn2`) for absolute-size context.
- A GPU/CPU end-to-end throughput measurement of the optimized NN vs. the original BN.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.*
- *"`optMaintainDepth` trivially preserves depth, making it a tautology"* — partially valid as a presentation critique (kept as minor), but the algorithm itself is a legitimate contribution, not a tautology.
- *"Larger benchmark suite required"* — Appendix G.2 reports additional BNs per the main text, so the request is partially addressed.
- *"Reproducibility concerns from undisclosed solver/time limits"* — implementation hyperparameter nitpick of the sort typically left to appendix; covered in minor weaknesses for transparency but not weighed heavily.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The Sierpiński-matrix characterization of [p]_σ as a polyhedron and the PN-invariance of the uniform/degree criteria are the genuinely novel observations, and they originate from the paper itself.

## Suggestions
- Reframe the abstract/intro: "lossless" should be unambiguously tied to functional-equivalence preservation, with explicit acknowledgement that ℓ₁ relaxation yields a heuristic, not provably optimal, solution.
- Promote `Cached` (not `Naive`) to the primary speedup baseline; report `Naive` only as an upper bound where computable.
- Add at least one absolute-size baseline (classical EDA tool such as ABC `resyn2`) and at least one runtime benchmark of the produced NN vs. the original BN.
- State the ILP solver, time limits, optimality-gap tolerance, and tie-breaking discipline in the main body.
- Replace "up to" headline numbers with mean/median per (circuit, K) tables.

## Evaluation by axis
- **Originality:** Genuine — the Sierpiński-matrix-based ILP formulation and PN-invariance machinery are novel.
- **Importance:** Niche but meaningful for NN-based technology mapping; the applied motivations are real but not directly evaluated.
- **Claim support:** Mixed — theoretical claims are well supported; the headline empirical claims are framed best-case and rest on the authors' own previous baseline.
- **Soundness of experiments:** Methodologically reasonable but narrow; lacks classical-EDA comparison and end-to-end throughput.
- **Clarity:** Good notation, careful theorem statements; results are figure-heavy with no main-body tables.
- **Value to community:** Solid for the NN-mapping/neurosymbolic subcommunity; less directly impactful for general logic-synthesis researchers without the EDA comparison.
