## Summary

This paper identifies a genuine problem in contrastive time-series representation learning: semantic imbalance, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality). It introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric that quantifies how recoverable one semantic component is from the composite embedding, and uses the resulting asymmetry factor Δ to dynamically reweight the contrastive losses of CoST's trend and seasonal encoders. The idea is conceptually interesting and well-motivated, but the experimental validation is incomplete in critical ways, the results are mixed, and the central claim that the method produces "consistently lower SDE values" is made without reporting SDE for the proposed method on any real dataset.

---

## Strengths

1. **Novel diagnostic metric for semantic imbalance.** The SDE provides a principled way to quantify whether one temporal component (trend vs. seasonality) is suppressed relative to another in the learned representation. The synthetic experiment in Table 1 convincingly shows that SDE and Δ vary systematically with component amplitude ratios on TS2Vec, confirming that the diagnostic can detect imbalance.

2. **Negative result is informative.** The paper honestly reports that directly using SDE as a regularization term (TS2Vec+SDE) does not improve forecasting and sometimes degrades it (Table 2). This negative result motivates the need for the proposed asymmetric weighting mechanism and strengthens the paper's internal narrative.

3. **Clear problem formulation.** The paper identifies three specific limitations of current contrastive time-series methods (no decomposition inductive bias, time-domain-only objectives, isotropic embedding collapse) and traces a logical path from diagnosis (SDE) to solution (asymmetric weighting). The framework diagram in Figure 1 is helpful in communicating the approach.

---

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **SDE values are not reported for the proposed method on real datasets.** The text at line 266 states that "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance," but Table 3 only contains MSE/MAE columns — no SDE values appear anywhere for CoST+APW. The paper repeatedly claims "consistently lower SDE values" but provides zero empirical evidence for this claim on the actual proposed method. This is a critical gap: SDE is the paper's diagnostic centerpiece, yet its behavior under the proposed intervention is never shown on real data.

2. **Forecasting results are mixed and the core claim is overstated.** On Electricity (long horizons 168–720) and Weather (horizons 336–720), CoST+APW is consistently *worse* than vanilla CoST, TS2Vec, and TNC. For example, on Electricity at horizon 168: CoST+APW MSE 0.566 vs CoST 0.425, TS2Vec 0.429, TNC 0.412; at horizon 720: 1.010 vs 0.911, 0.863, 0.859. The paper does not discuss these failures. The claim of "superior forecasting accuracy" is an overstatement — improvements are primarily on the ETT family, and the method degrades on high-periodicity datasets at longer horizons.

3. **No error bars or statistical significance reported.** All forecasting results are reported as point estimates without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Given that many improvements over CoST are small (e.g., ETT1 h24: both 0.04; Elec h24: 0.241 vs 0.242), it is impossible to determine whether the differences are meaningful or simply due to noise.

4. **Critical ablations are missing.** The method has three components beyond vanilla CoST: (a) an MLP that fuses trend and seasonal embeddings into a composite embedding, (b) SDE/Δ computation, and (c) dynamic loss reweighting. There is no ablation that isolates these components. Without comparing CoST+MLP (no reweighting) vs CoST+APW (full system), one cannot attribute any improvement to the novel reweighting mechanism rather than to the extra parameters of the MLP.

### Minor

1. **SDE metric is unvalidated as a training signal.** While SDE works as a post-hoc diagnostic (Table 1), the paper never validates that minimizing SDE correlates with improved downstream performance. The negative result in Table 2 (SDE regularization failing) suggests the metric may not provide useful gradients — but the paper's own weighting mechanism relies on Δ (derived from SDE) to adjust loss weights, which is a different use. Some validation (e.g., probing tasks measuring component recoverability from embeddings) would substantially strengthen the work.

2. **Dataset labeling is confusing.** Table 3 lists two entries as "ETT1" with different horizon sets (24/48/168/336/720 vs 24/48/96/288/672). These likely correspond to ETTh1 and ETTm1, but the paper's dataset descriptions (Section 4.1) use "ETT1h/2, ETTm1/2." This ambiguity makes the table hard to interpret.

3. **No representation quality evaluation beyond forecasting.** The paper motivates the problem using classification and anomaly detection but never tests on those tasks. Evaluating whether the rebalanced representations transfer to other downstream tasks would strengthen the generality claim.

- Computational overhead of the MLP and on-the-fly filtering/SDE computation should be reported.
- A discussion of whether Δ stabilizes during training or oscillates would address a natural concern about the feedback loop between the encoder parameters and the derived weighting.

## Removed Points

- **SDE linearity assumption (from Harsh Critic).** The critic claims the metric *assumes* the encoder is approximately linear. This is a misunderstanding: SDE is a diagnostic that *measures* how close the representation is to being linearly recoverable; no linearity assumption is made about the encoder. The paper explicitly says "a small SDE indicates that the contribution of a is linearly recoverable" — this describes what the metric means, not a modeling assumption. Removed as factually incorrect.
- **"The paper does not discuss how filtering errors affect SDE"** and **"Δ gradient feedback loop not analyzed."** These are speculative concerns about what the paper *could* have discussed. A paper is not required to pre-emptively analyze all possible failure modes of its components. The fundamental experimental gaps are the binding constraint.
- **"Method is built on CoST which already uses frequency-domain contrastive learning, weakening the narrative."** The paper is explicit about building on CoST; this is a design choice, not a weakness. The novelty is in the SDE-based reweighting, not in the frequency-domain encoding.

## Suggestions

1. **Report SDE values for CoST+APW on real datasets.** This is the single most important missing piece — without it, the central claim that the method "reduces SDE" is unsubstantiated.
2. **Add ablations: CoST vs CoST+MLP vs CoST+APW (no MLP) vs CoST+APW (full).** Isolate the effect of the weighting from the MLP.
3. **Include standard deviations or confidence intervals** for all forecasting results.
4. **Discuss the failure cases** (Electricity/Weather long horizons) to give a balanced assessment of where the method works and where it does not.
5. **Evaluate on at least one non-forecasting task** (classification or anomaly detection) to support the generality claims.
