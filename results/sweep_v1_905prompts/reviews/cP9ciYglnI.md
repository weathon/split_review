## Summary

This paper introduces a shape-adaptive guidance signal (weighted geodesic distance transform — WGDT) for interactive cortical sulcal labeling on spherical surfaces. The key technical innovation is solving the eikonal equation with a propagation speed modulated by mean curvature of the cortical surface (Eq. 4), so that user clicks propagate rapidly along sulcal valleys (H ≥ 0) and are contained on gyri (H < 0). Evaluated on 72 HCP subjects with 17 LPFC sulci, the paper shows that WGDT with a single click yields statistically significant Dice improvements over angular-distance (ADT) and binary-disk (Disk) signals on all 9 small/variable sulci. The method achieves under 0.5 seconds per click, making it practical for real-time use.

## Strengths

- **Novel curvature-aware guidance signal.** The WGDT formulation (Eq. 3–5) directly embeds cortical folding geometry into the click encoding by using an exponential mean-curvature speed function. This is a principled departure from prior geodesic- and angular-distance transforms that ignore surface anatomy. The qualitative difference is visible in Figure 3, where WGDT remains localized along sulcal folds while ADT/Disk spill into adjacent regions.

- **Clear, statistically rigorous advantage over equidistance baselines.** WGDT outperforms ADT and Disk on all 9 small/variable sulci with a single click (adjusted p < 0.05, FDR-corrected), with consistent advantages across multiple σ and k settings (Figure 4). The evaluation uses 5-fold cross-validation, 10 initial click locations per subject-sulcus, and paired t-tests with multi-comparison correction — a thorough protocol.

- **Practical runtime.** Table 2 reports total latency under 0.5 seconds per click (WGDT encoding: 175ms, re-tessellation: 208ms, forward pass: 28ms) on surfaces with 100k–170k vertices, supporting near-real-time interactive use.

- **Methodologically sound click simulation.** The iterative click simulation (Section 2.2) models a human annotator by clicking near the center of the largest mislabeled region with geodesic-distance-weighted variability, which is more realistic than uniform random sampling or single-shot multi-click simulations used in prior work.

## Weaknesses

### Major

None that threaten the core contribution. The paper's primary claim — that WGDT provides a better guidance signal than equidistance-based encoding — is well supported by the experimental evidence.

### Minor

1. **The "outperforms fully automatic methods" framing conflates task difficulty with method quality.** The interactive method trains a separate binary SPHARM-Net per sulcus, while the automatic baselines (Lyu et al. 2021, Lee et al. 2025a,b) are trained to label all 17 sulci simultaneously in a single multi-class model. These are very different tasks — binary + a click prior vs. multi-class with no prior — so the comparison in Figure 5 does not primarily reflect the superiority of WGDT over automatic methods. Any click-based signal (including ADT/Disk) would likely beat multi-class automatic methods on small sulci. The paper acknowledges complementary use in Section 5 but the abstract and introduction present the comparison as evidence of WGDT's effectiveness, which overreaches. **Fix:** Reframe this comparison to say that minimal human input resolves ambiguities that automatic multi-class models cannot, rather than claiming WGDT itself surpasses automatic methods. The central WGDT-vs-ADT/Disk comparison (Figure 4) already cleanly supports the paper's actual contribution.

2. **Missing ablation: the backbone without any guidance signal.** The paper compares WGDT to ADT and Disk but does not report the performance of SPHARM-Net when it receives only geometric features and no click-based guidance signal (i.e., a fully automatic per-sulcus binary classifier). This baseline would directly quantify how much of the improvement comes from *any* click signal vs. the *shape-adaptive* aspect specifically. Without it, readers cannot tell whether WGDT's advantage over ADT/Disk is large relative to the total gain from interaction. **Fix:** Add a "0 clicks" condition (or equivalently, train the same SPHARM-Net without guidance signal channels) and report its Dice scores alongside the interactive results.

3. **Initial click simulation uses optimal click placement.** The 10 initial clicks per subject-sulcus are generated by "selecting points to maximize both their distance from the label boundary and mutual separation" (Section 3.3). This produces near-optimal click locations unlikely to represent a real expert's first click. While all compared methods use the same clicks (so the relative comparison between WGDT and ADT/Disk is fair), the absolute performance numbers may overstate what would be achieved in practice. **Fix:** Run a robustness experiment with a more realistic initial-click strategy (e.g., random sampling inside the ground-truth label) and report whether the WGDT advantage persists.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Include an isotropic geodesic distance transform (GDT, without curvature weighting) as an additional baseline. This would cleanly isolate the effect of the curvature component.
- Report the overlap between the thresholded WGDT signal and the ground-truth manual label (e.g., Dice between the guidance signal region and the label) to directly validate the "shape-adaptive coverage" claim.
- A brief analysis of how the curvature exponent k affects Dice across sulci of different sizes would help guide hyperparameter choice.

## Suggestions

1. Add the "no click" ablation (SPHARM-Net without guidance signal channels). This is the single highest-value addition: it would allow readers to decompose the improvement into "any click" vs. "shape-adaptive click" and would also provide a fair per-sulcus binary automatic baseline.
2. Reframe the comparison to automatic methods in the abstract and introduction as a demonstration that minimal interaction resolves multi-class failures, not as evidence that WGDT is superior to automatic methods.
3. Run a robustness experiment with random initial clicks (rather than geometrically optimized ones) to validate that the WGDT advantage is not an artifact of favorable click placement.
4. Add a quantitative analysis of guidance signal alignment with ground-truth labels (e.g., Dice between the thresholded WGDT region and the manual label) to directly support the shape-adaptivity claim.
