## Summary

DeepScientist presents a fully autonomous, goal-driven AI research system that iterates through a three-stage loop (hypothesize, implement & verify, analyze & report) over month-long timelines. It frames discovery as Bayesian Optimization and uses a persistent Findings Memory. Across 20,000 GPU hours, the system generated ~5,000 ideas, tested ~1,100, and produced 21 progress findings, surpassing human SOTA on three AI tasks by 183.7%, 1.9%, and 7.9%. The papers generated by the system were evaluated by a human expert panel, achieving an average rating comparable to ICLR 2025 submissions.

## Strengths

- **Unprecedented empirical scale for autonomous research.** The system consumed 20,000 GPU hours, generated ~5,000 unique ideas, experimentally validated ~1,100, and produced 21 progress findings across three distinct tasks (Section 4.3, Figure 4). This is by far the largest-scale empirical demonstration of an autonomous AI research system to date.

- **Genuine SOTA improvements on real benchmarks.** The system improved step accuracy on Agent Failure Attribution from 12.07% to 47.46% (Who&When benchmark, ICML 2025 Spotlight baseline), AUROC on AI Text Detection from 0.800 to 0.863 (RAID benchmark, ICLR 2024 baseline), and tokens/sec on LLM Inference Acceleration from 190.25 to 193.90 (MBPP, ACL 2025 Outstanding baseline). All baselines are published, peer-reviewed methods — not strawmen (Section 4.1, Figure 3, Table 1).

- **Transparent human evaluation of generated papers.** Five full papers were reviewed by a three-person panel (two ICLR reviewers, one Area Chair) with Krippendorff's α = 0.739. Average rating of 5.00 closely matches ICLR 2025 average of 5.08, with two papers at 5.67 (Table 3, Appendix B). The full papers are included in the appendix — a rare level of transparency.

- **Convincing evidence for the necessity of the selection mechanism.** Ablation with random sampling yields zero success across all three tasks, demonstrating that the Findings Memory + acquisition function filtering is essential, not incidental (Section 4.3).

- **The progressive discovery trajectory on AI Text Detection shows genuine self-guided refinement.** The sequence T-Detect → TDT → PA-TDT emerged within two weeks, with each method building on and surpassing the previous SOTA, shifting the detection paradigm from global statistics to non-stationary signal analysis (Section 4.1, Figure 1).

- **Scaling analysis reveals near-linear relationship between parallel GPU resources and discoveries**, attributed to the shared Findings Memory rather than brute-force parallelism (Section 4.3, Figure 6). An additional Micronano evaluation on 154 ALGOTUNE tasks achieved 77.9% success rate (Appendix E).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None. The core claims — that the system can autonomously discover methods surpassing human SOTA on these three tasks — are supported by the evidence presented.

### Major

- **Human supervision is acknowledged but its extent is undocumented.** Three human experts "verify outputs and filter out hallucinations" (Section 4). The paper never quantifies how many ideas were rejected by humans vs. the system's own filtering, how often humans intervened to redirect the system, or what fraction of the 21 progress findings required human course correction. This makes the "fully autonomous" claim difficult to assess. Without this breakdown, a reader cannot distinguish between a system that autonomously found 21 breakthroughs and one where humans filtered 95% of bad ideas and the system merely polished the remainder.

- **The Bayesian Optimization framing is a veneer, not a validated mechanism.** The surrogate model (an LLM) produces heuristic integer scores (0–100) for utility, quality, and exploration value. No calibration against actual experimental outcomes is shown. The UCB parameters are fixed at wu = wq = κ = 1 with no task-specific tuning. The sole ablation compares against random sampling — a strawman baseline. The paper never compares UCB-based selection against simpler alternatives such as selecting by utility score alone or by a single LLM-generated "estimated improvement" score. This matters because the BO framing is presented as a core intellectual contribution (Section 3), but there is no evidence the UCB machinery adds value beyond the LLM's raw scoring.

- **The 1.9% improvement on LLM Inference Acceleration is marginal and unsupported by error bars.** A 3.65 tokens/second gain (190.25 → 193.90) could easily be within measurement noise, especially given no significance tests or error bars are reported (Figure 3c). While the paper acknowledges each task presents different difficulty, readers evaluating the system's reliability need to know whether this result is statistically meaningful.

### Minor

- **The AI Text Detection evaluation may conflate benchmark optimization with generalization.** The system iterated for two weeks on the RAID benchmark, producing three progressively better methods. Without a held-out evaluation on a separate dataset not used during the discovery loop, it is unclear whether the 7.9% AUROC gain reflects genuine methodological improvement or fitting to RAID-specific quirks. This is a standard concern in ML, but it weakens the "scientific discovery" framing.

- **The paper overstates the conceptual depth of discovered methods.** A2P is structured multi-step prompting built on the All-at-Once baseline, T-Detect replaces a Gaussian assumption with a t-distribution, and TDT applies a wavelet transform — all are legitimate contributions but are straightforward applications of known techniques rather than "redesigning core methodologies" as claimed. The human reviewers themselves noted "a lack of deep validation" (Appendix B.2). This does not invalidate the system's achievements but tempers the paper's rhetoric.

- **The human expert evaluation, while a strength in transparency, is not a rigorous substitute for blinded peer review.** Reviewers were aware the papers were machine-generated (each paper header states "Discovered and authored by DeepScientist"), the panel was small (3 people), and the sample was 5 papers. The comparison to ICLR 2025 average (5.08) is suggestive but not statistically meaningful given these differences in process, scale, and reviewer composition. The paper is appropriately modest about this in Section 4.2, but the abstract's implication that the papers are "publication-quality" overreaches.

### Trivial

- The UCB formula (Eq. 1) has a typographical error labeling both terms as "Exploitation Term" — the second should be "Exploration Term."

## Nice-to-Haves

- A held-out evaluation of discovered methods on an unseen dataset (e.g., a newer benchmark version or different domain) would substantially strengthen the claim of genuine discovery vs. benchmark overfitting.
- A comparison of the UCB-based selection against a simpler LLM scoring baseline (utility score only, no exploration term) would validate the BO framing.
- A quantitative breakdown of human intervention frequency and impact would help readers assess the degree of autonomy.
- A sensitivity analysis showing how performance varies with different UCB hyperparameters (wu, wq, κ) would address concerns about the untuned equal-weight configuration.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel empirical finding is the characterization of the discovery funnel: ~5,000 ideas → ~1,100 tested → 21 progress findings → 5 papers. This quantifies something the field has long suspected — that AI-driven scientific exploration generates vast quantities of ideas but genuine breakthroughs are exceptionally rare. The additional finding that ~60% of failures stem from implementation errors rather than flawed hypotheses (Section 4.3) provides concrete, actionable signal about where to invest engineering effort: better code generation, not better ideation, may be the highest-leverage improvement for autonomous research systems. The scaling analysis showing near-linear returns from shared memory (rather than just parallel compute) is also a useful empirical contribution to the emerging literature on scaling laws for AI-driven discovery.

## Suggestions

- Quantify human supervision: report what fraction of the 1,100 implemented ideas or 21 progress findings required human intervention beyond verification, and what form that intervention took. This is the single most important addition for establishing the paper's "autonomy" claims.
- Add a simple baseline for the acquisition function: compare UCB selection against selecting by utility score alone. This would either validate or refute the need for the BO machinery in one experiment.
- Report variance/error bars for the LLM Inference Acceleration result (Figure 3c) to allow readers to assess whether the 1.9% gain is statistically meaningful.
- Tone down the rhetoric about "redesigning core methodologies" and "publication-quality" work. The empirical results speak for themselves and don't need overclaiming.
- For the AI Text Detection task, evaluate the final method (PA-TDT) on a dataset not used during the discovery loop (e.g., a held-out domain from HART) to provide a cleaner signal about generalization.
