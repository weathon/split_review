## Summary
The paper proposes a fast constrained-sampling method for pretrained Stable Diffusion models, replacing denoiser backpropagation guidance with a finite-difference update intended to approximate a Jacobian-vector direction \(J e\) rather than the usual backpropagated \(J^\top e\). The method is evaluated on ImageNet inpainting and \(8\times\) super-resolution, and the paper also presents a qualitative “layer inference” application enabled by faster repeated inpainting.

## Strengths
- **Clear practical speed motivation with concrete runtime evidence.** Table 1 reports approximately **2 min/image** for the proposed method versus **8 min for LDPS, 12 min for PSLD, and 30 min for P2L**, and Figure 1 reports **17s** for the proposed half-image inpainting example versus **5 min** for PSLD. This supports the practical claim that avoiding denoiser backpropagation can substantially reduce inference cost.
- **Concrete algorithmic mechanism for avoiding backpropagation through the denoiser.** Section 3 and Algorithm 1 operationalize a finite-difference update using two denoiser forward evaluations, \([\hat{x}_0(x_t+\delta e)-\hat{x}_0(x_t)]/\delta\), instead of differentiating the constraint loss through the full denoiser. Even if the derivation is problematic, the computational idea is specific and potentially useful.
- **Competitive inpainting numbers under the paper’s latent-space protocol.** On ImageNet free-form inpainting, Table 1 reports the best PSNR and FID among the listed sampling-based baselines: PSNR **22.20** and FID **30.45**, compared with P2L at **21.99 / 32.82**, LDPS at **21.54 / 46.72**, and PSLD at **20.92 / 40.57**. LPIPS is worse than P2L, so this is not uniformly dominant, but the inpainting result is still a meaningful empirical positive.
- **The distinction between \(J e\) and \(J^\top e\) is an interesting observation.** Figure 2 provides evidence that sampled Stable Diffusion Jacobian entries are not symmetric, and Figure 3 gives a useful qualitative illustration that finite-difference directions can produce different propagation behavior than backpropagated gradients.
- **The paper evaluates both inpainting and super-resolution rather than relying only on cherry-picked visual examples.** Table 1 includes PSNR, LPIPS, FID, and approximate runtime, which makes the speed-quality tradeoff at least partially assessable.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper contains a real algorithmic idea and some empirical evidence, so it is not invalid as a whole. However, there are serious methodological and evidential problems.

### Major
- **The claimed optimization/Newton-style derivation does not justify the proposed update.** The paper’s stated constrained objective is \(C(x_t)=\|A\hat{x}_0(x_t)-y\|^2\) in Eq. (1)/(line 53). For a local linearization, perturbing \(x_t\) by \(h\) changes the estimate by \(Jh\), leading naturally to a least-squares/Gauss-Newton or gradient direction involving \(J^\top e\) or a pseudoinverse-type step. Instead, Section 3 chooses a desired clean-image-space move \(g=-\epsilon e\) and then derives \(h=Jg\), yielding \(h=-\epsilon J e\) (lines 87–90). This is a heuristic relationship between latent and denoised-space perturbations, not a derivation of descent on the original constraint loss. Since the paper frames the method as “Newton steps based on the inverse function,” this gap directly undermines the central methodological claim.
- **There is a serious sign/definition inconsistency in the finite-difference update.** Section 3 defines \(f(s)=\hat{x}_0(x_t-s e)\), whose derivative at \(s=0\) is \(-J e\) (lines 93–96). But the finite difference used in Eq. (99–100) and Algorithm 1 is \([\hat{x}_0(x_t+\delta e)-\hat{x}_0(x_t)]/\delta\), which approximates \(+J e\), not \(-J e\). The text even says this uses “the perturbed \(x_t\) (in the negative direction of the error vector)” at line 102, while the formula and Algorithm 1 use \(+\delta e\). Because the claimed contribution depends on the direction of the update, this is not a minor notation issue.
- **The empirical evidence supports a speed-quality tradeoff, not the stronger state-of-the-art comparability claims.** The abstract claims results “comparable even to the state-of-the-art tuned models,” but the tuned inpainting model appears only in the illustrative cat example, not in a quantitative benchmark. In Table 1, the method is strong on inpainting PSNR/FID but worse than P2L on inpainting LPIPS, and for \(8\times\) super-resolution it is worse than P2L on PSNR, LPIPS, and FID, and worse than LDPS/PSLD on PSNR and LPIPS. The speed advantage is credible, but the paper should frame the result as a fast heuristic with mixed quality, not as broadly matching state-of-the-art constrained solvers or tuned models.
- **The inpainting experiment solves a latent-space proxy rather than the stated pixel-space constraint.** Section 4.1 explicitly says that for inpainting the authors “dilate the pixel-level masks and directly perform inpainting in the latent space,” discarding some known image information and applying \(A,A^\top\) to latents rather than pixels (line 165). This may be a reasonable engineering choice, but it is not equivalent to the motivating pixel-space constraint that known pixels must match the input. The paper does not report known-region pixel reconstruction error or other measurement-consistency metrics, so the inpainting results do not fully establish that the method solves the original pixel-space constrained sampling problem.
- **The baseline and runtime comparisons are not controlled enough for the central speed-quality claim.** Figure 4 uses results directly from Chung et al. because code was unavailable (line 177), and Table 1 reports only approximate runtimes. The paper states that its experiments were run on an RTX A5000 (line 163), but it is unclear whether all baselines were evaluated under the same hardware, sampler, prompts, masks, diffusion steps, and evaluation pipeline. Because the main practical claim is fast constrained sampling, the absence of a controlled speed-quality Pareto comparison is a substantial evidential gap.
- **The super-resolution implementation is unclear and potentially inconsistent with the denoised-estimate objective.** Section 4.1 says the pixel constraint cost is \((A\mathcal{D}(x_t)-y)^T(A\mathcal{D}(x_t)-y)\) and that the error direction is obtained by backpropagating through the decoder with respect to the latent (line 167). This appears to use the noisy latent \(x_t\), not the denoised estimate \(\hat{x}_0(x_t)\) used in the objective and Algorithm 1. If this is intended, it changes the objective; if not, the method description needs correction. This matters especially because the super-resolution results are the weakest part of the evaluation.

### Minor
- **The paper does not isolate the contribution of the proposed \(J e\)-style finite-difference direction.** Warm restarts are used in both experiments, and super-resolution additionally uses random gradient perturbations to avoid blurry local minima (lines 169–170). There is no ablation comparing \(J e\), \(-J e\), \(J^\top e\), no guidance, latent replacement/projection, and the added restart/perturbation heuristics under matched compute. Without this, it is unclear how much of the performance comes from the proposed update rather than auxiliary heuristics.
- **The Jacobian-asymmetry evidence does not establish that \(J e\) is a better guidance direction.** Figure 2 shows that sampled Jacobian entries are asymmetric for one ImageNet input and random pixel pairs, and Figure 3 qualitatively illustrates different behavior on a synthetic grid. This supports “the directions differ,” but not “the proposed direction improves constrained sampling” or “asymmetry explains the empirical performance.”
- **Layer inference is interesting but preliminary.** The decomposition \(x_0=m x_0^1+(1-m)x_0^2\) is highly non-identifiable, and Section 4.2 evaluates only qualitative web-image examples guided by layer-specific prompts. This is best viewed as an application vignette rather than a validated new inference task.
- **The conclusion overgeneralizes the scope.** The paper concludes with language about sampling “under any condition,” but the experiments cover latent-space free-form inpainting, \(8\times\) super-resolution with mixed performance, and qualitative layer inference. The demonstrated scope is narrower than the wording suggests.

### Trivial
None. I am not counting parser artifacts, typos, or formatting issues.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Report measurement consistency directly: known-region pixel error for inpainting and low-resolution measurement error for super-resolution.
- Include confidence intervals or repeated-seed statistics for FID/LPIPS, especially since the inpainting advantage depends partly on FID over 1000 images.
- Plot a runtime-quality Pareto curve by varying inner iterations, restarts, and timestep stride.
- Show failure cases, particularly for super-resolution where the paper itself notes artifacts and texture misalignment.

## Novel Insights
The most important synthesis is that the paper’s useful contribution is not the claimed Newton/constrained-posterior derivation, but rather a pragmatic finite-difference guidance heuristic that trades quality for speed by avoiding denoiser backpropagation. The empirical story is strongest for latent-space inpainting and weakest for principled constrained sampling: the method can be fast and visually effective, but the sign inconsistency, mismatch between latent and pixel constraints, and lack of controlled ablations make it hard to know whether the proposed \(J e\) direction is genuinely responsible for the observed gains.

## Suggestions
- Reframe the method as a fast heuristic unless a correct descent or posterior-sampling derivation can be provided.
- Resolve the sign convention explicitly: define \(e\), the finite-difference perturbation direction, the sign of \(\lambda\), and the intended effect on \(\|A\hat{x}_0-y\|^2\).
- Add a controlled ablation comparing \(J e\), \(-J e\), \(J^\top e\), no guidance, and simple latent projection/replacement under matched runtime.
- Report measurement-consistency metrics, especially known-region pixel error for inpainting.
- Clarify whether super-resolution constraints are applied to \(\mathcal{D}(x_t)\) or \(\mathcal{D}(\hat{x}_0(x_t))\), and align the implementation with the stated objective.
- Either add quantitative comparisons to tuned inpainting/restoration models or remove the claim of comparability to tuned state-of-the-art models.
- Separate copied baseline numbers from controlled experiments and provide a fair speed-quality Pareto comparison on the same hardware/software setup where possible.
