## Summary

IncentRL proposes a reinforcement learning framework that augments standard task rewards with a KL divergence term between predicted outcome distributions _p(o|s,a)_ and preferred outcome distributions _q(o|s)_, with the incentive weight β treated as a Bayesian random variable adapted online. The idea of using preference-prediction alignment as an intrinsic signal is conceptually interesting and well-motivated from cognitive science perspectives. However, the paper has critical gaps: the central Bayesian adaptation mechanism is never algorithmically described, the construction of _p(o|s,a)_ is left unspecified for the non-toy experiments, the empirical evaluation compares only against a β=0 baseline (standard RL) with no comparisons to any existing intrinsic motivation or exploration method, and the paper's headline claim — that Bayesian adaptation eliminates manual tuning — is asserted without supporting performance data.

## Strengths

- **Conceptually interesting framework:** The core idea of using KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcome distributions as a shaping signal is novel and well-motivated by connections to cognitive science (dopamine-based RPE, the Free Energy Principle). Unlike standard KL-regularized RL (TRPO, PPO, SAC) that regularizes policy changes, IncentRL regularizes outcome alignment — a genuinely different use of KL divergence.

- **Empirical signal on sparse-reward tasks:** Despite limitations, the MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8 results (Figure 2) show meaningful improvement: β=0.01 reaches 98% success rate vs. 90.5% for the β=0 baseline, with episode length dropping from 75 to 29 steps. The MountainCar results (Table 2) show an 18% improvement with β=0.1. These provide preliminary evidence that the KL-based shaping can help, at least relative to doing nothing.

- **Posterior concentration evidence exists:** Figures 3–4 and Table 3 show the posterior distribution of β narrowing across rounds (std dropping from ~0.06 to ~0.02) and stabilizing near task-effective values (β≈0.1), suggesting the adaptation idea has empirical traction, even though the adaptation mechanism itself is undescribed.

- **Robustness to β in simple settings:** Table 1 shows stable performance across β ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1.0} in the toy 2-state MDP, indicating the method is not brittle to precise β choice in that domain.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None. The paper does not have errors that invalidate its core claims entirely — it has gaps that make those claims unevaluable.

### Major

- **Bayesian adaptation mechanism is never described, making the central contribution irreproducible and unevaluable.** The abstract, introduction, and conclusion all highlight Bayesian adaptation of β as *the* key novelty — "the central novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable, updated online." Yet Section 5.1 ("The IncentRL Algorithm") lists only five steps for computing shaped rewards and updating the policy; there is no step that adapts β. No prior, likelihood, posterior approximation, or update rule is given anywhere in the paper. Section 5.4 mentions "we tracked the posterior evolution of β using our Bayesian adaptation scheme" without specifying what that scheme is. The appendix (Table 3) reports only summary statistics of sampled β. A contribution whose core novelty rests on a Bayesian method but omits the method itself cannot be evaluated scientifically. This alone makes the paper's central claim unsupported.

- **No comparisons to any existing intrinsic motivation or exploration baselines.** All experiments compare IncentRL only against its own β=0 variant (plain DQN or Q-learning). The paper surveys ICM, RND, VIME, empowerment, and other intrinsic motivation methods in Section 2.1, yet none appear as baselines. On MountainCar, an 18% improvement over DQN without shaping does not demonstrate that IncentRL outperforms any established approach. On MiniGrid, only β=0.0 is used as a baseline. Because the paper's contribution is precisely that this shaping mechanism improves exploration in sparse-reward settings, the absence of comparisons to existing exploration methods means the claimed practical value is unsubstantiated.

- **Construction of p(o|s,a) is left unspecified for the non-toy experiments, making the method irreproducible.** The shaped reward depends critically on KL(p(o|s,a) ‖ q(o|s)), but how the predicted outcome distribution is obtained is never concretely defined for MountainCar or MiniGrid. Section 3.1 says p(o|s,a) "may be obtained from a forward model or from environment dynamics." In MountainCar (Section 5.3), the state is continuous; what random variable o refers to (full next state? discretized goal indicator?) and how a probability distribution is formed and updated during learning are unspecified. In MiniGrid (Section 5.4), no mention is made of whether a dynamics model is learned, how the distribution is parameterized, or whether true transition probabilities are used. Without this specification, the experiments cannot be replicated and the reported shaping effects cannot be meaningfully interpreted.

- **The claim that Bayesian adaptation matches the best fixed β is stated without performance data.** Section 5.4 asserts: "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value." No success rates, learning curves, or any performance metrics are provided. Figures 3–4 and Table 3 only show the distribution of β values, not the agent's task performance when using those adaptively chosen β values. The whole point of adaptation is to avoid manual sweeps; without showing that the adaptive agent actually learns as well as the best fixed-β agent, the central practical claim is unbacked.

### Minor

- **Theoretical analysis is thin and contributes little beyond basic observations.** Proposition 1 establishes that for sufficiently small β, the optimal policy under standard RL remains optimal under IncentRL — a generic continuity/small-perturbation argument that applies to any bounded reward shaping. Proposition 2 is the straightforward observation that as β→∞, the policy optimizes only the KL term. These do not establish convergence guarantees under function approximation, off-policy learning, or interactions with the Bayesian adaptation scheme. As a theoretical contribution, this is negligible.

- **Small number of seeds (3) for MountainCar and MiniGrid experiments** limits the statistical reliability of the reported improvements, especially given the variance observed (e.g., std of 535 on MountainCar with β=0.1 vs. mean improvement of ~633).

### Trivial

- The MountainCar metric "mean goal reaches per 5000 episodes" is unconventional and obscures per-episode learning dynamics; standard learning curves would be more informative.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Trajectory visualizations in MiniGrid showing example navigation paths with and without KL shaping would qualitatively illustrate behavioral differences.
- A sensitivity analysis examining how errors in the outcome prediction model p(o|s,a) affect IncentRL performance, since the shaped reward depends directly on that model's quality.
- Comparison of Bayesian β adaptation against simple alternatives (fixed schedule, population-based tuning, meta-gradients) to substantiate the claim of eliminating manual tuning.

## Suggestions

- **Provide a complete description of the Bayesian β adaptation algorithm** — including the prior over β, the likelihood function, the posterior approximation method, and the update rule — before any resubmission. This is the single most important fix.

- **Specify exactly how p(o|s,a) is constructed in each experiment**, including what random variable o represents, how the distribution is parameterized, and how it is updated during training.

- **Add comparisons against at least 2–3 standard intrinsic motivation baselines** (e.g., ICM, RND) on the same MountainCar and MiniGrid tasks, or remove claims about outperforming or replacing existing intrinsic motivation methods.

- **Report the actual task performance of the Bayesian β adaptation agent** (success rate, episode length, learning curves), not just the posterior distribution of β.

- **Increase the number of seeds** for MountainCar and MiniGrid, and report standard error or confidence intervals on learning curves.
