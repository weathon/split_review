## Summary
The paper proposes **GazeReward**, a framework that augments reward models (RMs) with synthetic eye-tracking (ET) features generated by frozen pre-trained ET predictors. Two integration strategies (GazeConcat, GazeAdd) are evaluated on OASST1, HelpSteer2, and RewardBench across three base RMs (Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B), reporting accuracy improvements of 10–25 points over a baseline RM.

## Strengths
- **Broad ablation matrix.** The paper covers 3 base RMs × 2 integration methods × 3 feature combinations × 2 ET predictors × 2 preference datasets, plus a RewardBench transfer test (Tables 2–4), which is more thorough than a typical first-paper exploration.
- **Generalization to an out-of-training benchmark.** RewardBench (Table 4), unused during training, shows consistent positive deltas, providing some evidence that improvements are not purely in-distribution overfitting.
- **Scalability via synthetic ET.** Avoiding hardware-collected ET (§3.1) makes the approach practically applicable, and using two independent ET predictors trained on different corpora is a reasonable robustness move.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None — the work has real engineering content; the issues below are major but not invalidating.

### Major
- **The "implicit human feedback" framing is not what the method tests.** The ET predictor is frozen and takes only `(prompt, response)` text as input (§3.1, §4.1) — the exact same text the RM sees. No human gaze is ever measured. The contribution is reframed throughout (Abstract; §1; §3.3) as integrating "human implicit feedback" and "cognitive signals," which overstates the relationship to human ET data. The paper does acknowledge synthetic ET, but the alignment-to-RLHF/RLAIF positioning rests on a human-in-the-loop story that the experiments do not realize.
- **No capacity-matched control for the projector and added tokens.** GazeReward adds a trainable MLP projector (two linear layers + LN + dropout + ReLU, §3.2), special `<eye>/</eye>` tokens, and a longer effective input, against a baseline QLoRA RM with none of these. With ~6K training examples, any of these can absorb signal. Obvious controls — random features, shuffled-across-examples ET features, surprisal/length statistics, or a constant feature — through the same projector are absent. Without them, attributing the 20–40% relative gain to "gaze" specifically is unsupported.
- **Out-of-distribution use of ET predictors, with no validation.** The predictors were trained on Dundee/ZuCo/GECO/PROVO — short, well-formed read sentences. They are applied to long multi-turn LLM dialogue, with a sliding window beyond the 512-token limit (§4.1). The paper provides no check that predicted TRT/FFD on these outputs bears any meaningful relationship to real human gaze on similar text. The central scientific claim relies on this transfer.
- **Statistical evidence is thin on small test sets.** Test sizes are 416 (OASST1) and 364 (HelpSteer2) — a 1-point swing is ~3–4 examples. No seeds, variance, or confidence intervals are reported in any cell of Tables 2–4. Several "wins" are within plausible run-to-run noise.
- **Headline numbers are relative gains on weak baselines.** RewardBench baselines of 41–50% are near random for two-way preference; the "over 40%" gain (Mistral 41.2→60.5) is impressive in relative terms but the underlying RM is undertrained. The abstract's "over 20% improvement" is relative, not absolute, and is not flagged as such.

### Minor
- **GazeAdd × Mistral cells are missing without explanation** (Tables 2 and 3). Given that GazeAdd requires remapping the ET predictor's tokenizer to the RM tokenizer ("we remap," §3.2 — the actual procedure is not described), the missing cells likely reflect an alignment problem that should be discussed.
- **Hyperparameter tuning fairness is ambiguous.** The grid over LR/batch/scheduler (§4.1) is reported, but whether the baseline received the same per-cell tuning budget as the gaze variants is not stated.
- **Feature-importance discussion conflates capacity with feature content.** The claim that fcomb_{2.5} can underperform fcomb_{1} is interesting, but feature-count changes also change projector input dimensionality and parameter count; no control isolates "which features help" from "how many parameters were added."
- **The "unaligned models benefit more" finding** (§4.2) is essentially "weaker baselines have more room to grow." It is reasonable to report, but it is not specifically evidence about ET.

### Trivial
- HelpSteer2 Mistral baseline is reported as "51.2535" (Table 3), suggesting a single-run number rather than an averaged result.
- Table layout could be tighter; the "diff(%)" formatting is hard to scan.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A capacity-matched random/shuffled-feature control through the identical projector.
- A small validation that predicted ET on LLM dialogue correlates with human ET, on a held-out human ET subset.
- Per-category RewardBench breakdown (Chat/Chat-Hard/Reasoning/Safety) since the overall baseline is near chance.
- Multi-seed runs with paired bootstrap on preference accuracy given the small test sets.
- Case studies where the augmented RM flips a decision, with the predicted gaze visualized.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.
- *Missing variance reporting framed as a reproducibility violation.* Kept above as a Major statistical concern, but the harsh critic's adjacent demands for full multi-seed grids and exhaustive hyperparameter logs cross into reproducibility nitpicking.
- *"OASST1/HelpSteer2 contamination affects baseline vs. augmented differently"* (harsh critic §4.1 note) — speculative; the paper acknowledges contamination and the symmetric-baseline argument is defensible.
- *Strength: "Practical finding that simpler ET features suffice"* (Strength Finder) — Removed: this finding cannot be cleanly disentangled from the parameter-count confound (see Minor weakness above), so it conflicts with a verified weakness.
- *Strength: "Demonstrated benefit for both aligned and unaligned model initializations"* — Removed: reduces to "weaker baselines have more room to grow," which is not specifically a gaze finding.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The most interesting empirical claim — that adding more ET features can hurt — is confounded with model-capacity changes and cannot be cleanly attributed to feature content.

## Suggestions
- Drop or sharply reframe the "human implicit feedback / cognitive signal" narrative; either replace synthetic ET with at least a pilot of real ET on LLM-generated responses, or position the method as "augmenting RMs with text-derived auxiliary statistics from ET predictors."
- Add the capacity-matched controls (random / shuffled / surprisal / constant features through the same projector) to isolate the gaze contribution.
- Report absolute deltas in the abstract alongside relative ones, and run multi-seed/bootstrap CIs given the small test sets.
- Fully specify the GazeAdd token remapping procedure and explain the missing Mistral cells.

**Evaluation along the requested axes.**
*Originality:* moderate — applying synthetic ET predictors to RMs is a fresh combination, though closely related to Kiegeland et al. (2024). *Importance:* the question is well-scoped within alignment research. *Support for claims:* weak — the central claim (gaze improves RMs) is not isolated from the projector-capacity confound; predictor outputs are not validated on dialogue. *Soundness of experiments:* limited by tiny test sets, no variance reporting, missing cells, and near-random RewardBench baselines. *Clarity:* generally readable; the GazeAdd remapping is under-specified. *Value to community:* the framework and ablation matrix are useful as a starting point but the headline claims should be discounted.
