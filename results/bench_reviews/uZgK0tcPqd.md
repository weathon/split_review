## Summary

GazeReward proposes augmenting large language model reward models with synthetic eye-tracking (ET) features generated by frozen prediction models (a BiLSTM-based model and a RoBERTa-based model), integrated either by concatenation (GazeConcat) or element-wise addition (GazeAdd). The paper conducts an ablation across three base models (Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B), three ET feature combinations, and two preference datasets (OASST1 and HelpSteer2), reporting consistent accuracy gains, with improvements exceeding 20% for some configurations. The work is the first to integrate ET-derived signals into reward modeling for LLM alignment.

---

## Strengths

- **Novel application with consistent empirical results**: GazeReward is the first framework to incorporate ET features into reward modeling. Improvements are consistent across all applicable configurations in Tables 1 and 2: every evaluated combination for Llama-3-8B and Llama-3-8B-Instruct is positive or neutral, with peaks of +24.8% on OASST1 (Llama-3-8B, GazeConcat/fcomb_2.2) and +21.3% on HelpSteer2 (Llama-3-8B, GazeAdd/fcomb_1).

- **Comprehensive ablation design**: The paper systematically crosses ET prediction model × feature subset × integration method × base model × dataset, yielding a thorough empirical map of design choices that is genuinely informative for practitioners interested in this direction.

- **Scalability insight**: Using frozen ET prediction models to generate synthetic gaze features removes the need for real eye-tracking hardware at both training and inference time, making the approach deployable at scale — a meaningful practical advantage noted correctly in Section 3.1.

- **Informative finding on feature importance**: The ablation reveals that fewer ET features (TRT alone or TRT+FFD) consistently outperform the full five-feature set (fcomb_2.5 sometimes degrades below baseline), with this pattern corroborated by prior literature on TRT/FFD correlating with transformer attention. This is a genuinely useful and non-obvious finding.

- **Differential benefit by alignment status**: Unaligned models (Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B) show >20% gains while the aligned Llama-3-8B-Instruct shows more modest gains, a coherent finding that ET features provide the most value when alignment priors are absent.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper's core experiments do show positive results, and the methods are coherent. However, the absence of key ablations means the *mechanism* for those results is unverified, which rises to Major level.

### Major

- **Missing ablation against text complexity baselines — the central mechanistic claim is unverified.** The ET features used (TRT, FFD, GPT, nFix, fixProp) are generated by frozen models trained on psycholinguistic reading corpora and are deterministic functions of token-level properties: word frequency, syntactic complexity, and predictability. They are text complexity proxies, not recordings of actual human readers engaging with these specific preference pairs. The paper never tests whether comparable gains could be achieved by feeding equivalent readability metrics (FKGL, Flesch reading ease, word frequency, surprisal from a language model) into the same MLP projector. Until this ablation is run, the reported gains could reflect "text complexity features help reward modeling" rather than "eye-tracking as cognitive feedback helps reward modeling." Section 3.1 frames the approach as capturing "underlying cognitive and perceptual processes," but no human ever read these texts — this framing is not earned. This is the most important missing experiment in the paper.

- **Sub-random baselines on RewardBench undermine the headline 40%+ gains.** Llama-3-8B-Instruct achieves 46.9% and Mistral-7B achieves 41.2% as baselines on RewardBench (Table 3) — both below chance for pairwise classification. Any auxiliary feature that perturbs a degenerate model away from a near-constant-output state will produce large measured gains; the +46.9% for Mistral going from 41.2% to 60.5% is not evidence that ET features are powerful, but that the baseline is broken. The paper acknowledges the evaluation is a "side contribution" and not intended for direct comparison with larger RMs, but the existence of sub-chance baselines should be explained: is this distribution shift from OASST1 to RewardBench? A calibration artifact? A QLoRA training instability? Without this explanation, Table 3 cannot be interpreted.

- **Missing Mistral-7B GazeAdd results with no explanation.** Tables 1, 2, and 3 consistently show "−" for all GazeAdd combinations applied to Mistral-7B. The paper never explains this. Was GazeAdd computationally infeasible for Mistral-7B? Did all results fail to converge or improve? Or did they show degradation and were omitted? This is a systematic gap across three tables that amounts to a one-third reduction in the experimental coverage for one of three base models.

### Minor

- **No statistical significance testing on modestly sized test sets.** OASST1 and HelpSteer2 test splits contain 416 and 364 examples respectively. At n=416, a 95% CI for 60% accuracy spans roughly ±4.7 percentage points. Multiple comparisons within tables (e.g., GazeConcat/fcomb_1 at 68.5% vs. GazeAdd/fcomb_2.2 at 70.0% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct on OASST1) are within this margin. The paper reports no confidence intervals and no significance tests, making fine-grained comparisons between configurations unreliable.

- **The severe degradation of GazeAdd/fcomb_2.5 (−19.6% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct on OASST1) is dismissed without analysis.** The paper attributes this to reading-time features being most informative, but this does not explain why adding more features causes a nearly 20-point drop. MLP projector overfitting, tokenizer remapping noise, or embedding space misalignment are candidate explanations that would help users understand method robustness and when it fails.

### Trivial
- The OASST1 filtering criterion ("two most distinct responses") is underspecified — "distinct" by what measure? A brief clarification would help reproducibility.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- **End-to-end RLHF evaluation**: The paper acknowledges in Section 5.1 that RM accuracy gains do not necessarily translate to better aligned LLM outputs. A downstream evaluation (win rates against a baseline-aligned model, MT-Bench, or even a small-scale PPO run) would transform this from a promising signal to a demonstrated improvement in alignment quality.

- **Analysis of ET feature distributions over chosen vs. rejected responses**: A scatter plot or ANOVA showing whether ET feature values differ systematically between preferred and rejected responses before any model training would clarify whether ET features carry preference-relevant signal, and what kind (fluency proxy? attention signal?).

- **Multiple runs with different seeds**: With small test sets, run-to-run variance is non-trivial. At minimum, three seeds would establish that reported differences are above the noise floor.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **[Harsh Critic, Weakness 1 partially]** The claim that the mechanism is "structurally invalid" is too strong. The paper is entirely transparent that ET features are synthetic (Section 3.1, Impact Statement). Using model-generated proxies for an auxiliary signal is a recognized and principled approach (similar in spirit to RLAIF). The weakness is retained as Major because the ablation against text-complexity baselines is missing, but the framing of "structurally invalid" is not fair. The paper does not claim its ET models recorded actual humans reading these texts.

- **[Harsh Critic, Weakness 2, partial]** The claim that baselines being "near-random makes percentage gains misleading" is partially valid but overstated for the main OASST1 and HelpSteer2 tables. On OASST1, Llama-3-8B-Instruct baseline is 64.6% (reasonable), Mistral-7B is 62.0% (reasonable), and Llama-3-8B is 58.6% (below strong published models but not catastrophically so). The critique is strongest for RewardBench (retained as Major above) and weaker for Tables 1–2.

- **[Harsh Critic, formatting/nitpick items]** Concerns about tokenizer mismatch boundary artifacts near segment boundaries (sliding window approach) and remapping quality are speculative — the paper describes the remapping procedure in Section 3.2 and there is no evidence these are generating meaningful artifacts in the results.

- **[Strength Finder, removed strength]** "Substantial and consistent accuracy improvements" as a standalone strength is qualified by the sub-random RewardBench baselines and the missing Mistral-7B GazeAdd data. The strength is partially retained above (consistent improvements on OASST1 and HelpSteer2 for the covered configurations) but not for RewardBench.

## Novel Insights

The observation that synthetic ET features trained on academic reading corpora can improve reward modeling for LLM chat preferences — even though there is zero domain overlap between ET training texts and LLM responses — suggests that low-level psycholinguistic readability signals may carry transferable preference signal across domains. Whether this is because preferred LLM responses are genuinely more fluent/readable, or simply because text complexity correlates with preference labels in these particular datasets, remains an open empirical question. Resolving this would be a concrete and interesting contribution to understanding what reward models actually learn from auxiliary signals.

## Suggestions

1. **Run the text-complexity ablation**: Train the same GazeConcat/GazeAdd architectures using FKGL, Flesch reading ease, per-token surprisal (from a small LM), and average word frequency as the auxiliary features. If these match ET gains, reframe the paper as "text complexity features improve reward modeling, and ET-derived features are one good source of such signals." If ET features outperform them, the cognitive framing is vindicated.

2. **Explain the RewardBench sub-random baselines**: Add a brief analysis in Section 4.2 attributing the below-chance baselines to distribution shift, and show that the GazeReward improvement is meaningful in an absolute sense (e.g., compare to the RewardBench leaderboard entries trained similarly).

3. **Report or explain the missing Mistral-7B GazeAdd rows**: If GazeAdd was not run for Mistral-7B, say so and give the reason. If it was run and failed, report those numbers — negative results are informative.

4. **Add variance across seeds**: Even two additional seeds per configuration would substantially strengthen the claim that improvements are reliable rather than lucky.
