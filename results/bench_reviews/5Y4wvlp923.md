## Summary

This paper identifies semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations (trend vs. seasonal components) and proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric that measures component recoverability, alongside an asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) mechanism that dynamically reweights contrastive losses based on the asymmetry factor Δ. The method is built on top of CoST and evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets for forecasting.

## Strengths

- **Novel diagnostic concept (SDE and Δ factor).** The SDE metric provides a principled, component-level measure of whether trend and seasonal information are equally recoverable from a learned embedding. The synthetic experiment (Table 1) convincingly shows that TS2Vec embeddings are sensitive to amplitude ratios and that Δ captures the resulting imbalance. This diagnostic lens is a genuine addition to the time-series representation learning toolbox.

- **CoST+APW improves forecasting on multiple settings.** On ETTm1 (all five horizons), ETT1 (horizons 168, 336), ETT2 (most horizons), and Elec (horizons 24, 48), CoST+APW achieves lower MSE than vanilla CoST. For instance, ETTm1 horizon 288: 0.056 vs 0.067 (−16%), and ETT1 horizon 168: 0.101 vs 0.107 (−5.6%). These are meaningful gains that suggest the weighting mechanism can help in some semantic-imbalance regimes.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **SDE values are promised but never reported for the proposed method.** Section 4.4.4 states that "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance," but Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE or Δ values appear for any dataset or method. The paper also claims "consistently lower SDE values" (line 303) without providing this evidence. Since the core technical claim is that APW reduces SDE, the evaluation is incomplete: the mechanism's intended effect is not directly measured on real data.

- **Results are mixed and the failure cases are not analyzed.** CoST+APW degrades relative to vanilla CoST on Electricity at horizons 168/336/720 (e.g., 0.566 vs 0.425 at horizon 168) and on Weather at horizons 336/720 (0.266 vs 0.256; 0.299 vs 0.278). The paper provides no analysis of *why* reweighting hurts on datasets with strong periodicity, nor does it discuss conditions under which the method should be applied. This weakens the claim of broad effectiveness.

- **The "pluggable" claim is contradicted by an unablated MLP fusion layer.** The abstract states the method "can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes," yet Section 4.4.2 introduces a learnable MLP that concatenates and transforms trend/seasonal embeddings to produce a composite embedding. This MLP is necessary because CoST lacks a combined encoder for the full signal, making it an architectural modification, not a plug-in. The paper never ablate this component: are the gains from the weighting, the MLP, or both? Even the Conclusion (Section 5) acknowledges this as an open question ("Second, we aim to analyze the role of the fusion MLP in greater depth"), confirming the analysis is absent.

### Minor

- **Diagnostic experiments are confounded by architecture changes.** Section 4.3 tests SDE regularization on TS2Vec (Table 2) while Section 4.4 tests APW on CoST (Table 3). These differ in both the method *and* the base architecture. A clean ablation would apply the weighting mechanism and the regularization to the *same* architecture to isolate which intervention drives the improvement. Without this control, the claim that "regularization fails while weighting succeeds" conflates architecture differences with method differences.

- **SDE is called three different names.** "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3). This inconsistency hurts reproducibility and clarity. The formulas are similar but the shifting terminology suggests the definition is not fully settled.

- **The hyperparameters γ, γ' are introduced without ablation.** Section 3.4 defines γ, γ' > 0 but never specifies how they are set, whether they are tuned per dataset, or how sensitive the method is to their values. This makes it hard to assess the robustness of the weighting scheme.

### Trivial

- Table 3 lists "ETT1" twice (horizons 24–720 then 24–672) without distinguishing ETT1h vs ETTm1 in the dataset column, making the table harder to interpret.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Reporting Δ evolution during training would provide direct evidence that the weighting mechanism behaves as intended.
- Testing the weighting mechanism on TS2Vec or TNC (without a fusion MLP) would support the "pluggable" claim.

## Suggestions

1. **Report SDE and Δ values for CoST+APW and all baselines on every real dataset.** This is the single most important fix: without it, the paper's central claim rests on indirect evidence.
2. **Ablate the MLP fusion layer.** Compare CoST + weight*ing* (without MLP, using a simpler composite embedding) vs CoST + weighting + MLP to disentangle the two contributions.
3. **Analyze the failure cases on Electricity.** Why does rebalancing hurt when periodicity is strong? A diagnostic analysis (e.g., plotting Δ dynamics during training) would strengthen the paper.
4. **Standardize SDE terminology** to a single name throughout.
5. **Report hyperparameter sensitivity** for γ, γ' and describe how they were chosen.
6. **Clarify Table 3** by renaming the dataset rows (ETT1h vs ETTm1).
