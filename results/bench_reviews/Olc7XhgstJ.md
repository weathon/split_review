## Summary

This paper proposes **Steady Thought (ST)**, a three-stage framework to mitigate "under-thinking" in Large Reasoning Models — the tendency to find a correct reasoning path early but then abandon it through excessive thought-switching. The method first segments model responses into thoughts using entropy-based detection, then generates switch-free completions of individual thoughts via logit suppression, and finally applies a thought-level preference optimization loss (STPO, a SimPO variant) that teaches the model to commit to promising thoughts rather than switch away. Experiments across three model scales (1.5B, 8B, 14B) and four benchmarks show accuracy improvements of up to 5.3% with token reductions of up to 39.3%.

## Strengths

- **Novel thought-level preference optimization framework**: The core idea — constructing preference pairs at the granularity of individual thoughts rather than full responses, and conditioning the preference loss on the shared prefix (Q, T_i) — is creative and well-motivated. Equation 7 operationalizes this cleanly as a SimPO-style loss applied at the divergence point. Table 4 validates that STPO outperforms both SFT and standard DPO on the same data, confirming the value of the thought-level formulation.

- **Consistent accuracy gains with substantial token reduction across model scales**: Table 1 shows ST improves overall accuracy by 1.9–3.12 percentage points while reducing tokens by 17.3–25.5% across 1.5B, 8B, and 14B models. The pattern holds across MATH-500, AIME 2024, GSM8K, and (partially) LiveCode — this is non-trivial breadth for a training-based method.

- **Mechanistic evidence of reduced wasteful switching**: Table 2 shows the percentage of correct intermediate thoughts (a proxy for invalid switches) drops sharply after ST — e.g., from 54.90% to 40.40% on MATH-500 for the 1.5B model, and from 45.20% to 39.00% on AIME 2024 for the 8B model. This directly supports the claimed mechanism: the model abandons fewer correct thoughts.

- **Careful ablation on training objective**: Table 4 compares SFT, DPO, and STPO on the same preference data, isolating the contribution of the preference optimization formulation. STPO achieves the best accuracy-token tradeoff, providing evidence that the optimization objective (not just the data construction) matters.

- **Thought segmentation tuned with sensitivity analysis**: Table 3 explores three entropy thresholds (2.8, 3.0, 3.2) for the 1.5B model on both MATH-500 and AIME 2024, showing the method's sensitivity to this hyperparameter and justifying the chosen value of 3.0.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Missing ablation on logit suppression during Thought Completion (Section 3.2)**: The "chosen" completions T_i' are generated by forcibly suppressing switch-trigger tokens ("wait," "alternatively") during decoding. This is an artificial constraint — the completions the model is trained to prefer were produced under a decoding hack the model never learned organically. There is no ablation comparing against completions generated without logit suppression (e.g., using an instruction prompt to "continue without switching"). Without this ablation, it is impossible to determine whether ST's gains come from learning to commit to thoughts or from imitating side-effects of the forced non-switching decoding. This is a core component of the pipeline, and its contribution is unexamined.

- **SEAL outperforms ST on the OOD LiveCode benchmark for Qwen3-8B**: On LiveCode, SEAL achieves 83.4% vs. ST's 77.1% for Qwen3-8B (Table 1) — a 6.3 percentage point gap. The paper claims "strong generalization" and highlights ST's LiveCode improvements (e.g., +5.3% over vanilla), but does not discuss or acknowledge that a competing baseline substantially outperforms ST on this task. This selective reporting undermines the generalization claim.

- **Suspicious NOWAIT baseline behavior on Qwen3-8B**: NOWAIT is supposed to reduce tokens by suppressing switch-trigger words, yet on Qwen3-8B it shows an 84.6% token *increase* (from 6122 to 11300 average tokens) along with massive accuracy drops (e.g., MATH-500 drops from 91.4% to 61.0%). This is inconsistent with NOWAIT's typical behavior (and with its performance on the 1.5B and 14B models in the same table, where it does reduce tokens). This suggests a possible implementation error or configuration issue with the baseline, which casts doubt on the fairness of the comparison for the 8B model — where ST's claimed gains are largest.

### Minor

- **Circularity concern in thought segmentation**: The entropy-based segmentation rule (Section 3.1) uses the model's own token-level entropy to detect thought boundaries, then ST trains the model — potentially changing its entropy distribution. After training, the same segmentation rule may detect different boundaries, making it unclear whether the reported reductions in thought count and increases in "last thought proportion" reflect genuine behavioral change or merely entropy-distribution shift. While this does not invalidate the accuracy gains (which are measured independently of segmentation), it complicates the mechanistic interpretation in Sections 4.4.1–4.4.2.

- **Preference pair construction edge case not discussed (Section 3.3)**: When the original response is correct (the model eventually reaches the right answer after switching), the rejected portion (T_{i+1}, ..., T_n) also leads to a correct answer. The preference signal becomes: "stick with T_i and get the answer faster" rather than "T_i is correct and switching is wrong." The paper does not discuss what fraction of training pairs fall into this case or how it affects the optimization signal.

- **No confidence intervals or significance tests**: AIME 2024 has only 30 problems; a 3–4 percentage point change corresponds to roughly one more problem solved. While the paper averages over eight runs, no confidence intervals, standard deviations, or significance tests are reported for any accuracy or token-count results, making it difficult to assess whether differences exceed stochastic variation — particularly on the smaller test sets.

- **Entropy threshold tuned only on 1.5B model**: Table 3 shows tuning for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. The paper states "We provide threshold tuning results on more models and datasets in the appendix D," but the appendix is stripped from the submission. The optimal threshold may be model-dependent, and the claim of general applicability would be stronger with evidence that the 3.0 threshold transfers across models.

### Trivial

- The formalization in Section 2.1 (steadiness score, Bradley-Terry model, Equation 2) is presented prominently but never directly operationalized — the actual method is SimPO conditioned on (Q, T_i). The formalism provides motivation but the disconnect between the formal setup and the implemented loss is noticeable.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An experiment on problems where the first promising thought is a dead end (requiring the model to deliberately switch) would directly test the claim that ST "preserves flexibility to explore." If ST merely reduces switching globally, performance on such problems should degrade.

- Characterizing how the model's entropy distribution changes after ST training would help address the circularity concern in thought segmentation.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:

- **Harsh Critic Claim: "The formalism does not contribute to the method and could be omitted"** — While the Bradley-Terry formalization is not directly operationalized in the loss function, it serves as conceptual motivation and grounds the problem in a preference optimization framework. This is a presentation choice, not a weakness. Moved to Trivial.

- **Harsh Critic Claim: "The dependency on the delimiter '.\n\n' is arbitrary and not validated"** — The delimiter is a standard sentence/paragraph boundary marker used as a pre-segmentation heuristic; the primary segmentation signal comes from entropy. This is a reasonable engineering choice and not a substantive weakness.

- **Harsh Critic Claim: "The choice of switch-trigger words is not justified by quantitative analysis"** — The words "wait" and "alternatively" are drawn from prior work (Wang et al. 2025a,c) that identified them as common switch-trigger tokens. The paper appropriately cites these sources.

- **Harsh Critic Claim: "The proportion of last thought metric could result from truncating earlier exploration"** — While the metric alone is ambiguous, the paper pairs it with accuracy improvements. If the model were merely truncating, accuracy would drop. The combination of higher accuracy + higher last-thought proportion supports the claimed mechanism.

- **Strength Finder Claim: "Thought completion via logit suppression provides clean training signal"** — This is the method description, not a strength. The logit suppression is actually the source of a valid methodological concern (no ablation). Removed.

- **Strength Finder Claim: "Entropy-based thought segmentation is a principled, hand-tuned component"** — While the tuning is shown, calling the segmentation "principled" overstates it; the choice of delimiter and entropy threshold is heuristic. The actual strength is the empirical tuning (Table 3).

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The paper's central insight — that under-thinking can be addressed through thought-level preference optimization rather than global switch suppression — is well-articulated and represents a genuine conceptual advance over prior work.

## Suggestions

- Add an ablation where chosen completions are generated *without* logit suppression (e.g., prompt: "Continue this reasoning without switching to a new approach"). If ST still works, the core claim is strengthened. If not, the paper should acknowledge that the gains depend on the decoding hack and reframe its contribution accordingly.

- Discuss and explain the anomalous NOWAIT result on Qwen3-8B. If it reflects an implementation issue, correct it or at minimum acknowledge the limitation.

- Report confidence intervals for accuracy and token counts, particularly for AIME 2024 (n=30). Even simple ±1 SEM based on the 8-run average would substantially strengthen the evidential claims.

- Discuss the LiveCode result where SEAL outperforms ST on Qwen3-8B (83.4% vs 77.1%). This is a genuine negative result that readers deserve to see discussed, not elided.

- Clarify what fraction of training preference pairs come from cases where the original response was already correct (model eventually reached the right answer after switching), and discuss whether this creates conflicting optimization signals.
