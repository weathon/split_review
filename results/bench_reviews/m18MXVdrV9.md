## Summary

INFO-SEDD introduces a novel method for estimating mutual information and entropy on high-dimensional discrete data by expressing KL divergences through Continuous Time Markov Chains (CTMCs) with absorbing states. The method leverages score functions from discrete diffusion models, requiring only a single joint-distribution model (thanks to an elegant absorbing-state trick) and seamlessly integrating with pretrained discrete diffusion backbones like MDLM and CADUCEUS. The paper demonstrates strong synthetic benchmark performance where competitors fail dramatically at high MI and high dimensionality, and shows practical utility on text summarization model selection and genomics motif discovery.

## Strengths

- **Dominant synthetic benchmark performance at scale (Table 1):** INFO-SEDD recovers MI accurately across the full range (e.g., 47.77 ± 1.18 vs. ground truth 50 for MI=50, D=50), while all competitors collapse — SMILE gives 18.97, MINDE gives 32.60, and GAN-DIME gives 17.27. The extreme-dimensionality experiments (D=1024) in Appendix C.1.7 further confirm this, with all competitors collapsing to zero while INFO-SEDD gives 0.53 (ground truth 0.5). This directly supports the central claim that the method uniquely handles high-MI, high-dimensional discrete regimes.

- **Clever absorbing-state CTMC trick for single-model marginal scores (Eq. 6, Appendix A.3):** The proof shows that with absorbing transition kernels, marginal scores can be read directly from a model trained only on the joint distribution. This enables INFO-SEDD-J to estimate MI between arbitrary sub-sequences without retraining, demonstrated concretely in the TATA-BOX motif discovery experiment (Figure 5) where sliding-window MI profiles would otherwise require separate models per window.

- **Theoretical error decomposition (Eq. 7, Appendix E):** The bound separates estimation error (linear in score-network error) from truncation bias (exponentially decaying in σ̄(T)). Critically, unlike importance-sampling approaches, the error does not grow exponentially with the true KL divergence — this is the key theoretical advantage in high-MI regimes. The consistency argument via the Continuous Mapping Theorem (Appendix E.3) is also rigorous.

- **Meaningful real-world validation:** In text summarization, INFO-SEDD-C's MI estimates correlate r=0.740 (Pearson) with human consistency judgments (Table 2), outperforming KL-DIME (0.214), HD-DIME (0.331), and SMILE (-0.074). In genomics, the sliding-window MI profile successfully recovers the known TATA-BOX location in Arabidopsis thaliana promoters (Figure 5). The Ising model entropy estimation (Appendix D.1) provides additional validation against a closed-form ground truth.

- **Empirical robustness demonstrated through ablations:** Appendix C.1.5 shows accurate MI estimates with as few as 10³ samples; Appendix C.1.6 demonstrates stable performance as support size |χ| increases from 12 to 132 while competitors' errors grow; Appendix C.1.7 tests extreme dimensionality (D=1024). These go beyond a single experimental condition.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None. The core derivation (Eq. 2 → Eq. 4, detailed in Appendix A.1) is mathematically correct. The equation KL(p₀ ∥ q₀) = E[log(→p₀(←X_T) / →q₀(←X_T))] uses the reverse-process state ←X_T (distributed as ←p_T = →p₀), not the forward-process state. All subsequent steps flow from this valid starting point.

### Major

None that would threaten acceptance. The paper's core claims are well-supported.

### Minor

- **Synthetic data construction relies on independence-based additivity for high-dimensional benchmarks (Appendix C.1.1):** The paper concatenates independent noise components and applies Cantor's pairing to scale MI and dimensionality. While all methods face identical data (making the comparison fair), the dependence structure is different from what arises in real-world discrete data. The real-data experiments partially address this, but a synthetic benchmark with non-trivial dependence (e.g., generated by a small Markov chain) would strengthen confidence. This does not invalidate the results — INFO-SEDD's dominance is too stark to be an artifact — but limits how much one can extrapolate from synthetic results alone.

- **Real-data evaluation lacks ground-truth MI:** The text summarization consistency test assumes linearity in ρ based on entropy-rate estimates from the literature, and the genomics test uses a classifier-based approximation with strong assumptions (Appendix C.4.1). The paper is honest about these limitations and the observed trends are convincing, but the evidence for absolute accuracy on real data is necessarily weaker than on synthetic data. This is largely an inherent limitation of the domain, not a methodological flaw.

- **No sensitivity analysis on score-model quality:** The paper relies on pretrained score models (MDLM, CADUCEUS) or trains from scratch, but does not investigate how estimation quality degrades with model capacity, training budget, or approximation error. While the theoretical bound (Eq. 7) addresses this analytically, an empirical ablation would strengthen practical guidance.

### Trivial

- The arrow notation (→ vs. ←) distinguishing forward and reverse processes, while well-defined in the preliminaries, may confuse readers not paying careful attention — this led to a reviewer misunderstanding the derivation. A brief reminder in Section 2.2 would help.

## Nice-to-Haves

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.

- **Harsh Critic #1 — "Core KL estimator contains a foundational error":** This is a misreading of the notation. The paper uses ←X_T (reverse process at time T) which is distributed as ←p_T = →p₀, not →X_T (forward process). The equality KL(p₀ ∥ q₀) = E[log(→p₀(←X_T)/→q₀(←X_T))] is correct: the reverse process at time T recovers samples from the initial distribution. The full derivation in Appendix A.1 (lines 1517–2236) goes through Dynkin's formula step by step and is rigorous. The claim that "X_T is distributed according to p_T (not p₀)" confuses the forward process with the reverse process. **Removed — factually incorrect.**

- **Harsh Critic #2 — "Synthetic experiments give INFO-SEDD an unfair advantage":** The claim that independent-noise construction "factorizes trivially" and gives INFO-SEDD an unfair advantage is not supported. All methods see exactly the same data. If the construction were trivially easy, the variational baselines would also perform well — they don't. INFO-SEDD's advantage comes from the method, not the data. **Removed — the comparison is fair by construction.**

## Novel Insights

The most striking insight from the reviews is methodological: INFO-SEDD's approach inverts the standard relationship between generative modeling and information estimation. Typically, MI estimators are built separately from generative models. Here, the CTMC framework reveals that score functions — the workhorse of discrete diffusion generative models — directly encode the information needed for KL divergence estimation. This means that any domain with a good pretrained discrete diffusion model (text, DNA, protein sequences) immediately gains access to MI estimation without additional architectural engineering. The absorbing-state trick (Eq. 6) deepens this insight: with the right perturbation process, a single model trained on joint distributions yields both joint and marginal scores, enabling arbitrary subset MI computation. This principled connection between two seemingly separate research threads is the paper's most original and potentially impactful contribution.

## Suggestions

- Add a one-sentence reminder in Section 2.2 clarifying that ←X_T (reverse process) is distributed as →p₀, to prevent notation confusion.
- Include an empirical study (even in appendix) on how MI estimates vary with score-model quality, providing practical guidance for practitioners.
- Consider a synthetic benchmark with dependent structure from a small discrete Markov chain or Ising-like model, complementing the independence-based construction.
- Report Monte Carlo variance for the time integral, even if only for one representative experimental setting.
