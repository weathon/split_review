## Summary

This paper proposes DIPOLE (Dichotomous Diffusion Policy Improvement), an RL algorithm for fine-tuning diffusion policies. The core idea is to design a greedified KL-regularized objective whose optimal policy decomposes into two "dichotomous" policies — a positive policy (reward-maximizing) weighted by σ(βG) and a negative policy (reward-minimizing) weighted by 1−σ(βG) — both trained with bounded sigmoid weights instead of the unbounded exponential weights of standard KL-regularized RL. During inference, actions are generated via a linear combination of the dichotomous score functions, resembling classifier-free guidance and enabling controllable greediness through a parameter ω. The method is evaluated on 39 RL tasks (ExORL and OGBench) and scaled to a 1B-parameter VLA model for autonomous driving on NAVSIM.

## Strengths

- **Novel dichotomous decomposition with practical benefits.** The paper identifies a genuine limitation of exp-weighted regression for diffusion policies (loss explosion, sample dominance) and proposes an elegant remedy: replacing the unbounded exponential weight with a pair of bounded sigmoid-weighted objectives. This is clean, principled, and directly addresses a known problem. The derivation from the greedified KL-regularized objective (Eq. 5) through Theorem 1 to the dichotomous policies (Eq. 7–8) is algebraically correct and follows standard KL-regularized RL machinery.

- **Principled connection to classifier-free guidance.** Showing that the optimal policy's score function reduces to a linear combination of the dichotomous scores (Eq. 10) — i.e., ̃ε = (1+ω)ε⁺ − ωε⁻ — and that this mirrors classifier-free guidance is a genuinely insightful observation. It provides both a theoretical justification for inference-time controllability and a clean implementation recipe.

- **Strong and broad empirical validation.** DIPOLE is evaluated on 39 tasks across ExORL and OGBench, with 8 seeds per experiment, against strong baselines including IQL, ReBRAC, CFGRL, IFQL, FQL, and IDQL. It achieves the best or near-best performance on most tasks (e.g., ExORL Table 1: DIPOLE surpasses all baselines on 7 of 9 tasks). The offline-to-online results (Table 3) show substantial improvements (e.g., humanoidmaze: 61→97).

- **Scalability to real-world-scale models.** Fine-tuning a 1B-parameter VLA driving model with DIPOLE on the NAVSIM navtrain split yields a meaningful improvement (PDMS 88.3→89.7), and the method integrates cleanly via LoRA modules for the dichotomous policies. This demonstrates that the approach is not limited to small-scale RL experiments.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation emerging across the reviews is that DIPOLE's score-space combination (Eq. 10) resembles classifier-free guidance not just superficially but structurally — the positive policy serves as the "conditional" model and the negative policy as the "unconditional" model in the CFG analogy, but with the crucial difference that both are learned from data with opposite reward-oriented weighting rather than being trained with and without conditioning. This suggests a deeper connection between RL-based policy improvement and guidance in generative models that goes beyond what prior work (CFGRL) established. The fact that this structure emerges naturally from a KL-regularized objective rather than being hand-designed is a point worth emphasizing. The negative policy's role as an explicit "repulsive" component that pushes the combined policy toward higher-return regions is conceptually novel and, if empirically verified, could motivate similar decomposition strategies in other RL settings.
