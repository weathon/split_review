## Summary

The paper presents two intertwined contributions: (1) **ACTOR**, an LLM-powered agent that simulates long-horizon human behavior in 3D household scenes via a perceive-plan-act loop incorporating hierarchical behavior priors, customizable real-valued and language-based value functions, and MCTS-based planning; and (2) **BehaviorHub**, a large-scale automatically constructed dataset of 10k scene-aware human behavior samples (linguistic plans + aligned 3D motions) spanning 1.5k scenes. BehaviorHub's utility is independently validated through improvements on downstream motion generation tasks (PROX, HumanML3D). ACTOR is evaluated on BehaviorHub's benchmark against LLM-based baselines and through systematic ablation.

---

## Strengths

- **Independent downstream validation of BehaviorHub (§6.3, Table 4):** Pre-training on BehaviorHub significantly improves scene-aware motion generation (FID 11.72→8.38 on PROX) and language-conditioned generation (FID 1.24→0.87 on HumanML3D). These benchmarks use independent human-annotated ground truth entirely unrelated to BehaviorHub's LLM-generated content, providing genuine evidence that the dataset captures useful behavior structure.

- **Systematic ablation validating each architectural component (Table 3a):** One-pass LLM baseline achieves GSR of 0.143; adding active tree search, then hierarchical prior, then value function progressively raises this to 0.389. Each component is individually necessary, and the comparison is among systems that all use LLMs, making the bias from LLM-generated references symmetrical across conditions and the improvements more credible.

- **MCTS outperforms simpler search alternatives (Table 3b):** MCTS achieves a GSR of 0.389 vs. 0.286 for greedy search and comparable improvements over beam search, supporting the claim that active search with a large branching factor benefits environment-aware planning.

- **Scale and scene-grounding of BehaviorHub (§5.3):** 10k samples across 1.5k 3D scenes with 15.7 average plan steps, combining linguistic plans with physically-aligned motion sequences (8.6M frames). The motion alignment pipeline (MOVER-based contact/collision optimization + Transformer-based blending + human verification) is a concrete engineering contribution beyond pure language generation.

- **Modular scalability demonstrated (Table 3c):** Performance improves monotonically from Vicuna-7B to GPT-3.5 to GPT-4, confirming that the framework architecture generalizes across LLM backbones and is not a one-model artifact.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Circular evaluation structure for automated metrics (§5.1, §6.1, §5.4):** The reference plans in BehaviorHub are generated by GPT-4/GPT-3.5 (§5.1), and ACTOR itself is powered by GPT-4/GPT-3.5 (§4.3). Evaluation via Sentence-BLEU and BERTScore (§5.4) therefore measures stylistic alignment with the same model family's outputs. An LLM-based system will naturally produce text more similar to LLM-generated references than non-LLM text would. This inflates ACTOR's BLEU/BERTScore advantage over LLMaP and HuggingGPT beyond what reflects genuine planning quality. The human evaluation in Table 2 partially mitigates this concern, but it involves only five participants on 300 samples with no inter-annotator agreement reported, making its reliability unclear. The ablation in Table 3a is less affected by this concern (all baselines are LLM-based), but the main comparison in Table 1 overstates the headline improvement.

- **Comparison baselines lack scene-grounding capability, making "nearly doubling GSR" misleading (§6.1):** The paper explicitly states that "LLMaP is designed to operate solely on textual inputs, and lacks the capability to perform behavior simulation" (§6.1). HuggingGPT is similarly not adapted for 3D household environments. The comparison therefore validates that ACTOR has the right components (scene perception + value functions + motion generation) versus baselines missing the entire simulation layer — not that ACTOR's specific planning strategy (MCTS, hierarchical prior, value functions) outperforms alternative strategies with equivalent grounding. A minimally-grounded LLM baseline (same scene description fed to a one-pass GPT call without MCTS/value functions) is what the evaluation most needs. The current ablation's "bare baseline model" (row 1 of Table 3a) partially serves this role but is not presented as a standalone competitor.

### Minor

- **MCTS rollout depth of 3 is unexplained relative to average plan length of 15.7 steps (§4.3, §5.3):** Section 4.3 sets "maximum rollout depth of the search algorithm, which is set to 3," while §5.3 reports average plans of 15.7 steps. The paper provides no explanation of how MCTS with depth 3 navigates sequences of this length. The likely answer is that MCTS operates iteratively (each call expands the next decision node), but this is not stated. Without this clarification, the relationship between MCTS depth and plan quality in the ablation (Table 3b) cannot be interpreted.

- **Empirical probability mapping for language-based value functions has no ablation (§4.2):** The conversion of LLM classification labels (sure / more-likely / less-likely / impossible) to probabilities (1.0 / 0.7 / 0.3 / 0.01) is described as "empirically converted" with no justification or sensitivity analysis. This mapping is a free parameter that directly shapes which plans are selected. Even a brief ablation of alternative mappings would help verify that performance is not sensitive to this hand-tuned choice.

- **Human evaluation lacks inter-annotator agreement (Table 2):** Five participants on 300 samples with a 5-point Likert scale and no reported IAA or statistical significance. With this few annotators and no reliability metric, it is difficult to assess whether the observed gaps (e.g., ACTOR 3.05 vs. HuggingGPT 2.71 on Completeness) are reliable.

### Trivial

- **BERTScore diversity filter (<0.5 threshold) treats cross-goal and within-goal diversity equivalently (§5.1):** Plans for entirely different goals would trivially pass this filter regardless of internal diversity. This is a minor methodological imprecision in the diversity argument but does not affect evaluation results.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Fully scene-grounded LLM baseline:** The single highest-value ablation would compare ACTOR against a one-pass LLM baseline that receives the same scene-description string ACTOR receives, but without tree search, value functions, or hierarchical prior. This would isolate how much gain comes from "giving an LLM the scene context" versus ACTOR's specific planning machinery.

- **Decomposed downstream analysis (§6.3):** The downstream task experiments are the paper's most convincing result but do not show which aspect of BehaviorHub drives the gain — whether pre-training on the linguistic plans alone, the aligned motions, or the combination. A brief ablation here would sharpen the dataset design claims.

- **Discussion of data generation failure modes:** The paper does not discuss how often motion alignment (§5.2) produces physically implausible or discontinuous sequences that pass human verification, which would be useful to know for downstream practitioners.

## Novel Insights

The paper's strongest contribution is arguably not the ACTOR architecture per se but the demonstration in §6.3 that automatically LLM-generated, scene-grounded, motion-aligned data transfers to improve performance on independently evaluated downstream tasks. This suggests that LLM-bootstrapped scene-conditioned data, even when generated with imperfect diversity controls, captures enough genuine human behavior statistics to benefit motion generation models. This is a non-trivial finding that goes beyond the paper's stated claims and warrants further investigation — specifically whether the motion-scene alignment component or the linguistic plan structure is the principal source of downstream benefit.
