## Summary

The paper addresses the problem of annotation dependence in multi-view diabetic retinopathy grading. It proposes two modules: (1) GALP, which self-generates "lesion proposals" from grade-conditioned evidence maps (CAM-based) using stage-wise auxiliary classifiers, eliminating the need for external lesion annotations; and (2) LGRF, a cross-view fusion mechanism that routes lesion proposals through a gated mixture-of-experts and applies Top-K-weighted cross-view attention. Evaluated on MFIDDR (four-view) and DRTiD (two-view), the method achieves 83.9% accuracy without external annotations (matching/surpassing several externally informed baselines) and 84.6% with lesion annotations (new SOTA).

## Strengths

1. **Self-derived proposals substantially reduce annotation dependence.** The core claim is directly validated: the lesion‑free variant (Ours w/o lesion, Table 1) achieves 83.9% accuracy and 83.5% F1 on MFIDDR, outperforming all end-to-end baselines and several externally informed methods such as LFMVDR (82.2%) and CVSA (82.6%). This demonstrates the practical value of generating grade-discriminative cues internally rather than relying on costly expert annotations.

2. **State-of-the-art performance when external annotations are available.** With lesion annotations incorporated via SPADE, Ours (with lesion) reaches 84.6% accuracy and 72.3% kappa on MFIDDR (Table 1), surpassing the previous best SMVDR-M (84.0%, 71.4%). Per-grade results (Table 2) also show the best F1 on Grades 2 (65.2%), 3 (74.8%), and 4 (51.6%) among all compared methods.

3. **Novel cross-view expert-guided fusion mechanism.** LGRF's design (Sec. 3.3) — dynamically gating which experts process the adjacent view's lesion proposals based on the current view's features, then fusing via Top-K-weighted cross-view attention — goes beyond standard cross-attention or fixed fusion. The load-balancing loss is a practical addition to maintain expert diversity.

4. **Generalizes across two-view and four-view settings.** The method is validated on two different datasets with different numbers of views (4-view MFIDDR and 2-view DRTiD). On DRTiD (Table 3), the end-to-end variant achieves 76.0% accuracy, surpassing all methods including externally informed CrossFIT (75.6%) and CVSA (74.7%).

5. **Ablation confirms component contributions.** Table 4 shows consistent degradation when removing each component: dropping GALP (−1.2% acc), removing the expert pool (−1.3% acc), or removing LGRF entirely (−1.6% acc). This provides evidence that both GALP and LGRF contribute to the overall performance.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Backbone differences confound the headline comparison against externally informed methods.** The proposed method uses Swin-B as its backbone, while the paper does not specify the backbones used by most externally informed baselines (CVSA, WGLIN, SMVDR, LFMVDR). For the baselines whose backbones *are* stated (MVCNN.R → ResNet-50, MVCNN.V → VGG-19), they are clearly weaker. The ablation in Table 4 shows the full proposed pipeline gains only ~1.6% accuracy over the "w/o LGRF" variant that still uses the same Swin-B backbone, yet the gap between the strongest end-to-end baseline (MVCINN, 80.1%) and the proposed method (83.9%) is 3.8%. This suggests the backbone may account for a substantial portion of the claimed advantage. Without re-baselining the competitors with the same Swin-B backbone, the paper's central claim — that self-derived proposals match or surpass externally informed SOTA *without external annotations* — is not fully supported by the evidence as presented. *Mitigation note*: The DRTiD results are cleaner (the method outperforms all baselines including externally informed ones), and the method also outperforms RETFound (ViT-Large, a strong backbone), partially mitigating this concern.

2. **Lesion proposals are never validated against actual lesion annotations.** The entire motivation for GALP rests on generating proposals that capture small, low-contrast lesions missed by standard transforms. However, the paper provides **no quantitative or qualitative evidence** that the selected Top-K regions correspond to actual lesions. The MFIDDR dataset includes lesion segmentation masks (stated in Sec. 4.1: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"), yet the paper performs no overlap analysis (e.g., IoU, Dice), no visual comparison, and no correlation study between the GEM-selected proposals and the provided masks. Without this validation, the claim that GALP provides "lesion proposals" that act as "surrogates for expert cues" remains an untested assumption — the proposals may simply be the most grade-discriminative regions (which could include non-lesion features). This weakens the mechanistic narrative of the paper, even if the empirical results are competitive.

### Minor

3. **Weak ablation baselines.** The "w/o LGRF" baseline uses simple concatenation of lesion proposals with cross-view tokens — a trivial fusion that any learned mechanism would outperform. The paper would benefit from a stronger baseline such as standard multi-head cross-attention (without expert routing) at matched parameter count. The "w/o GALP" ablation removes the entire GALP mechanism (both the auxiliary loss *and* the proposal selection), so it cannot attribute the 1.2% drop to either factor individually.

4. **Auxiliary loss not ablated separately from proposal selection.** The auxiliary loss weight λ_aux = 1.0 provides strong multi-stage supervision at three intermediate stages. Since GALP subsumes both this loss and the Top-K proposal selection, it is unclear how much of the benefit attributed to GALP comes from the auxiliary loss (deep supervision) versus the selective token reduction. An ablation that keeps the auxiliary loss but removes proposal selection (retaining all tokens) would isolate this factor.

5. **Important precision/recall tradeoff on Grade 4 (PDR) not discussed.** Table 2 reveals that Ours (w/o lesion) achieves Pre=81.8 for Grade 4 while some baselines achieve Pre=99.9, and Ours (with lesion) drops to Pre=69.6 while achieving a higher F1 (51.6 vs. 30.4 for SMVDR-M). This represents a qualitatively different clinical operating point — the method catches more PDR cases but with more false positives. The paper's claim of "superior" performance glosses over this tradeoff without discussing clinical implications.

6. **Computational cost not reported.** The MoE framework, multi-head cross-view attention across four stages, and multi-view processing add non-trivial overhead. The paper should report FLOPs, parameter counts, and/or inference time to allow practitioners to assess the practical tradeoffs.

7. **Interpretability is claimed but not demonstrated.** Contribution (2) claims "superior ... interpretability" for LGRF, and Fig. 1 lists interpretability as a benefit of the externally informed paradigm. However, the paper provides no interpretability analysis (e.g., attention map visualizations, case studies, saliency maps) to support this claim.

## Novel Insights

The reviewers' comments converge on a few key observations that go beyond the paper's own framing: (1) The claim that CAM-based proposals approximate expert lesion annotations is empirically plausible but untested — the paper has the data (masks) to settle this but does not. (2) The backbone discrepancy is not just a fairness issue; it exposes a structural tension between the paper's narrative (proposals drive performance) and the evidence (backbone choice appears to explain ~2/3 of the gap over the strongest end-to-end baseline). (3) The architecture itself — cross-view gated MoE routing conditioned on the current view's features — is a genuinely novel contribution that could generalize beyond DR grading to other multi-view medical imaging tasks, even if the current evaluation does not fully isolate its benefits from the backbone.

## Suggestions

- **Most impactful single experiment:** Re-run the strongest externally informed baseline(s) on MFIDDR with the exact same Swin-B backbone and training recipe. If the gap persists, the paper's central claim becomes much stronger.
- **Second most impactful:** Overlay the Top-K selected GALP proposals on fundus images alongside the provided lesion segmentation masks, and compute overlap metrics. If the proposals show reasonable agreement with actual lesions, the mechanistic story is supported; if not, the paper should reframe the contribution more carefully around "grade-discriminative region selection" rather than "lesion proposals."
- **Ablation clean-up:** Add a variant that retains the auxiliary loss but removes Top-K selection (all tokens), and a variant that replaces LGRF with standard cross-attention (no expert routing) at matched parameter count.
