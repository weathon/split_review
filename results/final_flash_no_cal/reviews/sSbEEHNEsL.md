## Summary

USR 2.0 addresses two key bottlenecks of the USR semi-supervised framework for unified speech recognition: (1) the computational cost of autoregressive pseudo-label generation and (2) the decoupled CTC-attention supervision that degrades out-of-distribution robustness. The core idea is **CTC-driven teacher forcing**—using greedily decoded CTC outputs from the teacher as conditioning inputs to generate attention-based pseudo-labels in a single forward pass, avoiding autoregressive decoding. A **mixed sampling** strategy (interleaving CTC-driven and AR modes at each training step with probability 0.5) mitigates the resulting exposure bias. The method halves training time, dramatically improves robustness to long utterances, noise, and cross-dataset shifts, and achieves state-of-the-art WER on LRS3, LRS2, and WildVSR across ASR, VSR, and AVSR with a single unified model.

## Strengths

- **~2× faster training.** CTC-driven teacher forcing eliminates the autoregressive decoding bottleneck during pseudo-label generation; combined with faster convergence (50 vs. 75 epochs), Figure 5 shows wall-clock training time is nearly halved across all model scales. This is a concrete, practically significant improvement over USR.

- **Large and clearly demonstrated OOD robustness gains.** On long utterances (Figure 3a), USR 2.0's greedy WER stays nearly flat (~25%) while USR's soars to >60% at 400 frames. Under additive noise (Table 1), USR 2.0 outperforms all baselines at every SNR level. On cross-dataset evaluation (Table 3), it achieves 15.4% vs. 25.3% (USR) on LibriSpeech and 73.7% vs. 80.0% (USR) on WildVSR under greedy decoding. These results directly support the claim that coupled CTC-attention supervision transfers CTC's robustness to the decoder.

- **State-of-the-art in-distribution performance with a single unified model.** USR 2.0 achieves the best reported WERs on LRS3 across ASR, VSR, and AVSR for multiple model scales, including a Huge model at 0.9% (ASR) and 0.8% (AVSR) (Table 2). It consistently outperforms USR and modality-specific self-supervised baselines that use separate models per task, an asymmetry that favors the baselines.

- **Well-designed ablation study (Table 4)** exhaustively isolates the contribution of each pseudo-label type to each branch in both CTC-driven and AR modes, convincingly showing that: (a) in CTC-driven mode, supervising the decoder with both CTC and attention PLs is crucial for both ID accuracy and OOD robustness; (b) the AR mode degrades sharply on OOD data without CTC PLs. This provides strong evidence for the design choices.

- **Mixed sampling provides a tunable and well-characterized trade-off.** Figure 4 systematically varies the AR sampling probability and reports ID WER, OOD WER, and relative training time, allowing practitioners to adjust the balance. The default 0.5 is shown to be a strong compromise.
