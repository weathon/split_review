## Summary

The paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework that repurposes SAM's output tokens for instance verification (using TS-SS similarity) and introduces a probabilistic prompt generation stage for spatial exploration. The method requires no training, no additional encoders, and no fine-tuning. On FSC-147 it achieves MAE 16.25 (best among SAM-only training-free methods), and on CARPK it achieves MAE 4.68 (outperforming all compared training-based methods).

## Strengths

1. **Novel use of SAM output tokens for counting verification.** The core idea—using SAM's decoder output tokens (rather than image embeddings) to distinguish positive from negative instances—is genuinely novel and underexplored. Tables 3 and 5 provide evidence that output token space outperforms image embedding space (MAE 16.25 vs 16.94) and that this approach works across standard similarity metrics. The PCA visualizations in Figure 5 give qualitative support for the improved separability.

2. **Competitive results within a training-free, SAM-only paradigm.** On FSC-147, the method achieves MAE 16.25, substantially improving over existing SAM-only training-free methods (TFOC 19.95, C-Anything 27.97, SAM 42.48) and even surpassing some training-based methods (C-DETR 16.79, FamNet 22.08). On CARPK, the method achieves MAE 4.68, outperforming all listed training-based methods (best: SAFECOUNT 5.33). These results are reported in Table 1 and demonstrate that a pure SAM pipeline can be competitive without training.

3. **Efficient inference with no extra models.** The method requires only SAM (no DINO, CLIP, or other auxiliary encoders), running at 1.69s per image on FSC-147. The claimed 2× speed advantage over TFCAC (which uses an additional DINO encoder) is a practical benefit, though no comparative runtime numbers are reported.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Abstract and introduction overstate results on FSC-147.** The abstract claims the method "achieves superior accuracy... outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." Looking at Table 1 on FSC-147, five training-based methods achieve lower MAE (LOCA 10.79, SAFECOUNT 14.32, BMNet+ 14.62, PseCo 13.05, Counting-DETR 16.79 vs. 16.25), and TFCAC (SAM+α) achieves 12.26. The claim is false for the primary benchmark. The body text is more measured ("competitive", "best among SAM-only"), but the abstract's global claim misrepresents the results. This undermines trust in the paper's framing and must be corrected before publication.

2. **Probabilistic prompt generation — a claimed core contribution — is not validated against a deterministic baseline.** The paper presents two key components: probabilistic prompt generation and output token verification. The ablation studies (Tables 3, 4, 5) thoroughly test the verification stage and iteration/prompt trade-offs, but never compare the probabilistic sampling strategy against a simple deterministic alternative (e.g., uniform grid, superpixel) while keeping the verification stage fixed. The paper claims the probabilistic approach "addresses the limitations of deterministic prompting methods" (Section 1), yet provides no experiment showing it is better. Without this comparison, a central claimed contribution is unsubstantiated.

3. **CARPK evaluation uses 12 exemplars without justification or comparison to baseline protocols.** The paper states: "For CARPK, following the testing procedure, we randomly selected 12 objects from the training set to use as exemplars." Few-shot counting on CARPK typically uses a small number of exemplars. The paper does not cite a standard protocol, does not state what exemplar count the compared methods used, and does not ablate with fewer exemplars. If baselines used fewer (e.g., 3), the comparison is potentially unfair. The strongest quantitative result (outperforming all supervised methods on CARPK) is difficult to evaluate without this information.

### Minor

4. **TS-SS improvement over cosine similarity is marginal and without significance testing.** Table 3 shows TS-SS achieves MAE 16.25 vs. cosine similarity 16.48 — a difference of only 0.23 MAE. No confidence intervals, error bars, or statistical tests are reported. The TS-SS selection threshold (300) is mentioned as set "based on validation experiments" but no details are given about how it was chosen, whether it was tuned per metric, or how stable it is across datasets. Given the small gain, the paper's emphasis on TS-SS as a critical contribution appears disproportionate.

5. **One-shot results are substantially weaker but not discussed.** On the one-shot FSC-147 task (Table 2), the method's RMSE is 135.86 — nearly double that of TFCAC (69.16) and LOCA (75.32) — and MAE (18.57) lags behind BMNet+ (16.89) and LOCA (12.53). The paper mentions this only briefly and provides no analysis of why false positives/negatives increase sharply in the one-shot setting, despite claiming the method "avoids similarity uncertainty."

6. **Missing implementation details and terminology inconsistencies.** The "t-scale factor of 0.066" for the SAM predictor is mentioned without explanation of what it controls or how it relates to the temperature parameter τ in Equation 1. The early stopping mechanism (IoU threshold 0.95, patience 5) appears only in the ablation study (Section 4.4) rather than in the main method description. The NAE and SRE columns in Table 1 show dashes for most baselines without explanation of why these metrics were omitted for those methods.

### Trivial

- Equation 2 could be clearer about whether MD is computed between the same vectors a and b (implied but not stated).
- Figure 3's heatmap values are included in the caption as raw numbers but are nearly impossible to read; a color visualization would be clearer.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Comparative runtime numbers.** The paper claims "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC but does not report actual inference times for any compared method. A runtime comparison table would strengthen the efficiency argument.
- **Statistical significance for ablation gains.** Given the small differences in Tables 3 and 5, confidence intervals or a simple paired test would help establish that improvements are not noise.
- **Breakdown by object size/density.** The Discussion mentions degradation for small instances, but quantifying this (e.g., performance vs. average object size or density) would help users understand when the method is reliable.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface one useful cross-perspective observation: the paper demonstrates that SAM's output tokens, which jointly encode semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention, can serve as an effective verification space for counting — a task typically performed via image-embedding similarity or density estimation.
