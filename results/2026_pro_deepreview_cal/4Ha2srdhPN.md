## Summary
This paper introduces GRAID, a framework for generating spatial reasoning VQA data using only 2D bounding box geometry, avoiding errors from single-view 3D reconstruction and LLM hallucinations. The authors apply GRAID to three driving datasets (BDD100k, NuImages, Waymo), producing over 8.5M VQA pairs across 22 question templates. They introduce SPARQ, a predicate-based filtering system achieving up to 1400× speedup during generation. Human evaluation on 317 pairs from the "without depth" variant shows 91.16% overall validity. Fine-tuning experiments demonstrate cross-dataset generalization, composition of basic spatial skills to held-out question types, and improvements on external benchmarks (BLINK, A-OKVQA) over a community SpatialVLM dataset.

## Strengths
- **High human-validated data quality**: A human evaluation on 317 VQA pairs from GRAID-BDD (without depth) finds 95.58% of questions valid and 93.69% of answers valid, yielding 91.16% overall validity — substantially higher than the 57.6% correct-answer rate found in the community SpatialVLM dataset (Section 4).
- **Generalization from basic primitives to held-out types**: Fine-tuning Llama 3.2 11B on only six question types from GRAID-BDD yields accuracy gains of +47.5 pp on BDD and +38.0 pp on NuImages across all question types, including entirely unseen categories (Figure 3), demonstrating transferable spatial concept acquisition.
- **SPARQ computational efficiency**: The predicate rejection system for `LargestAppearance` completes in 0.02ms and rejects 21.2% of cases, yielding over 1400× speedup over full question realization; `RightOf` predicates average 5.17ms vs. 46.95ms for realization (Section 3.2).
- **External benchmark improvements**: SFT on GRAID-BDD improves Llama 3.2 11B on BLINK by +15.94% overall (with +41.13% on Relative Depth and +30.77% on Spatial Relations) and on A-OKVQA by +32.5%, consistently outperforming SFT on the SpatialVLM community dataset across four model backbones (RQ3).
- **Clear, well-motivated methodology**: The framework explicitly grounds question generation in 2D bounding box geometry and qualitative spatial predicates, avoiding the cascading errors of depth estimation and generative hallucination that plague prior approaches; the design rationale is clearly articulated and compared in Table 1.
- **Scalability and domain coverage**: The framework is applied to three autonomous driving datasets with 22 VQA templates spanning spatial relations, counting, ranking, localization, and size comparisons, producing over 8.5M VQA pairs (Table 2).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

### Minor
- **Human evaluation is limited in scope**: The evaluation covers only 317 VQA pairs from a single variant (BDD100k without depth), yet 91.16% is presented as the headline accuracy. With 8.5M+ pairs across six dataset variants and 22 question types, this sample cannot fully represent the diversity of the full corpus. The paper would be strengthened by stratified sampling across question types and datasets, or by more explicitly scoping the claim to the evaluated subset.
- **Depth question quality is unevaluated**: The "with depth" variants (adding *Closer*, *Farther* questions) use monocular depth estimation and constitute a substantial fraction of the released data (1.48M additional BDD pairs). While the paper positions these as an extensibility demonstration and uses a `margin_ratio` to filter ambiguous cases, their quality is never assessed. The paper should either evaluate them or clearly cordon them off as an unevaluated extension.
- **Comparison baseline is a community implementation**: The human evaluation and RQ3 comparisons use OpenSpaces, a community re-implementation of SpatialVLM rather than the original authors' output. The paper acknowledges this transparently (Section 4: "community implementation of SpatialVLM"), but the headline framing (e.g., Abstract: "compared to 57.6% on a dataset generated by recent work") could more explicitly qualify that this is a community-produced dataset with known issues, not the original pipeline's output.
- **Algorithm-text inconsistency for `RightOf`**: Section 3.2 describes a "similar planes" condition as necessary for realizing `RightOf` questions (line 142: "they should lie on similar planes"), but Algorithm 1 uses only x-coordinates and IoU checks. The "similar planes" heuristic is not defined or implemented, creating a reproducibility gap.
- **No confidence intervals in RQ3 fine-tuning results**: The benchmark improvements (Tables 4-6) report only point estimates without standard deviations or confidence intervals, making it difficult to assess whether gains across models and benchmarks are statistically meaningful.

### Trivial
- The regression in `LessThanThresholdHowMany` and `MoreThanThresholdHowMany` (RQ2) is attributed to "overfitting" without supporting analysis (e.g., class distribution, answer balance), though the paper appropriately hedges this as "we suspect" (line 259).
- The depth estimation model used for the "with depth" variants is not specified, which affects reproducibility of those datasets.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation using an off-the-shelf detector (e.g., YOLOv8) instead of ground-truth annotations would demonstrate how output quality degrades under realistic conditions — important for users without perfect boxes.
- Stratified human evaluation sampling across question types and datasets would strengthen confidence in the quality claims.
- Reproducing the SpatialVLM pipeline on a small shared image set for a more controlled comparison would be ideal, though the current transparent qualification of the community implementation comparison is reasonable.

## Novel Insights
The paper makes a compelling empirical argument that qualitative spatial reasoning VQA data can be generated reliably from 2D bounding box geometry alone, without requiring the expensive and error-prone 3D reconstruction pipelines that dominate prior work. The SPARQ predicate-rejection design pattern — where cheap geometric checks (IoU, class count) filter candidates before expensive question realization — is a practical engineering insight that generalizes beyond spatial reasoning to any template-based data generation pipeline. The finding that fine-tuning on just six basic spatial question types transfers to 10+ held-out types (Figure 3) provides evidence that spatial reasoning is compositional in VLMs, complementing similar findings from synthetic settings (Tang et al., 2025b; Sparkle).

## Suggestions
- Clarify the "similar planes" condition for `RightOf` in Algorithm 1 or remove the mention from the text if it is not implemented.
- Specify which depth estimation model is used for the depth variants and report at minimum the `margin_ratio` threshold used.
- Add confidence intervals or standard deviations to the RQ3 results (Tables 4-6) to let readers assess the reliability of reported improvements.
- Consider a brief stratified human evaluation covering 2-3 additional question types (e.g., Counting, Ranking) to broaden confidence in the quality claims beyond the 317 BDD without-depth sample.
