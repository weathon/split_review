## Summary
This paper proposes Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a diagnostic metric for quantifying semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations, and an asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) scheme that dynamically reweights trend and seasonal contrastive losses within the CoST framework. The method is evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets for forecasting. The core idea—diagnosing and correcting representational imbalance between trend and seasonal components—is sensible, but the execution and empirical validation have significant gaps.

## Strengths
- **Genuine diagnostic via synthetic analysis**: Table 1 demonstrates that TS2Vec embeddings exhibit strong asymmetric recoverability of trend vs. periodic components across varying amplitude ratios, with Δ magnitudes up to ±1.34. This provides clean, controlled evidence for the semantic imbalance problem the paper targets.
- **Reasonable ablation structure**: Section 4.3 shows that naively regularizing with SDE directly in the loss (TS2Vec+SDE Reg) does not improve and often worsens performance (e.g., Electricity 168h: 0.449→0.508). Section 4.4 then demonstrates that coupling multi-view decomposition with asymmetry-aware weighting yields improvements in some settings. This ablation path correctly isolates the weighting mechanism as the active ingredient.
- **Pluggable design on an established baseline**: The APW scheme integrates into CoST without architectural modifications. The method requires only an MLP fusion layer and the Δ computation, making it straightforward to adopt.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **Empirical results do not support the central claim of consistent improvement.** The paper states that CoST+APW yields "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy" (line 303). However, Table 3 shows:
  - Electricity 168h: CoST+APW MSE 0.566 vs. CoST 0.425 (CoST is ~33% better)
  - Electricity 336h: CoST+APW MSE 0.677 vs. CoST 0.576 (CoST is ~18% better)
  - Weather 336h: CoST+APW 0.266 vs. CoST 0.256
  - Weather 720h: CoST+APW 0.299 vs. CoST 0.278
  - ETT1 48h: CoST+APW 0.07 vs. CoST 0.06
  - ETT1 720h: CoST+APW 0.152 vs. CoST 0.148

  On several dataset/horizon combinations the proposed method is *worse* than the CoST baseline. Without error bars, multiple seeds, or statistical tests, the reported marginal gains (e.g., ETT2 168h: 0.199 vs. 0.201) are indistinguishable from noise. The paper also claims "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics" (line 266), but the actual table contains only MSE/MAE—no SDE values appear anywhere for the real-data experiments.

- **Overclaimed contributions.** The conclusion (line 307) asserts the paper proposes "three complementary strategies: SDE-based regularization, multi-view contrastive learning, and asymmetry-aware perceptual weighting." However, SDE regularization is shown to *fail* in §4.3, and multi-view contrastive learning is CoST's contribution (Woo et al., 2022), not the authors'. Only the asymmetric weighting is both novel and shown to help. The introduction's framing of addressing "three fundamental limitations" inflates what is actually a single contribution.

- **Circular and heuristic use of SDE during training.** The asymmetry factor Δ is computed from the very learned representations that it then reweights (§4.4.2–4.4.3): v(a) and v(b) come from the trend/seasonal encoders, and v(a+b) comes from the MLP g_ϕ, all trained jointly with the reweighted objective. There is no analysis of whether this feedback loop converges to genuinely balanced representations or merely compensates for transient training artifacts. The multiplicative weighting form (1 + γ·Δ) is presented without theoretical justification or connection to existing principles (e.g., Pareto optimization, adaptive loss balancing). A simpler fixed upweighting of the weaker component's loss is not compared.

- **Missing methodological details.** The paper does not report values for γ, γ' or any sensitivity analysis. The MLP g_ϕ (line 248) produces v(a+b) = g_ϕ([v(a)||v(b)]), but the SDE definition (Eq. 1) requires v(a+b) − v(b). Since v(a+b) passes through the MLP while v(b) comes directly from an encoder, this vector subtraction is not guaranteed to be meaningful (the embeddings may reside in different spaces). The paper offers no discussion of this.

### Minor
- **Inconsistent naming throughout the paper.** The title and abstract use "Semantic Disentanglement Error," §3.2 uses "Semantic Separability Error," and §4.3 introduces yet another variant "Semantic Decomposition Error." While the underlying metric is the same, the shifting terminology obscures the definition.

## Suggestions
- Report SDE values on real-data test sets before and after training to substantiate the central claim that APW reduces semantic imbalance. Without this, the paper has no evidence that its mechanism works through the intended pathway.
- Run experiments with multiple seeds, report mean ± std, and conduct statistical tests. The current single-run results with inconsistent patterns across horizons do not support claims of improvement.
- Tone down the contribution claims. The paper contributes one thing: asymmetric reweighting of CoST's losses via SDE-derived Δ. The introduction and conclusion should reflect this honestly rather than packaging it as three strategies.
- Either analyze the dynamics of the Δ feedback loop (showing it converges stably) or replace it with a principled proxy that does not depend on the representations being optimized.
- Report γ, γ' values and provide a sensitivity sweep. The hyperparameters are central to the method but are entirely opaque.
