## Summary

This paper addresses a training-inference misalignment in tree-based speculative decoding: prior draft model training methods (EAGLE, HASS) use sequence-level objectives, while inference relies on tree-structured drafts. The authors propose two complementary solutions: (1) TALF (Tree-Aware Loss Function), which aggregates cross-entropy loss over all nodes of a tree generated by the target model during training, and (2) SALF (Stopping at Low Further Gains), a dynamic tree construction algorithm that stops expansion when the probability sum of candidate nodes falls below a threshold, with proven monotonic decrease. Experiments on three LLMs (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) and five benchmarks show the combined method achieves 15.6–39.4% and 6.5–24.4% end-to-end speedups over EAGLE-2 and HASS.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem diagnosis.** Section 3.1 and Figure 2 empirically demonstrate that draft models trained with EAGLE and HASS are poorly calibrated on lower-ranked tokens (ranks ≥5 account for >10% of tree nodes), directly motivating the need for tree-aware training. This quantitative grounding distinguishes this work from purely heuristic proposals.

- **Clean isolation of TALF and SALF contributions.** Table 2 systematically varies both the loss function (EAGLE-2, HASS, TALF) and tree construction method (beam search, optimal tree search, SALF) while holding the target model and inference setup fixed. This is the strongest evidence in the paper: TALF improves τ over HASS under every tree construction method (e.g., τ=3.98 vs 3.70 under optimal tree search on MT-bench), and SALF improves end-to-end speedup over optimal tree search for all loss functions despite lower τ. The ablation cleanly separates the effects of the two proposals.

- **SALF's provable stopping criterion.** Theorem 1 proves the monotonic decrease of the probability sum S_i, providing a principled justification for early stopping. The empirical results (Table 4) show that SALF successfully trades off a modest τ reduction (e.g., 6.3% for TALF) for a substantial speedup improvement (14.4%), confirming that the approach is well-calibrated.

- **Comprehensive evaluation across diverse settings.** Results span three target LLMs, five benchmarks (MT-bench, HumanEval, GSM8K, Alpaca, CNN/DailyMail), and both greedy and non-greedy sampling. The consistent improvement patterns across all configurations support the generality of the method.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Training-duration confound for EAGLE-2 comparisons on Llama models.** For Llama-2-7B and Llama-3-8B (Section 4.1), the EAGLE baseline uses a draft model trained for 10 epochs, while HASS and TALF use models that received 10 epochs of EAGLE training *plus* 3 additional epochs of HASS/TALF training. The EAGLE-2 baseline in Table 1 therefore has 10 epochs of training versus 13 for SALF&TALF, confounding the effect of the loss function with additional training. This overstates the improvement over EAGLE-2. **Crucially, this does not affect the TALF-vs-HASS comparisons** (both received 13 epochs), and the DeepSeek results (24-hour training per method) are not affected. However, since the paper prominently claims "15.6–39.4% over EAGLE-2" in the abstract, this confound is a significant limitation that should be corrected with a controlled experiment (e.g., fine-tuning the EAGLE checkpoint for 3 additional epochs with the original loss).

- **Missing ablation: regression loss removal.** TALF drops the feature regression loss used by EAGLE and HASS (Section 3.2, line 118), training solely on token probability distributions. This introduces a second difference between TALF and HASS beyond the tree-structured objective. The paper states this was "sufficient" without an explicit ablation. While Table 3 provides indirect evidence (TALF(top-1) ≈ HASS, suggesting the regression loss removal is not the primary driver), a controlled comparison of TALF with and without the regression loss would cleanly isolate the tree structure's contribution.

### Minor

- **No variance or statistical significance reported.** Speedup numbers in Tables 1, 2, and 4 are reported as single values without error bars, multiple runs, or significance tests. While single-run evaluation without variance reporting is standard in the speculative decoding literature (e.g., EAGLE-2, HASS), the absence of any indication of variability makes it harder to assess the reliability of finer-grained differences (e.g., the 2.04 vs 2.02 speedup gap between TALF and HASS under optimal tree search on Alpaca in Table 2).

- **SALF threshold justification.** The paper chooses th=0.6 as the default, yet Table 4 shows th=0.5 yields a consistently higher mean speedup (2.62× vs 2.59×) for the model tested. The explanation ("more consistent performance improvements for the tested target LLMs") is stated without supporting data for other models. A cross-model sensitivity analysis would strengthen this design choice.

- **No training cost comparison.** The paper does not report the computational overhead of TALF training (tree preprocessing, tree-attention forward passes) relative to HASS or EAGLE. Including training-time wall-clock or throughput comparisons would help practitioners assess the practical cost-benefit tradeoff.

## Novel Insights

The harsh reviewer identified the training-duration confound, which is the most important issue. However, a closer reading shows that the confound is more contained than the reviewer implies: it only affects the EAGLE-2 baseline comparison on Llama models, not the HASS comparison (both HASS and TALF received the same extra training) and not the DeepSeek results. The strength finder correctly noted that Table 2 provides the cleanest evidence by holding tree construction fixed and varying the loss function — the fact that TALF consistently improves τ over HASS under every tree construction method (beam search, optimal tree search, SALF) is the paper's strongest single result and withstands the training-duration concern. The two reviewers' perspectives are largely complementary rather than contradictory: the harsh critic correctly flags a real confound in one experimental axis, while the strength finder correctly identifies the stronger evidence on another axis.

## Suggestions

1. **Address the training-duration confound.** Fine-tune the 10-epoch EAGLE checkpoint for 3 additional epochs with the original EAGLE loss and report the resulting speedup in Table 1. If the gap between EAGLE-2 and SALF&TALF narrows but remains positive, the core claim is preserved; if it vanishes, the paper's claims would need significant revision.

2. **Add an explicit ablation of TALF with and without regression loss** (TALF+reg vs TALF) to isolate the contribution of the tree-structured loss from the removal of the regression loss. This is a quick experiment that would substantially strengthen the paper.

3. **Include variance information** across tasks (e.g., standard deviation of per-query τ or speedup, or at minimum report min/max ranges) to give readers a sense of variability.

4. **Show SALF threshold sensitivity for at least one additional LLM** (e.g., Llama-3-8B) to support the claim that th=0.6 provides "more consistent performance improvements."
