## Summary

This paper identifies a "semantic imbalance" problem in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality). It proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric quantifying how recoverable one component is from the joint embedding, and uses it to dynamically reweight the trend and seasonal contrastive losses in the CoST framework. The method is evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather forecasting benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **SDE diagnostic is validated on synthetic data.** Table 1 shows that the asymmetry factor Δ monotonically tracks the amplitude ratio r for TS2Vec embeddings, confirming that SDE can quantify which semantic component dominates. This is a clean and convincing synthetic validation of the diagnostic concept.

- **The problem framing is well-motivated and practically relevant.** Semantic imbalance between trend and seasonal dynamics is a real issue in time-series representation learning, and the paper articulates it clearly using the TS2Vec case study. The vector-arithmetic analogy (Section 3.2) provides an intuitive geometric interpretation.

- **The core idea of turning a diagnostic (SDE) into a training signal via asymmetric weighting is conceptually appealing.** Rather than adding SDE as a regularization term (which fails, Table 2), the paper uses it to modulate existing loss weights, a more principled intervention that respects the original optimization landscape.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The experimental results do not reliably support the claim of "consistent gains."** Table 3 shows a mixed picture:
  - On Electricity (horizons 168–720), the proposed method is *substantially worse* than plain CoST (e.g., horizon 168: 0.566 vs 0.425 MSE; horizon 720: 1.010 vs 0.911 MSE).
  - On Weather (horizons 336, 720), TS2Vec and TNC outperform the method by wide margins.
  - On ETT1, the method beats CoST at some horizons and loses at others.
  - No error bars, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported anywhere.
  Given the claim that this is a method to *improve* forecasting accuracy, the evidence that it actually does so is inconclusive at best.

- **The abstract claims the approach is "pluggable… without architectural changes," but the method introduces an MLP fusion layer (Section 4.4.2) that is an architectural change.** The SDE computation requires a composite embedding v(a+b), which does not exist in vanilla CoST. The MLP $g_\phi$ is introduced to produce this composite embedding from the concatenated trend/seasonal embeddings. This directly contradicts the "no architectural changes" claim. The paper does not acknowledge this discrepancy or ablate whether the MLP alone (without weighting) accounts for any gains.

- **SDE values for the proposed method are not reported despite being claimed.** The text introducing Table 3 states it "reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance," but the table only contains MSE and MAE columns. No SDE or Δ values are shown for CoST+APW. This makes it impossible to verify the claim that the method "leads to consistently lower SDE values." The only SDE table (Table 1) is for TS2Vec, where the proposed method is never applied.

### Minor

- **No ablation separates the MLP from the weighting.** Three changes are conflated: (i) adding an MLP fusion layer, (ii) computing Δ from SDE on the composite embedding, (iii) reweighting losses with Δ. Without ablations (e.g., CoST+MLP without weighting, CoST+fixed-weighting without the MLP), it is impossible to attribute any observed changes to the weighting mechanism specifically.

- **The naming of SDE is inconsistent across the paper.** It is introduced as "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), redefined as "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3), and the title uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error." Each uses the identical formula. This is confusing and suggests the paper has not settled on what it is measuring or what SDE stands for.

- **No analysis of the Δ dynamics.** The asymmetric weighting creates a feedback loop (the MLP influences Δ, Δ reweights losses, losses train the MLP), but the paper provides no plots, convergence analysis, or stability discussion. It is unclear whether Δ stabilizes over training, how often it is recomputed, or whether the weighting can collapse into trivial solutions.

## Removed Points

- **Circular dependency / feedback loop concern**: The harsh critic frames this as a critical issue requiring convergence analysis, but feedback loops between loss weights and model parameters are standard in meta-learning and adaptive weighting (e.g., uncertainty weighting, GradNorm). The paper does not need a formal convergence proof for what is, in practice, a simple running-weight scheme. This concern is overblown.

- **SDE formula justification**: The harsh critic claims SDE is presented "without justification." The paper explicitly provides the word-embedding analogy (Section 3.2, citing Baevski et al. 2020). The justification is brief but present. Time-series-specific validation is deferred to Table 1, which empirically confirms the metric behaves as intended.

- **Negative weight concern**: The harsh critic worries that (1+γΔ) could become negative. Since γ is a freely chosen hyperparameter (reported as positive), any reasonable tuning would set γ small enough to keep weights positive. This is a trivial hyperparameter consideration, not a flaw.

- **Method does not diagnose the problem on CoST before applying the fix**: The harsh critic argues the paper establishes the problem on TS2Vec but validates only on CoST. This is reasonable research methodology — show the problem exists on a simpler model, then build a fix on a more capable framework. The critique is overstated.

- **"Consistent gains" overstatement**: The paper does claim "consistent gains" in the abstract. I have kept this implicitly through the major weakness on experimental results, but the specific phrasing critique is absorbed into the broader point about inconclusive evidence.

## Suggestions
