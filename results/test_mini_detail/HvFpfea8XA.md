## Summary

This paper proposes AMADEUS, a training-free RAG framework for role-playing agents, composed of three components: Adaptive Context-aware Text Splitter (ACTS), Guided Selection (GS), and Attribute Extractor (AE). It also contributes CharacterRAG, a dataset of 15 fictional characters (976K chars, 450 QA pairs) designed for RAG-based role-playing. The paper evaluates on in-knowledge questions (CharacterRAG) and out-of-knowledge questions (MBTI/BFI personality prediction).

## Strengths

- **Method design is well-motivated and clearly structured.** ACTS adapts chunk size to each character's persona (adaptive chunk length + hierarchical context), GS uses LLM-guided selection to find chunks that support inference rather than just semantic similarity, and AE extracts character attributes from those chunks. The motivation — that naive RAG overuses irrelevant chunks for out-of-knowledge queries — is supported by Figure 1's chunk duplication analysis, which shows AMADEUS achieves more uniform chunk usage (34.93% → 43.84% average chunk usage rate).

- **Consistent in-knowledge superiority across multiple LLMs.** Table 4 shows AMADEUS outperforms Naive RAG, CRAG, and LightRAG on the CharacterRAG dataset across all three LLMs (GPT-4.1: 92.67% ACC vs. Naive RAG 91.33%; Gemma3-27B: 88.00% vs. 86.44%; Qwen3-32B: 78.89% vs. 78.44%). While margins are modest (1–2 points over Naive RAG on GPT-4.1), the consistency is meaningful.

- **Human evaluation of GS+AE confirms reasonable attribute extraction.** Table 3 reports average Likert ratings of 3.970 (BFI) and 3.902 (MBTI) on 5-point scales with Cronbach's alpha values of 0.825 and 0.810 (both > 0.8, indicating high internal consistency among 14 evaluators). This separately validates that the GS→AE pipeline produces human-plausible attribute inferences.

- **CharacterRAG fills a gap.** The dataset is the first designed specifically for RAG-based role-playing, with persona documents cleaned of external/editorial information and annotated with hierarchical sections and six attribute types.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The issues identified are significant but not fatal — the paper's core contribution (the AMADEUS method + CharacterRAG dataset) is recoverable with substantial revision.

### Major

- **The MBTI/BFI prediction pipeline is completely opaque, undermining the out-of-knowledge evaluation.** The paper's central claim is that AMADEUS maintains persona consistency for queries beyond a character's explicit knowledge. The primary evidence is Table 1, where characters respond to MBTI/BFI questions and the system predicts a personality type. However, the paper never describes *how* the character's responses to 60 (MBTI) or 120 (BFI) questions are converted into a single predicted type (e.g., "ENFP"). The paper cites prior work (Wang et al., 2024b; Park et al., 2025) and states it follows "similar experimental protocol," but the mechanism — whether an LLM classifies the responses jointly, whether a rule-based mapping is applied, whether each question is scored independently — is entirely absent. Without this information, the numbers in Table 1 are uninterpretable. This is not a minor omission; it is the linchpin of the paper's signature claim. (Section 5.2, Table 1, Section 5.3)

- **The LLM used as judge for the three core metrics (ACC, ACC_L, HS) is never specified.** Section 5.2 defines these metrics but names no judging model. If GPT-4.1 judges responses generated by a system that also uses GPT-4.1 (as GS and AE do), there is a risk of circular evaluation. Without specifying the judge and ideally demonstrating correlation with human judgments, the validity of all CharacterRAG results in Table 4 is uncertain. This is a basic reproducibility requirement. (Section 5.2)

- **No ablation separates the contributions of the three components.** Table 2 shows ACTS variants but not the full pipeline ablation. It is impossible to determine how much of the performance gain comes from ACTS (chunking) vs. GS (guided selection) vs. AE (attribute extraction). A version with ACTS alone (no GS, no AE), ACTS+GS (no AE), and full AMADEUS would cleanly attribute the improvements. (Table 4, Section 5.3)

### Minor

- **Only dimension-level accuracy is reported for MBTI/BFI; type-level accuracy is absent.** The paper reports 85.00% accuracy for AMADEUS on MBTI, computed as (60 − ∑|d|)/60 = 51/60 dimensions correct. Exact 4-letter type match accuracy from the same table is 60% (9/15 characters). Reporting only the more favorable metric without acknowledging the lower type-level accuracy gives an incomplete picture. (Table 1)

- **LightRAG HS values are missing in Table 4 without explanation.** LightRAG shows dashes for HS across all three LLMs on the CharacterRAG evaluation. The paper does not explain whether LightRAG failed to generate any responses, whether HS was not computable, or whether the metric was not applied. This omission raises questions about whether LightRAG results are complete. (Table 4)

- **Sweeping claim about graph-based RAG unsuitability is unsupported.** Section 5.3 states "Graph-Based RAG and Web Search-Based RAG Are Unsuitable for Role-Playing" based on a single configuration of LightRAG on one dataset. This overgeneralizes from thin evidence. (Section 5.3)

- **The ground-truth personality types are from a crowd-sourced fan site.** Table 1's ground truth comes from personality-database.com, where "thousands of actual participants' votes" determine characters' types. This is the methodology used in prior work (Wang et al., 2024b), but it introduces unknown reliability into any accuracy computation. The paper does not discuss this limitation.

- **Limited dataset size with no inter-annotator agreement.** CharacterRAG has 15 characters and 450 QA pairs. The paper claims "high-quality" annotation but provides no measure of agreement between annotators.

- None that warrant listing.
