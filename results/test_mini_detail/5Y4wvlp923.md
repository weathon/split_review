## Summary

This paper identifies semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant temporal components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality). It proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric to quantify this imbalance, and an asymmetric weighting mechanism that dynamically reweights view-specific contrastive losses in the CoST framework. The key evidence includes a clean synthetic validation (Table 1) and forecasting results on four benchmark suites (Table 3).

## Strengths

1. **SDE metric provides a directional measure of semantic imbalance that is validated on synthetic data.** Table 1 shows a clean monotonic relationship between the amplitude ratio of trend/seasonal components and the asymmetry factor Δ (ranging from Δ = -1.29 at r=0.1 to Δ = 1.11 at r=10), convincingly demonstrating that TS2Vec embeddings suppress weaker components. This is the paper's strongest empirical contribution — prior work lacked such a diagnostic.

2. **Controlled ablation showing that direct SDE regularization fails (Table 2), strengthening the case for the weighting approach.** The paper reports that adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec *degrades* performance (e.g., ETTm1 horizon 288: 0.101 → 0.126 MSE; Electricity horizon 168: 0.449 → 0.508). This negative result is honestly reported and meaningfully supports the paper's thesis that the *weighting* mechanism (not the metric alone) drives improvements.

3. **Plug-and-play design without architectural changes.** As stated in the abstract and Section 3, the method only modifies loss weighting and adds a small MLP fusion module to CoST, without changing the underlying encoder architecture. This is a practical strength compared to approaches requiring full architectural redesign.

4. **Comprehensive evaluation across multiple datasets and horizons.** Table 3 reports results on ETT (two resolutions), Electricity, and Weather across 25 dataset/horizon combinations with both MSE and MAE. The method shows improvements over vanilla CoST on a substantial subset of settings (e.g., ETTm1 all five horizons, ETT2 horizons 24/168/336).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The proposed method does not consistently outperform baselines, and the paper overclaims the results.** The paper claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and "consistently lower SDE values" (Conclusion). However, Table 3 tells a mixed story:
   - On Electricity at horizons 168/336/720, CoST+APW is *worse than all baselines* (e.g., Elec 720: CoST+APW 1.010 MSE vs. CoST 0.911, TS2Vec 0.863, TNC 0.859).
   - On Weather, CoST+APW is never the best method across any of the 5 horizons — it is consistently outperformed by TNC and often by TS2Vec.
   - Against the CoST baseline it extends, CoST+APW shows gains on roughly half of settings and losses on the other half, often by margins small enough to be within noise.
   
   The paper does not report any variance measures (error bars, multiple seeds) or statistical significance tests, making it impossible to assess whether the observed differences are meaningful. This is an **evidential weakness**: the data does not clearly support the central claim.

2. **Unresolved conceptual gap between SDE as a regularizer vs. SDE as a weight.** Section 4.3 reports that direct SDE regularization *hurts* performance and hypothesizes this is because SDE "fails to provide constructive optimization gradients" (line 214). Yet the proposed asymmetric weighting mechanism also computes Δ (derived from SDE) and uses it to scale the contrastive losses — the gradients of the reweighted losses still depend on the same SDE computation. The paper offers no explanation for why SDE gradients would be problematic as a direct regularizer but beneficial when used to compute weighting factors. This is a methodological gap that undermines confidence in the mechanism's design rationale.

3. **Missing ablation of the MLP fusion module.** Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP \( g_\phi \) that maps concatenated trend/seasonal embeddings to a composite embedding. Its effect is never isolated from the asymmetric weighting. Since the CoST+APW method differs from CoST in two ways (the MLP and the weighting), it is impossible to attribute any observed improvements to the weighting mechanism specifically. The MLP alone could account for the gains.

### Minor

1. **γ and γ' hyperparameters are neither reported nor ablated.** Equation (line 104) defines γ, γ' > 0 as scaling hyperparameters, but their values used in experiments are never stated, and no sensitivity analysis is provided. For a method whose core claims rest on dynamic reweighting, this omission is significant.

2. **Inconsistent naming of the metric across the paper.** The same quantity is called "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (Abstract, line 13), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2, line 72), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.1, line 153; Section 4.3, line 181). While the mathematical definition is consistent, the terminological inconsistency suggests conceptual drift and harms readability.

3. **Synthetic SDE analysis (Table 1) is performed on TS2Vec, not on CoST.** Since the proposed method is built on CoST, it would be much more informative to show that CoST also exhibits the same imbalance — and that the proposed weighting reduces it. Without this, the connection between the diagnostic experiment and the proposed fix is indirect.

4. **The additive assumption behind SDE is unverified.** The SDE formula \( \text{SDE} = 1 - \cos(v(a+b) - v(b), v(a)) \) assumes that the embedding space supports subtractive vector arithmetic — that removing the representation of component b from the composite leaves a meaningful approximation of a's representation. The paper acknowledges this is "inspired by vector arithmetic in word embeddings" (line 80) but provides no empirical verification that time-series contrastive embedding spaces satisfy this property. If the additive assumption fails, SDE could measure something unrelated to component recoverability.

5. **No evaluation on downstream tasks beyond forecasting.** The introduction and motivation discuss classification and anomaly detection as tasks harmed by semantic imbalance, but the experimental section only evaluates forecasting. If the proposed method improves representation quality, it should transfer to other tasks. The absence of such evaluation limits the evidence for representational improvement.

6. **Table 3 contains a labeling error.** The dataset column lists "ETT1" twice (lines 277 and 287). The second block appears to be ETTm1 based on the horizons (24/48/96/288/672), but the label is incorrect, making the table confusing.

### Trivial
- The claim about addressing "isotropic embedding collapse" (Section 1, point 3) is never empirically evaluated with collapse metrics (e.g., uniformity, dimensional collapse).
- The paper's discussion of "consistent gains" in Section 5 is contradicted by the mixed results in Table 3.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface one genuinely novel observation not fully articulated in the paper: the paper's strongest contribution may be the *diagnostic* (SDE metric) rather than the *mitigation* (weighting mechanism). The clean synthetic validation in Table 1 convincingly establishes that contrastive TS embeddings exhibit semantic imbalance, and SDE provides a way to measure it. But the experimental evidence for the weighting mechanism is mixed enough that the paper's claimed contribution (fixing the imbalance) is not well supported. An interesting direction not explored by the paper or the reviews: SDE could be used as an evaluation tool for future contrastive methods without necessarily being integrated into training.
