## Summary

This paper frames language model decoding as a constrained optimization problem: minimize reverse KL divergence to the base LM while matching expected metric scores of human texts across multiple aspects (repetition, coherence, diversity, information content). The optimal solution takes the form of an energy-based model (EBM) that scales the LM distribution with a sequence-level energy function (Proposition 1). The authors prove that this solution guarantees perplexity improvement over the base LM (Proposition 2) and use Sampling-Importance-Resampling (SIR) for tractable generation. Experiments on Wikipedia and News with GPT-2 XL and OPT-6.7B show consistent improvements over sampling-based (Top-k, Nucleus, Typical) and search-based (Contrastive Decoding, Contrastive Search) baselines on both automatic metrics and human evaluation.

## Strengths

1. **Theoretical guarantee of perplexity improvement**: Proposition 2 proves that the optimal decoding distribution satisfies \( H(p_d, q_{\text{opt}}) = H(p_d, p_\theta) - D_{\text{KL}}(q_{\text{opt}}\|p_\theta) \), establishing a strict reduction in perplexity over the base LM. This is a genuine result that existing heuristic decoding methods cannot provide, since their ad-hoc treatments (truncation, contrastive objectives) do not yield a tractable distribution for perplexity evaluation.

2. **Principled closed-form solution from constrained optimization**: Section 2.1 formulates decoding as minimizing reverse KL subject to expected-metric constraints (Eq. 1), and Proposition 1 derives an analytical solution \( p_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{x}) \propto p_\theta(\mathbf{x}) \exp[-\mu^\top \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})] \). This connects decoding to information projection and provides a theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic truncation or contrastive objectives — the connection to I-projection in information geometry is acknowledged and gives a clear interpretation.

3. **Empirical dominance across domains and model scales**: Table 1 shows DAEMON achieves the closest scores to human references on repetition (SR-4), coherence (COH), diversity (DIV), information content (e^ENT), and MAUVE for both GPT-2 XL and OPT-6.7B on both Wikipedia and News. The consistency across two model families and two domains — 24 separate comparisons in the main table — makes the empirical evidence robust.

4. **Human evaluation confirms quality transfer**: Table 3 reports pairwise human judgments where DAEMON wins over CD, CS, Nucleus, and Typical on fluency, coherence, and informativeness, with most results statistically significant at p<0.005. This provides independent validation beyond the metric-constrained evaluation.

5. **Ablation studies validate design components**: Table 4 shows that removing any metric from the constraints causes a clear deterioration in the corresponding score, confirming the constraints are active and effective. Figure 3 shows monotonic improvement in alignment as candidate count \(M\) increases, consistent with the SIR convergence theory.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Metrics used as constraints are the same as those used for automatic evaluation, creating a partial circularity.** The paper explicitly optimizes to match human-text expectations on a set of nine metrics (SR-N, TR-L, COH, DIV, e^ENT), then evaluates on those same metrics in Table 1. The paper does acknowledge this in Section 3.2 ("Note that these metrics are also used in the automatic evaluation part..."), and the human evaluation and MAUVE scores provide genuinely independent validation. Nevertheless, the core claim of "alignment with human texts" is primarily demonstrated through metrics that the method is *designed* to match. The paper would benefit from a more explicit discussion of what the metric-based evaluation actually measures (successfully optimized proxies) versus what the human and MAUVE evaluations independently confirm.

2. **Gap between the theoretical optimal solution and the practical approximation is under-analyzed.** The theory assumes: (a) exact \( \mathbb{E}_{p_d}[f_k] \) estimated from a finite dev set, (b) exact minimization of reverse KL yielding \( p_{\theta,\mu} \), and (c) exact sampling from \( p_{\theta,\mu} \). In practice, all three are approximate. The most significant gap involves the temperature-modulated SIR proposal: the paper samples from \( p_\theta^\tau \) (with \( \tau < 1 \)) rather than \( p_\theta \), but the importance weight \( \exp(-E_\mu(x)) \cdot p_\theta^\tau(x)/p_\theta(x) \) used in Algorithm 2 is *not* the correct weight for approximating \( p_{\theta,\mu} \propto p_\theta \exp(-E_\mu) \). The paper mentions in Appendix A.3 that temperature modulation preserves computable perplexity, but does not analyze its effect on SIR approximation quality or the bias introduced. The theoretical perplexity improvement guarantee (Proposition 2) applies to \( q_{\text{opt}} \), not the approximate distribution. The ablation in Figure 3 uses \( \tau = 1.0 \) to isolate the effect of \( M \), but the main results use \( \tau < 1 \) (0.97, 0.99), meaning the practical system operates under conditions not covered by the formal guarantees. This does not invalidate the empirical results, but the paper should either bound the combined approximation error or more carefully delineate which claims are theoretical and which are empirical.

### Minor

3. **Baseline hyperparameters are fixed at original-paper defaults while DAEMON tunes \(\mu\), \(M\), and \(\tau\) on the development set.** The paper states "For baselines, we follow the hyper-parameter settings in the original papers which are shown to work well in general." This asymmetry means some of DAEMON's advantage could come from tuning rather than the method itself. The concern is partially mitigated by (a) DAEMON's consistent advantage across two domains and two model sizes, and (b) the human evaluation confirming preference without relying on tuning. Still, a sensitivity sweep over baseline hyperparameters (e.g., Nucleus \(p \in \{0.8, 0.9, 0.95\}\), Typical \(ty \in \{0.85, 0.95, 1.0\}\), CD/CS \( \alpha \) variants) on the same development set would make the comparison cleaner.

4. **The final learned coefficients \(\mu_k\) are not reported.** The estimated coefficients \(\mu\) determine which metrics dominate the energy function \(E_\mu(\mathbf{x}) = \mu^\top \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})\). Without reporting these values (or their relative magnitudes), the reader cannot assess whether the optimization relies heavily on, say, coherence versus repetition metrics. This is relevant for understanding the behavior of the method and for practitioners who might want to adapt it.

5. **Perplexity improvements are modest (e.g., 23.1→22.0 for GPT-2 XL on Wikipedia).** The theoretical result guarantees improvement, and Table 2 shows it is consistent across settings. But the magnitude is small, especially for OPT-6.7B (16.4→16.2). The paper should discuss whether such improvements are practically meaningful beyond validating the theory.

6. **Computational cost is reported only as relative latency (vs. greedy) and for varying \(M\), without absolute time or throughput numbers.** The latency in Figure 3 is relative to greedy decoding (e.g., ~6× for \(M=25\)), but absolute tokens/second would help practitioners assess practicality. The one-time cost of the \(\mu\) estimation step (Algorithm 1) is also not reported.

### Trivial

7. **Figure 2 (quality-diversity trade-off) does not report the specific hyperparameter values swept for each baseline, only showing the resulting curves.** The paper would benefit from indicating what ranges were explored.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Provide a synthetic experiment** where the true \(p_d\) is known, to explicitly measure how the combination of finite \(N\), finite \(M\), temperature \(\tau\), and RMSRE-based \(\mu\) estimation affects the final distribution relative to the theoretical \(q_{\text{opt}}\).
- **Validate metric set completeness** by testing whether optimizing the nine chosen metrics degrades an unmeasured but important dimension (e.g., factuality) that human raters might care about.
- **Include qualitative failure cases** where DAEMON underperforms baselines, which would give insight into the method's limitations (e.g., does it sometimes generate overly conservative texts due to strong constraints?).
- **Discuss metric redundancy and selection**: The ablation (Table 4) shows inter-dependence (e.g., SR-N and DIV). Could a smaller subset achieve similar performance with less constraint pressure?

## Novel Insights

The two reviewers identify diverging frames for the same core weakness: the Harsh Critic treats the metric-evaluation circularity as a structural concern about conflating optimization target with evaluation yardstick, while the Strength Finder implicitly treats the metric-constrained evaluation (Table 1) as evidence of success. Neither reviewer examines whether *specific* metrics in the set might be poor proxies for human preference on their own, and whether the method's alignment generalizes to *different* operationalizations of the same conceptual axes (e.g., a different coherence metric). This gap — evaluating with the exact same functions used in the energy — is the paper's most significant vulnerability, and the community would benefit from an analysis that systematically varies the evaluation metric while keeping the constraint set fixed.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a discussion of the practical approximation gap**: explicitly state that the theoretical guarantees (Proposition 2) apply to the idealized \(q_{\text{opt}}\), while the implemented system produces an approximation whose quality is empirically characterized by the SIR ablation (Figure 3) and should be verified independently. Distinguish more carefully between theoretical and empirical claims throughout.

2. **Add a baseline hyperparameter sensitivity analysis** for at least one domain/model: sweep over Nucleus \(p\), Typical \(ty\), CD/CS \(\alpha\) values on the development set and report the best-performing configurations. This would either confirm DAEMON's advantage is robust or reveal a fairer comparison.

3. **Report the learned \(\mu\) values** (or relative weights) to show which metrics dominate the energy function, improving interpretability and enabling practitioners to understand the method's behavior.

4. **Include a small synthetic experiment** (e.g., on a controlled discrete distribution) that directly measures the gap between the theoretical \(q_{\text{opt}}\) and the practical SIR-approximate distribution, validating that the approximation error from temperature modulation, finite \(M\), and finite \(N\) is well-controlled.
