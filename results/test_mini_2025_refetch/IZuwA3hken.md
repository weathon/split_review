## Summary

This paper studies the interplay between context influence and hallucination in LLM summarization. It defines a formal measure of context influence (Definition 3.1), introduces Context-Influence Decoding (CID, a reformulation of CAD), and provides theoretical analysis linking context influence to PMI (Theorem 3.1) and differential privacy (Theorem 3.2). The empirical evaluation measures how context influence and faithfulness vary with λ, model capacity, context size, response length, and token n-grams across models (OPT, GPT-Neo, LLaMA 3) on CNN-DM and PubMedQA. A key quantitative finding is that CAD (λ=1.5) improves ROUGE-L by ~10% while increasing context influence by ~1.5× for LLaMA 3 on CNN-DM.

## Strengths

1. **Thorough empirical characterization of factors affecting context influence.** The paper systematically measures how context influence varies with response length (Figure 4a shows the first ~10 generated tokens are most influenced by context), n-gram size (Figure 4b shows a peak at n=128), and position within the context (Figure 4c shows earlier content is more influential). These are concrete, practically useful findings that go beyond merely documenting the obvious λ→influence trend.

2. **Insightful cross-model comparison with different pre-training data.** The comparison between OPT-1.3B and GPT-Neo 1.3B on PubMedQA (Section 4.2) — where OPT's pre-training excluded PubMed abstracts while GPT-Neo's included them — provides a clean demonstration that smaller context influence does not imply smaller privacy risk, because pre-training data overlap matters. This is a genuine insight, not just a validation of the proposed method.

3. **Actionable practical recommendations grounded in experimental observations.** The discussion (Section 6) derives concrete guidelines from the experiments: placing privacy-sensitive information later in the context (supported by Figure 4c's recency effect) and using token-position-dependent privacy budgets (supported by Figure 4a's response-length effect). These follow naturally from the empirical results and are useful to practitioners irrespective of the theoretical issues.

4. **Concrete quantitative illustration of the influence–faithfulness tradeoff.** Table 1 provides a clean, comprehensive comparison across multiple models, datasets, and λ values. The specific numbers (e.g., LLaMA 3 on CNN-DM: ROUGE-L 17.42→19.22, influence 64.61→98.99 for λ=1.0→1.5) give the reader a tangible sense of the magnitudes involved.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The written definition of context influence (Eq. 3) does not match what the empirical results imply.**  
   Eq. 3 defines  
   $$f_{\text{inf}} = |\log p(y_t|D)| - |\log p(y_t|D\setminus D')|.$$  
   Since log probabilities are negative, this simplifies to $\log\big(p(y_t|D\setminus D')/p(y_t|D)\big)$. When a substring $D'$ *increases* the probability of $y_t$ (the natural interpretation of positive influence), this expression is *negative*. Yet all values reported in Table 1 are positive (13.20, 45.66, 98.99…), indicating that the actual computation differs — likely the absolute difference $|\log p(y_t|D) - \log p(y_t|D\setminus D')|$ was used instead. The paper also states (line 149) that "calculation of context influence follows from Eq. 5" (which is an inequality, not a computation formula), adding confusion.  
   **Why this is major**: The theoretical results (Theorems 3.1, 3.2) build on this definition. Until the definition is corrected and the theorems re-derived for the intended measure, the theoretical analysis rests on an unstable foundation. The empirical findings are likely still meaningful under a corrected definition, but the paper must clarify this discrepancy.

2. **Theorem 3.2's differential privacy claim is technically unsupported and contradicted by the paper's own prose.**  
   Theorem 3.2 states that a token generated by CID with $\lambda^* = \epsilon/(2\cdot\text{pmi})$ is $\epsilon$-DP with respect to $D$. However, $\lambda^*$ depends on the specific token $y_t$ and its PMI value, which itself depends on $D$, $x$, and $y_{<t}$ — the construction is circular (one must know the output to set $\lambda$). Moreover, DP requires the bound to hold uniformly over all adjacent datasets and all possible outputs, not just one pre-specified pair $(D, D')$. The prose later (lines 106–107) concedes that "this does not achieve DP since it could be $\epsilon = \infty$," directly contradicting the theorem statement.  
   **Why this is major**: The DP framing as presented is misleading. The idea of using context influence as an *audit* lower bound is sensible and does not require a theorem claiming DP satisfaction. The paper would be stronger by removing the formal DP claim and simply presenting $f_{\text{inf}}$ as a local sensitivity measure, which is what the experiments actually use it for.

### Minor

3. **The term "apmi" in Theorem 3.1 (Eq. 5) is never defined in the main text.** The bound $f_{\text{inf}} \leq |\text{apmi}(\cdot)|$ relies on an undefined quantity. Whether this is "absolute PMI," "amortized PMI," or something else is unclear. The reader cannot evaluate the theorem's meaning without searching the appendix for the proof.

4. **Inconsistent notation across the paper.** Table 1's column header is $\mathbb{E}[f_{\text{Mem}}(\bar{p}_\theta)]$, while the text and definition use $f_{\text{inf}}$, and Section 4.3 uses $f_{\text{mn}}$. These appear to refer to the same quantity but the notation is never unified.

5. **The claim "larger models are less influenced by context" is not well-supported by the data.** The model-size experiment (Figure 2) shows noisy, non-monotonic behavior: influence at 30B is higher than at 1.3B, and 66B is roughly similar to 125M. With no confidence intervals or trend tests, the paper overstates the strength of this conclusion.

6. **Claims about CID's novelty relative to CAD are overstated.** The paper positions CID (Eq. 4, linear logit interpolation) as a "slight reformulation" of CAD (Eq. 2, multiplicative PMI scaling), then labels λ=1.5 as "CAD" in Table 1. The experimental design does not isolate whether differences come from the CID-vs-CAD formulation or simply from increasing λ. A controlled ablation matching effective PMI weight would clarify this.

### Trivial

- No confidence intervals or variance estimates are reported for any experimental results, despite stochastic sampling.
- The qualitative selection for Table 3's heatmap does not state how the example was chosen or whether it is representative.

## Suggestions

5. **Add confidence intervals** to key figures (especially Figure 2) and soften the claim about model-size effects to reflect the noisy data.
