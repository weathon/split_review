## Summary

This paper proposes VQ-CD, a framework for continual offline RL that addresses the under-explored problem of task sequences with varying state and action spaces. It combines two modules: Quantized Spaces Alignment (QSA), which uses vector quantization to project per-task observations and actions into a unified representation space, and Selective Weights Activation (SWA), which applies task-specific binary masks to a U-Net diffusion model's convolutional kernels to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Experiments on 15 CL tasks (Ant-dir, Continual World, D4RL) show that VQ-CD outperforms or matches 16 baselines, including achieving strong results on cross-environment sequences (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah) where baselines with padding struggle.

## Strengths

- **First general continual offline RL method handling varying state/action spaces.** The QSA module uses per-task vector-quantized encoders/decoders to project different observation and action spaces into a fixed-dimensional, bounded latent space. This enables continual training on task sequences where previous CORL methods could not operate (e.g., Hopper → Walker2d → HalfCheetah). The D4RL experiments (Figure 3) provide direct evidence of this capability, and the ablation against AE/VAE alternatives (Figure 4) plus the quantitative feature-difference analysis (Table 2, showing 8.83 state difference for VQ vs 51.31 for AE) confirm that VQ produces more consistent representations across tasks.

- **Strong empirical performance across diverse benchmarks.** VQ-CD achieves a mean return of 558.22 ± 1.14 on Ant-dir 10-15-19-25, substantially ahead of the next-best baseline CoD at 478.19 ± 15.84 (Table 1). On Continual World (CW10), it matches the Multitask upper bound while baselines degrade (Figure 2). On D4RL cross-environment settings, it surpasses all baselines including Multitask on low-quality datasets (Figure 3a, 3b). The evaluation covers 15 task settings against 16 baselines, providing broad empirical support.

- **Ablation confirms VQ's advantage over AE/VAE for space alignment.** The ablation study (Figure 4) shows that replacing VQ with AE or VAE substantially harms performance across all four D4RL settings. The accompanying analysis (Table 2) explains why: AE produces much larger feature differences across tasks (e.g., state difference 51.31 vs. 8.83 for VQ), making diffusion model training less stable. This directly supports the QSA design choice.

- **Parameter sensitivity analysis guides hyperparameter selection.** Experiments varying codebook size for states (Figure 5) and number of latent vectors for actions (Figure 6) show clear trends (e.g., codebook size near 512 is optimal, too many latent vectors hurt reconstruction), providing practical guidance for deploying the method.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The random mask allocation strategy lacks justification and comparative analysis.** The paper states (line 160) that task masks are generated by "randomly sampling unmasked positions from the remaining available mask positions." No principle, analysis, or ablation is provided to justify this choice over alternatives such as importance-based allocation (as in PackNet, which the paper cites). The paper does not analyze mask quality, weight reuse patterns, or interference across tasks. While random subnetwork allocation is a known CL technique, omitting any comparison against structured allocation leaves a significant gap in the evaluation of the core SWA mechanism. This concern is amplified by the fact that the method's strong results — even with random masks — are presented without explanation of why random selection suffices.

- **The SWA module is not ablated.** The ablation study (Section 4.5) investigates QSA variants (VQ vs. AE vs. VAE) and the diffusion backbone (MLP vs. U-Net), but never removes or replaces the selective weights activation itself. There is no experiment showing VQ-CD without task masks (e.g., sequential fine-tuning on QSA-aligned features, or rehearsal-based training with QSA). Since SWA is one of the two core contributions, the lack of a direct ablation makes it impossible to attribute how much of the observed performance is due to weight isolation versus the QSA module or the diffusion model capacity. The comparison against baselines provides indirect evidence, but a controlled ablation is needed to isolate SWA's contribution.

### Minor

- **D4RL results lack numerical values, error bars, and forgetting metrics.** The D4RL cross-environment experiments (Figure 3) are presented only as bar charts without numerical values or error bars, unlike the Ant-dir results (Table 1) which report mean ± std. Additionally, no forgetting metrics (e.g., backward transfer, per-task performance retention) are reported for these settings — only final aggregate scores. For a continual learning paper, this limits the reader's ability to assess whether tasks are truly retained or whether final performance masks significant forgetting. The CW10 results provide learning curves (Figure 2), which partly address this, but the D4RL experiments lack equivalent detail.

- **The term "aligned spaces" is somewhat overstated.** The paper acknowledges (line 142) that "the quantized spaces of different tasks are separated" — each task gets its own encoder, decoder, and codebook entries. There is no cross-task constraint enforcing semantic correspondence between the latent codes of different tasks. The "alignment" is essentially projecting to a fixed dimension with bounded value ranges, not learning a shared semantic space. While the method is effective, the terminology could mislead readers into expecting stronger cross-task representation alignment than is actually enforced.

- **Scalability of the mask allocation to long task sequences is not discussed.** The mask positions are depleted as tasks accumulate (line 161: "the remaining available mask positions decrease"), but the paper provides no analysis of how many tasks can be supported, how per-task sparsity is chosen, or what happens when positions are exhausted. This is a practical concern for deployment on long task sequences, though it is shared with other parameter-isolation methods.

- **The weight assembly description could be clearer.** The paper describes two options for weight updates — a sparse optimizer or "extract and assemble weights at end" — but the framing as a novel contribution ("propose extracting and assembling the corresponding weights at the end of the training") describes standard masked training practice (where masked weights receive zero gradients but the optimizer still maintains state for them). The contribution here is the additive assembly due to mask exclusivity, which is the genuinely novel part.

### Trivial

- Figure 3 uses "fr," "mr," "m," "me" as abbreviations for D4RL dataset qualities that are defined only in the caption but not in a legend — a small readability issue.
