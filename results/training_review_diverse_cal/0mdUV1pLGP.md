## Summary

This paper proposes ENHP, a Hawkes process variant where the impact function between event types is modeled as a neural network kernel in a learned event embedding space, rather than assuming a parametric form (e.g., exponential decay). An optional transformer encoder (ENHP-C) can contextualize these embeddings at the cost of interpretability. The method is evaluated on six real-world datasets via log-likelihood and a simulation study. The core idea — factorizing the impact matrix through low-dimensional embeddings with a neural time-decay kernel — is sensible and fills a genuine gap: it provides more flexible impact shapes than parametric HPs while retaining the additive structure that supports post-hoc analysis of event interactions.

## Strengths

1. **Flexible impact kernel in embedding space that preserves additive structure**: The paper proposes a clean formulation (Equation 8) where the impact function is computed as ϕᵢⱼ(Δt) = (W e⁽ⁱ⁾)ᵀ K(Δt) (W e⁽ʲ⁾). This decomposes the M×M impact matrix into a D×D neural kernel with learned embeddings, reducing the dimensionality from O(M²) to O(D² + M·D). The additive Hawkes structure (intensity = baseline + sum of past impacts) is retained, enabling the kind of pair-wise influence analysis that is lost in RNN/Transformer-based neural HPs.

2. **Accurate recovery of diverse impact functions in simulation (Section 4.3)**: The simulation study demonstrates that ENHP recovers step, cosine, and exponential impact kernels from data generated by a known multivariate HP, outperforming the traditional tick library's step-function approximation. This is direct evidence that the neural kernel can learn non-parametric impact shapes, including discontinuities, which is the paper's primary modeling contribution.

3. **Scalability to large event-type spaces**: ENHP is evaluated on MemeTrack with 5,000 event types (Table 1), where the embedding formulation makes the problem tractable. Other neural HP baselines cannot run on this dataset due to the quadratic explosion of parameters, giving ENHP a practical advantage.

4. **Interpretability analyses with domain insight**: Sections 4.5 and 4.6 provide concrete interpretability exercises — heatmaps of cumulative impact across categories (Amazon, StackOverflow) and topic-level analysis linking embedding dimensions to medical procedures (MIMIC-IV). The MIMIC analysis reveals clinically plausible relationships (e.g., culture results → line placement; intubation → X-Ray/EKG), demonstrating that the embedding space captures semantically meaningful structure.

5. **Competitive average rank across datasets**: ENHP achieves an average rank of 2.0 across five benchmark datasets (Table 2), tied with NHP for the best average rank and outperforming THP and FullyNN-TPP. This suggests the method is consistently competitive despite being more constrained than fully black-box alternatives.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Final results reported on the validation set, not a held-out test set.**  
   Section 4.4 explicitly states: "All likelihoods presented below are reported on the validation set." This is a serious methodological concern. Validation-set scores guide hyperparameter selection and can overstate performance relative to a genuinely held-out test set. Standard practice in this area (including the EasyTPP benchmark the authors cite) is to report test-set results. Without test-set numbers, the competitive claims (average rank 2.0, "often unnecessary" transformer conclusion) rest on incomplete evidence. The reported rankings could shift on a proper test set.

2. **Missing SAHP baseline weakens the central interpretability comparison.**  
   SAHP (Self-Attentive Hawkes Process) is described in Section 2 as a related model that "extends the traditional Hawkes process" and "provides an interpretable measure of how past events affect subsequent occurrences." This makes SAHP a directly relevant baseline for both performance and interpretability. The paper states it was excluded due to "a configuration issue in easyTPP" (Section 4.4). This is not adequately justified. SAHP results from the original paper on the same datasets, or a working EasyTPP configuration, should be provided. Without it, the claim that ENHP "is the only model that offers complete interpretability" among compared methods is hollow, since the most directly comparable interpretable baseline is missing.

3. **The "complete interpretability" claim is overblown.**  
   Line 197 states: "Crucially, among all the methods compared, ENHP is the only model that offers complete interpretability." The word "complete" is indefensible. The impact kernel is a neural network (fully connected + ReLU + linear), and the embeddings are learned latent dimensions. Inspecting heatmaps of cumulative effects and manually labeling top-loading procedures on one dataset (MIMIC-IV, D=3) is a reasonable exploratory analysis, but it does not constitute "complete" interpretability — there is no formal definition, no user study, no comparison to known ground-truth causal structures on real data, and no ablation measuring how interpretability degrades with larger D. The paper's own framing is more measured elsewhere (abstract: "more interpretable than other neural network approaches"), and the conclusion should match that measured tone.

### Minor

1. **The claim that transformer layers are "often unnecessary" is based on limited evidence.**  
   Table 1 shows similar log-likelihoods for ENHP and ENHP-C, and the paper concludes that "this sacrifice [of interpretability] is rarely necessary." However: (a) these results are on the validation set (overlapping with weakness 1), (b) no statistical significance or confidence intervals are reported for the differences, (c) the transformer architecture (layers, heads, hidden dimension) is not specified, making it unclear whether ENHP-C was properly tuned. A more thorough investigation — e.g., varying transformer complexity, checking test-set performance — would strengthen this claim.

2. **Incomplete training details for reproducibility.**  
   The paper specifies the kernel architecture (FC + ReLU + linear) and the use of EasyTPP for baselines, but does not report learning rate, batch size, number of epochs, embedding dimension per dataset, or random seeds for ENHP. These details are necessary for independent reproduction of the results.

### Trivial

- None. The paper is clearly written and well-organized.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Prediction-based metrics** (next-event type accuracy, time prediction error) would complement log-likelihood and provide a more practical performance assessment.
- **A more systematic interpretability evaluation** — e.g., comparing recovered influence graphs against known structure in domains where such structure exists, or ablating embedding dimension D while tracking both performance and a quantitative interpretability proxy (sparsity, stability, alignment with known relationships).
- **SAHP results** should be included; if the EasyTPP configuration issue cannot be resolved, publishing SAHP's published results on the same datasets (with proper citation) would at least give readers a reference point.

## Removed Points

- **Criticism about the impact kernel being a neural network making it "not qualitatively different" from other neural HPs**: The critic argues that the kernel is also a black-box function. This conflates "neural network" with "uninterpretable." ENHP retains the additive Hawkes structure where influence is channeled through explicit pair-wise impact functions, which is fundamentally different from NHP (hidden-state dynamics via RNN) or THP (self-attention weights as influence proxy). The paper's distinction is valid and grounded in the structure of the model, not just the activation functions used.
- **Criticism about no user study or formal interpretability definition being a "fatal" flaw**: The paper provides concrete interpretability analyses (heatmaps, topic discovery) that are standard for this kind of work. A user study or formal definition would be nice but is not expected for a methods paper at this venue. Downgraded to Major weakness 3.
- **Criticism about SAHP being included in Related Work but not compared**: Already addressed in Major weakness 2 above — kept but reframed as a missing baseline rather than an inconsistency.
- **Criticism about no "knob to adjust interpretability vs flexibility"**: The paper explicitly provides this knob — adding or removing the transformer encoder. The critic's framing of a "knob" as a continuous control is a wishlist item, not a missing feature.
- **Criticism about simulation study being "suggestive" without ground truth**: The simulation study has explicit ground truth (known kernels). The critic seems to have misread this section. Removed.
- **Criticism that SAHP, NHP, and AttNHP "also permit analysis of hidden states" as an argument against ENHP's interpretability advantage**: Analyzing RNN hidden states or attention weights is not equivalent to inspecting explicit pair-wise impact functions. NHP's hidden states are complex recurrent representations that do not directly yield interpretable influence measures. This conflates different kinds of interpretability.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation emerging from the reviews — one that goes beyond what the paper itself develops — is that the paper's core methodological contribution (embedding-factorized neural impact kernel) is largely separable from the interpretability claim. The embedding factorization is a strong technical contribution regardless of how one evaluates the interpretability analysis: it enables scaling to thousands of event types (where full M² kernels are intractable), it provides a natural inductive bias (low-rank structure in the impact matrix), and the simulation study validates that it recovers complex impact shapes. The interpretability analyses are a downstream use of this architecture, not its defining feature. Reframing the paper's contribution around the embedding-based parametrization rather than the interpretability tradeoff would likely strengthen its impact, since the factorization is what is genuinely novel, while the interpretability properties follow naturally from the additive structure the paper chose to retain.

## Suggestions

1. **Report test-set likelihoods** for all datasets and models. If the EasyTPP framework provides test splits, use them. If not, hold out a portion of the training data as test data. This is the most critical fix.
2. **Include SAHP results** or, failing that, clearly acknowledge the limitation and cite published SAHP results on the same datasets for reference.
3. **Tone down the "complete interpretability" claim** to match the paper's own evidence. Replace "complete interpretability" with something like "structured interpretability through pair-wise impact functions and topic-level embeddings."
4. **Specify the transformer architecture** used for ENHP-C and report training hyperparameters for all proposed methods.
5. **Add confidence intervals or statistical tests** for the ENHP vs ENHP-C comparison to support the claim that transformer layers are unnecessary.
6. **Consider reframing the contribution** around the embedding-factorized neural impact kernel itself, which is the strongest and least contested part of the paper.
