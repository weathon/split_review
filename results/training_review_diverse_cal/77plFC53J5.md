## Summary

This paper studies temporal feature redundancy in SNNs and proposes the Feature Overlapping Phenomenon — the observation that feature maps across adjacent time steps are highly similar due to weight sharing and repeated input encoding. The authors propose a Temporal Differential Decoupling (TDD) method to decompose features into static (reused) and dynamic (changing) components, and a Gradient Sensitivity Criterion (GSC) to identify sensitive features in the differential domain. The combined Differential Domain Low-Sparsity Approximation (DDLA) algorithm is claimed to reduce spike counts while maintaining accuracy.

## Strengths

1. **Conceptually motivated direction.** The paper identifies a genuine source of computational waste in SNNs — temporal feature redundancy that is separate from the time-step reduction question. Shifting focus from "how many time steps" to "what within each time step is redundant" is a sensible reframing that addresses a limitation of prior work on dynamic time-step configuration.

2. **Spike reduction numbers.** The paper reports concrete spike reduction statistics: up to 80.9% reduction in spikes per timestep and 57.8% total spike count reduction on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 (Table 2, Section 7). These figures suggest the method is capable of substantially reducing spiking activity.

3. **Differential domain formulation.** The mathematical decomposition \(O_l^T = O_l^{T-1} + \Delta_l^T\) and the transformation into the differential domain (Definition 1, Table 1) provide a clean framework for reasoning about temporal redundancy, which is a useful conceptual tool independent of the specific implementation.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **Accuracy numbers are completely absent from the experimental section.** The paper's central claim is that DDLA achieves spike reduction "while ensuring accuracy comparable to baseline models" (abstract, line 27). Yet Section 7 reports only spike counts and spike reductions. Table 2 is explicitly titled "Comparison of spike number and simulation length T" — it contains no accuracy column. Figures 4 and 5 show spike counts, not accuracy. The text discusses "recognition accuracy" only qualitatively (line 249). Without accuracy numbers — neither for baselines nor for the proposed method across any condition — the core value proposition is unverifiable. The reader cannot tell whether the claimed 80.9% per-timestep spike reduction comes at a 0.5% accuracy cost or a 30% cost. This omission invalidates the paper's main empirical contribution.

### Major

- **The computational mechanism for "feature reuse" is never specified.** The paper defines \(\Delta_l^t = O_l^t \oplus O_l^{t-1}\) (Definition 1) and states that static features can be reused from the previous time step (Section 4.2). However, it never explains how \(\Delta_l^t\) can be obtained *without* first computing the full forward pass \(O_l^t\). The XOR operation is a post-hoc characterization, not a computational savings mechanism. Section 5.2 describes DDLA only in vague prose — no pseudocode, no step-by-step procedure, no specification of which quantities are computed at each time step and how the "reuse" is realized algorithmically. The paper's entire computational claim rests on an unspecified mechanism.

- **GSC's inference-time role is unclear and potentially self-defeating.** The paper states: "Our method relies on the output at time \(t\) to compute gradients and identify sensitive neurons at time \(t+1\)" (Section 7, line 240). This strongly suggests gradient computation is needed during inference. The paper never clarifies whether the sensitivity mask is (a) fixed after training and applied as a static pruning pattern, or (b) computed online at each time step. If (b), the gradient computation would likely outweigh any spike savings. If (a), this needs to be stated explicitly with the training procedure described.

- **Overclaimed novelty.** The claim of being "the first to discover the Feature Overlapping Phenomenon" (abstract, contribution 1) is not well supported. The observation that feature maps across consecutive time steps are similar follows directly from weight sharing and repeated input encoding in rate-coded SNNs — it is a natural consequence, not a discovery. Prior work on temporal redundancy, skip-frame computation, and dynamic time-step methods in SNNs has implicitly or explicitly noted this property. The paper's brief related work section (Section 2) does not engage critically enough with this prior art to justify the strong novelty claim. The specific differential-domain transformation may be novel, but the phenomenon itself is not.

### Minor

- **No baseline comparison in the experiments.** Table 2 compares different maximum time steps \(T\) under DDLA, but there is no row/column for the unmodified model without DDLA. Similarly, Figures 4 and 5 lack a no-DDLA baseline curve. This makes it impossible to contextualize the reported reductions — e.g., does the 57.8% total spike reduction for T=6 mean the model went from 100K to 42K spikes, or from 10K to 4K? The paper should report both absolute spike counts for the baseline and the DDLA-modified model.

- **Ablation on the filtering ratio \(k\) is missing.** Theorem 2 identifies \(k\) (the filtering ratio) as a key control parameter that bounds the approximation error. Yet no experiment varies \(k\) and reports the resulting accuracy–spike trade-off. This is a direct, testable prediction of the theoretical analysis that is left unvalidated.

- **Theoretical error bound is not empirically connected to behavior.** Theorems 1 and 2 bound the approximation error in terms of weight norms, firing rates, and the filtering ratio, but these quantities are never measured or compared to observed accuracy. The bound's practical relevance is unclear.

### Trivial

- **Terminology inconsistency.** The Introduction uses "feature shadowing" (line 18), while the rest of the paper uses "Feature Overlapping Phenomenon." The manuscript was not unified on this point.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic's observation that the paper defines \(\Delta\) via XOR of outputs without showing how to compute it without the full forward pass is the most incisive point. It reveals that the paper conflates a *mathematical decomposition* (which is always valid) with a *computational savings mechanism* (which requires a genuinely different computation path). The differential-domain transformation is an interesting representational idea, but the paper never bridges the gap between "we can write the output this way" and "we can compute it more efficiently this way." This is a structural disconnect that the strengths about mathematical formalization do not resolve.

## Suggestions

1. **Report accuracy for every condition.** The paper must provide a table showing classification accuracy for the baseline model and the DDLA model at each time step \(T\) and each filtering ratio \(k\) on all datasets. Without this, the central claim is not empirically supported.

2. **Provide pseudocode or a clear algorithmic description** of the DDLA inference procedure, specifying exactly what is computed at each time step, what is reused from the previous step, and how the sensitivity mask (from GSC) is applied.

3. **Clarify whether GSC operates at training time or inference time.** If the sensitivity mask is learned during training and frozen at inference, state this explicitly. If gradients are computed at inference, analyze the overhead.

4. **Remove or substantially temper the "first to discover" claim.** Acknowledge that temporal feature redundancy is a natural property of rate-coded SNNs and position the contribution as a specific method (differential-domain transformation + sensitivity-based filtering) to exploit it.

5. **Add baseline comparisons** (spike counts and accuracy for the unmodified model) to contextualize the reductions, and include ablation experiments varying the filtering ratio \(k\).
