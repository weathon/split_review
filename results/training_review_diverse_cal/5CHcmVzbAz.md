## Summary

This paper proposes SePPO (Semi-Policy Preference Optimization), an iterative self-improvement method for aligning diffusion models with human preferences. SePPO uses only the winning side of human preference data (no reward model, no paired losing images), generates reference/losing samples from previous checkpoints, and introduces an Anchor-based Adaptive Flipper (AAF) to determine whether those reference samples are actually "winning" or "losing" relative to the current model. The method achieves SOTA results on Pick-a-Pic, HPSv2, and Parti-prompt benchmarks for text-to-image generation and shows improvements on text-to-video.

## Strengths

- **Avoids reward models and paired human preference data**: SePPO requires only winning images from an existing preference dataset, eliminating the need for training a separate reward model or collecting paired (winning/losing) human annotations. The abstract correctly states "without relying on reward models or *paired* human-annotated data." This is a practical advantage over DDPO/SPO (which need reward models) and Diffusion-DPO (which needs paired preference data).

- **Anchor-based Adaptive Flipper (AAF) effectively addresses uncertain reference quality**: The paper identifies a real limitation of prior self-improvement methods (SPIN-Diffusion, DITTO) — reference-generated samples are not guaranteed to be "losing." The AAF mechanism with the sign criterion provides a principled way to detect and handle this. The ablation study (Table 4) convincingly demonstrates this: removing AAF drops PickScore from 21.57 to 20.88 and ImageReward from 0.615 to 0.366.

- **State-of-the-art text-to-image results across multiple benchmarks**: SePPO achieves the highest scores on PickScore (21.57), HPSv2 (27.20), and ImageReward (0.615) on the Pick-a-Pic validation set, outperforming methods that use reward models (DDPO, SPO) and those using paired data (Diffusion-DPO, SPIN-Diffusion). It also achieves the best scores on out-of-distribution HPSv2 and Parti-prompt datasets (Table 2), showing generalization beyond the training distribution.

- **Well-designed reference model sampling strategy**: The paper systematically compares three strategies for selecting which previous checkpoint to use as the reference model, demonstrating that random sampling from all previous checkpoints (${\rm ref}=[0,k-1]$) avoids the overfitting of fixing the initial checkpoint and the instability of using only the latest checkpoint.

- **Ablation study is thorough and informative**: Table 4 ablate both AAF and reference sampling strategies, plus compares the sign function to an indicator function variant, providing clear evidence for each design choice.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Overstated claim about "without human annotation" in the contribution list**: The contribution section (line 27) states the model "can achieve preference alignment without human annotation." This is misleading — the method uses winning images from the Pick-a-Pic dataset, which are human-preferred/human-rated images. The *abstract* correctly says "without paired human-annotated data" (i.e., the losing side is not needed), but the contribution phrasing expands this to "without human annotation" altogether. This inaccuracy will confuse readers about what data the method actually requires. The authors should consistently use the more precise "without paired human-annotated data" or "without a reward model and without the losing side of human preference pairs."

- **Theoretical justification for the sign criterion does not match its application**: Theorem 1 establishes that if *the expectation* of the noise prediction error difference is ≤ 0, then one model is almost surely better than another. However, the sign in Eq. (7) is computed from a *single* Monte Carlo sample (one noise draw, one timestep). The paper then claims (line 212): "According to Theorem 1, if sign=-1, it means that x_0^ref generated by ε_ref has a high probability to be better than the output of ε_θ." This goes beyond what the theorem supports — there is no concentration argument or statistical guarantee connecting a single-sample sign to the expectation-based condition. The theorem provides useful intuition but not rigorous justification for the single-sample decision rule. The authors should honestly characterize this as intuition/heuristic and note that the empirical ablation (not the theorem) is the real support.

### Minor

- **Training procedure description is ambiguous**: Algorithm 1 shows each iteration performing a single gradient update ($\theta_k \leftarrow \theta_{k-1} - \eta_{k-1} \nabla\mathcal{L}$) with $K=7$ iterations. However, the text (line 139) says "we save a checkpoint every 30 updates ($K=7$ iterations)." These are inconsistent — if $K=7$ iterations each have one update, there would be only 7 total updates, not 30. The algorithm implies an inner loop of gradient steps within each iteration but does not show it. The authors should clarify the training loop structure.

- **Figure 3 axis label is inconsistent with its content**: The caption states "The x-axis is the number of diffusion steps $T$," which is the same notation used for diffusion timesteps in the background section. However, the figure shows how scores (PickScore, HPSv2, ImageReward) change over training progression, not over diffusion timesteps. The text (line 139) also refers to "the number of steps $T$ increases" which is ambiguous. This makes the ablation results harder to interpret than they should be.

- **No uncertainty estimates for quantitative results**: The margins over SPIN-Diffusion on key metrics (PickScore: 21.57 vs. 21.55; HPSv2: 27.20 vs. 27.10) are small. Without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or multiple-seed results, readers cannot assess whether these differences are statistically reliable. While single-run evaluation is common practice in this area, the small margins make this more concerning than usual. Even bootstrapped confidence intervals from the evaluation data would help.

- **Text-to-video evaluation lacks preference optimization baselines**: The video experiment compares SePPO only against raw AnimateDiff and an SFT baseline, not against any other preference optimization method applied to video (e.g., a video variant of Diffusion-DPO or SPO). While such variants may not be readily available, the current comparison makes it difficult to attribute gains specifically to SePPO's iterative preference optimization design versus any additional training on the MagicTime dataset. The paper should at minimum acknowledge this limitation.

### Trivial

- The algorithm pseudocode uses the comment "# Or other optimizer, e.g., AdamW" which is unhelpful — specifying the actual optimizer used (AdamW, as stated in the setup) would be clearer.

## Nice-to-Haves

This is a systematic issue worth noting beyond this specific paper. ## Suggestions

2. Restrict Theorem 1 to the role of intuition/motivation and explicitly state that the single-sample sign decision is a heuristic validated empirically (via the ablation), not rigorously justified by the theorem.
