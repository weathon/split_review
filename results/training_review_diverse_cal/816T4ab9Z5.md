## Summary

This paper studies Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) and argues that perfect alignment of positive pairs in GCL may be "poisonous" to generalization. The key empirical finding (Figure 1) is that as augmentation strength increases, intra-class distances do **not** decrease (i.e., better gathering does not drive gains), while inter-class separation increases and accuracy improves — indicating that contrastive learning helps downstream tasks primarily by separating different classes rather than gathering similar ones. The paper provides a theoretical analysis connecting the downstream cross-entropy loss to the InfoNCE loss and augmentation distance (Theorem 2), derives corollaries from information theory and graph spectral theory, and proposes two practical augmentation methods (gradient-guided information preservation and spectrum smoothing) that can be dropped into existing GCL algorithms. Experiments on six benchmarks show consistent improvements across GRACE, GCA, and AD-GCL when augmented with either method.

## Strengths

- **Core empirical finding (Figure 1).** The paper directly measures positive center distance (intra-class), negative center distance (inter-class), and accuracy as functions of dropout rate. The data show that as augmentation strengthens, accuracy rises alongside inter-class separation while intra-class separation does not decrease — directly supporting the claim that GCL benefits mainly from separating classes, not from intra-class gathering. This finding is novel and practically informative for the GCL community.

- **Theoretical framework linking augmentation to downstream generalization.** Theorem 2 derives a lower bound on the downstream mean CE loss in terms of the InfoNCE loss, augmentation distance δ_aug, and other terms. This formally establishes a relationship between contrastive objectives and downstream performance, and explains why stronger augmentation (larger δ_aug) can benefit generalization even though it worsens alignment. The bound's analysis that a weaker constraint on the CE loss permits better achievable performance is conceptually sound (it "does not enforce better performance, but shows a potential better solution" [line 118]).

- **Practical applicability.** The information-based method (gradient-guided importance retention) is clearly described and can be integrated with any GCL algorithm that uses edge/feature masking. The spectrum-based method shows empirical improvements even if underspecified. Table 1 demonstrates consistent improvements across multiple algorithms and datasets, with several comparisons achieving strong statistical significance (e.g., GRACE+S p=0.003, AD-GCL+I p=0.003, AD-GCL+S p=0.006).

- **Mechanistic validation.** Figure 2 shows that the proposed methods achieve higher augmentation distance while preserving similar mutual information between views, consistent with the theoretical recommendation (Corollary 3.1). Figure 3 shows that spectrum augmentation alleviates over-smoothing, enabling deeper models.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The spectral method is operationally underspecified to the point of irreproducibility.** The paper describes modifying eigenvalues λ_i via an update rule driven by changes in θ_i (lines 234–247), but never explains how these eigenvalue modifications translate to concrete graph-structure changes (edge additions/deletions). Eigenvalues of the augmented adjacency matrices A' and A'' are not free parameters that can be independently adjusted — they depend on graph structure. Without specifying how to map the desired eigenvalue changes back to graph edits, a reader cannot reproduce the method. The experimental results for GRACE+S and other spectral variants are thus unverifiable from the description alone. The method needs either: (a) a concrete algorithm for modifying graph structure to achieve spectrum smoothing, or (b) clarification that the eigenvalues are being modified in a different manner (e.g., in a spectral convolution computation) that does not require graph edits.

### Minor

2. **Imprecise interpretation of upper bounds in Theorem 1.** The paper states (line 90) that "both positive and negative center distance are positively related to the magnitude of augmentation δ_aug" based on Theorem 1, which gives *upper* bounds (PCD ≤ δ_y⁺ + ⅔δ_aug, NCD ≤ δ_y⁻ + ⅔δ_aug). An upper bound increasing with δ_aug does not logically imply the actual distance is "positively related" — the actual values could decrease or remain flat even as the bound loosens. However, the paper does validate this relationship experimentally in Figure 1, so the logical gap does not undermine the overall conclusion. The text should be clarified to acknowledge that the bounds alone do not guarantee the relationship, and that the experimental evidence is what confirms it.

3. **Mixed statistical significance.** Several of the reported improvements have p-values exceeding the conventional 0.05 threshold (GRACE+I p=0.155, GCA+I p=0.089, GCA+S p=0.147). While the best variants achieve significance, the paper should discuss which results are statistically robust and which are suggestive but not conclusive. The claim of "consistent and statistically significant improvements" (from the abstract framing) is overstated for the full set of comparisons.

4. **Variance terms in Theorem 2 are mentioned but not analyzed.** The bound includes var(f(v⁺)|y), var(f(v⁰)|y), and var(μ_y). The paper states they "can be represented by inherent properties of the graph and the augmentation distance δ_aug" (line 133) but provides no derivation or experimental measurement of these terms. They could potentially dominate the bound and offset any benefit from increasing δ_aug. An analysis (even empirical) of their magnitude relative to the δ_aug terms would significantly strengthen the theory.

### Trivial

5. **Minor notation/formatting issues.** The paper defines δ_aug² as E||f(v_i⁰)-f(v_i⁺)||² but sometimes uses δ_aug as a linear term in the bound without clarifying whether the linear dependence arises from expansion of δ_aug² or from a separate quantity. The bound in Theorem 2 mixes δ_aug and δ_aug² terms without explanation of the source of the linear term.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Direct test of "poisonous alignment."** While Figure 1 provides correlational evidence, a cleaner experiment comparing a model explicitly constrained to have near-perfect alignment (e.g., very weak augmentation) against one with deliberately worse alignment (stronger augmentation) would more directly validate the central claim.

- **Simple baseline comparison.** A natural sanity check would be: does simply increasing the random dropout ratio in GRACE/GCA achieve similar gains to the proposed methods? This would help disentangle whether the theoretical machinery is needed or whether the improvement simply comes from stronger augmentation.

- **Ablation separating information content and augmentation distance.** The proposed information method jointly affects both MI and δ_aug. An ablation controlling one while varying the other would clarify which factor drives the improvement.
