## Summary

This paper develops a computational model of reciprocally coupled continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs) to explain how place cells in hippocampus (HPC) and grid cells in medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) complement each other for spatial representation. The authors derive theoretically that the coupled CANN dynamics implement gradient-based optimization of the posterior (GOP), demonstrate via simulations that the model achieves Bayesian optimal cue integration (variance matches the theoretical prediction), and show that it avoids the non-local error problem inherent to grid cell phase coding.

## Strengths

- **Principled theoretical link between coupled attractor dynamics and Bayesian inference.** The paper derives that the simplified bump-center dynamics of the coupled CANNs (Eqs. 17–18) are equivalent to gradient ascent on the log posterior (Eq. 19), establishing a mechanistic neural implementation of a specific form of Bayesian decoding. This goes beyond prior descriptive models of HPC-MEC interaction by providing an explicit optimization-theoretic grounding.

- **Demonstration that the model achieves Bayesian optimal cue integration.** The variance of the network's combined decoding matches the theoretical Bayesian prediction (Fig. 3c–d) across noise levels, confirming that the model weights the environmental and motion cues by their reliabilities—the standard operational definition of Bayesian optimal cue combination (Ernst & Banks, 2002).

- **Resolution of non-local errors without sacrificing coding efficiency.** The coupled CANNs avoid the large non-local errors of phase coding (Fig. 4a–b) while retaining the high coding efficiency of phase space coding. This contrasts with prior "constrained range" approaches that resolved non-local errors by restricting the coding range, which also reduced efficiency.

- **Biologically plausible encoding of the correlation prior via congruent connections.** Reciprocal connections are set so that place cells and grid cells with matched preferred location and phase are strongly connected (Eq. 9), and the derivation shows this encodes the correlation prior between sensory cues. This provides a clean, testable synaptic mechanism.

- **Lyapunov function guarantees convergence.** The paper shows the negative posterior acts as a Lyapunov function (Eq. 20), ensuring the dynamics always converge to a local maximum of the posterior—a formal stability guarantee that strengthens the model's plausibility.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "Bayesian optimal" claim conflates two distinct senses of optimality.** The paper uses "Bayesian optimal integration" to describe both (a) weighting cues by their reliabilities in the standard Ernst & Banks sense (which the simulations do validate), and (b) the GOP method itself, which the paper terms "a Bayesian optimal way of information integration" (line 272). GOP is a *local* optimization method: it finds a local maximum of the posterior (explicitly acknowledged in lines 108, 228). While the cue-integration variance result is legitimate, the unqualified language in the abstract ("our model achieves Bayesian optimal integration…") invites readers to infer global MAP optimality, which the model does not guarantee. The paper should sharply distinguish these meanings and qualify the claim to reflect that the optimality demonstrated is specifically about reliability-weighted cue combination under unimodal (or effectively unimodal) posteriors.

- **Missing quantitative metrics for key simulation results.** Figure 3b (scatter plot comparing network decoding to direct GOP) reports no correlation coefficient or R². Figure 3c (distribution overlay) reports no statistical test comparing the network's combined distribution to the Bayesian prediction. Figure 4b (error vs. noise level) has an unlabeled y-axis and unspecified noise values. For a computational study that grounds its central claims in simulations, visual inspection alone is insufficient. The authors should report quantitative agreement measures (e.g., Pearson correlation, mean squared error), confidence intervals or error bars on variance estimates, and specify trial counts and noise levels used.

### Minor

- **Derivation relies on an approximation whose error is uncharacterized.** The derivation projecting CANN dynamics onto two dominating modes (height and position, the first two terms of a Taylor expansion of the bump shape) is mathematically standard for CANN analysis, but the paper does not characterize the approximation error or numerically verify the specific predicted relationship between reciprocal connection strength \(J_{gi,p}\) and the prior variance parameter \(\sigma_{\phi^i}^2 = (8\pi A_g \tau_g)/(\lambda_i J_{gi,p} \rho_p \hat{R}_p)\). A numerical check (e.g., varying \(J_{gi,p}\) and confirming the inferred prior variance changes as predicted) would substantially strengthen the theoretical contribution. (Note: Figure 3b provides indirect validation that the overall dynamics match GOP; the request here is for direct verification of the parameter mapping.)

- **Numerical parameter values not listed.** The paper introduces numerous parameters (\(\tau_p, \tau_g, A_p, A_g, a_p, a_g, J_p, J_g, J_{gi,p}, \rho_p, \rho_g, k_p, k_g, \alpha_p, \alpha_{gi}\), number of cells and modules, spacings \(\lambda_i\), spatial range \(L\)) without reporting their numerical values in the simulations. A table of values is essential for reproducibility.

- **Readout method and trial/initialization details unspecified.** The paper states the bump position of place cells is "read out" but does not specify whether this is the center of mass, peak position, or another method. The number of trials for Figs. 3–4 is not reported. The initial bump position at simulation start is not stated (line 256 clarifies that the *previous* bump position serves as the initial point during tracking, but the first time step is unaddressed).

- **The coding efficiency analysis (Section 2.1) is too rough to be informative.** It uses an average spacing \(\bar{\lambda}\) and a simplified neuron count but does not connect to the paper's core contributions. This section could be condensed or moved to a supplement without loss.

### Trivial
- Figure 4b y-axis is unlabeled in the caption/text description.
- The caption of Figure 2 lists mathematical symbols in a somewhat confusing order.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A robustness experiment testing how quickly the network recovers if the initial bump position is displaced far from the true location (e.g., after a large transient noise). This would test the model's dependence on initial conditions.
- Direct quantitative comparison to alternative models of HPC-MEC interaction (e.g., Agmon & Burak 2020, Li et al. 2020) on decoding accuracy under noise, beyond the qualitative discussion already present in Related Works.
- The insight from Section 5.2 about the previous bump position preventing non-local errors could usefully appear earlier (e.g., in the theoretical analysis section) to clarify why GOP avoids non-local errors despite being a local method.

## Suggestions

1. **Sharpen the "Bayesian optimal" language.** Qualify the claim in the abstract and conclusions to reflect that the demonstrated optimality is about reliability-weighted cue integration under locally unimodal posteriors, not global MAP estimation. Replace "Bayesian optimal integration" with "Bayesian optimal cue integration" or "GOP-based cue integration that achieves Bayesian-optimal variance" to avoid conflation.

2. **Add quantitative metrics to all simulation figures.** Report correlation coefficients for Fig. 3b, a goodness-of-fit measure for the Bayesian prediction in Fig. 3c, and label axes fully in Fig. 4b. Provide trial counts and error bars.

3. **Include a table of numerical parameter values** used in each simulation to ensure reproducibility.

4. **Specify the readout method** for extracting the bump position from the place cell activity profile.

5. **Directly verify the predicted relationship** between \(J_{gi,p}\) and \(\sigma_{\phi^i}^2\) in simulation, or at minimum discuss why this verification is not necessary given the indirect validation in Fig. 3b.

## Score and Decision

This paper makes a genuinely novel theoretical contribution by linking coupled CANN dynamics to gradient-based posterior optimization, with biologically plausible mechanisms and supporting simulations. The core claims are well motivated and the theoretical derivation is sound. The weaknesses are primarily about precision of language, completeness of reporting, and quantitative rigor—all addressable in revisions. No fatal flaws undermine the central contribution.
