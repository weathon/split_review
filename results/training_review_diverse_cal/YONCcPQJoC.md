## Summary

The paper proposes fine-tuning LLMs with auxiliary tasks that involve generating intermediate planning steps (summaries, outlines, key information) alongside the final long-form document, all within a single inference pass. To overcome the lack of human-written planning data, the authors use a stronger LLM (Gemini Ultra) to synthetically generate intermediate steps from full articles, selecting the best candidates via a scoring function. Experiments on SciNews (academic paper → science news) and Wikipedia (topic → article) show consistent ROUGE improvements over standard fine-tuning (e.g., +2.5–4.4% RLsum), and human evaluations show win/loss ratios above 1 on both datasets.

## Strengths

1. **Novel single-turn planning paradigm with empirical validation.** The paper introduces a clean approach where planning steps are integrated into training so the model generates coherent long-form documents end-to-end. Table 3 directly demonstrates that single-turn inference (RLsum 46.61) substantially outperforms multi-turn inference (RLsum 43.73) using the same trained model, validating the core architectural claim.

2. **Synthetic intermediate data generation addresses a real bottleneck.** Algorithm 1 provides a scalable, domain-agnostic method to produce planning data (summaries, outlines, key information) from existing full articles, using few-shot LLM prompting with a scoring function that filters candidates by length ratio and bidirectional entailment. This is a practical contribution that other practitioners could adopt.

3. **Consistent and meaningful empirical gains across settings.** The method (FT w/ I, Output w/ I) achieves +2.5% RLsum on SciNews (44.14→46.61) and +4.4% on Wikipedia (41.28→45.67) over fine-tuning without intermediate steps (Table 1). The gains replicate on Gemini 1.5 Flash (Table 5: RLsum 42.62→43.77), showing robustness to the base model. Human SxS evaluation yields overall win/loss ratios of 3.60 (SciNews) and 1.56 (Wikipedia).

4. **Comprehensive ablation of training recipes.** Table 2 systematically evaluates six combinations of training mixtures and inference tasks, showing that the best configuration (mixture of $x_i \rightarrow y_i$ and $x_i \rightarrow \bz_i \oplus y_i$ with inference producing intermediate steps) outperforms all alternatives. This provides actionable guidance for practitioners.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Confounding distillation effect from the plan-generation model is not controlled for.** The intermediate steps are generated by Gemini Ultra (line 149), a stronger model than the one being fine-tuned (Gemini Pro). The observed improvements could partially or wholly reflect distillation of knowledge from a stronger model rather than the planning mechanism per se. A necessary control would be to generate plans using Gemini Pro itself (the same model being fine-tuned) and check whether improvements persist. Without this control, the core claim that "planning is the source of improvement" is not fully disentangled from "training on data from a stronger model helps." The paper does not acknowledge or discuss this confound.

2. **Human evaluation shows a large unexplained discrepancy between datasets, with no inter-annotator agreement reported.** The human SxS results (Table 4) show SciNews overall W/L = 3.60 vs. Wikipedia W/L = 1.56, and SciNews Verifiability W/L = 7.75 vs. Wikipedia Verifiability W/L = 1.40. The paper mentions this only briefly ("On Wikipedia, we also observed a positive result") without analyzing *why* the gap exists—e.g., whether the SciNews baseline is unusually weak on verifiability, whether Wikipedia articles are harder to improve because the baseline is already stronger, or whether the evaluation setup differs between datasets. No inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen's κ) is reported for either dataset, making it difficult to assess reliability of the judgments. Given only 50 items per dataset, the 7.75 ratio on SciNews is a very strong signal that warrants discussion of potential biases or asymmetries.

3. **The multi-turn baseline comparison (Table 3) uses a model not explicitly trained for the multi-turn setup.** The model in Table 3's multi-turn condition was trained on $x_i \rightarrow \bz_i \oplus y_i$ (generating both plan and article in one pass) but is then used to first generate $\bz_i$, then in a *separate* inference call generate $y_i$ from $x_i \oplus \bz_i$. The model was never explicitly trained on $x_i \oplus \bz_i \rightarrow y_i$ as a standalone task, so the second inference step is zero-shot. While Table 2 shows that including $x_i \oplus \bz_i \rightarrow y_i$ in training does not improve results, the comparison in Table 3 is not a fully controlled test of single-turn vs. multi-turn inference because the two conditions differ in whether the model performs a task it was trained for. The paper's claim that "single-turn outperforms multi-turn" is supported but not as cleanly as claimed.

### Minor

1. **The composition of $\bz_i$ in the experiments is underspecified.** The methodology (Section 4.1) defines three distinct types of intermediate steps (document summary, document outline, document key information) and states "we concentrate on three distinct types," but the experiments never specify whether $\bz_i$ is one type, a concatenation of all three, or something else. The construction section (line 149) says "$\bz_i$ can be any kinds of structure information including document summary, document outline, and document key information," which is ambiguous. This makes it difficult to replicate the experiments.

2. **The scoring function for selecting intermediate steps (Algorithm 1) is presented without validation.** The algorithm uses a sinusoidal scoring function described as "derived from empirical observations" and an entailment score from a hallucination model, but no analysis is provided showing that the selected plans are actually of higher quality than random selection or simpler baselines. While this is an engineering detail in the data pipeline rather than a core scientific claim, its unvalidated status means the data quality that drives the main results rests on an unverified component.

3. **No statistical significance or variance reporting for ROUGE scores.** The paper reports ROUGE scores as point estimates without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Given that improvements are on the order of 1–3 ROUGE points (which is typical for this style of work), the reliability of these differences is unclear. While this practice is common in LLM fine-tuning papers, it would strengthen the claims to at least report variance across evaluation samples.

4. **The "single auxiliary task" phrasing in the abstract is slightly imprecise.** The abstract claims "a single auxiliary task that does not require multiple rounds of prompting or planning," but the methodology (Section 4.3) introduces *two* auxiliary tasks: $x_i \rightarrow \bz_i \oplus y_i$ and $x_i \oplus \bz_i \rightarrow y_i$. While these implement a single *type* of auxiliary task (planning), the wording could mislead readers about the complexity of the training mixture.

## Novel Insights

The reviews reveal that the paper's central tension is between two interpretations of its results: (1) planning-as-auxiliary-task genuinely improves long-form generation, vs. (2) the gains come from distillation from a stronger model (Gemini Ultra) combined with longer training sequences. These interpretations are not mutually exclusive, but the paper would be substantially stronger if it disentangled them. The stark asymmetry between SciNews and Wikipedia human evaluation scores—especially the 7.75 Verifiability ratio on SciNews versus 1.40 on Wikipedia—suggests that the method's effectiveness may be domain-dependent in ways the authors have not yet characterized. SciNews involves abstract-to-news generation where the input paper provides rich structured content, while Wikipedia generation starts from a single-sentence prompt; the planning signal may be more beneficial when there is more structure to extract from the input.

## Suggestions

1. **Control for the plan-generation model**: Repeat the main experiment with plans generated by Gemini Pro (the same model being fine-tuned) rather than Gemini Ultra. If improvements persist, the planning mechanism is likely driving the gains; if they diminish, the paper's findings are more about distillation, which is still interesting but requires different framing.

2. **Analyze and discuss the human evaluation discrepancy**: Provide hypotheses for why SciNews shows much larger gains than Wikipedia (e.g., baseline quality differences, task difficulty, evaluation artifacts). Report inter-annotator agreement for both datasets.

3. **Specify the exact composition of $\bz_i$** used in the experiments (which types of intermediate steps, how they are structured/concatenated) to enable replication.

4. **Provide variance estimates** (e.g., bootstrap confidence intervals) for ROUGE scores to give readers a sense of reliability.

5. **Validate the scoring function** with a simple comparison (e.g., human preference for algorithm-selected plans vs. randomly selected plans, or downstream ROUGE from different plan selection strategies).

## Score and Decision

This paper proposes a reasonable approach with a clear hypothesis and generally supportive experiments across two datasets and two base models. The main claims are plausible and the ablations (Table 2) are informative. However, the presence of a distillation confound that is neither controlled nor discussed, combined with unexplained human evaluation discrepancies and the absence of inter-annotator agreement, means the evidence does not yet convincingly isolate planning as the source of improvement. The contribution is promising but needs stronger controls before the core claims can be accepted at face value.

**Overall assessment**: The paper presents a sound idea with preliminary positive evidence, but the experimental design has gaps that prevent full confidence in the claimed benefits of the planning mechanism. Recommended for revision rather than acceptance in current form.
