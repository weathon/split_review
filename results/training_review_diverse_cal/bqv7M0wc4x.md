## Summary

This paper introduces ICL-TSVD, a continual learning method that integrates the empirically strong RanPAC approach with the theoretically grounded Ideal Continual Learner (ICL) framework. The key insight is that random ReLU features — used in RanPAC — are highly ill-conditioned, causing instability in both min-norm ICL (numerical errors) and RanPAC (generalization/double descent). ICL-TSVD addresses this by continually truncating the SVD of the lifted features, discarding the smallest singular values. The method is efficient (O(E(k_{t-1}+m_t)^2) vs RanPAC's O(E^3)), uses a single hyperparameter (truncation percentage ζ) that is robust across a wide range, and is accompanied by theoretical bounds on estimation and generalization error. Experiments on 8 datasets across multiple incremental settings show consistent improvements over RanPAC and other baselines, with especially dramatic gains in the challenging Inc-1 (one-class-at-a-time) setting where RanPAC collapses on some datasets.

## Strengths

- **Identifies the root cause of instability and addresses it with a principled fix.** The paper convincingly shows that ill-conditioning of random ReLU features (eigenvalues dropping to 10⁻⁵) directly correlates with accuracy collapse in min-norm ICL (Figure 1, Figure 3). This diagnosis — that the problem is the near-zero singular values — is clean, well-supported, and motivates the TSVD remedy naturally. The distinction between the cause of instability in min-norm ICL (numerical errors from tiny eigenvalues) versus RanPAC (generalization errors / double descent) is insightful.

- **Strong empirical performance, especially in the Inc-1 setting.** Table 4 (Inc-1) is the paper's strongest piece of evidence: ICL-TSVD achieves 77.65% final accuracy (avg. across 8 datasets) vs RanPAC's 66.00%, and on StanfordCars ICL-TSVD reaches 74.44% while RanPAC collapses to 1.19%. This demonstrates robustness to long task sequences (hundreds of classes) where cross-validation for RanPAC's λ fails due to small validation sets. Across the main Inc-{5,10,20} results (Table 1), ICL-TSVD consistently outperforms or matches all baselines, including RanPAC.

- **Efficiency and scalability are real advantages.** The O(E(k_{t-1}+m_t)^2) complexity vs RanPAC's O(E^3) is a genuine algorithmic contribution, enabling the use of larger embedding dimensions (E=10⁵) while being faster. Figure 5 shows ICL-TSVD can be up to 1000× faster than RanPAC at the same E=25,000. This is not a trivial engineering improvement — it follows directly from the SVD truncation reducing the effective rank.

- **Robust to its single hyperparameter.** Figure 4a shows stable accuracy across ζ ∈ [10%, 90%], a stark contrast to RanPAC's sensitivity to λ (Figure 2). This removes the need for expensive cross-validation and makes the method practical for settings where validation data is scarce.

- **Theoretical guarantees in a setting where prior work had none.** Theorems 1 and 2 provide bounds on training and generalization errors for the continual truncation setting, which prior PCR theory only covered in the offline (single-truncation) case. The recurrence relation (Lemma A.1) that captures the continual learning dynamics is non-trivial and extends the ICL framework.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The theory relies on a strong linear model assumption that is not justified for classification.** The guarantees (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2) assume Y_{1:t} = W_true H_{1:t} + ε, i.e., that the one-hot label vectors are generated by a linear function of the random ReLU features plus noise. This is a strong structural assumption. One-hot labels for classification are not, in general, well-approximated as a linear function of any fixed feature representation. The paper's defense ("This suggests the linear model assumption is adequate," line 270) is somewhat circular — the empirical success of the method is used to validate the assumption that the theory requires, rather than the theory explaining the empirical success independently. If the true process deviates substantially from linearity, the terms ∥W_true∥_F² and ∥ε∥² in the bounds could be large enough to render them vacuous. This does not invalidate the method — it may still work well — but it weakens the claim that the theory "proves" small errors in a broadly applicable sense. The paper would benefit from an explicit discussion of misspecification and what happens when the linear model is only approximately correct.

### Minor

- **The accuracy comparison conflates the effect of the method with the effect of a larger embedding dimension.** ICL-TSVD uses E=10⁵ while RanPAC uses E=10⁴ (its default). The paper argues this is fair because ICL-TSVD's efficiency enables the larger E. This is a reasonable systems-level argument, but it prevents the reader from disambiguating how much of the improvement comes from the TSVD strategy versus simply having more random features. The paper mentions scaling laws (appendix) and provides same-E runtime comparisons, but does not show same-E accuracy. Adding an experiment with ICL-TSVD at E=10⁴ (matching RanPAC's default) on a subset of datasets would cleanly isolate the truncation contribution. Without this, the claim that ICL-TSVD "uniformly outperforms" RanPAC is slightly overstated — it uniformly outperforms RanPAC operating at its default settings, which is a practically meaningful but not fully controlled comparison.

- **The theoretical bounds are expressed in data-dependent quantities (γ_t, a_t) rather than directly in terms of the truncation hyperparameter ζ or threshold δ.** While the paper provides empirical intuition for why γ_t is large and a_t is small, the bounds do not offer a direct, actionable relationship like "if eigenvalues smaller than δ are truncated, the training error is bounded by c·δ·∥W_true∥_F² + …". The current presentation, while technically correct, obscures the direct quantitative implications. The paper would be stronger with a corollary or simplified bound in terms of δ.

- **No explicit limitations or failure-mode discussion.** The paper does not discuss scenarios where ICL-TSVD might struggle — e.g., when pre-trained features are poor, when the linear model assumption is substantially violated, or when memory constraints limit the SVD storage for very long task sequences with large E. A brief limitations paragraph would improve the paper's scientific honesty.

- **RanPAC's hyperparameter selection is not fully specified.** The paper states that RanPAC's cross-validation can fail when the validation set is small, but does not clarify how the reported RanPAC results were obtained: did the authors use the original RanPAC cross-validation procedure, or a fixed λ? This matters for interpreting the reported RanPAC numbers.
