## Summary

MG-LLaVA extends the LLaVA architecture by incorporating three granularities of visual features: low-resolution global features (ViT), high-resolution fine-grained features (ConvNeXt) fused via a Conv-Gate network, and object-level features extracted through RoI alignment from an open-vocabulary detector (RAM + OWL-ViT). The model is trained on a large corpus of public instruction-tuning data (including ALLaVA) and evaluated across 11 image/video benchmarks with LLMs ranging from 3.8B to 34B parameters.

## Strengths

- **Novel integration of object-level features from an open-vocabulary detector into an MLLM.** The paper is the first to incorporate open-vocabulary detection (RAM → OWL-ViT) into a LLaVA-style pipeline to extract object-centric visual tokens via RoI alignment, going beyond prior work that either used fixed-category detectors (COCO 80) or no object-level features at all. The ablation in Table 3c confirms that RAM-based tagging outperforms COCO-80 tags (69.2 vs 68.3 MMBench-Dev, 34.5 vs 32.9 MMStar), supporting the design choice empirically.

- **Lightweight Conv-Gate fusion network with thorough ablation.** The Conv-Gate fusion (Eq. 4) combines low-res ViT and high-res ConvNeXt features via a gating mechanism while maintaining the original token count. Table 4 systematically compares it against three alternatives (Resampler: 55.6, Channel Concat: 68.9, Patch Info Mining: 68.3), with Conv-Gate winning at 69.8 MMBench-Dev, while adding only 0.45 TFLOPS over the baseline.

- **Competitive performance across diverse benchmarks and model scales.** With Yi1.5-34B, MG-LLaVA achieves 80.1/79.1 on MMBench-Dev/Test and 73.7 on SEEDBench-Image, surpassing GPT-4V on those specific benchmarks. It also outperforms all open-source models of comparable size and shows gains on video QA (MSVD-QA 71.5, MSRVTT-QA 59.8, exceeding Video-LLaVA).

- **Comprehensive ablation study.** Tables 3a-c systematically ablate each design choice: component contributions (object features, fusion), fusion module variants, object-feature merging strategies (cross-attention vs. concatenation), and tagging models. This provides clear empirical justification for each architectural decision.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The comparison to GPT-4V is not contextualized with limitations.** The paper claims to "notably surpass" GPT-4V on "various multimodal benchmarks" (lines 81-82, 94, 496). While the paper names the specific benchmarks (MMBench, SEEDBench) where MG-LLaVA leads, it does not acknowledge benchmarks where GPT-4V outperforms MG-LLaVA by larger margins: TextVQA (78.0 vs 70.0), ScienceQA-Image (82.1 vs 77.0), or that MMStar results are comparable (49.7 vs 47.9). More critically, the model is trained on 1.4M+ examples from ALLaVA (lines 267), which is synthetic data generated by GPT-4V itself. Training on GPT-4V outputs and then claiming superiority over GPT-4V on overlapping benchmarks — without any contamination analysis, discussion of benchmark cutoff dates, or evaluation on post-training benchmarks — undermines the credibility of this comparison. This does not invalidate the paper's contribution (which primarily concerns multi-granularity features) but the GPT-4V framing is overclaimed relative to the evidence.

- **Missing ablation: Conv-Gate fusion without object features.** Table 3 compares (a) baseline, (b) object-features-only, and (c) both. There is no condition with Conv-Gate fusion alone (high-resolution features) without object features. This is the most informative condition for disentangling whether the gains come primarily from the high-resolution fusion (already studied in LLaVA-HR) or from the object-level features (the novel contribution). Without this, the marginal contribution of object-level features on top of high-resolution fusion cannot be determined, weakening the central claim that multi-granularity (especially object-level) features matter.

- **ALLaVA data contamination concern unaddressed.** The training pipeline uses 708K ALLaVA captions for pretraining and 692K ALLaVA instructions for fine-tuning — data generated by GPT-4V. The paper's comparison to GPT-4V on widely-used benchmarks (MMBench, SEEDBench) without any contamination analysis, discussion of data overlap, or evaluation on benchmarks released after ALLaVA's cutoff is a significant omission. This concern is heightened because MMBench and SEEDBench are popular benchmarks likely included in the GPT-4V training or distillation pipeline. The paper should at minimum acknowledge this limitation and discuss what safeguards would be needed.

### Minor

- **Conv-Gate fusion architecture is underspecified for reproducibility.** Equation 4 gives $\mathbf{E}_F = \mathbf{E}_L + G(Conv(\mathbf{E}_L), Conv(\mathbf{E}_H)) \times \mathbf{E}_H$, but the function $G$, the number of 1D convolution layers, kernel sizes, activation functions, and the exact gating mechanism are not described. The paper says "inspired from" LLaVA-HR but does not specify deviations or adaptations. For a central architectural component, this level of detail is insufficient for independent reproduction.

- **Scaling claim is partially confounded by LLM family.** The paper claims "clear scalability" (line 93) when MG-LLaVA is scaled from 3.8B to 34B. However, comparing across different LLM families (Phi3, Vicuna, LLaMA3, Yi1.5) confounds model quality with model size. Vicuna-13B (72.2 MMBench-Dev) performs essentially identically to Vicuna-7B (72.1) and worse than Phi3-3.8B (73.4), suggesting the performance scaling is driven more by base LLM quality than by the MG-LLaVA method. A cleaner demonstration would fix the LLM family (e.g., Vicuna-7B/13B/33B) to isolate the method's scaling properties.

- **Resampler collapse not discussed.** Table 4 shows that substituting the Conv-Gate fusion with a Flamingo-style Resampler causes a catastrophic 13.6-point drop (55.6 vs 69.2 baseline) on MMBench-Dev. This is a striking failure of a well-established architectural component that merits explanation (e.g., does the Resampler fail due to training instability, token count mismatch, or representation conflict?). Without discussion, this omission weakens the analysis.

- **Single-seed runs with no variance estimates.** The paper fixes all seeds (line 314) but reports no variance or confidence intervals. For ablation differences of 0.4–1.0% (Table 3), it is impossible to assess whether these are meaningful or within noise. Multi-seed runs or statistical testing would significantly strengthen the empirical claims.

## Suggestions
1. **Add the missing ablation condition** — Include a row in Table 3 with Conv-Gate fusion (high-resolution) but no object-level features. This is the single most informative missing experiment for evaluating the object-level contribution.
2. **Either remove the GPT-4V comparison or contextualize it honestly** — Acknowledge benchmarks where GPT-4V leads and add a contamination analysis or discussion of ALLaVA's provenance. 4. **Report variance** — Run ablations with at least 3 seeds or report existing published variance for the benchmarks used.
5. **Fix the LLM family for the scaling claim** — Present results with a consistent LLM family (e.g., Vicuna-7B/13B/33B or LLaMA-7B/13B/70B) to substantiate the "clear scalability" claim.

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a genuine contribution — integrating open-vocabulary detection-based object features into a multi-resolution MLLM — and backs it with extensive benchmarking and structured ablations. However, the overclaimed GPT-4V comparison (especially without addressing ALLaVA contamination), the missing ablation that would disentangle the object-level contribution from high-resolution fusion, and the underspecified architecture details are significant gaps. These are addressable with revisions. The core technical contribution is modest but solid.
