## Summary

This paper proposes Compose and Conquer (CnC), a conditional diffusion framework with two technical contributions: (1) **Depth Disentanglement Training (DDT)**, which uses synthetic image triplets (foreground, background, mask) to train a local fuser that processes two depth maps through separate streams, enabling the model to learn relative depth ordering of objects; and (2) **Soft Guidance**, which masks cross-attention similarity matrices using a binary foreground mask to restrict CLIP image embeddings (foreground/background tokens) to attend only to their corresponding spatial regions, enabling localized injection of global semantics. Evaluations on COCO-Stuff and Pick-a-Pic show competitive performance against baselines including ControlNet, Uni-ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and Composer.

## Strengths

- **DDT enables relative depth understanding without explicit 3D supervision.** The synthetic triplet construction (compositing a foreground object over an inpainted background and extracting depth maps from each) provides training signal for the model to learn that objects in the foreground depth map should occlude those in the background depth map. Figure 2 qualitatively demonstrates that DDT-trained models correctly handle occlusion while models trained on single depth maps fail. The depth MAE improvements in Table 2 provide quantitative support.

- **Soft guidance prevents concept bleeding while injecting localized global semantics.** Section 3.3 describes how the cross-attention similarity matrix is masked via a Boolean matrix M', forcing foreground/background CLIP tokens to attend only to their respective latent regions. Figure 5 (conflicting semantics) shows that increasing λ_bg intensifies background style while the foreground "igloo" identity remains intact — a clear demonstration of disentangled semantic injection without cross-contamination.

- **Competitive quantitative performance against multi-condition baselines.** Table 1 shows the finetuned CnC achieves the best CLIPScore (0.296) and competitive FID/IS against ControlNet, Uni-ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, Composer, and GLIGEN on COCO-Stuff. Table 2 reports the lowest LPIPS and best depth MAE on both datasets, with strong SSIM on Pick-a-Pic.

- **Staged training strategy is well-motivated.** The local fuser (28 epochs) and global fuser (24 epochs) are trained independently before joint finetuning (9 epochs). Table 1 confirms that the joint finetuning phase substantially improves over the independent-inference version, providing evidence that this staged approach helps the two modules coordinate.

- **Dataset choice reasoning is thoughtful and explicitly discussed.** Section 4.3 explains why both COCO-Stuff (real images, pixel-wise annotations) and Pick-a-Pic (synthetic, aligned with SD's prior) are used, arguing that the synthetic dataset prevents prior drift while providing rich posterior conditioning. This level of design rationale is a strength.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Soft guidance mask acquisition at inference is underspecified, harming reproducibility.** The paper describes in detail how the binary foreground mask M is obtained during training (from COCO-Stuff annotations or U²-Net salient object detection on Pick-a-Pic). However, it never specifies how a user obtains M at inference time. The mask is required by the soft guidance mechanism to construct M' and restrict attention (Section 3.3, Eq. for M' = concat(J, φ(M), 1-φ(M))). Without this detail, a reader cannot reproduce the method's inference procedure. The claim that soft guidance works "without the use of any additional localization cues" (abstract) is also misleading without clarifying whether M is derived from the depth maps (e.g., by thresholding), provided as user input, or generated by an auxiliary segmentation model. This gap does not invalidate the core idea — the mechanism itself is clearly described — but it is the single biggest obstacle to reproducibility and must be resolved.

### Minor

- **Depth ordering is not directly validated with a dedicated metric.** The paper claims that DDT enables "infer[ring] the absolute depth placement of objects" and "3D depth-aware placement," yet the evaluation does not include a metric that directly measures depth ordering accuracy (e.g., percentage of generations where the intended foreground object correctly occludes the background, measured via a pretrained depth estimator). Depth MAE (Table 2) is a reasonable proxy — it captures overall depth fidelity — but it does not isolate the ordering claim. The qualitative results (Figure 2) are compelling but not systematic. A dedicated depth-ordering metric would substantially strengthen this core claim.

- **Quantitative comparison is asymmetric in conditioning inputs.** CnC uses two depth maps and two CLIP image embeddings as conditions, while baselines (ControlNet, Uni-ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) use at most one depth map and one exemplar image. The paper acknowledges this asymmetry in Section 4.2 ("our model takes in an additional depth map with inpainted regions that may not reflect the prior distribution"), but this honest acknowledgment does not fully resolve the methodological concern: the superior reconstruction metrics (LPIPS, depth MAE) may partially reflect the extra conditioning rather than the architectural innovation. A controlled comparison where baseline models are given a simple fusion of two depth maps (e.g., concatenated or averaged) would help disentangle the effect of DDT from the effect of more input channels.

- **Missing ablation: DDT vs. simple depth concatenation.** The paper compares DDT-trained models against models trained on depth maps of I_s (the original composite image), which is a valid but limited baseline. It does not compare DDT against a simpler approach of feeding two separately encoded depth maps that are naïvely fused (e.g., concatenated before entering the network) without the separate-stream architecture. Such an ablation would clarify whether DDT's benefit comes from its late-fusion architecture or simply from having two depth maps as input.

### Trivial

- Figure 2 would benefit from an explicit annotation indicating which samples use the foreground depth map vs. background depth map vs. combined depth maps, to make the comparison more self-contained.

## Nice-to-Haves
