## Summary

This paper proposes using Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to study mechanistic similarity between Transformer (Pythia-160M) and Mamba (130M) language models. The authors find that SAE features exhibit substantial cross-architecture similarity (mean MPPC = 0.74), introduce skylines (model seed variant and SAE seed variant) to bound the marginal effect of architectural change, and provide a circuit-level analysis of Mamba's induction mechanism—identifying an "Off-by-One" motif where token information is written into the SSM state at the next position rather than combined directly.

## Strengths

1. **First cross-architecture feature comparison via SAEs.** The paper demonstrates that SAEs can extract comparable features from Transformers and Mambas, while a neuron baseline (MLP neurons vs. Mamba post-SiLU activations) shows near-zero similarity (Section 4.4, Figure 3a). This establishes SAEs as a necessary tool for architecture-agnostic interpretability and provides direct evidence that most features transfer across these architectures.

2. **Principled skyline methodology.** The paper introduces two skylines (model seed variant and SAE seed variant) and compares them to the cross-architecture MPPC. The finding that >60% of Pythia features have near-zero MPPC difference between cross-arch and model-seed-variant similarity (Figure 3b) is a concrete, well-designed quantification that architectural variation alone does not strongly disrupt feature universality.

3. **Novel circuit analysis of Mamba's induction mechanism.** Path patching experiments identify layer 17's SSM state and the local convolution at token B₁+1 as critical for Mamba's induction circuit (Section 6.1). The "Off-by-One" motif—where Mamba writes token information into the SSM state at the *next* position via its convolution layer—is a genuine mechanistic finding that advances understanding of how SSM-based models implement in-context learning differently from attention-based models.

4. **Depth specialization across architectures.** The finding that matched feature pairs concentrate on a diagonal when mapping Pythia layers (ℓ out of 12) to Mamba layers (~2ℓ out of 24) (Figure 3c) extends prior neuron-level depth specialization work to cross-architecture comparison and suggests a scaled universal motif.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Missing SAE training hyperparameters (reproducibility gap).** The paper reports only the dictionary size (F=32D=24576) but omits learning rate, batch size, optimizer, number of training steps, sparsity coefficient (L1 penalty weight), and reconstruction metrics (loss, L0). SAE training is the paper's central experimental method; these details are essential for reproducibility and for evaluating whether the SAE seed variant skyline (which assumes well-converged SAEs) is meaningful. Without them, other researchers cannot reproduce or build on the results.

2. **Unsubstantiated RWKV claim.** The paper states "We repeated the experiments conducted on Mamba for RWKV, supporting our conclusions" (Section 4.4) but provides no data, figure, or even qualitative description. This dangling claim cannot be evaluated and either needs to be removed or substantiated with actual results in the main text.

### Minor

3. **MPPC null distribution not established.** The maximum pairwise Pearson correlation is taken over ~25,000 candidate features, but the paper does not establish what MPPC values would arise by chance (e.g., via a permutation test shuffling token-feature correspondence). The comparative claims (cross-arch vs. skylines) are robust to this issue since the same metric is applied across conditions, but the absolute claim that ">30% of features have MPPC > 0.95" lacks a calibration baseline and could be inflated by multiple comparisons. A null distribution would substantially strengthen the quantitative arguments.

4. **Auto-interpretation scoring not validated.** Section 5.2 uses GPT-4o to assign complexity and monosemanticity scores to features and draws conclusions about how MPPC relates to these properties. No human validation is provided—not even a small sample. While automated interpretability is widely used in the SAE literature, the paper's specific claim that "subtracting the model-seed skyline cancels out the effect of feature complexity" (Section 5.2) rests on these scores, and the observed correlations (Figures 5a, 5b) could reflect LLM scoring heuristics rather than genuine feature properties. A small human evaluation (e.g., 100 features, 3 raters) would address this.

5. **Circuit analogy to Transformers is interpretive, not demonstrated with parallel methodology.** Section 6 compares Mamba's induction circuit to the Transformer induction head circuit, but the Transformer side is described from prior work, not analyzed with the same path-patching setup on Pythia-160M under identical conditions. The "Off-by-One" motif is a solid Mamba finding, but the paper's claim of "structural analogy" (Section 6.2) is asserted rather than demonstrated with parallel evidence. Providing the Transformer comparison experimentally, or explicitly framing it as a literature-based comparison rather than a demonstrated equivalence, would improve clarity.

6. **Data preprocessing details incomplete.** The paper states "1 million tokens sampled from OpenWebText" (Section 4.2.1) but does not specify how sequences were constructed, whether tokens came from the same context, or whether the data was de-duplicated relative to training data. These details affect activation patterns and thus the correlation analysis.

### Trivial

7. **Depth specialization analysis lacks statistical significance test.** The diagonal pattern in Figure 3c is qualitatively compelling, but no null model (e.g., random permutation of layer labels) is provided to confirm the pattern is non-trivial. Given that both models have different numbers of layers, a monotonic mapping might arise simply from how the residual stream evolves; a permutation test would cleanly establish reliability.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A null distribution for MPPC (shuffling feature-token correspondence) to calibrate the absolute similarity values.
- Human validation of a small sample of GPT-4o feature interpretations.
- A parallel path-patching analysis on Pythia-160M's induction circuit for direct comparison.
- A discussion of whether differences in embedding matrices (even with the same tokenizer) could confound feature comparisons.
- If the method were extended, a different non-linear decomposition (e.g., PCA or ICA) could serve as an additional baseline beyond the current neuron baseline.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting insight that emerges from the reviews—beyond the paper's own contributions—concerns the interaction between feature complexity and cross-architecture similarity. The paper shows that simple features (single-token detectors) achieve near-perfect cross-arch MPPC while complex features show lower similarity, and the skylines help bound this effect. This suggests that the "universality" the paper finds may be real but is partially driven by the fact that both models learn the same low-level linguistic primitives (punctuation, digits, suffixes). Whether higher-level, more abstract features are equally universal is less clear from the current evidence. The depth specialization result (Pythia layer ℓ ≈ Mamba layer 2ℓ) is also a genuinely novel structural observation that could inform future cross-architectural comparisons.

## Suggestions

1. Report all SAE training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, training steps, L1 coefficient, final reconstruction loss and L0) in a reproducibility table.
2. Provide the RWKV data or remove the claim.
3. Add a null distribution for MPPC via a permutation test (shuffle feature-token assignment within each model and recompute cross-arch MPPC).
4. Validate a small random sample of GPT-4o feature interpretations with human raters.
5. Run the same path-patching induction analysis on Pythia-160M for a direct quantitative comparison, or reframe the Transformer comparison as literature-based.
6. Add a statistical baseline for the depth specialization diagonal (e.g., randomly permuting layer assignments).

## Score and Decision

The paper tackles an important question with a novel methodology and produces several genuinely interesting findings (cross-arch feature similarity, skyline quantification, Mamba induction circuit with Off-by-One motif). The central quantitative claims are supported by comparisons across conditions even if the absolute MPPC values lack calibration. The main weaknesses are fixable: missing SAE training details (a significant reproducibility gap) and the dangling RWKV claim. The other issues (null distribution, auto-interpretation validation) are standard methodological improvements that would strengthen the paper but do not undermine its core contributions.

I recommend acceptance with major revision to address the reproducibility gap and substantiate the RWKV claim. The paper makes a solid contribution to mechanistic interpretability and cross-architecture analysis.
