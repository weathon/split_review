## Summary

This paper presents ARGMINN, a mechanistically interpretable neural architecture for regulatory genomics. The architecture has two main innovations: (1) a filter-overlap regularizer that penalizes convolutional filters from sharing overlapping weights/activations, forcing each filter to encode a distinct motif; and (2) a modified attention mechanism with a memory stream that allows motif instances and syntax to be read directly from attention scores and filter activations in a single forward pass. The paper evaluates extensively on motif discovery, motif instance calling, QTL prioritization, robustness, and interpretable sequence generation, consistently outperforming baselines (standard CNN, ExplaiNN, MoDISco). It also includes a theoretical expressivity claim (Theorem 1) and an empirical validation on a challenging REST binding task.

## Strengths

- **Consistently superior motif discovery across multiple datasets**: ARGMINN identifies known relevant motifs with greater accuracy, fewer redundancies, and fewer extraneous motifs compared to standard CNN, ExplaiNN, and MoDISco on both simulated and experimental datasets. For FOXA2 in HepG2, ARGMINN found 4 motifs whose top match was a relevant motif (vs. 2 for CNN), achieved the highest motif similarity to ground truth (Figure 1c), and produced the fewest extraneous patterns (Figure 1d).

- **Direct motif instance and syntax extraction without post-hoc pipelines**: The architecture design allows motif instance calling and syntax discovery by tracing attention scores back to filter activations. On REST data, ARGMINN directly recovered the known spacing preferences of the two half-sites (Figure 2b), and its motif instances quantitatively outperformed MoDISco+FIMO in a precision-recall comparison (Figure 2c), including when FIMO was given the same ARGMINN-discovered motifs.

- **Empirical demonstration of expressivity on complex syntax**: On a simulated REST dataset with strict half-site ordering and variable spacing requirements, ARGMINN achieves 90.9% accuracy versus 73.4% for ExplaiNN, showing it captures grammars and interactions that prior interpretable architectures cannot.

- **Robustness to adversarial and natural variations**: ARGMINN's performance degrades least under varying GC content, resists adversarial attacks that drop a standard CNN's accuracy from 88% to 55%, and Ledidi-generated sequences from ARGMINN contain significantly stronger true SPI1 motifs than those from CNN or ExplaiNN (p = 8.95e−18 and 5.53e−146).

- **Interpretable sequence generation validated by independent oracles**: Generated HepG2-accessible sequences are predicted as accessible as natural sequences (p = 0.75) by the independent oracle Borzoi, and far more accessible than shuffled backgrounds (p = 1.78e−32), demonstrating that the mechanistic interpretations enable truly controlled generation.

- **Transferable filter-overlap regularizer**: Applying the same regularizer to a standard CNN yields filters that encode relevant, non-redundant motifs (Supplementary Figure), showing broader utility beyond ARGMINN.

- **Predictive performance robustness to hyperparameter choices**: ARGMINN's performance and interpretability remain stable across orders of magnitude of the loss weights for its regularizers (Supplementary Figure), making it practical for end users.

- **The filter-overlap regularizer penalizes weight overlap rather than directly penalizing activation-position overlap.** While related (overlapping weights with co-activation is a strong signal of redundancy), the stated goal is to prevent filters from "activating on overlapping sequences," but the loss $\mathcal{L}_o$ penalizes the L1 norm of overlapping filter *weights* scaled by the other filter's activation. ## Removed Points

The following points from the reviews were removed with justification:

1. **"Filter-overlap regularizer cannot be backpropagated due to argmax non-differentiability"** — Removed as factually incorrect. The loss uses argmax to select which filter indices to compare, but gradients flow through the L1 norms of the selected weight matrices (differentiable) and through the activation multiplier $A_{j,b}$ (differentiable). The argmax is a hard selection operation, which is standard practice in deep learning (c.f. straight-through estimators, hard attention). The weights receive gradients through the L1 penalty terms.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting finding across these reviews is the tension between the paper's genuinely impressive empirical results and the incompleteness of its formal specifications. The architecture clearly works well across multiple challenging benchmarks—motif discovery, instance calling, adversarial robustness, sequence generation—but the paper underspecifies the mechanisms (missing softmax, conceptual proxy in the regularizer) in ways that make it harder than necessary for readers to understand why it works.
