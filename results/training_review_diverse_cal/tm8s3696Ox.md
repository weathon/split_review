## Summary

This paper proposes Co-Boosting, a one-shot federated learning framework that jointly and iteratively improves both the quality of synthetic data for knowledge distillation and the ensemble aggregation weights. In each epoch, hard samples are generated adversarially (using GHM-based difficulty weighting and ensemble-server disagreement), the ensemble is refined via gradient-based client reweighting on these samples, and the server is distilled from the improved ensemble on the enriched data. Across five datasets and multiple non-IID and heterogeneous-model settings, Co-Boosting consistently and substantially outperforms existing baselines (FedDF, F-ADI, F-DAFL, DENSE).

## Strengths

- **Demonstrated mutual enhancement via isolation experiments**: The ablation study (Table 5) systematically isolates the contributions of hard sample generation (GHS), on-the-fly diverse hard samples (DHS), and ensemble enhancement (EE). The full Co-Boosting method (65.40% on SVHN, 47.20% on CIFAR-10) significantly outperforms any single component (e.g., EE alone: 62.67% on SVHN; GHS alone: 61.18%), directly supporting the core claim that co-boosting is the operative mechanism.

- **Consistent, often large gains over strong baselines**: In Table 1, Co-Boosting outperforms all baselines (FedDF, F-ADI, F-DAFL, DENSE) across all five datasets at three heterogeneity levels. Under extreme non-IID (α=0.05), gains over the best baseline reach 12.87% (MNIST), 8.83% (CIFAR-10), and 5.16% (SVHN) absolute. The pattern holds under C_cls partition (Table 4, e.g., 53.91% vs. 38.14% for 3-class/client) and heterogeneous client architectures (Table 3, e.g., 62.30% vs. 58.67% on CIFAR-10 α=0.1).

- **Practicality by design**: The method requires no client-side training modifications, no additional data or model transmissions, and handles heterogeneous client architectures. This makes it directly applicable to model-market scenarios where only pretrained models are available.

- **Theoretically grounded formulation**: The min-max problem (Eq. 16) formalizes the interplay between data perturbation and ensemble weight optimization, providing a clean optimization perspective linking the two components.

## Weaknesses

### Major

None that threaten the paper's core claims. The empirical evidence is strong and consistent across diverse settings.

### Minor

- **Insufficient mechanistic analysis of the reweighting step.** The ensemble is reweighted by optimizing cross-entropy on synthetic data that were generated (in part) to be correctly classified by the ensemble. The paper does not provide diagnostic analysis showing *why* the learned weights generalize to real data — e.g., whether the weights correlate with ground-truth per-class client expertise, whether they are stable across random seeds, or whether they differ meaningfully from uniform or data-amount-based weights beyond aggregate accuracy. The paper's own Limitation section (line 331) acknowledges this disparity versus an ensemble trained on real data. The strong empirical results make this a concern about depth of understanding rather than validity, but the mechanism remains somewhat black-box.

- **Computational cost not reported.** Algorithm 1 involves per-epoch generator training (T_G iterations with adversarial loss requiring forward passes through both ensemble and server), on-the-fly perturbations, gradient-based weight updates, and server distillation. The paper claims practicality but reports no wall-clock time or relative cost versus baselines (e.g., DENSE). For one-shot FL methods, server-side compute is a practical concern, and the trade-off should be quantified.

- **Ambiguity about the number of weight update steps.** Algorithm 1 (line 151) states "Update the mixing weights with $\mathbb{D}_S$ according to Eq.(11)" without specifying whether this is a single sign-gradient step or repeated iterations. The text (Section 3.3) says "every time after getting each batch" but the pseudocode shows one update after the full $\mathbb{D}_S$ is constructed. Clarifying this is needed for reproducibility.

- **The adversarial loss uses a one-epoch-old server model without discussing stability.** The generator's adversarial loss $\mathcal{L}_A$ (Eq. 6) maximizes KL divergence between the current ensemble and the server model from the *previous* epoch. The paper does not discuss whether this lag introduces instability, or whether using the concurrently updated server would be better.

- **Motivation for the diverse hard-sample perturbation is indirect.** The on-the-fly perturbation (Eq. 9) perturbs each sample along a direction that maximizes dot product with a *random* vector $\bm{u}$ in logit space, not toward regions of actual ensemble-server disagreement. The paper claims this makes samples "hard for the server to fit," but the connection is indirect. The empirical success compensates, but the motivation would benefit from clearer justification or analysis of diversity (e.g., inter-sample cosine similarity).

### Trivial

- **Convergence not characterized.** The paper does not show how test accuracy evolves across outer epochs $T$ or whether performance saturates. A simple convergence plot would help establish robustness.

- **Clarify whether $\mathbf{x}_s$ or $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_s$ is used in the reweighting loss.** Algorithm 1 line 151 uses $\mathbb{D}_S$ for weight updates after the diversity step (line 149). The paper should state explicitly whether the diversified $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_s$ samples are used for reweighting.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Provide diagnostic analysis of the learned weights: correlation with oracle weights on real validation data, stability across seeds, or per-class specialization.
- Report wall-clock time or relative compute cost versus DENSE and FedDF.
- Study sensitivity of ensemble accuracy to the number of reweighting steps per epoch.
- Compare a version of FedDF that uses synthetic (rather than real validation) data to more fairly isolate Co-Boosting's advantage.

## Suggestions

1. Add a diagnostic analysis (e.g., scatter plot of learned weights vs. per-client real-data accuracy) showing that the synthetic-data-based reweighting captures meaningful differences in client expertise, to address the mechanism concern.
2. Report wall-clock time or relative server-side training cost to support the practicality claim.

With minor revisions addressing the analysis gaps and reporting details, this would be a fully convincing paper.
