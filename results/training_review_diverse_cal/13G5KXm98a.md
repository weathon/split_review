## Summary

This paper proposes a Voronoi tessellation-based confidence decision boundary visualization for active learning. The key idea is to project data into 2D (via t-SNE), construct Voronoi cells from the projected points, and use a ridge confidence metric ($C_{\text{pred}}=1-\sum_k P(\hat{y}_i=k)P(\hat{y}_j=k)$) to color decision boundary segments by uncertainty. The method is demonstrated on MNIST (CNN) and CIFAR-10 (ViT) to analyze eight AL query strategies, yielding qualitative observations about sampling behavior patterns.

## Strengths

- **Novel visualization approach combining Voronoi tessellation with ridge confidence.** Using Voronoi cells to partition the 2D feature space and assigning confidence values to boundary segments is a creative synthesis that goes beyond standard scatter plots or iso-contours (Section 2.2). The method explicitly addresses the sparsity problem near decision boundaries and provides a more informative view than uniform-boundary visualizations.

- **Ridge confidence metric shows alignment with ground-truth boundaries.** Figure 3 demonstrates that high-confidence predicted ridges (red) visually correspond to regions where the true class boundaries lie. Figure 4 provides quantitative evidence that ridge accuracy improves with tighter confidence intervals and over AL rounds, suggesting the metric captures meaningful structure. This validation grounds the metric in something observable (true labels) rather than being purely circular.

- **Reveals interesting qualitative distinctions among AL strategies.** The visualizations surface phenomena that accuracy curves alone do not explain: entropy sampling's "high-risk boundary-crossing" into minority-class outliers (Section 4.1), the shift in margin sampling from resolving easy uncertainty to targeting noisy regions as accuracy improves (Section 4.2), and KCenterGreedy's class imbalance due to model bias (Section 4.3). These observations, while qualitative, generate testable hypotheses and demonstrate the method's utility as an analytical tool.

- **Demonstration across two datasets and two architectures (CNN and ViT).** The method generalizes beyond a single setting, showing feasibility on both MNIST (simple, CNN) and CIFAR-10 (more complex, ViT). The ground-truth boundaries for the visualization model achieve high accuracy (0.9907 on MNIST, 0.9831 on CIFAR-10), providing a reliable reference coordinate system within the chosen framework.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper's core contribution — a Voronoi-based confidence boundary visualization — is novel and intact. The weaknesses below are significant but addressable through additional validation rather than requiring a fundamentally different approach.

### Major

- **The analysis uses a separately trained "visualization model" whose feature space may differ substantially from the AL models under study.** The paper trains a model on 100% of the pool data to optimal accuracy and uses its 2D features as the fixed coordinate system for all strategy comparisons (Section 3.3). The AL models themselves are trained on at most 1.225% of the pool and produce representations that can differ dramatically. The paper acknowledges this choice ("facilitating a consistent comparison") but provides no evidence that the visualization model's geometry is relevant for understanding the AL models' behavior. Figure 8d versus 8e hints at the problem: KCenterGreedy looks qualitatively different in the AL model's feature space. While the paper uses this contrast as a finding about model bias, the core analysis for all uncertainty-based strategies (Figures 5–7) relies exclusively on the visualization model's features, making it unclear whether the observed patterns reflect genuine AL dynamics or artifacts of the fixed projection. This is the most significant limitation of the work.

  *Verification*: Paper Section 3.3 states this choice explicitly. Figure 8 caption confirms that (d) uses visualization model features while (e) uses AL model features. The paper does not provide a similar comparison for the uncertainty-based methods whose analysis forms the bulk of the contribution.

- **The ridge confidence metric assumes Voronoi cell prediction homogeneity without verification, and quantitative t-SNE evaluations are referenced but never reported.** The paper asserts that "all points in $V(p_i)$ can be represented by point $p_i$" (Section 2.2) to justify using only the two cell-center predictions for the confidence metric. This property holds geometrically (nearest-neighbor distance) but does not guarantee that the model's predictions are homogeneous within a cell — especially for large cells in sparse regions. The paper mentions conducting "Correlation Test" and "Local Structure Preservation" evaluations on the t-SNE projection (Section 3.3) but does not report any results, so the reader cannot assess whether the neighborhood structure is preserved well enough to trust the Voronoi cells. Without this evidence, the reliability of the confidence values in the visualizations is unknown.

  *Verification*: Line 52 in Section 2.2 makes the representativeness claim. Line 94 mentions the t-SNE evaluations but provides no results, no table, no numbers — just the statement that they were conducted.

### Minor

- **Key insights about AL strategies are qualitative visual observations without systematic quantification.** Claims such as "entropy sampling frequently engages in high-risk boundary-crossing" (Section 4.1), "BALD dropout distributes sampling more broadly" (Section 4.1), and the two uncertainty trends (insufficient data vs. noisy regions) are derived from visual inspection of a few figures. The paper does not report, e.g., distances from sampled points to ground-truth boundaries, counts of boundary-crossing events, or statistical comparisons across multiple runs. Given the t-SNE stochasticity and the separate visualization model concern, the reader cannot easily distinguish systematic patterns from idiosyncratic artifacts of specific random seeds or projection parameters.

- **The ridge accuracy metric $A_{\text{ridge}}$ is underspecified.** The metric $A_{\text{ridge}} = |\mathcal{B}_{\text{pred}} \cap \mathcal{B}_{\text{gt}}| / |\mathcal{B}_{\text{pred}}|$ (Section 3.3) involves the intersection of two sets of line segments (ridges) in continuous 2D space. The paper never defines the criterion for when a predicted ridge "intersects" a ground-truth ridge — no tolerance, no discretization scheme, no approximation. This makes the quantitative validation in Figure 4 difficult to interpret or reproduce.

  *Verification*: The formula appears at line 102. No operational definition of the intersection is provided anywhere in the paper.

- **t-SNE stochasticity and parameter sensitivity are not discussed.** The paper relies entirely on t-SNE for dimensionality reduction without addressing how its stochastic nature, perplexity choice, or random seed affect the resulting Voronoi cells, ridge configurations, or the qualitative findings. A single t-SNE run may not be representative.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface a tension that the paper inadvertently highlights but does not resolve: AL visualization faces a fundamental trade-off between stability and fidelity. Using a fixed well-trained model's features provides a stable canvas for cross-strategy comparison (the paper's choice), but it decouples the visualization from the actual model being studied. Using the AL model's own features maintains fidelity but introduces instability (features change every round, making comparison across strategies difficult). The paper chooses the former without adequately acknowledging the trade-off, and this choice drives most of its limitations. A potential resolution — not explored in the paper — would be to use the AL model's features but align them across rounds using a common reference (e.g., Procrustes alignment), combining stability with fidelity.

## Suggestions

1. **Validate the visualization model's relevance explicitly.** Run the main analysis (Figures 5–7) using both the visualization model's features and the AL model's own features for at least one representative strategy per category. Show that the qualitative patterns (boundary-crossing, banded-region sampling, class imbalance) are consistent across both representations, or honestly caveat which observations depend on the fixed coordinate system.

2. **Report the t-SNE quality metrics** (Correlation Test, Local Structure Preservation) that are already referenced. A simple table showing these numbers for both datasets would go a long way toward establishing trust in the Voronoi cells.

3. **Validate the cell homogeneity assumption.** For each Voronoi cell in the 2D projection, compute the fraction of points that share the center point's predicted label. Report the distribution of this fraction across all cells. If it is consistently high, the assumption holds; if not, the ridge confidence metric needs revisiting.

4. **Define the ridge intersection criterion** used in $A_{\text{ridge}}$ — even a brief description of the discretization or tolerance suffices.

5. **Add a stability analysis.** Run the t-SNE with at least 3 random seeds and check whether the qualitative findings (e.g., "entropy sampling shows boundary-crossing") are reproducible across seeds.

6. **Convert at least one key claim into a quantitative number.** For example, measure the distance from each queried point to the nearest ground-truth ridge for entropy vs. margin sampling, and report the means and variances. This would significantly strengthen the paper without sacrificing its visualization-first identity.

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a genuinely novel visualization method that has the potential to be a useful tool for understanding active learning strategies. The ridge confidence metric is well-motivated and shows promising alignment with ground truth. However, the reliability of the analysis is undermined by the disconnect between the visualization model's feature space and the AL models being studied, and by the unverified assumption about Voronoi cell prediction homogeneity. The paper mentions but does not report crucial validation metrics for the t-SNE projection. These are not fatal flaws — they are addressable through additional experiments and analysis — but in their current form they prevent the paper from fully supporting its claims about AL strategy behavior. I recommend rejection with the expectation that the authors can address these issues in a substantially revised version.
