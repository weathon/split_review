## Summary

The paper proposes SemiCLIP, a semi-supervised method to adapt CLIP to target domains using limited image-text pairs plus many unlabeled images. It has two key innovations: (1) a semantic concept mining module that extracts nouns from labeled captions and trains a classifier to predict concepts for unlabeled images, and (2) a trapezoidal consistency loss that enforces geometric constraints between labeled and unlabeled pairs without directly aligning noisy surrogate captions to unlabeled images. The method achieves consistent and substantial improvements over S-CLIP and other semi-supervised baselines across remote sensing, fashion, scientific figures, and comic domains.

## Strengths

1. **Novel semantic concept mining that goes beyond mini-batch nearest-neighbor.** Unlike S-CLIP (which reconstructs captions from labeled data within the same mini-batch), SemiCLIP extracts and maintains a global concept vocabulary from all labeled captions, trains a classifier to predict concept presence, and uses it to generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled images. This enables exploitation of all labeled data, not just batch-local information, and the gains over S-CLIP (e.g., +3.75% average zero-shot improvement, +12.15% on fashion sub-class classification in Table 3) confirm the benefit.

2. **Trapezoidal consistency elegantly avoids direct alignment of noisy surrogate captions.** The key insight is that surrogate captions for unlabeled images are inherently noisy, so rather than forcing image↔surrogate-caption similarity (which causes a 4.07% drop when done directly, per Figure 2c), the trapezoid constraints enforce equal diagonals and legs while leaving the lower base (unlabeled image ↔ surrogate caption distance) free. The ablation in Table 6 confirms that removing CTC drops zero-shot accuracy by 5.25% and retrieval by 1.33%.

3. **Consistent state-of-the-art results across four diverse domains.** SemiCLIP outperforms Hard-PL, Soft-PL, and S-CLIP on every benchmark evaluated (zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval on remote sensing, fashion, SciCap, and Simpsons datasets). Gains are particularly notable on challenging settings: +12.15% on fashion sub-class classification (Table 3), +6.35% average R@5 on fashion retrieval (Table 4), and +2.94% on the L≠U domain-shift setting (Table 1).

4. **Robustness to domain shift (L≠U setting).** When unlabeled images come from a different distribution than labeled data (e.g., RESISC45 as unlabeled for remote sensing), SemiCLIP achieves larger relative gains (+2.94% vs. +1.72% for L=U), suggesting the concept mining and trapezoidal consistency generalize beyond in-distribution unlabeled data.

5. **Data augmentation design is well-motivated for concept-level consistency.** The paper correctly identifies that strong augmentation (RandomAugment) is suitable for concept-level consistency because concepts (nouns denoting entities) are robust to color and position changes — whereas the same augmentation would hurt vision-language alignment. The ablation confirms a 3.1% zero-shot drop when strong augmentation is replaced with weak augmentation.

### Minor

1. **The trapezoidal loss (Eq. 5) is applied to all pair combinations in D̃ = D^l ∪ D^P without explicitly justifying the labeled–labeled case.** The geometric narrative in Figure 1b depicts one labeled pair and one unlabeled pair forming the trapezoid. However, the actual loss double-sums over all i,j in D̃, which includes labeled–labeled and unlabeled–unlabeled pairs. The leg constraint for two labeled pairs forces ⟨I_i^e, I_j^e⟩ ≈ ⟨T_i^e, T_j^e⟩ — requiring image-image similarity to equal text-text similarity. This is not an unreasonable structural regularizer (it encourages cross-modal structure preservation, a standard goal in multi-modal learning), and the paper briefly justifies it ("ensures that the similarity between images and the similarity between texts are treated equally in the feature space," lines 114–115). The ablation shows removing the leg constraint causes only a 0.92% drop. Still, the paper would benefit from explicitly addressing why this constraint is sensible for labeled–labeled pairs and acknowledging that the geometric trapezoid analogy strictly applies only to cross-pairs.

2. **The pseudo-label update schedule during fine-tuning is underspecified.** Pseudo-labels ŷ_{I_j} are assigned to unlabeled images "at the end of the [pre-]training" (line 71) using ψ. During semi-supervised fine-tuning, L_SCM^u (Eq. 3) minimizes the divergence between ψ's predictions on strongly augmented images and these fixed pseudo-labels. The paper does not state whether ψ's predictions on unlabeled data are ever recomputed/refreshed during fine-tuning, nor whether the pseudo-labels themselves are updated. While many SSL methods use fixed pseudo-labels from a pre-trained classifier, the paper's mention of "confidence filtering" (line 91) referencing FixMatch — which uses online pseudo-labeling — creates ambiguity. This should be clarified.

3. **The selection schedule for the top-P% subset D^P is not specified.** The set D^P is defined as images whose embedding has top-P% cosine similarity to their surrogate caption (line 108). Since both the visual encoder and learnable prompts change during training, the membership of D^P is a moving target. The paper does not state whether D^P is recomputed each epoch, each iteration, or fixed from an initial forward pass. Either choice is defensible but should be stated, as the update frequency could affect training dynamics.

4. **Ablation results are reported only as averages across datasets (Table 6).** While this is a reasonable space-saving choice, a brief note or supplementary table showing per-dataset results would help confirm that the ablation patterns (e.g., leg constraint removal → 0.92% drop) are consistent rather than driven by a single dataset.
