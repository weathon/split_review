## Summary

This paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL rewards with a KL divergence penalty between predicted outcomes p(o|s,a) and preferred outcomes q(o|s), weighted by a parameter β. The paper's central claimed novelty is a Bayesian scheme that adapts β online, eliminating manual tuning. Experiments on a 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8 show that positive β values improve performance over β=0.

## Strengths

- **KL-based incentive shaping is a well-motivated idea with clear positive results on MiniGrid**: The paper demonstrates that adding even a small KL penalty (β=0.01) improves success rate from 90.5% to 98% on MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8 and reduces mean episode length from 75 to 29 steps (Figure 2). This is a non-trivial improvement in a challenging sparse-reward environment, averaged over 3 seeds.

- **Clean differentiation from related approaches**: The paper clearly contrasts its KL-over-outcome-predictions formulation with policy-regularization methods (TRPO/SAC) and with the Free Energy Principle's belief-revision approach, helping position the contribution.

- **The code is provided**, which is helpful for reproducibility.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **The paper's central novelty — Bayesian β adaptation — is completely undescribed.** The paper repeatedly claims "Bayesian adaptation (novelty)" as its headline contribution and states that treating β as a Bayesian random variable eliminates manual tuning. Yet the main text never specifies: (a) the prior on β, (b) the likelihood relating β to observed data, (c) how the posterior is updated online across rounds. The algorithm in Section 5.1 lists five steps — Predict Outcomes, Specify Preferences, Compute Incentive, Shape Reward, Update Policy — without any mention of β updating. The only description of the scheme is the phrase "we tracked the posterior evolution of β using our Bayesian adaptation scheme" (Section 5.4), which describes nothing. A claimed central contribution cannot be evaluated when its mechanism is absent from the paper. This alone is fatal for publication in its current form.

### Major

- **No comparison to existing intrinsic motivation methods.** The experimental evaluation compares only different values of β within the same method (β=0, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0 and β=0, 0.01). There is no comparison to curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al.), RND (Burda et al.), ICM, count-based exploration, or any other standard intrinsic motivation baseline. The paper's stated claim — that IncentRL "improves sample efficiency and final performance over standard RL and fixed-regularization baselines" — is tautological when the "fixed-regularization baselines" are alternative β settings of the same method. Without comparisons to actual prior methods, the paper cannot substantiate that its approach offers anything beyond what existing exploration methods already provide.

- **Factual reporting error in the primary evidence for Bayesian adaptation.** The paper repeatedly states (in captions, main text, and the strength finder's summary) that "the posterior mean quickly concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)." However, Table 3 reports a Round-4 posterior mean of β = 0.0173, and Figure 3's own description states the posterior drops from ~0.11 in Round 1 to ~0.02 by Round 4. The posterior does NOT concentrate near 0.1; it converges to approximately 0.02. This is a clear factual error in how the paper reports its own results. (Note: the best fixed β on MiniGrid was 0.01, so β≈0.02 is actually close to the correct value — the method finds a reasonable β, but the paper's numerical claim about what value it finds is wrong.)

- **Critical implementation details for p(o|s,a) are absent.** For the non-toy experiments (MountainCar with continuous state space and MiniGrid), the paper never specifies how p(o|s,a) is obtained. The text says it "may be obtained from a forward model or from environment dynamics" (Section 3.1). For MountainCar, the paper states q(o|s) "assigns all probability to the goal (x ≥ 0.5)" but does not explain what p(o|s,a) is — a learned neural network? A fixed model? How is it trained, and on what data? Without this, the experiments cannot be reproduced and readers cannot assess whether the results are driven by the claimed mechanism or by ad hoc implementation choices.

- **Limited experimental design.** (a) MountainCar uses only 3 seeds with very high variance (e.g., β=0.3: mean 1654.67 ± 908.94). (b) MiniGrid's main experiment compares only β=0 vs β=0.01 — a single binary comparison. (c) No statistical significance tests are reported. (d) The Bayesian adaptation is tested exclusively on MiniGrid, with the claim that "in additional runs (not shown)" it matches the best fixed β — results that cannot be verified. (e) The 2-state MDP in Experiment 1 uses only fixed β values, not the Bayesian adaptation; it merely confirms that a positive β helps in a trivially simple environment.

### Minor

- **The theoretical analysis (Propositions 1 and 2) is standard and adds no practical insight.** Proposition 1 (for small β, the optimal policy is preserved) is a basic continuity argument for finite MDPs — essentially a bounded perturbation result. Proposition 2 (as β→∞, extrinsic rewards are ignored) is definitional. Neither proposition provides guidance on choosing or adapting β, nor do they connect to the Bayesian adaptation claim.

- **No analysis of how the KL term evolves over training.** Does the KL divergence decrease as the agent learns? Does it provide a denser gradient signal than extrinsic reward? Such analysis would clarify the mechanism but is absent.

### Trivial

- None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Expanding the experimental evaluation to include comparisons with curiosity-driven methods (ICM, RND) and possibly other KL-regularized RL methods would be needed to substantiate the paper's claims.
- Testing the Bayesian β adaptation on additional environments beyond MiniGrid.
- Providing learning curves alongside the final aggregate statistics.

## Removed Points

- **"The posterior concentration contradiction is a decisive evidential failure"** (harsh critic). Removed as overstatement. The posterior mean (0.0173) actually converges close to the best tested β on MiniGrid (0.01). The method's output is reasonable; only the paper's textual description of the value (claiming 0.1) is wrong. This is a significant reporting error but not a fatal invalidation of the method.

- **Criticism that the paper lacks confidence intervals** (softened, not removed). While the MountainCar results would benefit from them, single-run evaluation with 3 seeds is standard practice in this subfield for computational reasons, so this is weakened to a minor concern embedded in the "Limited experimental design" point.

- **Criticism about the lack of ablation for KL term** (merged into "No analysis of how the KL term evolves over training" above).

- **Strength about "posterior concentration near β≈0.1"** (Strength Finder). Removed because it is factually contradicted by the paper's own data (Table 3: Round 4 mean = 0.0173). The strength would be valid if it said the posterior converges near the best tested β, but it says 0.1, which is wrong.

- **Strength about "theoretical characterization"** — downgraded to minor. The propositions are correct but standard; they do not rise to the level of a major strength.

## Suggestions

1. **Fully specify the Bayesian adaptation mechanism** — prior, likelihood, posterior update procedure — ideally with an algorithmic description. Without this, the paper's central novelty is unevaluable.

2. **Correct the numerical reporting error**: the posterior mean converges to β≈0.02, not β≈0.1, on MiniGrid. The text, captions, and figures must be consistent.

3. **Add comparisons to at least one existing intrinsic motivation method** (e.g., ICM, RND) on the same environments to demonstrate that IncentRL offers meaningful advantages over prior approaches.

4. **Specify how p(o|s,a) is obtained** in the MountainCar and MiniGrid experiments (e.g., is it a learned neural network? How is it trained?).

5. **Increase the number of seeds** and report confidence intervals or statistical significance tests where appropriate.
