## Summary

This paper revisits the trade-off between watermark strength and speculative sampling efficiency in LLMs. It introduces a quantitative watermark-strength measure (expected KL divergence between watermarked and original distributions), uses this measure to characterize the trade-off as a Pareto frontier, and proposes Algorithm 1 — which makes acceptance decisions pseudorandom rather than truly random — to simultaneously achieve maximal watermark strength and maximal sampling efficiency. Theorem 4.1 proves this breaks a prior impossibility result, and experiments show detectability improves while efficiency is maintained.

## Strengths

- **Quantitative watermark-strength measure (Definition 3.1, Equation 7).** Replacing the prior binary definition with expected KL divergence between the watermarked and original distributions is well-motivated and enables continuous trade-off analysis. Theorem 3.1 connects this measure to the exponential decay rate of the \(p\)-value under the likelihood ratio test, providing a direct sample-complexity interpretation. This is a genuine conceptual advance over Hu & Huang (2024).

- **Full characterization of the trade-off as a constrained optimization (Definition 3.2, Equation 10).** Casting the trade-off curve as a Pareto frontier and solving it explicitly for linearly interpolated decoder classes is clean and general. Figure 1 visualizes the curves for multiple watermark classes, showing that Google's class outperforms Hu's at matched efficiency and that neither reaches the theoretical optimum — empirically demonstrating that the trade-off is nuanced, not binary.

- **Algorithm 1 with pseudorandom acceptance and Theorem 4.1.** The key insight — that the inherent randomness in standard acceptance decisions prevents the final output from being a deterministic function of pseudorandom variables, thus limiting watermark strength — is compelling. Theorem 4.1 proves that Algorithm 1 simultaneously achieves unbiasedness (a), maximum sampling efficiency \(1-\text{TV}(Q,P)\) (b), and maximum watermark strength \(\text{Ent}(P)\) (c). This directly overturns the impossibility result of Hu & Huang (2024) within this watermark class.

- **Experimental demonstration that efficiency is maintained and detectability improves.** Figure 2 (left panel) confirms that Average Accepted Tokens Per Step matches the standard speculative-sampling baseline. The middle and right panels show that Ars-\(\tau\) and Bayes-MLP (which exploit the pseudorandom acceptance variable) achieve higher TPR at FPR=1% with fewer tokens than prior methods, approaching oracle performance at 200 tokens. The appendix also reports Per Token Time and Log Perplexity, confirming practical acceleration and unbiasedness.

- **Theorem 3.2 and 3.3 (maximum-strength characterization).** Theorem 3.2 proves that max WS equals \(\text{Ent}(P)\) and is attained iff the watermarked distribution is degenerate. Theorem 3.3 verifies that both Gumbel-max and SynthID (as \(m\to\infty\)) achieve this bound, unifying two major schemes under the same measure.

### Fatal

### Minor

- **Experiments measure detectability, not watermark strength directly.** The paper defines watermark strength as expected KL divergence (WS), and Theorem 4.1 proves Algorithm 1 achieves \(\text{WS}=\text{Ent}(P)\) theoretically. However, the experiments measure TPR (detectability) rather than directly estimating KL divergence between the output token distribution and the original. While the paper acknowledges this distinction (Remark 3.1) and argues that measuring WS directly would require knowing true token distributions, the empirical section would be strengthened by estimating WS on synthetic controlled settings where \(P\) is known, or by comparing texts generated by Algorithm 1 vs. standard watermarked speculative sampling on a common trade-off plot (efficiency vs. strength). As presented, the experiments primarily show that *using the \(\zeta^R\) variable helps detection* — a valuable but narrower claim than "the trade-off is broken at the generative level."

- **Limited model/dataset scope in main text.** Only the Llama 68M/7B pair and ELI5 dataset are presented in the main paper. C4 results and Gemma results are relegated to the appendix. Testing at higher temperatures (beyond 0.5/0.7) and on additional domains would strengthen generalizability claims.

- **Empirical comparison focuses on detector improvement, not generative trade-off comparison.** The evaluation compares Ars-\(\tau\)/Bayes-MLP (using \(\zeta^R\)) against Ars-Prior/Bayes-Prior (not using \(\zeta^R\)) *on the same generated text*. This isolates the detection engineering improvement but does not directly compare text from Algorithm 1 against text from standard watermarked speculative sampling using a fixed optimal detector — which would more directly demonstrate that the generative process itself yields a better trade-off. The theory (Theorem 4.1) already takes this further step, but the experiments could be more tightly aligned with the headline claim.

### Trivial

- The pseudorandom generator \(G\) in Algorithm 1 is not specified (e.g., which PRNG algorithm). Mentioning the specific mechanism would aid reproducibility.
- Calibration of \(\tau\) in Ars-\(\tau\) (threshold on held-out validation set) is described but sensitivity of detection performance to \(\tau\) choice is not reported.
