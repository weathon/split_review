## Summary

This paper systematically explores semi-supervised strategies for generating instruction-tuning data without using closed-source models. It compares Self-Training (ST — generating responses for existing instructions) and Instruction-Generation (IG — generating instructions for existing documents), finding IG significantly outperforms ST. The paper proposes two enhancements: an instruction filtering strategy (selecting instructions that minimize response perplexity) and an extract-then-generate strategy to improve diversity. Experiments on LongForm and Alpaca Eval show the method's data can outperform Alpaca (which relies on closed-source models) and that weak models can self-align comparably to distilling from stronger models.

## Strengths

- **Systematic comparison reveals IG significantly outperforms ST under a controlled setup**: Figure 1 shows IG achieves consistently higher METEOR scores across different seed data sources (Lima, Dolly). This is a clean finding with practical implications for practitioners who want to generate instruction data without closed-source models.

- **Instruction filtering via perplexity minimization is a conceptually clean and effective technique**: The paper proposes selecting the instruction that minimizes the perplexity of the response from among multiple candidates (Section 4.1). The ablation (Figure 4a) confirms this brings clear improvement over no filtering. The idea of using PPL to score instruction-response alignment is intuitive and well-motivated.

- **Extract-then-generate strategy improves diversity and performance**: Three extraction methods are explored (Keywords, Random Sentence, LLM Extraction). LLM Extraction combined with the original document achieves the best performance (Figure 4b), and the diversity analysis (Figure 5) visually confirms increased variety in root verbs and noun objects.

- **Weak models achieve self-alignment on par with distillation from stronger models**: Experiments on Baichuan2-7B and BLOOMZ-7B1 (Figure 6) show that when these models generate instruction data for themselves, their performance is comparable to using data generated by the more powerful LLaMA-7B. This is a non-obvious and practically important finding.

- **Robustness to seed instruction data**: Figure 1 shows varying seed data sources (Lima vs. Dolly) and random subsampling have minimal impact on IG's performance, indicating the method does not depend on a specific high-quality seed set.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Potential data leakage between training responses (minipile) and LongForm evaluation benchmark**: The paper uses minipile (a subset of The Pile) as the response corpus for instruction generation. LongForm's evaluation set includes C4, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange, and WikiHow subtasks — all of which overlap with document types found in The Pile (Pile-CC, Wikipedia, StackExchange subsets). The paper provides no decontamination analysis or deduplication against the evaluation set. If the training responses overlap with evaluation examples, METEOR scores could be inflated, particularly for the "outperforms all baselines" claim in Table 1. This is the most significant threat to the paper's empirical conclusions, though it does not undermine the core methodological contributions (IG vs. ST comparison, ablation studies, self-alignment finding).

- **No statistical significance or variance reporting**: All results are reported as single runs with no standard deviations or confidence intervals. METEOR differences of ~0.5–1 point between conditions could easily be within the noise range. Replication with multiple seeds is needed to establish the reliability of the claimed improvements.

### Minor

- **Preliminary study comparing ST vs. IG uses different data sources (ShareGPT for ST, minipile for IG)**: The ST strategy uses ShareGPT user inputs as unsupervised instructions, while IG uses minipile documents as candidate responses. This introduces a confound — performance differences could be partly driven by data source quality rather than strategy difficulty. A controlled comparison using the same source corpus for both strategies would strengthen the conclusion.

- **Instruction filtering model not specified**: Section 4.1 states "use the instruction-following model to calculate the perplexity" but does not specify whether this is the seed instruction-following model from the preliminary study, the base LLaMA-7B model, or some other model. While the critic's claim of "circular reasoning" is unfounded (filtering happens before training the final model, Step 3 before Step 4), the ambiguity harms reproducibility. The paper should explicitly state which model computes the PPL.

- **"No filtering" ablation baseline is unclearly defined**: The ablation in Figure 4a compares filtering vs. no filtering, but doesn't specify whether "no filtering" means randomly selecting one instruction from the candidates or simply using one generated instruction without sampling. This makes the ablation difficult to interpret precisely.

- **Claim that "generating instructions is easier than generating responses" lacks direct evidence**: The paper hypothesizes this based on the IG > ST performance gap, but provides no direct analysis (e.g., human evaluation of generation quality, BLEU/ROUGE between generated and reference instructions/responses) to support the claim.

- **Diversity analysis is purely illustrative**: The root verb/noun object diagrams (Figure 5) show different distributions but don't quantify diversity (e.g., distinct n-gram coverage, type-token ratio) or formally link diversity gains to downstream performance improvements.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the review process surfaces a tension between the paper's two main claims. Claim A ("IG outperforms ST because response validity is ensured") relies on comparing different strategies under the same evaluation, while Claim B ("SASS outperforms all existing baselines including those using closed-source models") relies on absolute benchmark comparisons. These have different evidential requirements: Claim A is robust because it's an internal comparison, while Claim B is more vulnerable to data leakage and requires stronger controls. The paper would benefit from explicitly decoupling these claims and acknowledging that the core methodological contribution (instruction filtering + extract-then-generate) does not depend on the absolute superiority over closed-source-based methods.
