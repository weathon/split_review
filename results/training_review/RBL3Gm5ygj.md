## Summary

This paper extends SAM (Segment Anything Model) to handle multi-modal sensor data (e.g., RGB+Thermal, RGB+Depth, RGB+LiDAR) via two lightweight, mask-free training modules: Unsupervised Cross-Modal Transfer (UCMT), which aligns non-RGB embeddings to SAM's RGB embedding space using an L2 loss, and Weakly-supervised Multi-Modal Fusion (WMMF), which fuses modality embeddings through a learned Selective Fusion Gate (SFG). The approach is evaluated across seven datasets and eight sensor modalities, consistently outperforming SAM applied to false-color representations of non-RGB data.

## Strengths

- **Consistent and large gains across diverse sensor modalities.** MM-SAM improves over SAM (on false-color baselines) by 4–14+ mIoU points across thermal, depth, LiDAR, HSI, SAR, and DSM modalities (e.g., Thermal mIoU 72.3 vs. SAM Thermal* 64.5 on MFNet; SAR 67.5 vs. SAM SAR* 53.0 on DFC2023; Table 2, 3). Fusion further outperforms both single-modality variants, supporting the claim that the overall approach is effective.

- **Parameter- and label-efficient adaptation.** UCMT adds only 344.8K–9.7M parameters per modality and WMMF adds 148.1K, both tiny fractions of SAM's 91M parameters (Table 1). Training requires no mask annotations — UCMT uses unsupervised embedding alignment and WMMF uses pseudo-labels from geometric prompts.

- **Zero-shot generalization to unseen domains.** Models trained on one dataset transfer effectively to others (MFNet→FreiburgThermal: 70.8 RGB+Thermal vs. SAM RGB 65.3; SUN RGB-D→NYU: 81.4 vs. 79.5; Table 4), providing initial evidence of domain robustness.

- **Scalability beyond two modalities.** On DFC2018 (RGB+HSI+MS-LiDAR), MM-SAM achieves 89.3 IoU, surpassing all pair-wise fusions (86.5–88.5) and single modalities. It also demonstrates fusion of two non-RGB modalities without RGB input, showing the framework's generality.

- **Adaptive fusion is qualitatively interpretable.** Visual analysis (Figure 7) shows the SFG dynamically assigns higher weight to the thermal modality in regions where RGB is degraded by headlight glare, providing direct evidence that the weighting mechanism responds to modality-specific strengths under challenging conditions.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No ablation of the fusion architecture against simple alternatives.** The Selective Fusion Gate (SFG) is compared only against single-modality baselines and a no-fusion baseline. There is no comparison against trivial fusion strategies such as averaging the two modality embeddings, concatenating them with a projection layer, or the patch-wise max-confidence selection rule used for pseudo-label generation. Without these baselines, the paper's claim that SFG specifically enables "effective sensor fusion under complicated and dynamic situations" is unsubstantiated — the experimental results show that *some form* of fusion helps, but not that the proposed *design* is responsible for the gains. This is the most significant gap in the current submission.

- **No comparison against existing multi-modal segmentation methods.** The paper benchmarks MM-SAM only against SAM variants (RGB, false-color X*). There is no comparison with any published task-specific multi-modal segmentation method (e.g., FuseNet for depth, MFNet's own baseline for RGB-Thermal) to contextualize whether adapting SAM offers accuracy benefits over dedicated architectures. While the paper's contribution is about extending a foundation model, such comparisons would substantially strengthen the empirical case.

- **UCMT training objective is not ablated.** The L2 embedding-alignment loss is used throughout, but never compared against alternatives such as a contrastive loss (which would pull paired embeddings together without forcing exact alignment) or training the X encoder using only the WMMF pseudo-label loss. Consequently, it is unclear whether the gains from UCMT come from the specific alignment loss, the LoRA adaptation, or the combination. The ablation study (Figure 5) varies only the PEFT method (LoRA vs. AdapterFormer vs. VPT), holding the objective fixed.

### Minor

- **The pseudo-labeling confidence measure is unspecified.** The paper states that pseudo-labels are generated by "selecting the most confident predictions from corresponding patches of the paired modalities" (Section 3.2) but never specifies how confidence is measured (max softmax probability? predicted IoU? something else?). This is a reproducibility gap.

- **MFNet results use an unexplained three-number format.** Results such as "68.2/72.6/65.1" (Table 2) and "65.3/71.8/60.7" (Table 4) are not defined in the table captions or main text. These appear to be overall/day/night splits (MFNet has both day and night scenes), but this should be stated explicitly.

- **No variance or error bars.** Given the small test sets (e.g., MFNet: 393 images, FreiburgThermal: 64 images), single-run results without variance estimates make it difficult to assess the reliability of reported improvements.

- **The patch embedding adaptation for differing channel counts is underspecified.** The paper mentions "adjusting input channel numbers to match X" but does not describe the architectural change (e.g., whether a 3×3 conv is simply replaced with an N-channel conv, or whether additional processing is needed for high-channel modalities like HSI with 48 channels).

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The core insight — that aligning non-RGB embeddings to SAM's RGB latent space via a simple L2 loss enables effective cross-modal segmentation, and that a lightweight learned fusion gate operating in this shared embedding space can adaptively combine modalities — is well-motivated and demonstrated. The finding that this shared space also supports zero-shot transfer and fusion of non-RGB modalities without RGB input (HSI+MS-LiDAR) is a useful secondary insight about the generality of the approach.

## Suggestions

1. **Add fusion baselines.** Compare SFG against at least three alternatives on 2–3 datasets: (a) averaging modality embeddings, (b) concatenating with a learned projection to the original dimension, and (c) the patch-wise max-confidence rule used for pseudo-label generation (to isolate the fusion *architecture* from the *training signal*). This directly addresses the most critical weakness.

2. **Ablate the UCMT loss.** Compare the current L2 alignment against a contrastive loss (pull paired embeddings together, push unpaired apart) and against training the X encoder using only the WMMF pseudo-label loss without any embedding alignment.

3. **Add at least one external baseline.** Report performance of a published multi-modal segmentation method (e.g., the MFNet baseline on MFNet, FuseNet on SUN RGB-D) to contextualize the results.

4. **Define the MFNet three-number format** in the table caption and specify the confidence metric used in pseudo-labeling.

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a timely and well-motivated problem with a clean, parameter-efficient solution. The core contributions — that SAM can be extended to diverse non-RGB modalities via embedding-space alignment, and that fusion in this shared space is effective — are supported by extensive experiments across seven datasets and eight modalities. However, the paper's two central methodological claims (that the *specific* SFG design and the *specific* UCMT objective are responsible for the observed gains) are not validated against reasonable alternatives. These gaps are significant but addressable with additional experiments; they do not invalidate the overall empirical finding that the MM-SAM pipeline works. I recommend conditional acceptance with the expectation that the authors address the missing ablations in a revision or rebuttal.
