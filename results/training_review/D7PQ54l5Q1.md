## Summary

This paper proposes Diffusion Posterior MCMC (DPMC), an algorithm that combines annealed MCMC with diffusion models to solve inverse problems. The core idea is to define a sequence of intermediate distributions using DPS's posterior approximation and run multiple MCMC sampling steps at each noise level, reducing the accumulated error from DPS's inaccurate posterior approximation at high noise levels. Experiments on FFHQ and ImageNet across five inverse problems show DPMC consistently outperforms DPS with fewer function evaluations and is competitive with or superior to several other baselines.

## Strengths

- **Consistent and substantial improvement over DPS across multiple tasks with fewer NFE**: On nearly every linear inverse problem, DPMC improves both LPIPS and FID over DPS while using only ~700 NFE versus DPS's 1000 NFE (e.g., FFHQ Gaussian deblur: LPIPS 0.206 vs 0.265, FID 12.88 vs 23.88; ImageNet super-resolution: LPIPS 0.299 vs 0.365, FID 13.44 vs 23.37). Improvement also holds on nonlinear phase retrieval.

- **Clear, well-motivated problem framing**: Section 3 clearly explains why DPS's posterior approximation fails at high noise levels (the Tweedie estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ can be far from $\mathbf{x}_0$ when $p(\mathbf{x}_0|\mathbf{x}_t)$ is multi-modal), and motivates why multiple MCMC steps per noise level should help by letting samples better explore each intermediate distribution before moving to the next.

- **Ablation studies on key hyperparameters**: Table 4a (varying K from 1 to 4) shows monotonic improvement, Table 4b (varying T) shows more intermediate distributions help, and Table 4c (varying $\xi_t$ schedule) identifies $\bar{\alpha}_t^3$ as the best weighting — providing practical guidance for future work.

- **Competitive with or superior to strong baselines**: DPMC achieves best or second-best results on most tasks compared to ΠGDM, DDNM, RED-Diff, DiffPir, FPS, and others, while being robust to the noisy measurements ($\sigma=0.05$) that cause artifacts in RED-Diff and ΠGDM.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

### Major

### Minor
- **The effect of MCMC is not fully isolated from compute redistribution.** DPMC uses 200 intermediate distributions with 4 MCMC steps on the middle 60% (~680 NFE total), while DPS uses 1000 steps. The improvement could partially come from allocating more compute to mid-range noise levels (where the approximation error is highest) rather than from the MCMC mechanism itself. While the K-ablation (Table 4a) shows that increasing K helps at fixed T=200, this doesn't fully disentangle the effect because K=1 with the MCMC setup is not the same as standard DPS. A simple control — DPS at the same 200-step noise-level schedule — would clarify whether the MCMC steps provide additional benefit beyond budget redistribution.

- **The 60% window for MCMC application is not ablated or justified.** The paper applies the "proposal-exploration step" to the middle 60% of noise levels, using DPS steps for the first 30% and last 10%. No sensitivity analysis is provided for this split, and no explanation is given for why 60% (rather than, e.g., 40%, 80%, or 100%) was chosen. The exception for ImageNet box-mask inpainting (where MCMC is applied until clean images) further suggests this heuristic is task-dependent.

- **Only one measurement noise level ($\sigma=0.05$) is tested.** All experiments use $\sigma=0.05$. Testing at least one additional noise level would strengthen claims about robustness.
