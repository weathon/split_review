## Summary

This paper introduces CAPGen, a framework for generating adversarial patches that use environment-extracted base colors to achieve visual stealthiness while maintaining attack performance. The paper makes two main contributions: (1) a color probability matrix optimization that constrains patch colors to environment base colors while optimizing adversarial patterns, and (2) a decomposition study of adversarial patches into pattern vs. color components, leading to a fast generation strategy that recolors existing high-performance patches. The key claimed finding is that patterns dominate colors in determining attack effectiveness.

## Strengths

- **First systematic decomposition of adversarial patches into pattern vs. color components.** The paper designs experiments that isolate the influence of pattern (color-agnostic texture) and color contributions to attack performance. The large gap between CAPGen-P (pattern preserved, colors swapped: avg mAP₅₀ = 22.92) and CAPGen-R (colors preserved, random pattern: avg 82.18) provides genuinely suggestive evidence that pattern is the dominant driver of attack effectiveness. This decomposition is a novel conceptual contribution that goes beyond prior work's focus on attack performance alone.

- **Practical fast generation strategy grounded in the pattern-dominance finding.** The insight that recoloring a pre-optimized adversarial patch with environment base colors largely preserves attack effectiveness (CAPGen-P1 at 22.92 vs. AdvPatch at 19.58 in white-box, a gap of only 3.34 points) is practically useful. The method bypasses costly retraining when adapting to new environments, and the result that CAPGen-P1 can slightly *outperform* AdvPatch in the Yolov4-as-substitute black-box setting (37.99 vs. 38.64, a ~1.7% improvement they claim) demonstrates the potential of this approach.

- **Comprehensive evaluation across multiple detectors.** Results are reported on six models (Yolov2/3/4/5s/5m, Faster R-CNN) in both white-box and black-box settings, plus ablations on patch size and number of base colors. This breadth strengthens the empirical foundation.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No quantitative evaluation of visual stealthiness, despite stealthiness being the paper's primary claimed advantage.** The introduction and method repeatedly claim that CAPGen patches "blend seamlessly with the background," "are visually concealed," and "can fool human observers." The only evidence provided is a single qualitative figure (Fig. 1) and an unreferenced physical experiment described with the phrase "It is clear that..." (line 43). There is no human perception study, no stealthiness metric (e.g., color histogram distance to environment, detection rate by human observers), and no comparison to baselines on any stealthiness measure. Tables 1–3 exclusively report mAP₅₀ (attack performance). Without quantitative evidence for the claimed stealthiness advantage, a central pillar of the paper's contribution is unsubstantiated.

- **The "patterns are more important than colors" claim rests on a confounded experimental design.** The comparison in Section 4.3 contrasts CAPGen-P (pre-optimized AdvPatch pattern with colors swapped) against CAPGen-T (pattern optimized from scratch under the constraint of fixed base colors). This confounds the factor of interest (pattern vs. color importance) with optimization difficulty: CAPGen-P inherits a pattern optimized across hundreds of gradient steps *without* color constraints, while CAPGen-T must solve a harder optimization problem (learning a pattern under the restriction of only 3 discrete colors). The large performance gap (22.92 vs. 48.04) may simply reflect that the AdvPatch pattern is much better optimized, not that patterns are inherently more important. A cleaner test would hold the pattern fixed and independently swap colors vs. hold colors fixed and degrade the pattern, measuring the relative degradation from each manipulation. The current design does not support the strong causal claim.

- **The CAPGen gradient-driven optimization (CAPGen-T) produces patches far weaker than simple recoloring of an existing patch, and this is not adequately analyzed.** In Table 1, CAPGen-T1 (avg 48.04) and CAPGen-T2 (avg 48.15) are dramatically worse than CAPGen-P1 (22.92) and CAPGen-P2 (26.08). Since CAPGen-P is generated by a post-processing step (recoloring), not the proposed optimization pipeline, this raises a fundamental question: what does the gradient-based color probability matrix optimization (Section 3.2) add? The paper states this finding supports the pattern-dominance conclusion, but the confound above weakens that interpretation. The poor performance of CAPGen-T could stem from the harsh temperature τ=0.1 making the color assignment too discrete, insufficient optimization, or a fundamental limitation of optimizing patterns under tight color constraints — but none of these possibilities are explored or discussed.

- **Black-box transferability of CAPGen-P is inconsistent.** Contrary to the claim in the introduction that CAPGen-P "even surpasses the mainstream algorithm by about 1.7%," this holds only for the single case of Yolov4 as substitute. Across all six substitute-model settings, AdvPatch achieves lower (better) average mAP₅₀ in 4 out of 6 cases (Yolov3, Yolov5s, Yolov5m, Faster R-CNN), and the margins where CAPGen-P wins (Yolov2, Yolov4) are small. This undermines the claim that the fast generation strategy "does not sacrifice attack performance" in black-box settings.

### Minor

- **Disconnect between the problem formulation and implementation.** Equation (2) introduces an explicit stealth objective term S(P;ε) with a regularization coefficient λ₂, but the actual method (Section 3.2) implements stealth entirely through the choice of base colors — there is no S(P;ε) term in the optimization loss. The paper justifies this as intentional decoupling, but the formulation in Eq. (2) suggests an optimization trade-off that is not what is executed. This is confusing and should be reconciled.

- **The "3.34 points lower" framing (line 244) is ambiguous.** The paper states "Even AdvPatch is only 3.34 points lower than CAPGen-P1, further illustrating our approach's advantage." Lower mAP₅₀ is better for attack, so AdvPatch (19.58) outperforms CAPGen-P1 (22.92). The intended meaning (CAPGen-P1 is close to AdvPatch despite color constraints) is recoverable but the phrasing could mislead readers unfamiliar with the convention.

### Trivial

- The choice of k=3 base colors is stated as a default (line 103) with an ablation showing performance improves with more colors, but no heuristic or principle for choosing k is provided for practitioners applying the method to new environments.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A controlled experiment that independently swaps pattern (randomize the color probability matrix while keeping base colors) and swaps colors (keep probability matrix, change base colors) on the *same* base patch, measuring the performance drop from each manipulation separately. This would cleanly separate pattern vs. color importance.
- A human perception study or a quantitative stealthiness metric (e.g., color histogram distance, detection-by-human baseline).
- Analysis of why CAPGen-T underperforms: is it the τ=0.1 discretization, insufficient training under the constraint, or a fundamental limitation?

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviews surface a genuinely useful insight that the paper does not address: the comparison between CAPGen-P and CAPGen-T is not a clean test of pattern vs. color importance because it confounds the manipulated factor (color constraint) with optimization difficulty (inherited vs. from-scratch optimization). This confound means the paper's central empirical claim is weaker than presented, and a properly controlled experiment would either corroborate or refute it.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a quantitative stealthiness evaluation.** At minimum, report color histogram distances (e.g., Earth Mover's Distance) between generated patches and the environment background, and compare against AdvPatch, DAP, and NAP. A small-scale human detection study (e.g., MTurk observers spotting which image contains a patch) would substantially strengthen the stealthiness claim.
2. **Run a controlled pattern vs. color experiment.** Take a single converged adversarial patch and run two perturbations: (a) randomize the color probability matrix (destroy pattern, keep colors) and (b) randomize the base colors (keep pattern, destroy colors). Measure the relative attack-performance drop from each perturbation. This isolates the factor of interest without confounding by optimization quality.
3. **Analyze why CAPGen-T underperforms.** Diagnose whether the issue is the temperature τ=0.1 (excessive discretization), insufficient optimization steps under the color constraint, or a fundamental limitation. Show loss curves, and consider a sweep over τ.
4. **Reconcile Eq. (2) with the actual method.** Either remove the explicit S(P;ε) term from the formulation or explain how the decoupled design (base colors for stealth + probability matrix for attack) instantiates it.
5. **Clarify the framing in Section 4.3.** Explicitly state that AdvPatch outperforms CAPGen-P1 (lower mAP₅₀ is better) and that the key point is the small margin (3.34 points), not that CAPGen-P1 is superior.
