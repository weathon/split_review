## Summary

This paper introduces SF-EUOT, a simulation-free algorithm for solving the Entropic Unbalanced Optimal Transport (EUOT) problem. The authors derive a dynamical formulation of EUOT, extend the dual formulation via stochastic optimal control, and exploit the reciprocal property to parametrize the transport path using a static generator (avoiding SDE/ODE simulation during training). On CIFAR-10, EUOT-Soft achieves FID 3.02 with NFE 1 and no diffusion-model pretraining, whereas all prior SB methods require pretraining to obtain competitive scores.

## Strengths

- **First competitive SB/EUOT image generation without pretraining.** All prior Schrödinger Bridge models (IPF, IMF, ENOTDiffusion) fail to converge on CIFAR-10 without diffusion-model pretraining (FID ≥100), whereas SF-EUOT achieves FID 3.02 with NFE 1 and no pretraining (Table 1). This is the single most compelling piece of evidence for the paper's core scalability claim.

- **Simulation-free training via the reciprocal property is a genuine algorithmic advance.** By parametrizing the conditional transport plan with a static generator \(T_\theta\) and exploiting the reciprocal property (Eq. 7), the method avoids SDE/ODE simulation during both training and evaluation. Prior SB approaches (IPF, IMF) require neural ODE simulation with 100–200 NFE, making this a concrete practical improvement.

- **Strong image-to-image translation results.** On Male→Female (CelebA 64×64), EUOT-Soft achieves FID 8.44, outperforming the prior OT-based NOT (11.96) and CycleGAN (12.94). On Wild→Cat (AFHQ 64×64), it achieves FID 14.59 while DSBM and sb-flow both exceed 25 (Table 2). These results demonstrate improved scalability on real-world transport tasks.

- **Theoretical extension: dynamical and dual formulations of EUOT.** Theorem 1 derives the dynamical form of EUOT, generalizing the Schrödinger Bridge, and establishes that the optimal path measure retains the reciprocal property. Proposition 1 extends the weak-OT dual formulation from EOT to EUOT, providing a principled foundation for the learning objective.

- **Novel bilevel parametrization combining value function and path measure.** The method reinterprets the EUOT dual as a bilevel optimization problem (Section 4.1), combining HJB optimality conditions with a generator that implicitly satisfies the Fokker-Planck equation via the reciprocal property. This avoids explicit PDE solvers and yields a clean design.

### Minor

- **The generator loss gradient flow is underspecified.** In Algorithm 1 (lines 206–208), the generator loss is \(\lambda_G R\), where \(R\) is the HJB residual (defined in terms of \(v_\phi\)). The paper does not state whether \(v_\phi\) gradients are stopped when updating \(T_\theta\), nor how the gradient of \(\mathcal{L}_\theta\) (Eq. 21) is computed through the static generator parametrization. While standard practice would treat \(v_\phi\) as fixed during the \(T_\theta\) update (gradients flow through \(\theta\) only via \(\hat{y}\) and \(x_t\)), this should be explicitly stated for reproducibility.

- **"State-of-the-art" claims are imprecisely scoped.** The conclusion states the method "achieves state-of-the-art results in generative modeling on CIFAR-10 without diffusion model pretraining." In context this refers to SB-based methods without pretraining, where the claim holds (FID 3.02 vs. all baselines ≥100 without pretraining). However, diffusion models such as LSGM (2.10) and Score SDE (2.20) achieve better FIDs, so the unqualified phrase could mislead. The abstract and contribution list are more carefully worded ("comparable to the state-of-the-art results of the SB model with pretraining"; "first EOT model that presents competitive results without pretraining"), which are accurate. The conclusion should follow the same careful scoping.

- **Missing ablations for key hyperparameters.** The paper does not ablate the HJB loss exponent \(p\), the regularization weights \(\lambda_D\) and \(\lambda_G\), or the number of time steps \(N\). Only \(\sigma^2\) (entropic regularization) is ablated (Fig. 5). These hyperparameters likely affect the trade-off between HJB satisfaction and terminal matching, and their sensitivity is uncharacterized.

- **No uncertainty estimates for any experimental result.** All reported numbers (FID on CIFAR-10, I2I benchmarks, Gaussian benchmark) are single runs without standard deviations or confidence intervals. Given the complexity of the training procedure (PINN-style objectives, adversarial-like generator updates), variance across runs could be meaningful.

- **The derivation from the dual form (Eq. 19) to the bilevel formulation (Eq. 20) is terse.** Several algebraic steps are compressed, and Eq. 22 (\(\mathcal{L}_\rho\) as the dual of Eq. 21) is stated without derivation. While the overall logic is discernible, the presentation could be clearer, especially for readers less familiar with SOC duality.

- **Gaussian benchmark shows higher error than some competitors.** On the high-dimensional Gaussian EOT benchmark (Table 3), the method achieves 2.9% mean error, 3.0% variance error, and 3.72% covariance error — below 4% but worse than IMF-b (0.9%, ≥10%, ≥10%) and DSBM-IPF (2.3%, 0.14%, 1.63%) on key metrics. The paper acknowledges this but does not analyze why the method is less accurate on this tractable setting.
