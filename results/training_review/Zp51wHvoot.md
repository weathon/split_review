## Summary
The paper introduces ACDC, a zero-shot, training-free method that combines autoregressive models (ARMs) for global context with diffusion models (DMs) for local correction via SDEdit, augmented with an LLM-based memory module to preserve global narrative information. Experiments on story generation (with Show-o, UIO-2) and autoregressive video generation (with LWM) show consistent improvements in frame consistency, CLIP similarity, and aesthetic quality.

## Strengths
- **Zero-shot, training-free combination of ARMs and DMs**: The method requires no fine-tuning or architectural modifications, validated across three different ARMs (Show-o, UIO-2, LWM) and two tasks, as stated in Section 1 and demonstrated in Tables 1 and 3.
- **Significant reduction of error accumulation**: ACDC improves frame consistency for Show-o from 0.8211 to 0.9062 (+10.4%), CLIP similarity from 28.76 to 30.82 (+7.16%), and ImageReward by 0.574 (Table 1). For video generation (Table 3), subject consistency improves from 0.7369 to 0.7622 and aesthetic quality by 7.33%.
- **LLM memory module demonstrably contributes to quality**: Ablation results (Table 2) show ACDC with memory achieves frame consistency 0.888 vs. 0.835 without memory, and improves CLIP similarity from 29.43 to 30.85, confirming the module's role in distilling global context.
- **Model- and task-agnostic framework**: ACDC is applied to both story generation (2 ARMs) and autoregressive video generation (1 ARM) with consistent improvements, demonstrating generality.
- **Extension to physical constraints via inpainting**: Section 3 shows ACDC can integrate user constraints (e.g., inpainting masks) to correct physically implausible artifacts without additional training, highlighting the flexibility of the framework.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Circular FID evaluation limits the strength of quantitative claims**: The paper computes FID against "pseudo-ground truth" images generated by SDXL-lightning (line 190). Since ACDC's correction module (Stable Diffusion) is in the same model family as the FID reference, DM-corrected outputs are systematically favored over pure ARM outputs. The paper acknowledges this limitation (line 192) but it weakens the FID comparison as evidence. The other metrics (frame consistency, CLIP similarity, ImageReward) do not suffer from this confound and still show improvements, but the FID results should be interpreted with caution.
- **No direct quantification of identity/subject preservation after SDEdit**: The paper claims SDEdit with t'∈[0.4,0.5] "does not alter the content of the image" (line 105), but provides no per-sample quantitative measurement (e.g., LPIPS, face/object similarity, or character consistency pre- vs. post-correction). Frame consistency measures similarity between *different frames*, not between the ARM output and its corrected version for the same frame. Without this measurement, it is unclear whether ACDC is refining the ARM's output or overwriting it with a DM-generated image that merely respects the text prompt — which would weaken the claim of "correction" as opposed to "replacement."

### Minor
- **Synthetic benchmark limits comparability and generalizability**: The story generation benchmark is generated by GPT-4o-mini from 10 handcrafted examples (line 185). While the authors justify this choice (existing benchmarks are not "approximately in-distributed to both the base ARM and DM"), the lack of evaluation on established multi-frame story benchmarks means the results cannot be directly compared to prior work. The improvements are internally consistent but their generalizability to real-world story distributions is unclear.
- **Re-encoding loss not analyzed**: The corrected pixel images are re-encoded via the ARM's VQGAN encoder ($\tilde{\rvv}^i = \mathcal{E}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_0^i)$) to resume ARM sampling (line 106). The paper does not analyze the distortion introduced by this round-trip (ARM-decoded → DM-corrected → VQGAN-re-encoded). If this re-encoding introduces significant errors, it could partially offset the benefits of correction and silently degrade subsequent ARM predictions.
- **Ablation inconsistency unexplained**: Table 2 shows ACDC #6 with memory yields lower frame consistency (0.853) than ACDC #2 with memory (0.888) — i.e., correcting more frames gives worse frame consistency. The paper notes this (line 235: "frame consistency is slightly compromised compared to ACDC #2") but offers no explanation. This is a non-trivial observation for a method claiming to reduce error accumulation.
- **Video experiment tests one-time correction, not iterative error mitigation**: The video experiment (Section 4.2) corrects the first 16 frames and then generates the remaining 16. While this does demonstrate that better initial conditions prevent later errors, the paper's claim of "mitigating the accumulation of errors" would be further strengthened by showing that repeated correction at multiple points yields additional benefit.

### Trivial
- The specific LLM used for the memory module and the exact system/user prompts are not stated in the main paper (the description at lines 107-113 mentions an LLM parameterized by φ and a system prompt d but gives no concrete model name or prompt template), making full reproduction dependent on the appendix or code release.

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a conceptually clean and practically useful idea — using a pre-trained DM as a drop-in correction module during ARM inference — and demonstrates consistent improvements across two tasks and three ARMs. The main weaknesses are: (1) the FID evaluation is circular, (2) identity preservation after SDEdit is not directly quantified, and (3) some implementation details are underspecified. None of these are fatal; they weaken but do not invalidate the core claims. The paper would benefit from additional analysis and a more standard evaluation protocol, but the contribution is solid.
