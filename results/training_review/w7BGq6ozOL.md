## Summary

This paper proposes integrating six large language models (LLMs) with Deep Q-Network (DQN) and Double DQN (DDQN) reinforcement learning agents for algorithmic trading. The authors introduce Stock-Evol-Instruct, a domain-adapted instruction evolution algorithm that generates fine-tuning datasets for LLM-based trading agents using rule-based responses (buy/sell/hold based on price vs. moving-average thresholds). They evaluate the approach on two stocks (SLV, JPM) using real-world news data, reporting that fine-tuned Mistral-7B achieves 53.15% ROI on JPM and 48.36% on SLV, substantially outperforming FinRL and FinGPT baselines.

## Strengths

- **Systematic evaluation of six LLMs across three prompt types and two RL algorithms.** The paper tests GPT-4o, LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3, Mistral-7B, Falcon-7B, and OpenELM with zero-shot, instruction-based, and exemplar-based prompts, mapping model–prompt synergies (e.g., Falcon-7B+DDQN achieves SR 2.18 for SLV with Prompt-2). This provides a useful empirical landscape for practitioners combining LLM signals with RL trading agents (Section 4.2, Section 3.3.1).

- **Stock-Evol-Instruct is a conceptually novel adaptation of Evol-Instruct to the stock domain.** The algorithm introduces domain-specific steps including rule-based response generation from time-series features (avoiding LLM hallucination in ground-truth labels) and elimination of hallucinated placeholders, representing a structured methodology for creating financial instruction datasets (Section 3.4.2).

- **Transparent discussion of metric inconsistencies.** The paper explicitly analyzes cases where high Sharpe Ratio does not guarantee positive ROI (e.g., Mistral-7B+DDQN: SR 2.29, ROI -10.39%) and warns against over-reliance on a single metric, reflecting nuanced understanding of real-world trading evaluation (Section 4.2, Section 5).

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **The fine-tuning ground truth is a simple price-based rule that is never benchmarked as a baseline, making the fine-tuned agent results uninterpretable.** The ground-truth responses in Stock-Evol-Instruct are generated by a deterministic rule: buy if close > open AND close > 2-day MA, sell if close < open AND close < 2-day MA, else hold (Section 3.4.2). The fine-tuned LLMs are trained to imitate this rule from instructions that encode the same price features. The paper never evaluates the performance of this simple rule itself on the test sets. Given that the fine-tuned Mistral-7B achieves 53.15% ROI, it is impossible to determine whether this reflects genuine LLM capability or simply successful replication of the rule. If the rule itself achieves comparable returns, the fine-tuning contribution is vacuous. The comparison with FinGPT (a general financial LLM not trained on this rule) and FinRL (a DRL system with a completely different objective) does not substitute for this missing baseline.

2. **The LLM–RL reward integration is a heuristic without ablation or comparison to alternatives.** The paper modifies the RL reward by doubling it when actions align with the LLM signal, assigning fixed positive reward for "hold" recommendations, and penalizing divergence (Section 3.2). While this design is not unreasonable, the paper provides no ablation isolating whether the improvement comes from the LLM signal content or from the reward modification itself. No comparison is made with alternative integration strategies (e.g., using the LLM output as an additional state feature within a standard reward function, or learning a Q-function over joint LLM+price actions). This means the comparison with pure RL baselines (FinRL using standard reward functions) conflates two changes: adding LLM signals AND changing the reward function, invalidating any attribution of improvement.

3. **Evaluation lacks financial-standards rigor on multiple dimensions, all pointing in the same direction.** (a) **Only two stocks (SLV, JPM)** are tested, justified by data availability in a 1.85M-news dataset (Section 4.1.2) — this does not support generalization to "algorithmic trading." (b) **No temporal validation**: a single train–test split with no walk-forward analysis, no reporting of performance across different market regimes, and no cross-validation. In financial time series, single splits are known to produce non-generalizable results. (c) **No statistical significance**: Sharpe ratios and ROIs are reported as point estimates without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or any significance test. (d) **Transaction costs are not mentioned** — for daily trading on two stocks, even small commissions or bid-ask spreads can materially alter reported ROIs. These issues collectively mean the empirical support for the paper's central claims is weaker than the headline numbers suggest.

### Minor

1. **The fine-tuning paradigm has a logical inconsistency concerning news signals.** The instruction templates include news forecast information (Section 3.4.1), but the rule-based ground truth (Section 3.4.2, Response Generation) is a pure function of price features (close vs open, close vs 2-day MA) — it does not actually incorporate news forecasts. The fine-tuned model is therefore trained to produce price-rule outputs even when given news inputs; an ideal model would learn to *ignore* the news. This contradicts the paper's narrative of "LLM-driven insights" in the fine-tuning pipeline. If news is actually used in the rule, the paper must specify how.

2. **The exemplars for Prompt 3 (exemplar-based forecasting) are described as "human-annotated" (Section 3.3.1) but their origin, selection criteria, and potential overlap with the test set are not specified.** This matters because exemplar overlap with test data would inflate results.

3. **Hyperparameters, random seeds, training/test date ranges, and data split sizes are not reported.** These details are standard for reproducibility in ML-for-finance papers and their absence makes independent verification difficult.

4. **The "only reported the best models per stock" for FinRL (line 141) is not a fatal asymmetry** — the proposed method also tests multiple configurations (DQN/DDQN, 6 LLMs, 3 prompts) and reports many combinations. However, for the fine-tuned agent comparison, only two fine-tuned models (LLaMA-3, Mistral-7B) are compared against baselines, while FinRL gets to pick its best algorithm per stock (PPO for JPM, TD3 for SLV). This asymmetry should be disclosed and discussed.

### Trivial

- Section 3.1 (DQN/DDQN) contains textbook-level DRL exposition that is not adapted to trading beyond generic description; this space could be used for trading-specific implementation details.
- The paper claims "significant potential to outperform" in the abstract but the empirical scope (2 stocks, no rule baseline) does not support this strength of claim.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An equity curve (cumulative returns over the test period) for the fine-tuned agents versus the simple rule versus baselines would visually reveal performance stability and drawdowns that Sharpe and ROI alone miss.
- Concrete examples of news headlines, LLM predictions, and resulting trades would help readers understand when the LLM signal adds value.
- A sensitivity analysis with realistic transaction costs (e.g., 0.1% per trade) would strengthen practical relevance.

## Suggestions

1. **Add the simple rule as a formal baseline.** Run the exact buy/sell/hold rule (close vs open, close vs 2-day MA) used for ground-truth generation directly on the test sets. If it matches or exceeds the fine-tuned models' ROI, the fine-tuning contribution is minimal. If it performs substantially worse, this is the strongest evidence for the paper's thesis.

2. **Ablate the reward modification.** Compare: (a) DQN/DDQN with standard reward, (b) DQN/DDQN with LLM signal as an *additional state feature* and standard reward, (c) DQN/DDQN with the proposed reward modification. This isolates whether the LLM signal's value comes from its content or from the reward shaping.

3. **Expand the evaluation to at least 6–10 stocks** from diverse sectors, with a walk-forward validation (rolling windows) and report mean ± std of ROI and Sharpe. Include confidence intervals via bootstrapping.

4. **Disclose all implementation details**: hyperparameters, random seeds, training/test date ranges, and class distribution in each split.

5. **Clarify the role of news in the fine-tuning ground truth.** Either formally incorporate news into the rule-based response or acknowledge that the ground truth is purely price-driven and discuss the implications for the fine-tuned model's behavior.
