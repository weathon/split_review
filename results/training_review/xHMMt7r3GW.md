## Summary

This paper introduces LieRE (Lie group Relative position Encodings), which generalizes Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) to n-dimensional inputs. The core idea is to replace RoPE's block-diagonal 2D rotation matrices with dense, high-dimensional rotation matrices generated by exponentiating learned skew-symmetric matrices. The non-commutative structure of these dense rotations (vs. the commutative block-diagonal structure of RoPE-Mixed) is the key differentiator. Experiments on CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and UCF101 show relative accuracy improvements over absolute position encodings and modest gains over prior relative encodings, along with training-step and data efficiency advantages.

## Strengths

- **Principled generalization of RoPE to n-dimensional inputs via Lie groups.** The paper correctly identifies that RoPE and RoPE-Mixed use commutative (block-diagonal) rotations, and that LieRE relaxes commutativity by learning dense skew-symmetric bases and exponentiating them (Section 3). When block size is set to 2, LieRE recovers RoPE-Mixed, making the connection explicit. This is a clean, well-motivated conceptual contribution.

- **Consistent accuracy improvements across multiple baselines and modalities.** On CIFAR-100, LieRE achieves 10.0% relative improvement over the absolute-encoding DeiT III baseline and 2.2% over RoPE-Mixed; on UCF101 (3D video), 15.1% relative over absolute encodings (Section 5.1). Gains are demonstrated without pre-trained weights and with a fixed backbone.

- **Systematic ablation of design choices.** The paper varies block size (2→8→64), parameter sharing across heads/layers, and backbone size (ViT-T/B/L), characterizing the trade-offs (Sections 5.3.1–5.3.3). The ablations provide useful guidance for practitioners.

- **Multi-resolution generalization.** LieRE is evaluated at five inference resolutions on ImageNet (196–448 px) and outperforms RoPE-Mixed, especially at larger resolutions (Figure 2, Section 5.1).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Insufficient evidence that non-commutative structure outperforms commutative (block-diagonal) structure.** The paper's central theoretical claim is that dense, non-commutative rotation matrices are superior to the commutative block-diagonal matrices used in RoPE-Mixed. Yet the only reported numerical advantage on this axis is a **2.2% relative gain on CIFAR-100** (Section 5.1). On ImageNet, the text merely states "similar accuracy trends observed" without providing the actual accuracies for LieRE vs. RoPE-Mixed. Given that LieRE adds parameters relative to RoPE-Mixed (block size 2 vs. 8 or 64), the paper does not control for capacity to isolate the effect of non-commutativity. A 2.2% gain from added parameters alone would not be surprising, and without ImageNet numbers the reader cannot assess whether the advantage generalizes.

2. **No wall-clock time measurement despite overhead from matrix exponential.** LieRE requires computing a matrix exponential of a learned skew-symmetric matrix per forward pass (per layer/head). This is computationally more expensive than the closed-form 2D rotations in RoPE/RoPE-Mixed. The paper reports a 3.9× reduction in training steps to match the absolute-position baseline (Section 5.4), but never measures wall-clock time. If each step is slower, the actual speedup could be much smaller. Since training efficiency is a headline contribution, this omission is critical.

3. **Efficiency claims only contextualized against absolute-position baselines.** The 3.9× step reduction (Section 5.4) and 70% data efficiency (Section 5.2) are measured solely against the DeiT III (absolute encoding) baseline. The paper asserts LieRE gives "the largest reduction in training time compared to recent works such as VisionLlama and RoPE-Mixed" but does **not report the reduction numbers for those baselines**, so the reader cannot verify whether LieRE's efficiency advantage over relative encodings is real or whether RoPE-Mixed already achieves similar savings.

### Minor

1. **No analysis of *why* non-commutativity helps.** The paper correctly identifies commutativity vs. non-commutativity as the key distinction (Eqs. 1–2, Section 2.1), but provides no theoretical or empirical investigation of why breaking commutativity improves performance. The capacity argument (more parameters → more expressive rotations) is conflated with the structural argument (non-commutative → richer relational encoding). An analysis of attention distances, effective position resolution, or learned rotation structure would strengthen the paper.

2. **Alternative interpretation of patch-shuffling results.** The paper shows a larger accuracy drop when patches are shuffled (Table 3) and interprets this as "more capable at using positional information" (Section 5.5). However, a larger drop could equally indicate that LieRE overfits to the training position distribution and does not generalize to unseen arrangements. The experiment does not distinguish between these competing explanations.

3. **No absolute ImageNet accuracy numbers in the accessible text.** For ImageNet, the text says only "similar accuracy trends observed" (Section 5.1). The absolute numbers are presumably in Table 1 (an unparseable image), but the text should report them explicitly so readers can assess practical significance without cross-referencing a figure.

4. **Hyperparameter reuse may favor LieRE.** The paper states "we did not optimize any hyperparameters for the LieRE model" (Section 4.1.2), reusing baseline settings. If LieRE is simply more robust to suboptimal hyperparameters (e.g., because it adds learnable parameters for the rotation basis), this could inflate its relative performance. The paper does not discuss this confound.

### Trivial

1. **Numerical inconsistency between abstract and conclusion.** The abstract and Section 5.4 state a "3.9-fold" / "3.9 times" reduction in training steps/epochs, while the conclusion states "3.5 times less training compute." One of these is a typographical error.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation that controls for parameter count: train a RoPE-Mixed variant with additional learnable parameters (e.g., extra MLP layers) to match LieRE's parameter count, then compare accuracy. This would disentangle the effect of non-commutative structure from added capacity.
- Wall-clock training time comparison for LieRE vs. RoPE-Mixed on CIFAR-100.
- Attention heatmaps comparing LieRE, RoPE-Mixed, and absolute encodings on the same input to illustrate whether LieRE learns qualitatively different spatial patterns.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation from the reviews is that the paper sits at an awkward epistemic threshold: the theoretical contribution (relaxing commutativity in RoPE via Lie groups) is clean and well-motivated, but the experimental case for its practical value rests on a 2.2% relative gain over the closest baseline on a single dataset, with no ImageNet confirmation and no wall-clock timing. This creates a mismatch between the generality of the claim ("LieRE outperforms existing methods") and the thinness of the evidence for the specific advantage that distinguishes it from RoPE-Mixed. The reviews collectively suggest the paper would be significantly strengthened not by more theory or more baselines, but by a single carefully controlled experiment that isolates the effect of non-commutativity from added parameter capacity.

## Suggestions

1. Report absolute accuracy numbers for LieRE vs. RoPE-Mixed on ImageNet in the main text.
2. Add a wall-clock training time comparison (LieRE vs. RoPE-Mixed) on CIFAR-100.
3. Add a controlled ablation where a RoPE-Mixed baseline receives additional parameters (e.g., an MLP after the attention) to match LieRE's parameter count, isolating the effect of non-commutative structure.
4. Fix the discrepancy between 3.9× and 3.5× in the conclusion.
5. Discuss the alternative interpretation of the patch-shuffling results and acknowledge the hyperparameter-reuse confound in the limitations section.
