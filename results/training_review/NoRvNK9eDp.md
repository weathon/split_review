## Summary

The paper introduces "level-set parameters" (the weights of a neural SDF network) as a continuous representation for 3D shape analysis. It proposes (1) a two-stage training procedure with a learned initialization μ to establish correlations in the parameter space, (2) a hypernetwork (HyperSE3-SDF) that generates pose-dependent first-layer SDF parameters conditioned on SE(3) transformations, and (3) an encoder that processes these parameters for classification/retrieval, plus (4) a correspondence-free 6D pose estimation method using SDF reconstruction loss. The paper demonstrates these capabilities on ShapeNet and Manifold40.

## Strengths

- **Continuous representation for 3D shape analysis**: The paper demonstrates that SDF network weights can serve as a standalone representation for classification and retrieval. In reference-pose (I,0) and rotation-augmented settings, the proposed LSPData achieves results competitive with point-cloud-based methods (Table 2), showing the viability of this approach.

- **Hypernetwork for parameter-space transformations**: The decomposition of parameters into pose-dependent (first-layer) and pose-independent subsets, coupled with a hypernetwork that generates the former conditioned on R,t, is a conceptually clean mechanism for handling rotations in the non-Euclidean parameter space. The paper shows this outperforms the analytic Euclidean transform (Eq. 4) in Table 1.

- **Pose estimation without correspondences or training data**: The registration method using SDF reconstruction loss with a grid search over initializations achieves highly accurate results under significant noise (σ=0.03) and 30% outliers (e.g., RRE 0.95° for chairs), substantially outperforming optimization-based baselines (ICP, FGR, TEASER++) in challenging settings (Table 4).

- **Improved shape correlation via learned initialization μ**: The two-stage training (learning a shared μ from a few shapes, then fitting Δθ per shape) demonstrably improves semantic clustering in t-SNE embeddings (Figure 3) and classification accuracy by over 13% compared to random initialization (Table 1).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

### Major

- **Point cloud baseline comparisons are not tightly controlled**: The classification comparisons (Tables 2, 3) report literature results for point cloud methods rather than re-running them on identical train/test splits. The paper's training set is 200 shapes per class in ShapeNet, while the cited baselines were typically trained on full ShapeNet (often thousands per class). While the asymmetry in training set size actually favors the baselines (making the paper's results more impressive if replicated), the lack of controlled comparison prevents definitive claims about "outperforming rotation-equivariant networks."

- **Pose estimation comparison setting differs structurally from baselines**: The proposed registration method assumes the target object's level-set parameters are available (requiring SDF training per reference object), while ICP/FGR/TEASER only need the reference point cloud. The method also requires a global search over 3375 initializations (~50 seconds). This is a fundamentally different setting — informative for what level-set parameters enable, but the comparison to methods operating from raw point clouds should be contextualized more explicitly as a different regime rather than a direct competition.

### Minor

- **Framing as a "new data modality" is somewhat overclaimed**: The paper presents "level-set parameters" as a novel data modality on par with point clouds and meshes. However, these are neural network weights — a learned representation generated by the authors' pipeline, not an acquired geometric data type. The paper is transparent about their origin (SDF network parameters), and the technical work is valid, but the "data modality" framing creates an expectation mismatch. The contribution would be more accurately described as "using neural SDF weights as features for shape analysis."

- **The encoder is architecture-specific**: The encoder processes tensors of exact sizes 256×4, 6×256×257, and 1×257, corresponding to the specific 8-layer MLP architecture used. This limits the generality of the approach and the conclusions that can be drawn about "level-set parameter analysis" more broadly.

- **Noise level definition is unclear**: The noise levels σ ∈ {0.01, 0.03} in the pose estimation experiments are not defined relative to shape scale (e.g., as a fraction of shape diameter or bounding box size), making the results harder to interpret.

## Novel Insights

The reviews reveal a genuine tension in the paper: the main technical contribution (using SDF weights as features, with a hypernetwork for transformation) is solid and well-executed, but the contribution is framed at the wrong level. The paper would be stronger if it presented itself as "learning-based features from neural SDF weights for 3D shape analysis" rather than introducing "level-set parameters" as a new data modality. The disconnect between framing and content is the primary source of the harsh critic's objections. Notably, the two-stage prior-learning approach addresses a real problem in weight-space analysis (lack of correspondence across independently trained networks) and the hypernetwork's handling of pose is genuinely novel — these technical contributions are more significant than the "new data" framing suggests.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the contribution**: Describe the work as "neural SDF weight-space features for 3D shape analysis" or "learning-based features from level-set parameters" rather than introducing level-set parameters as a new data modality.
