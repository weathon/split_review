## Summary

The paper proposes TA-STVG, which replaces zero-initialized object queries in DETR-style spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) with target-aware queries generated by two cascade modules: text-guided temporal sampling (TTS) and attribute-aware spatial activation (ASA). TTS selects target-relevant frames guided by holistic text features, while ASA exploits fine-grained visual attributes (color, shape, action) to produce spatially activated features for query initialization. Experiments on HCSTVG-v1/v2 and VidSTG show consistent improvements over a zero-query baseline and achieve competitive or state-of-the-art results.

## Strengths

- **Strong empirical motivation with an oracle experiment**: The paper provides direct evidence that target-specific cues in object queries significantly improve STVG. Using groundtruth-generated queries (Fig. 2), the oracle experiment shows absolute gains of +18.0% m_IoU and +13.5% m_vIoU over zero-initialized queries on HCSTVG-v1. This cleanly motivates the central idea and is specific, cited, and replicable in concept.

- **Consistent improvements across multiple benchmarks and metrics**: TA-STVG achieves best or competitive results on all metrics for HCSTVG-v1, HCSTVG-v2, and VidSTG (Tabs. 1–3). The gains over the zero-query baseline are meaningful (e.g., +3.1% m_tIoU on HCSTVG-v1, +2.1% on HCSTVG-v2, +2.2% on VidSTG declarative), demonstrating that the proposed approach delivers practical improvements beyond what small random perturbations would yield.

- **Detailed ablation studies isolating each component**: Tab. 4 shows that TTS alone yields +2.3% m_tIoU, ASA alone gives +1.5%, and together they achieve +3.1%. Further ablations (Tabs. 5–6) confirm that both appearance/motion branches in TTS and both appearance/motion attributes in ASA contribute, and Tabs. 8–9 explore hyperparameter sensitivity. This provides empirical grounding for the design choices.

- **Generality experiments on other architectures**: Applying TTS/ASA to TubeDETR and STCAT (Tab. 10) yields consistent gains (+1.7–2.3% m_tIoU), showing the modules are architecture-agnostic and can benefit existing systems.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The core claim — that target-aware queries improve STVG — is supported by consistent improvements over the zero-query baseline across three benchmarks.

### Major

- **Lack of statistical significance / variance reporting**: No confidence intervals, standard deviations, or significance tests are reported for any experiment. The improvements over the strongest competing method (CGSTVG) appear to be small on some metrics (e.g., the paper highlights a +1.6% absolute gain on vIoU@0.3, while other metric margins may be narrower). Without multiple runs or variance estimates, it is impossible to assess whether the gains over prior art are statistically reliable. This is the most serious weakness because it undermines the "state-of-the-art" claim.

- **Hyperparameter selection on test set**: HCSTVG-v1 has only training (4,500) and test (1,160) splits — no validation set. The ablation studies (Tabs. 5–6, 8–9) and hyperparameter analyses for δ and θ are reported on HCSTVG-v1 without mention of reserving a validation subset. This means the thresholds δ=0.5 and θ=0.7 may have been chosen to maximize test performance, inflating the reported gains and violating standard evaluation protocol.

- **Generality experiments lack capacity controls**: Applying TTS and ASA to TubeDETR and STCAT (Tab. 10) adds new trainable parameters and auxiliary losses. The reported improvements (+1.7–2.3 m_tIoU) could partly stem from increased model capacity or additional supervision rather than the specific design of target-aware queries. A control experiment (e.g., adding a similarly sized generic module to the baselines) is absent, weakening the claim that the design itself is responsible for the gains.

### Minor

- **Core ASA details deferred to supplementary**: The subject extraction procedure and the construction of "explicit weak attribute labels" from text are both deferred to the supplementary material with "due to limited space." While supplementary material exists, ASA is a central contribution, and these details are essential for understanding and reproducing the method from the main paper alone.

- **TTS and ASA combined gain less than sum of individual gains**: TTS alone yields +2.3%, ASA alone yields +1.5%, but together they yield +3.1% (vs. a sum of 3.8%). This suggests overlap or interference between the two modules, which the paper does not analyze. Understanding this interaction would strengthen the contribution.

- **Large oracle-to-method gap unaddressed**: The oracle experiment shows +18% m_IoU gain, but the full method captures only +3.1%. The paper never discusses why this gap is so large or analyzes what information the text-driven cues fail to capture (e.g., inaccurate temporal sampling, weak attribute supervision, limited subject extraction). This disconnect between motivation and result deserves examination.

- **Generality experiments use only appearance branch**: Since TubeDETR and STCAT use only appearance features, only the appearance branches of TTS and ASA were applied. The paper acknowledges this, but it limits the generality demonstration to appearance-only variants, meaning the motion-aware aspects of the design are not validated for transferability.

- **Some ablation differences are very small**: The gain from using the subject in ASA (Tab. 6) is only 0.4% (53.0 vs. 52.6). The gain of attribute-aware over instance-level activation (Tab. 7) is 0.2% (53.0 vs. 52.8). While these differences are in the expected direction, their small magnitude suggests the specific design choice matters less than the overall TTS+ASA pipeline.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Statistical significance testing**: Running each condition 3+ times with different seeds and reporting mean/std would substantially strengthen the empirical claims.
- **Controlled generality baseline**: Adding a module with comparable parameter count (e.g., an extra cross-attention layer) to TubeDETR and STCAT would disentangle capacity effects from design effects.
- **Failure case analysis**: Showing examples where TA-STVG fails but the groundtruth-generated queries succeed would illuminate the limitations of the text-driven approach and suggest future directions.
- **Overlap analysis between TTS and ASA**: Analyzing attention maps or feature correlation would clarify whether the two modules learn redundant information.

## Suggestions

2. **Validate hyperparameters on a held-out set**: For HCSTVG-v1, either create a training/validation split from the training data (e.g., 4,000 train / 500 val) or explicitly state that hyperparameters were fixed before any test-set evaluation. 3. **Add a capacity-controlled generality experiment**: Add a module with comparable parameter count and auxiliary loss (but without the TTS/ASA structure) to TubeDETR and STCAT to show the gains are not solely from increased capacity.
4. **Discuss the oracle-to-method gap**: Analyze what information is missing from the text-driven cues (e.g., inaccurate temporal sampling, weak attribute supervision) and quantify the loss at each stage (TTS vs. ASA) to provide a roadmap for future improvements.
