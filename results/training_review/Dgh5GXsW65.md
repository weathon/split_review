## Summary

This paper empirically studies the geometric relationship between initial Gaussian noise, images generated by diffusion models, and the latents obtained via DDIM inversion. The main findings are: (1) DDIM-inverted latents deviate from Gaussianity and retain image structure, especially for pixel-space models; (2) these latents lie geometrically between the initial noise and the generated image along the generation trajectory; and (3) the mapping from noise to image (measurable via L2 distance assignment accuracy) emerges very early in training and does not improve with prolonged training.

## Strengths

- **Clear demonstration that DDIM latents deviate from Gaussianity (Section 4.2).** Figure 1 provides visual evidence of image structure in inverted latents, and Table 1 quantifies this via top-10 Pearson correlations, showing that latents contain correlated pixel values while true Gaussian noise does not. The effect is consistent across models.

- **Geometric localization of latents between noise and image (Section 4.3).** Using angle analysis (Figure 2) and distance heatmaps between intermediate trajectory steps and the noise–latent interval (Figure 3), the paper shows that DDIM-inverted latents lie along the generation trajectory between the initial noise and the sample, and that the trajectory eventually passes the latent. This provides a precise geometric characterization of DDIM approximation error.

- **Demonstration that the noise–image mapping emerges early in training (Section 4.4, Figures 4–6).** The paper tracks angles, L2 assignment accuracy, and image similarity metrics across training checkpoints and shows that the noise–image relationship stabilizes within the first fraction of training. This extends prior observations about score function convergence to the specific setting of noise–image pair assignment.

- **Generality across architectures and datasets.** Experiments are conducted on three diverse models (DDPM on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, LDM on CelebA), and the main findings are consistent across all models, which strengthens the generality of the conclusions.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Quantitative claims lack statistical rigor throughout.** The paper reports that results are "averaged over 1K samples from 3 models trained with 3 random training seeds" (line 71), but it never reports the variance across these seeds. The claim that "the angle located at the image's vertex is acute but never zero" (line 98) and the numerical values in Tables 1–2 and Figures 2–6 are presented without confidence intervals, error bars, or any measure of uncertainty. For an empirical analysis paper whose claims hinge on precise geometric distinctions, this is a significant gap. Without variance, the reader cannot assess whether observed differences (e.g., acute vs. obtuse angles, small differences in correlation) are robust or within the noise.

- **LDM-specific differences are noted but not analyzed.** The paper observes that the LDM model produces near-Gaussian latents (Table 1: "almost not noticeable for LDM") and exhibits symmetric assignment accuracy (Table 2: high accuracy in both directions), which differs qualitatively from the DDPM results. These observations are reported without analysis or explanation, and they could indicate that the paper's main findings are specific to pixel-space DDPMs and do not generalize to latent-space models. This undermines the broad claims in the abstract.

### Minor

- **L2 assignment experiment lacks comparison to alternative metrics.** The paper reports >99% accuracy for assigning images to their initial noise using L2 distance (Table 2) but does not compare to other distance or similarity metrics (cosine similarity, normalized Euclidean, correlation, Mahalanobis). Without this comparison, it is unclear whether L2 distance is specifically meaningful or whether any reasonable distance metric would work. (Note: the result is clearly meaningful relative to random chance — >99% on 1000-way classification — so the lack of a random baseline is not a concern.)

- **No analysis of distance distributions for the assignment task.** The paper does not show the distribution of L2 distances from a given image to all 1000 noises, nor the ratio of the correct-pair distance to the mean incorrect-pair distance. This would make the result more interpretable and reveal whether the correct noise is a clear outlier or only marginally closer.

- **Explanation for assignment asymmetry is speculative.** The paper attributes the failure of noise→image assignment to "generations with large plain areas of low pixel variance" (line 124) but provides no experimental evidence for this mechanism. It is offered as a plausible hypothesis without supporting analysis.

- **The claim of structure "unaccounted by theory" (Figure 3, line 106) is not substantiated.** The paper observes geometric structure in the distance heatmap and calls it "unaccounted by theory," but it does not identify what specific theoretical predictions this contradicts. This framing overstates the finding.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Comparison with more exact inversion methods (e.g., Renoise) to test whether the geometric findings are specific to DDIM or hold for better inversion techniques.
- Theoretical discussion of why L2 distance can identify the correct noise–image pair. A toy experiment with a known linear mapping could serve as a sanity check.
- Distance distribution histograms for the assignment experiment to visually show the separation between correct and incorrect pairs.
- Analysis of why LDM behaves differently, to clarify the scope and limits of the paper's conclusions.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed — treat them with caution:

1. **"L2 result likely reflects an artifact"** — The reviewer claimed the >99% accuracy "likely reflects an artifact" of high-dimensional geometry. The paper reports internally consistent results that vary meaningfully with the number of diffusion steps T and with the direction of assignment (image→noise vs. noise→image). These variations are inconsistent with a trivial artifact. The >99% accuracy on 1000-way classification is a genuine empirical finding, regardless of whether the mechanism is fully understood.

2. **"No comparison to random baseline (0.1%)"** — The >99% accuracy is self-evidently far above the 0.1% chance level; explicitly stating this is unnecessary. This point is subsumed by the legitimate request for alternative metric comparison.

3. **"Angle analysis is just a restatement of known error analysis"** — The paper explicitly frames its contribution as an empirical study of the *consequences* of the DDIM approximation error (line 55: "we empirically study the consequences of this approximation error"). The geometric characterization (Figures 2–3) is the novel contribution, not the existence of the error itself.

4. **"Contributions are modest and the framing overstates significance"** — This is a subjective judgment, not a specific, actionable weakness. The paper is an empirical analysis and does not claim to propose a new method or theoretical framework.

5. **"Missing related works"** / **"Missing appendix or proofs"** — These criticisms are not valid; the parser strips these sections. The paper's novelty should be evaluated on what it presents, not on speculative omissions.

## Suggestions

1. **Report uncertainty in all quantitative results.** Add error bars, confidence intervals, or per-seed variance to Tables 1–2 and Figures 2–6. The paper already runs 3 seeds — reporting the spread is straightforward and would substantially strengthen the claims.

2. **Compare L2 distance to alternative metrics** (cosine similarity, normalized Euclidean, correlation) for the assignment experiment in Section 4.4 to establish that the finding is specifically about L2 distance rather than any distance metric.

3. **Provide distance distribution histograms** for the assignment task (e.g., for a representative image, show the distribution of L2 distances to all 1000 noises, highlighting the correct pair). This would make the result immediately interpretable.

4. **Analyze the LDM-specific behavior.** The LDM results differ qualitatively from DDPMs; understanding why (e.g., the role of the autoencoder's latent space normalization) would clarify the scope of the paper's conclusions and potentially reveal new insights.

5. **Substantiate or soften the "unaccounted by theory" claim** (line 106). Identify what specific theoretical predictions the observed structure contradicts, or reframe the observation as a novel empirical characterization.

## Score and Decision

This paper presents genuine empirical contributions — the geometric characterization of DDIM latents and the demonstration that the noise–image mapping stabilizes very early in training — that are novel and supported by experiments across diverse models. The main weakness is the lack of statistical rigor (no error bars or confidence intervals on any quantitative claim), which reduces confidence in the precision of the reported values. The paper would benefit from additional analyses (alternative metrics for L2 assignment, analysis of LDM differences) but these are addressable and do not invalidate the core findings.

Given the paper's real contributions and the absence of fatal flaws, but with significant room for improvement in rigor and completeness:
