## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of modelling protein structure ensembles in internal coordinates (dihedrals and bond angles). The key idea is to induce a full covariance structure over internal degrees of freedom by imposing constraints on atom fluctuations in 3D space using the Lagrange multiplier formalism. Rather than estimating a full covariance matrix (quadratic in the number of atoms), the method needs only to predict one Lagrange multiplier per atom, from which the full precision matrix is constructed. The approach is demonstrated in a VAE for both unimodal (small fluctuations, low data) and multimodal (larger conformational changes, high data) settings, with the strongest results on the former.

## Strengths

- **Principled derivation of structured covariance from first principles:** The paper derives a precision matrix of the form \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}_{\text{prior}} + 2\sum_m \lambda_m \boldsymbol{G}_m\) from maximum-entropy constraints on Cartesian atom fluctuations (Section 3.1–3.3). This provides a principled parametric form that incorporates global structural information, unlike ad-hoc covariance parameterizations.

- **Dramatic reduction in parameter count:** The method reduces the number of predicted parameters from \((2M-5)^2\) (full covariance over internal coordinates) to \(M\) Lagrange multipliers (one per backbone atom). This makes full-covariance internal-coordinate modelling tractable for full protein backbones.

- **Compelling unimodal low-data results:** For the unimodal setting (25–400 data points), the VAE produces atom-position variance profiles along the chain that closely match MD/NMR reference, while all baselines (fixed κ-prior, learned diagonal, standard estimator, flow-based model) exhibit severely inflated or mispatterned fluctuations, especially in loop regions (Figure 3, 1pga/1unc/1fsd). This is a genuine advance for settings where data is scarce (e.g., NMR ensembles).

- **Rotational invariance without equivariant architectures:** Because the method operates in internal coordinates and the constraint formulation uses squared atom displacements, the model is SE(3)-invariant by construction, avoiding the need for pose alignment or specialized equivariant layers.

- **Honest acknowledgment of limitations:** The paper transparently notes that the flow-based baseline outperforms the VAE in the multimodal setting, and identifies the first-order approximation and the proof-of-concept nature of the VAE implementation as limitations (Sections 4.3, 6).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **First-order approximation error is not quantified for the multimodal regime.** The covariance derivation (Eqs. 7–9) relies on a linearized Taylor expansion \(\Delta \boldsymbol{x}_m \approx \sum (\partial \boldsymbol{x}_m / \partial \kappa_i) \Delta \kappa_i\). For the multimodal test cases (cln025, 2f4k), which involve folding/unfolding transitions with dihedral changes of tens of degrees, this approximation becomes quantitatively unreliable. The paper adds an auxiliary loss to keep \(\lambda\) in a valid range (line 140), but does not measure the approximation error \(\|\Delta x_m - \sum (\partial x_m/\partial \kappa_i)\Delta \kappa_i\|\) or analyze how it propagates into the covariance quality. Since the parametric form of the precision matrix is derived from this approximation, its domain of validity for large displacements is unclear, which is a gap for the multimodal experiments.

- **Constraint verification is indirect.** The paper frames the method as "imposing constraints" with target variances \(C_m\), but in practice the Lagrange multipliers \(\lambda_m\) are learned by the VAE and the \(C_m\) values are the trace values implied by the learned \(\lambda\) (Eq. 10), not fixed pre-specified targets. The paper provides a correlation analysis (appendix, \cref{compare_constraints}) between predicted \(C\) and true atom variance, which is correlational evidence. However, the paper would be strengthened by directly verifying that the constraints are satisfied in the sense intended: demonstrating that the sampled structures have atom-position variances close to explicitly chosen target values. The constraint derivation is still meaningful as a principled route to the specific parametric form of the covariance, but the "constraint enforcement" framing is somewhat aspirational relative to the actual implementation.

### Minor

- **Multimodal experiments serve only as a proof-of-concept.** The VAE underperforms the flow-based baseline (Köhler et al.) in the multimodal setting, as the paper acknowledges. While the benefit of the induced covariance over the fixed κ-prior is visible for cln025, it is less clear for 2f4k. The central claim that the induced covariance structure improves density quality in the multimodal regime would benefit from either (a) an ablation within a more expressive model (e.g., replacing the diagonal covariance in a diffusion model with the induced covariance), or (b) evidence that the covariance parameterization itself—not simply the VAE's latent space—provides a measurable advantage. Currently, the multimodal results support "the VAE can capture multiple modes via its latent space" more than "the constraint-based covariance improves multimodal sampling."

- **Experiment scale is modest.** The largest protein tested (1pga, 56 residues / 168 backbone atoms) is a reasonable step beyond the 35-residue limit of the flow baseline (Köhler et al.), but it is not large by current structural biology standards. The claim of scaling "to full protein backbones" is accurate in the sense that all backbone atoms are modelled (not Cα-only or fragments), but the empirical demonstration is on relatively small proteins.

- **Quantitative metrics are limited to second moments.** The unimodal evaluation relies on atom-position variance and Ramachandran plots. Distributional distances (e.g., KL divergence, Wasserstein distance) between sampled and reference ensembles are not reported, which would provide a more direct measure of density quality.

### Trivial
- The paper does not specify whether the flow baseline (Köhler et al.) also uses internal coordinates; this is clarified only indirectly in the Related Work section (line 208) where the method is described as "using internal coordinate inputs." A brief clarification in Section 4.2 would help.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A direct verification experiment: fix target \(C_m\) values to known atom variances from MD, solve for \(\lambda\) numerically (e.g., via gradient-based optimization), and confirm that samples from the resulting Gaussian achieve the target variances.
- Ablation comparing the VAE's performance when the induced covariance is replaced with a learned full-rank covariance (with appropriate regularization) to isolate the benefit of the parametric form.
- Per-structure log-likelihood or ELBO on held-out test data for both settings.
- Inclusion of Ramachandran plots for all unimodal test cases in the main text (currently only 1pga is shown in the main figure).

## Removed Points

- **"The model is doing unconstrained maximum-likelihood training with a carefully structured covariance parameterization, not imposing constraints."** This mischaracterizes the paper: the constraint derivation is the principled route to the parametric form \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}_{\text{prior}} + 2\sum \lambda_m \boldsymbol{G}_m\). The paper transparently states that λ is predicted rather than solved for fixed C (line 135). The framing is not misleading—the parametric form is derived from constraints, which is the key contribution.
- **Criticism about missing Ramachandran plots for 1unc/1fsd in the main text.** The paper states "additional visualizations can be found in \cref{fig:samplespymol}" and directs to the appendix. The main text shows the most complex case (1pga) with three proteins in one figure.
- **"Circular dependence" criticism about U-Net architecture.** The decoder output → U-Net → precision → ELBO → encoder update is a standard feedforward conditional architecture, not circular. It is a well-defined computational graph.
- **Criticism that linear encoder/decoder architectures are not detailed.** The paper directs to \cref{details} in the appendix, which is standard for conference papers (the appendix was stripped by the parser).
- **"Scale to full protein backbones is overstated."** The phrase refers to modelling the full backbone representation (N, Cα, C of every residue) as opposed to Cα-only or fragments. At 56 residues (168 atoms), this is larger than the 35-residue limit of the comparable baseline, making the claim proportional.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The key insight—that a structured internal-coordinate covariance can be derived from Cartesian-space constraints and parameterized by one scalar per atom—is the paper's own contribution, and the reviews do not add new perspectives beyond validating or questioning its strength.

## Suggestions

1. **Quantify the first-order approximation error** for both unimodal and multimodal regimes (e.g., plot \(\|\Delta x_m - \sum (\partial x_m/\partial \kappa_i) \Delta \kappa_i\|\) as a function of \(\|\Delta \kappa\|\) for representative structures). This would clarify the operating regime in which the derived covariance is reliable.
2. **Add a constraint-satisfaction experiment** where \(C_m\) values are fixed to known MD variances and λ are solved numerically, to directly validate that the derived Gaussian can achieve specified atom fluctuations.
3. **Reframe the contribution as "inducing covariance structure via constraint-inspired parameterization"** rather than "enforcing constraints," to align the narrative with the actual implementation (λ learned, C implied).
4. **Demonstrate the covariance parameterization in a more expressive generative model** (e.g., replace the diagonal covariance in a diffusion model with the induced covariance) to show that the core idea provides benefit beyond a simple VAE in the multimodal regime.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a mathematically clean method for parameterizing internal-coordinate covariances via Cartesian-space constraints. The unimodal low-data results are genuinely strong and represent a practical advance for NMR ensemble modelling. The method's parameter reduction from \(O(M^2)\) to \(O(M)\) is significant. However, the paper's main weaknesses limit its impact: the first-order approximation's validity is not quantified for the multimodal regime where it is tested, the constraint framing is not fully validated, and the multimodal experiments are too weak to convincingly demonstrate that the induced covariance improves sampling in challenging settings. These are addressable in revision, and the core contribution is solid.

**Score:** 5.5 (borderline accept)

**Decision:** Weak Accept — the paper has a genuine contribution (principled covariance induction for internal-coordinate modelling, strong unimodal results) but the multimodal validation and first-order approximation analysis need strengthening before the claims are fully convincing.
