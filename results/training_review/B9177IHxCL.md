## Summary

The paper addresses an important practical problem — current LLMs for molecule generation tend to produce structurally similar outputs, but drug discovery requires diverse sets of candidates. The authors propose a two-stage fine-tuning pipeline: (1) supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to adapt an LLM to autoregressively generate multiple molecules in one sequence, followed by (2) reinforcement learning (RL) to maximize structural diversity among generated molecules. The method is self-improving (no external diverse-molecule dataset required) and is evaluated on the ChEBI-20 benchmark and property-based prompts across multiple base models (BioT5+, MolT5, DrugAssist).

## Strengths

- **Problem formulation is well-motivated and empirically grounded.** The paper shows concretely that ChatGPT-4o generates different SMILES strings that map to the *same* molecule, and that diverse beam search / contrastive beam search improve textual diversity without corresponding to molecular structural diversity (Figure 2). This goes beyond anecdotal motivation by establishing a measurable gap.

- **Self-improving pipeline avoids reliance on external diverse-molecule corpora.** Both stages train on molecules generated by the LLM itself (Section 4, "Note that both stages do not require external diverse molecular datasets and are purely based on self-improvement procedures"). This is a practical advantage over methods requiring curated diverse datasets.

- **Multi-stage RL formulation is principled and ablation-verified.** The paper explicitly identifies the credit assignment problem in generating a sequence of molecules and proposes molecule-wise RL to address it. The ablation (Table in Section 5.4) confirms that multi-stage RL substantially outperforms single-stage RL, providing causal evidence for the design choice.

- **Consistent gains across two base models and against strong decoding baselines.** The proposed method achieves higher NCircles than diverse beam search, contrastive beam search, and random sampling on both BioT5+ and MolT5 (Figure 4), demonstrating that fine-tuning for diversity is superior to decoding-only strategies.

- **Generalization to unseen properties is demonstrated.** When applied to DrugAssist, the method improves diversity not only on seen properties (HB donors, HB acceptors, Bertz complexity) but also on the held-out QED property (Figure 6), suggesting the learned diversity behavior transfers.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **BLEU-based description-filtering in ChEBI-20 experiments creates tension with diversity.** The paper evaluates whether a generated molecule "satisfies the description" using BLEU > 0.7 against a *single* ground-truth molecule (Section 5.1, Metrics, footnote). While this follows prior work (MolT5, BioT5+), it is problematic for *diverse* generation: a structurally novel but chemically valid molecule that genuinely satisfies the same description could receive low BLEU and be discarded, while a molecule sharing SMILES substrings with the reference could pass without truly matching the description. The NCircles, IntDiv, and Accepted & Unique metrics all depend on this filter. The paper does not discuss this limitation, nor does it provide a calibration study (e.g., what fraction of molecules with BLEU > 0.7 actually possess the described properties). **However**, the DrugAssist experiment (Section 5.3) uses property predictors rather than BLEU, which partially mitigates the concern, and the BLEU filter is applied symmetrically to all methods, so the relative ranking of methods is still informative.

- **The "first to explore LLMs for diverse molecular generation" claim is overstated.** Line 37 claims "We are the first to explore the use of LLMs for generating diverse molecules." While the specific formulation (fine-tuning with multi-stage RL for structural diversity) is novel, strong "first" claims are unnecessary and risk being contested. The paper's contribution stands on its own merits without this framing.

### Minor

- **Limited evidence that the model learns structural conditioning from SMILES alone.** The RL stage conditions on previously generated molecules via their SMILES/SELFIES strings in the prompt. Since SMILES similarity does not preserve structural similarity, it is unclear how the model infers "how different is the next molecule from prior ones" at the token level. The diversity reward is computed externally from fingerprints and applied post-hoc. The paper does not analyze whether the model actually learns to map SMILES patterns to structural diversity (e.g., through attention analysis or probing). While standard RL exploration-exploitation can in principle learn this mapping, the mechanism remains undersupported.

- **Single Tanimoto threshold in the hard-filtering ablation.** The SFT-vs-RL ablation (Section 5.4) uses only one Tanimoto threshold (0.65) for hard filtering. Varying this threshold could change the relative comparison, and a sensitivity analysis would strengthen the claim that RL is inherently superior.

- **Time comparison uses different GPU counts.** The time-vs-performance analysis (Section 5.4) compares the proposed method (1 GPU) against beam search (4 GPUs). While the paper acknowledges this, it would be fairer to normalize or provide an apples-to-apples comparison.

- **Subsampling of test set for LLM comparison.** The comparison with existing LLMs (Section 5.2) uses only the first 500 of 3,300 ChEBI-20 test descriptions. The paper does not justify this subsampling or show that results are representative of the full test set.

### Trivial

- The "Preliminaries" section (Section 3) is very brief and could be expanded or merged into the Method section for better readability.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Property-predictor verification for ChEBI-20 experiments.** Adding a property predictor (e.g., RDKit descriptors, Chemprop) to verify that BLEU-accepted molecules truly satisfy the described properties would significantly strengthen the results' credibility and address the major concern above.

- **Non-LLM baseline for diverse generation.** Comparison with a dedicated non-LLM diverse molecular generation method (e.g., a VAE with diversity-promoting latent sampling or a genetic algorithm) would establish where LLM-based approaches stand relative to classical methods.

- **Confidence intervals.** The main results are reported as single numbers without variance across seeds or splits. RL-based methods are known to be noisy, so error bars would increase confidence in the findings.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the most notable insight from reviewing process is the identification of a structural tension in the evaluation: the same BLEU-based filter that the paper inherits from prior work may systematically disadvantage the diverse-generation objective it aims to measure. This tension — between using a single-reference text overlap metric while optimizing for structural diversity — is not specific to this paper but would affect any method attempting diverse molecule generation on the ChEBI-20 benchmark. The paper's DrugAssist experiment (using property predictors) implicitly validates the alternative approach.

## Suggestions

1. **Acknowledge and analyze the BLEU limitation explicitly.** Add a paragraph discussing (a) that BLEU against a single reference may penalize structurally diverse but valid molecules, (b) whether this biases the evaluation for or against the proposed method, and (c) ideally, supplement ChEBI-20 results with property-predictor verification.

2. **Tone down the "first" claim.** Replace "We are the first to explore" with a more precise statement like "To our knowledge, no prior work has fine-tuned LLMs specifically for generating structurally diverse molecules."

3. **Add analysis of how the model learns structural conditioning.** Show case studies or attention maps demonstrating that the model's internal representations distinguish molecular structures, or at minimum discuss the credit assignment path more explicitly.

4. **Report results with confidence intervals** across multiple seeds for the key tables.

5. **Provide sensitivity analysis** for the Tanimoto threshold in the hard-filtering ablation.

## Score and Decision

The paper tackles a well-motivated problem with a clean, principled approach. The two-stage SFT+RL pipeline is novel for this setting, the ablation studies are informative, and the empirical results consistently favor the proposed method across multiple base models and evaluation setups. The main substantive weakness — the BLEU-based evaluation for the ChEBI-20 experiments — is a real limitation that should be addressed, but it does not invalidate the core contribution, especially since (a) the DrugAssist experiment uses proper property predictors, (b) the filter is applied symmetrically, and (c) the paper's relative comparison still supports its claims. The paper would benefit from acknowledging this limitation and adding property-predictor verification, but the contribution is solid.
