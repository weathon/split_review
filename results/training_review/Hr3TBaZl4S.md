## Summary

This paper proposes Vector-Quantized Continual Diffuser (VQ-CD), a framework for continual offline RL. It combines: (1) a Quantized Spaces Alignment (QSA) module that uses vector quantization to map tasks with different state/action spaces into a unified representation space, and (2) a Selective Weights Activation (SWA) module that uses task-specific parameter masks in a U-Net diffusion model to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The paper claims state-of-the-art performance across 15 CL settings compared to 16 baselines.

## Strengths

- **Addresses a genuine gap in CORL**: The paper correctly identifies that most prior CORL work assumes identical observation/action spaces across tasks, which is unrealistic. The problem of continual RL across tasks with *different* state and action spaces is important and underexplored.

- **Strong results on traditional CL settings (same spaces)**: In the Ant-dir (10-15-19-25) setting, VQ-CD achieves mean return 558.22±1.14, substantially outperforming the next best method CoD (478.19±15.84) and all other baselines by wide margins (Table 1). On CW10 (Figure 2), VQ-CD maintains near-Multitask performance across all 10 tasks while baselines show clear performance attenuation. This provides strong evidence that the SWA module effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting.

- **Ablation study supports the value of vector quantization for alignment**: On D4RL settings, replacing VQ with AE or VAE substantially degrades performance (Figure 4), and the feature-difference analysis (Table 2) shows VQ produces much smaller alignment variance (state difference ~8-9 vs ~42-58 for AE). This provides meaningful evidence that VQ is a more effective alignment technique than alternatives.

- **Parameter sensitivity analysis**: Figures 5-6 investigate the effects of codebook size and number of latent vectors, showing clear trends that guide practical deployment.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The central technical contribution (QSA module) is not tested in the setting it was designed for.** The paper's headline claim is that QSA "aligns the different state and action spaces of various tasks, facilitating continual training in the same space" (Line 9, Line 39). However, the main experiments for the *arbitrary* CL setting (D4RL, Figure 3) explicitly state: **"We leverage state and action padding to align the spaces"** (Figure 3 caption, Line 273). The QSA module appears only in the ablation study (Figure 4), where it is compared against AE and VAE but **never against padding**. This creates a fundamental disconnect: the paper claims QSA is the enabler for arbitrary-space CL, yet the primary experimental evidence for arbitrary-space CL comes from a padding-based variant. The ablation provides *some* evidence that VQ > AE/VAE for alignment, but it does not establish that QSA is necessary or beneficial compared to the padding actually used in the headline results. The paper needs at minimum: (a) a direct comparison of QSA vs. padding on the same D4RL settings, or (b) a clear explanation of why padding was used and a repositioning of QSA's role.

2. **The training procedure for Selective Weights Activation is ambiguous and potentially contradictory.** Section 4.2 describes forward propagation with masked weights (Equation 4), then presents *two* approaches for weight updates: (1) using a sparse optimizer, or (2) "extracting and assembling the corresponding weights at the end of the training rather than updating the corresponding weights during training" (Lines 175-178). It is unclear which method is actually implemented. Method 2 is particularly problematic: if weights are *not* selectively updated during training but only extracted at the end, the masked forward pass serves no apparent purpose (all weights would be updated by backprop regardless), and the mechanism for preventing forgetting collapses. The paper never resolves this ambiguity, making the method irreproducible. The authors must clearly state which procedure is used and explain how it works.

### Minor

3. **The inverse dynamics model is mentioned but never described in the method section.** The abstract and preliminary (Line 97) mention an inverse dynamics model Ψ(s_t, s_{t+1}) to produce actions, but Section 4 never explains how this model is trained, how it interacts with the diffusion model, whether it also uses QSA or SWA, or how it is integrated into the overall pipeline. This is a missing component central to the pipeline.

4. **Mask generation strategy lacks justification and analysis.** Masks are generated by "randomly sampling unmasked positions from the remaining available mask positions" (Line 160). The paper provides no rationale for random allocation, no analysis of how many weights are allocated per task, no discussion of how mask sparsity affects performance or capacity, and no ablation on different mask allocation strategies. This makes it difficult to assess scalability as the number of tasks grows.

5. **Codebook design description is ambiguous.** The paper states "the quantized spaces of different tasks are separated so that we can expediently train new task-related encoders, decoders, and quantized vectors" (Line 142). If codebook entries are task-specific rather than shared, the features from different tasks may not actually be in a common embedding space, undermining the "alignment" claim. The paper should clarify whether the codebook is shared or task-specific, and if task-specific, how alignment is achieved.

6. **Incomplete presentation of claimed experimental scope.** The paper claims evaluation on "15 CL task settings" against "16 baselines," but the main paper explicitly shows only a subset: Table 1 (9 baselines, 1 Ant-dir setting), Figure 2 (CW10), and Figure 3 (4 D4RL settings with padding). The D4RL results (Figure 3) also lack error bars, making it impossible to assess statistical significance. While some of these results may be deferred to the appendix (stripped by the parser), the main paper should be self-contained enough to support the core claims.

### Trivial

7. The D4RL ablation results (Figure 4) and main results (Figure 3) use different alignment methods (VQ vs. padding) without explicit cross-reference or explanation of the relationship between the two figures, which could confuse readers.
