## Summary
DriveGPT4 presents a multimodal LLM that unifies interpretable question-answering (descriptions, justifications, free-form queries) with open-loop control signal prediction (speed, turning angle) for autonomous driving. The authors build a 56K visual instruction-tuning dataset (16K BDD-X QAs + 40K ChatGPT-generated conversations), use a two-stage mix-finetuning strategy, and demonstrate quantitative improvements over the previous SOTA (ADAPT) across text generation and control tasks on BDD-X.

## Strengths
- **Novel integration of interpretable QA and control prediction in a single LLM-based system.** Prior work either generates text alone (ADAPT) or predicts controls without interpretability (Hu et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023). DriveGPT4 is, to the best of my knowledge, the first system that handles both in a unified LLM architecture, which is a practically appealing contribution.
- **Creation of a 56K instruction-tuning dataset for autonomous driving.** The dataset (16K BDD-X QAs + 40K ChatGPT-generated conversations using privileged information) fills a genuine gap — no prior visual instruction-following dataset existed for autonomous driving. The ablation study (Tab. 6) confirms both components contribute positively.
- **Clear and consistent quantitative improvements over the relevant baseline.** DriveGPT4 outperforms ADAPT on all BDD-X metrics: full-text CIDEr (99.10 vs. 85.38), speed RMSE (1.30 vs. 3.02), turning angle RMSE (8.98 vs. 11.98), and shows strong gains on the harder scene splits. The advantage is systematic across description, justification, and full-text generation (Tab. 3, 4, split results in Tab. 2).
- **Mix-finetuning strategy is validated by ablation.** Removing mix-finetuning causes CIDEr on BDD-X questions to drop from 99.10 to 76.51 and speed RMSE to rise from 1.30 to 4.67 (Tab. 6), convincingly demonstrating the strategy's necessity.
- **Zero-shot generalization to unseen domains** (NuScenes, GTA video game scenes) is shown qualitatively, suggesting the model learns transferable driving reasoning beyond its training distribution.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **GPT4-V comparison claim is unsupported.** The abstract states DriveGPT4 yields "close or even improved results in terms of autonomous driving grounding when contrasted with GPT4-V," but the only evidence is a single qualitative example (Fig. 6). No quantitative comparison (e.g., running GPT4-V on a subset of BDD-X and computing standard metrics) is provided, making this claim unsubstantiated. This overreach should be corrected.
- **Synthetic training data quality is unvalidated.** 40K of the 56K training samples (≈71%) are generated by ChatGPT using privileged information (YOLOv8 detections, ground-truth control signals, captions). The paper provides no analysis of hallucination rates, factual accuracy, or diversity of these generated conversations. Since these data drive the model's claimed flexibility, an audit (e.g., human rating of a random sample) is needed to ensure the training signal is not systematically flawed.
- **No human evaluation for interpretability.** The paper's central claim is interpretability, yet all evaluation is automatic (n-gram metrics and ChatGPT scoring). For a task where the output is human-facing text intended to explain vehicle behavior, human judgment of correctness, informativeness, and usefulness is the gold standard. Its absence leaves the core contribution less grounded than it should be.

### Minor
- **Test-set split into Easy/Medium/Hard is not rigorously defined.** The categories (e.g., "Lane switch," "Traffic light change") are listed but the precise criteria for assignment are not specified, and the split is highly imbalanced (1202/295/312). However, the paper also reports results on the full test set ("All" column in Tab. 2), so this is not a critical omission — it merely limits the reproducibility of the per-split analysis.
- **ChatGPT evaluation score is acknowledged as unstable but reported without variance.** The paper notes the ChatGPT score is "not stable" and reports the mean of three runs, but does not provide standard deviation or confidence intervals. This weakens the reliability of the ChatGPT-based metric in Tab. 5.
- **ChatGPT data generation prompts are not provided.** The paper states the prompt "generally follows the prompt design used in LLaVA" without giving the exact prompts or a template. While this practice is common in the MLLM literature, it hinders exact reproducibility of the data creation pipeline.
- **Control evaluation only compared to ADAPT.** ADAPT is the correct baseline for the combined interpretability+control task, which is the paper's stated scope. However, the paper could strengthen its claims by at least situating its control results (speed RMSE 1.30, angle RMSE 8.98) relative to reported numbers from pure end-to-end driving methods or a simple CNN/MLP ablation baseline trained on the same features, to clarify whether the LLM architecture helps or hurts control accuracy.

## Novel Insights
The review process surfaces one insight not foregrounded in the paper: the mix-finetuning results (Tab. 6) reveal an interesting asymmetry — removing BDD-X QAs destroys BDD-X question performance (CIDEr drops from 99.10→10.40) but removing ChatGPT QAs also substantially hurts BDD-X question performance (CIDEr drops from 99.10→95.75 without ChatGPT QAs). This suggests the ChatGPT-generated data provides more than just "flexible QA"; it may serve as a regularizer or data augmenter that improves the model's understanding of even the canonical BDD-X tasks. This interdependence between synthetic and ground-truth data is worth further study.

## Suggestions
1. **Either remove or properly support the GPT4-V claim.** The abstract's assertion of "close or even improved results" compared to GPT4-V cannot rest on a single qualitative example. Either run GPT4-V quantitatively on a BDD-X subset and report metrics, or drop the claim.
