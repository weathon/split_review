## Summary

This paper introduces Graphical Multioutput Gaussian Process (GMOGP), a framework that models output dependencies via a directed graphical prior with an attention mechanism. Unlike LMC-based models that use symmetric covariance matrices, GMOGP learns asymmetric, directed dependencies among outputs (αᵢⱼ ≠ αⱼᵢ) and scales as O(SN³) instead of O((SN)³). The framework is extended to non-Gaussian data via per-output normalizing flows (TGMOGP). Experiments on synthetic and four real datasets show consistent improvements over LMC-based baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel graphical representation of asymmetric output dependencies via attention**: The paper proposes a directed graphical model over outputs where each output's conditional distribution depends on a learned parent set via attention (Eq. 15–18). This enables asymmetric dependence (αᵢⱼ ≠ αⱼᵢ) and automatic pruning of low-weight parents, which is a genuinely new modeling approach distinct from LMC's symmetric covariance. The learned graphs on real data (Table 3) show interpretable structure corroborated by Pearson correlations.

- **Scalability through decomposed covariance and distributed learning**: By modeling each output's prior as a separate N×N kernel (Eq. 19) rather than a full SN×SN matrix, GMOGP reduces complexity from O((SN)³) to O(SN³) and enables independent inference per output. The distributed scheme (Remark 3.2) avoids block-diagonal approximations and fusion methods, which is a practical contribution for larger datasets.

- **Per-output non-Gaussian extension (TGMOGP)**: The framework applies independent normalizing flows (e.g., SAL flow) to each target node (Section 3.4), overcoming the inflexibility of single-latent-process transformations in prior TGP models. This yields measurable improvements on the non-Gaussian synthetic output (Figure 5(c), 3rd output) and on negatively-skewed SARCOS data.

- **Consistent empirical gains across datasets**: On both synthetic (Table 1, Figure 5) and four real datasets (Table 2), GMOGP and TGMOGP achieve lower RMSE and NLL than LMC-based and variational baselines, often with less wall-clock time.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The core idea is novel and the empirical results generally support the method's value despite gaps in the evaluation.

### Major

- **The theoretical foundation of the graphical model is incompletely specified, undermining the mathematical claims.** The paper states that the joint distribution "only admits directed acyclic graphs" (line 111) but provides no mechanism to enforce acyclicity. The initial parent set for each output contains all other outputs (line 119), and attention pruning can leave bidirectional edges without any topological ordering constraint. Furthermore, Proposition 3.1 derives the marginal variance as ∑α²ᵢⱼk_θⱼ + k_θᵢ, which assumes parents are independent in the prior — if parents share ancestors (e.g., both depend on a common third output), this formula misses cross-covariance terms and is incorrect. The paper never derives the proper full joint covariance over all outputs. These gaps mean the claimed graphical framework is not fully justified mathematically.

- **Missing critical baselines for the non-Gaussian extension.** The paper's TGMOGP is motivated by handling non-Gaussian data, and Section 4 discusses deep GP (Damianou & Lawrence, 2013) and transformed GP (Maroñas et al., 2021) as related approaches. Yet neither is compared against experimentally. Without these baselines, the claimed superiority of TGMOGP on non-Gaussian data (synthetic output 3, SARCOS) is unsubstantiated — the comparison is only against LMC models with Gaussian likelihoods that are not designed for non-Gaussian data.

- **Confounded timing comparisons for distributed learning.** In Table 2, the "shadowed results" for KUKA and SARCOS₂ use two distributed computing units for GMOGP-based models, while baselines are presumably run on a single machine. The reported time advantage is therefore not an apples-to-apples comparison.

- **The Pareto optimality claim is unsupported.** The paper states that the weighted sum method with equal weights "provides a sufficient condition for Pareto optimality" of the hyperparameters (Section 3.3, citing Marler & Arora, 2010). However, this sufficient condition requires the objectives to be convex, which the negative log marginal likelihood is not. Without convexity, the weighted sum yields no optimality guarantee. The multi-objective optimization framing adds nothing beyond summing per-output losses.

### Minor

- **No uncertainty quantification in experimental results.** Tables 1–2 report single RMSE/NLL values with no error bars, number of trials, or significance tests. Given randomness in data splits and optimization, the reported gains could partially reflect random variation.

- **The synthetic data evaluation uses circular validation.** Output 3 is generated as y⁽³⁾ = sinh(2·arcsinh(f₁+f₂) + ε) (Eq. 22), which is a specific instance of the SAL normalizing flow (ζ=1, ρ=2, λ=0) used by TGMOGP. This matches the transformation TGMOGP is designed to learn, weakening the generality of the synthetic evaluation.

- **Attention mechanism update procedure is underspecified.** The scoring function (Eq. 18) uses inner products of latent function values f_X^(i). The paper says observations y^(i) are substituted "at the initial learning phase" (line 168) but does not describe how attention coefficients are updated during training as latent functions are inferred. The mutual dependency between attention coefficients and latent functions is not discussed.

- **Proposition 3.1's independence assumption is not justified.** The marginal variance formula assumes parent processes are independent of each other and of ψᵢ. Since parent processes can share ancestors in the graph, this assumption may be violated, making the implied covariance structure incorrect for some configurations.

- **Insufficient baseline configuration detail.** The paper does not specify the number of latent processes Q for LMC baselines in most settings, nor report implementation details for FICM, GPRN, or CMOGP baselines, making reproduction difficult.

- **No verification that learned graphs are acyclic.** Table 3 reports attention coefficients α₂,₄ and α₃,₄ for SARCOS but does not report α₄,₂ or α₄,₃, leaving open whether cycles exist — a basic sanity check for the claimed DAG structure.

## Removed Points

The following reviewer criticisms were flagged for removal after verification against the paper:

- **"Scoring function uses inner product without definition"** — ⟨f_X^(i), f_X^(j)⟩ is the standard Euclidean inner product of N-dimensional vectors; the notation is conventional.
- **"LeakyReLU makes score sensitive to sign in uninterpretable ways"** — This is a design choice allowing gradient flow for negative values while softmax yields positive αᵢⱼ.
- **"Distributed learning and shared hyperparameters are contradictory"** — Remark 3.2 does not claim zero communication, only that certain complex adjustments (block-diagonal approximations, fusion methods) are unnecessary.
- **"Scoring function depends on training points and won't generalize"** — The inner product of function values at training points is used within a learned attention mechanism, which is standard practice.

## Suggestions

1. **Fix the theoretical foundation.** Either: (a) provide a proper derivation of the full joint distribution over all outputs under DAG constraints and enforce acyclicity via topological ordering or regularization; or (b) clarify that each output's marginal is defined independently and explain how this still constitutes a "graphical model." Correct Proposition 3.1 to account for cross-covariances when parents share ancestors, or justify the independence assumption.

2. **Add missing non-Gaussian baselines.** Compare TGMOGP against deep GP and transformed GP on non-Gaussian data to support the claim of improved handling of non-Gaussianity.

3. **Report uncertainty.** Provide error bars, confidence intervals, or multiple-run statistics for all experimental results.

4. **Disambiguate timing comparisons.** Either run all methods on identical hardware with the same number of computing units, or clearly state that shadowed results are not directly time-comparable.

5. **Tone down or properly qualify the Pareto optimality claim.** Acknowledge the convexity requirement is not met and describe the weighted sum simply as a principled aggregation of per-output losses.

6. **Verify and report acyclicity** of learned graphs. If cycles exist, explain how the model handles them.

7. **Clarify the attention update procedure** — how are αᵢⱼ updated during training once latent functions are being inferred?

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a genuinely novel modeling idea — directed graphical MOGP with attention — and demonstrates consistent empirical improvements on several benchmarks. However, the theoretical foundation has significant gaps (the joint distribution is not properly defined, the DAG requirement is acknowledged but never enforced, and a key variance formula assumes independence that may not hold). The experimental evaluation is incomplete for the paper's strongest claimed contribution (non-Gaussian handling lacks proper baselines) and has confounded timing comparisons. The Pareto optimality framing is unsupported. These issues collectively prevent the paper from being accepted in its current form. A substantially revised version addressing the theoretical gaps and providing rigorous comparisons against proper non-Gaussian baselines would be needed for reconsideration.
