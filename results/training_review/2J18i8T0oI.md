## Summary

This paper investigates mechanistic similarity between Transformer (Pythia-160M) and Mamba (130M) language models, using Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) features as architecture-agnostic primitives. The authors propose a hierarchy of variation with controlled skylines to isolate the marginal effect of architecture on feature similarity, finding that cross-architecture MPPC (mean 0.74) is close to the model-seed-variant baseline (0.76). They also conduct path-patching experiments to identify an induction circuit in Mamba that is structurally analogous to the well-known Transformer induction circuit, while revealing a nuanced "Off-by-One" motif wherein Mamba's local convolution writes token information into the SSM state at the next position.

## Strengths

- **Novel application of SAEs for cross-architecture comparison.** The paper introduces SAE features as an architecture-agnostic decomposition tool for comparing Transformers and Mambas, overcoming the limitation of neuron-level comparisons that empirically fail across architectures (neuron baseline MPPC near zero, Section 4.4). This is a principled methodological step forward for mechanistic universality research.

- **Hierarchy of variation with controlled skylines.** The paper proposes a conceptual framework distinguishing SAE variables, model variables, and architectural variables (Section 4.3, Figure 2), and operationalizes it with two skylines (model seed variant and SAE seed variant). The observation that cross-architecture MPPC (0.74) is very close to the model-seed-variant skyline (0.76), with >60% of features showing near-zero difference, provides a reasonable quantitative basis for claiming that architectural change contributes only modestly to representational divergence.

- **Discovery of the "Off-by-One" motif in Mamba induction circuits.** Through hierarchical path patching (Section 6.1–6.3), the paper identifies that Mamba's local convolution aggregates information from tokens A and B into the SSM state at position B+1, delaying token mixing by one position relative to Transformers. This is a concrete, testable architectural difference that offers insight into how SSM-based models implement in-context learning without attention heads.

- **Complexity-based interpretation of similarity metrics (Section 5).** The paper honestly investigates what MPPC actually measures, finding that correlation with GPT-4o-rated feature complexity explains much of the variation. The finding that high MPPC (>0.9) is driven by trivial token-level features while moderate MPPC (0.3–0.8) corresponds to thematically-similar complex features is insightful and responsibly reported.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No systematic null-distribution baseline for MPPC.** The paper computes maximum correlations over a large pool (~600K Mamba features × ~300K Pythia features), creating a risk that observed MPPC values are inflated by multiple comparisons. Without a proper chance-correlation baseline (e.g., permuting feature labels or computing MPPC between SAEs trained on non-overlapping data halves), the reader cannot assess whether the reported MPPC values (mean 0.74) significantly exceed chance. The skylines partially mitigate this concern (since they share the same dictionary structure), but a formal null test is needed.

- **The subtraction argument for isolating architectural effects assumes linear additivity without verification.** The paper interprets the difference between cross-architecture MPPC and model-seed-variant MPPC as the "marginal effect of architectural changes" (Section 4.4) and later claims that subtracting the latter "cancels out the effect of feature complexity" (Section 5). This assumes that the complexity-MPPC relationship is identical in both settings—an untested assumption. While both curves show a similar monotonic trend (Figure 5a), the paper does not verify that the relationship is sufficiently close for cancellation to be valid, nor does it address potential interaction effects between feature complexity and architecture.

- **Circuit analysis is narrow in scope and lacks an apples-to-apples comparison.** The path-patching experiments identify one component (layer 17 convolution output at position B+1) as critical for induction in Mamba, but this is demonstrated for only one prompt pattern ([A][B]…[A]) without reported variation in templates, token types, or n-gram patterns. Critically, the paper does not apply the same path-patching methodology to Pythia—relying instead on the known Transformer induction circuit from prior work—making the "structural analogy" claim a comparison between a new Mamba result and a literature reference rather than a controlled experiment under identical conditions.

### Minor

- **The central claim about "most features being similar" conflates different correlation regimes.** The paper's own analysis (Section 5) shows that features with MPPC > 0.9 are almost exclusively trivial token-level features (spaces, punctuation, digits), while features in the moderate range (0.3–0.8) are described as having only "thematic" similarity—a qualitative judgment not quantitatively validated. Since the distribution of MPPC values (Figure 3a) spans a wide range, the claim that "most features are similar" would benefit from a clearer accounting of what fraction of features fall into each similarity regime and whether "thematic similarity" for moderate-MPPC features is reproducible.

- **The Off-by-One motif is identified but not explained.** The paper honestly acknowledges "we are still unclear why such mechanism forms or what it is for" (Section 6.3). While honesty is commendable, this means the circuit-level comparison remains incomplete—it identifies a difference but cannot attribute it to architectural constraints versus learned preferences, nor assess whether it is universal across SSM-based architectures (e.g., RWKV is mentioned but not analyzed for this motif).

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Behavioral validation via steering.** Selecting high-MPPC feature pairs and testing whether steering (activating/deactivating) the Pythia feature produces similar behavioral changes in Mamba would directly test functional similarity beyond activation correlation.
- **Full circuit ablation in Mamba.** Patching the entire hypothesized induction path (from convolution through SSM state to logits) and verifying that the effect is not explainable by indirect paths would strengthen the circuit claim.
- **Layer-matching control.** Shuffling layer labels and showing that the diagonal structure in Figure 3c disappears would validate that depth specialization is not an artifact of the matching procedure.

If candidate count alone explained the difference, the neuron baseline's near-zero correlation would still be unexplained. The skylines provide a proper control for dictionary size.

## Novel Insights

The most valuable insight emerging from these reviews that goes beyond the paper's own contributions is the tension between the statistical fragility of MPPC as a similarity metric and the qualitative richness of the Off-by-One motif. The paper would be stronger if it reframed its contribution around the nuanced architectural difference (Off-by-One) rather than the broad universality claim, since the former is better supported by controlled experiments while the latter's statistical foundation is incomplete. The hierarchy of variation framework (Figure 2) is a genuine conceptual contribution that future work should build upon regardless of whether the specific MPPC results hold up under more rigorous statistical testing.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a null-distribution baseline.** Compute MPPC between SAE features trained on the same model but split into two non-overlapping data halves to establish chance-level MPPC. Also consider a permutation test where layer or feature labels are shuffled.

2. **Run path patching on Pythia.** Apply the same hierarchical path-patching methodology to Pythia to enable a direct apples-to-apples comparison of induction circuits under identical experimental conditions.

3. **Expand the circuit analysis.** Test the induction circuit across diverse prompt templates (e.g., varying token positions, repeated n-grams, multiple [A][B] pairs) and assess whether the Off-by-One motif generalizes to other SSM-based architectures (e.g., RWKV).

4. **Provide quantitative validation for moderate-MPPC "thematic similarity."** For features in the MPPC 0.3–0.8 range, conduct a blinded human or LLM evaluation to determine whether human raters agree that matched feature pairs share a theme.

5. **Clarify the cancellation argument.** Either provide evidence that the complexity-MPPC relationship is quantitatively similar between cross-arch and model-seed-variant settings, or reframe the subtraction as a heuristic rather than a rigorous cancelation of confounds.
