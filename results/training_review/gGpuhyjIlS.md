## Summary

The paper proposes Task-Aware Learngene (TAL), a graph-hypernetwork-based method that predicts initial model parameters for descendant networks conditioned on both the target model's computational graph (specifying architecture and scale) and a task embedding derived from the ancestry model's features. TAL extends the GHN/LoGAH line of work by introducing a task-specific layer (TSL) that applies FiLM-style conditioning to the graph encoder's representations, enabling a single hypernetwork to generate task-adapted parameters for models of varying sizes. Experiments on the Visual Domain Decathlon benchmark and three unseen tasks show that TAL-initialized models outperform both random initialization and prior GHN methods, often by substantial margins.

## Strengths

- **Novel dual-conditioning of parameter prediction on both model scale and task identity**: TAL is the first method in the learngene/GHN literature to condition parameter generation on both architecture (via the computational graph) and task (via ancestry-model-derived task embeddings). This is a concrete advance over prior learngene methods (Wang et al., Shi et al., Xia et al.) that are tied to the ancestry model's architecture and lack task awareness, and over prior GHN methods that are task-agnostic. The idea is well-motivated and the design (FiLM-style conditioning on graph features) is principled.

- **Consistent quantitative improvements over strong multi-task baselines**: While the headline 24.39% figure compares against a single-task version of LoGAH (v1), the fairer comparisons against multi-task baselines (LoGAH v3: multi-task from scratch; LoGAH v4: IN-1K pre-trained + multi-task tuned) still show TAL outperforming by 18.89% over v3 and by 0.52%–32.96% per task over v4 (Table 5). This demonstrates genuine gains from the task-conditioning mechanism beyond what multi-task training alone provides.

- **Effective generalization to unseen tasks**: Table 4 shows TAL-initialized descendant models outperform both RandInit and LoGAH v1 on three unseen datasets (Fashion MNIST, FER2013, HAM10000) after only 5 or 100 training epochs, with gains of up to 3.15% (FER2013, 6-layer ViT-Small). This supports the claim that TAL's task conditioning learns to adapt to new task characteristics rather than simply memorizing seen tasks.

- **Ablation studies isolate key components**: Table 7 shows that removing the TSL module reduces average accuracy by 12.66%, and removing ancestry model guidance (KL distillation) reduces accuracy by 6.57%. This provides direct causal evidence that both components contribute meaningfully to TAL's performance.

- **Comprehensive evaluation across diverse model sizes and datasets**: Experiments cover 14 datasets and 4 model configurations (ViT-Tiny/Small with 3, 6, 12 layers) under multiple training schedules (0, 5, 75, 100, 200 epochs), demonstrating breadth and supporting the claim of flexible-scale customization.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
None. The paper's core claims are supported by the experimental evidence. The most serious concern raised by the reviewer (unfair baseline comparison) is partially valid as a presentation issue but does not threaten the core contribution, since the fair multi-task comparisons (LoGAH v3/v4 in Table 5) still show clear advantages for TAL.

### Minor

- **The headline 24.39% result (abstract, §1, §4.2) compares against a single-task baseline (LoGAH v1, trained on ImageNet-1K only), while TAL benefits from multi-task tuning on the evaluation tasks' training distributions.** The paper is transparent about the baselines and provides fairer multi-task comparisons (LoGAH v3/v4) in §4.3 that still show substantial gains. However, the abstract and introduction feature the inflated number without caveat, creating a misleading first impression of the method's advantage. The authors should either (a) report the comparison against the multi-task baseline (v3: 18.89%) as the primary result, or (b) explicitly state in the abstract that this comparison is against a single-task version of the prior method.

- **The task embedding generation conflates two potential sources of improvement: the conditioning mechanism itself and the privileged access to aggregated task statistics via ancestry model features.** Task embeddings are generated by averaging ancestry model features over all training images of each task (§3.2). This provides TAL with a compact summary of the task's data distribution that the baselines lack. The paper does not ablate what happens with simpler task identifiers (e.g., one-hot vectors), making it unclear whether the gains come from the conditioning architecture or from this additional information channel. An ablation comparing ancestry-model-derived embeddings against simpler conditioning signals would clarify this.

- **The specification of how the task-specific layer (TSL) integrates with the transformer-based encoder is incomplete.** Equation (3) applies scaling and bias to the computational graph representation f^G, but the paper does not specify whether this conditioning is applied to node features, edge features, or a graph-level representation, nor at which points within the stack of transformer blocks the TSL operates. While the overall approach is clear enough from context (FiLM-style feature-wise affine transformation, following Perez et al., 2018), reproducibility would benefit from more precise specification.

- **The per-task performance breakdown reveals substantial variance in TAL's advantage over LoGAH v4** (Table 5: +32.96% on UCF vs. +0.52% on SVHN), with some tasks showing very modest gains. The paper does not analyze or explain this variance, which would strengthen the reader's understanding of when and why task conditioning helps most.

### Trivial

- The PCA visualization (Fig. 5) shows that task-conditioned graph encodings cluster by task, which is consistent with the method's design. The visualization is useful but provides limited additional insight beyond confirming the mechanism works as intended.

## Suggestions

4. **Analyze per-task variance**: Discuss why TAL's advantage is dramatic on some tasks (UCF: +32.96%) and marginal on others (SVHN: +0.52%), linking task characteristics to method behavior.
