## Summary

The paper introduces ARGMINN, a mechanistically interpretable neural network architecture for regulatory genomics. The architecture consists of: (1) a convolutional motif-scanner with a novel filter-overlap regularizer that enforces one-filter-one-motif mapping, and (2) a syntax-builder attention module with an explicit memory stream that reveals motif instances and their syntax in a single forward pass. The paper provides extensive empirical results on motif discovery, motif instance calling, QTL prioritization, robustness, and interpretable sequence generation across multiple simulated and experimental datasets.

## Strengths

- **Novel filter-overlap regularizer enforces one-filter-one-motif mapping (Section 3.1, Eq. 1).** The regularizer penalizes different filters from activating on overlapping sequence positions, directly yielding filters that encode non-redundant motifs. This is empirically validated in Figure 2: ARGMINN misses the fewest relevant motifs and discovers the fewest redundant/extraneous motifs compared to standard CNN filter interpretation, ExplaiNN, and MoDISco. The regularizer is also shown to be transferable to standard CNNs (Supplementary Figure S5), making it a general contribution beyond ARGMINN.

- **Syntax-builder attention with explicit memory stream enables direct motif instance and syntax discovery (Section 3.2, Eq. 2).** Each attention layer derives keys/values directly from the original motif-scanner activations, so attention scores trace back to specific motif instances. This is validated by recovering the two-half REST binding syntax (Figure 3b) and by quantitative motif-instance calling that outperforms FIMO even when FIMO uses ARGMINN's own motifs (Figure 3c). This eliminates the need for expensive post-hoc pipelines.

- **Strong empirical results across multiple benchmarks.** ARGMINN consistently outperforms baselines on motif discovery (Figure 2), motif instance calling (Figure 3c), QTL prioritization (Figure 4a), and is robust to GC-content variation (Supplementary Figure S2a). The comparisons include strong baselines (standard CNN, ExplaiNN, MoDISco) across both simulated and real experimental datasets.

- **Interpretable sequence generation validated by multiple independent oracles (Section 4.5).** ARGMINN's learned motifs and syntax are used to construct novel sequences that are validated by three independently trained models (Borzoi and two others), not just the model that generated them. Generated sequences are significantly more accessible than shuffled backgrounds (p=1.78×10⁻³²) and comparable to natural sequences (p=0.75).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Theoretical results are claimed without rigorous proof (Section 5).** Theorem 1 (ARGMINN can recognize any first-order-logic-definable motif configuration) and Corollary 1 (ExplaiNN cannot) are presented as formal contributions, but the "proof" consists of a brief intuitive sketch (~5 lines). There is no formal definition of the first-order logic language, no construction of the ARGMINN classifier for arbitrary FOL sentences, no argument about required depth/width, and no proof of the negative result for ExplaiNN. The paper's abstract and introduction highlight "theoretical evidence of our architecture's full expressivity" as a central contribution. The experimental follow-up (REST binding accuracy 90.9% vs. 73.4%) is a single data point that does not substitute for a mathematical proof. The authors should either provide a proper proof or reframe these as conjectures/empirical demonstrations. **Why it matters:** These are labeled as theorems and used to claim superiority over previous architectures; without proof, the theoretical claims are unsubstantiated.

2. **Adversarial robustness evaluation is ad-hoc and unsystematic (Section 4.4).** The paper claims ARGMINN "remains robust" against adversarial attacks, but the evaluation uses custom-constructed sequences (substrings that activate/deactivate filters) rather than any standard adversarial framework (e.g., gradient-based optimization of sequence edits, evolutionary attack, or PGD on a differentiable surrogate). No quantitative comparison is provided showing ARGMINN's accuracy under the same attack — only the CNN's accuracy drops (88%→55%/48%) are reported. **Why it matters:** The robustness claim as presented cannot be assessed relative to baselines or standard attacks.

### Minor

1. **Filter-overlap regularizer uses argmax without discussing differentiability (Section 3.1, Eq. 1).** The loss function $\mathcal{L}_o$ depends on $\text{argmax}\{A_i\}$ to identify the maximally activated filter at each position. The paper does not mention how gradients are backpropagated through this operation — whether a soft relaxation (e.g., softmax with temperature), straight-through estimator, or Gumbel-softmax is used. This is a documentation gap; standard relaxations exist and the experimental results suggest a working implementation, but the mechanism should be clarified for reproducibility.

2. **Key experimental details are deferred to supplementary material without sufficient specification in the main text.** (a) The motif similarity metric used for matching discovered motifs to ground truth (Tomtom p-value? Pearson correlation? IC-weighted?) is not named. (b) The criteria for "redundant" and "extraneous" motifs are not operationalized with thresholds. (c) The nature of the ground-truth binding footprints (Vierstra 2020) — whether DNase footprints, ChIP-exo, etc. — is not described. The paper would benefit from specifying these in the main text or at minimum in a table.

3. **The first attention layer's query is input-independent (Section 3.2, Eq. 2).** Because $m_0$ is initialized as a vector of ones, $q_1 = W_{Q,1} \cdot \mathbf{1}$ is learned but identical across all inputs. The attention scores $a_1 = K_1 q_1$ are therefore a learned linear function of the keys only (input-dependent through keys, but the query itself is fixed). While multi-head attention and subsequent layers mitigate this limitation, the design choice and its implications are not discussed.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Wet-lab validation (e.g., MPRA, luciferase assay) of generated sequences would strengthen the "interpretable generation" claims, but this is beyond what is standard for a computational methods paper and does not detract from the current contribution.

- Applying the filter-overlap regularizer to a standard CNN with full quantitative motif discovery metrics (not just qualitative Supplementary Figure S5) would strengthen the claim that the regularizer is a general-purpose contribution.
