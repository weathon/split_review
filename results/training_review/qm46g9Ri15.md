## Summary

AlignCLIP is a CLIP fine-tuning method targeting two specific misalignment problems in open-world generalization: (1) attention misalignment (background bias in multi-head attention) and (2) predictive category misalignment (semantically incoherent misclassifications). The method introduces an Attention Alignment Loss (AAL) that uses a Siamese architecture with a D-V structure (replacing softmax(QK^T) with a diagonal matrix) to produce a cleaner similarity distribution as an alignment target, plus a Semantic Label Smoothing (SLS) loss that replaces uniform label smoothing with a class-similarity matrix derived from CLIP's text encoder. The paper evaluates across three open-world settings (domain shift, unseen classes, and both combined) and reports consistent improvements over baselines like CLIPood, CoOp, and CoCoOp.

---

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem decomposition.** The paper identifies two concrete, under-studied misalignment problems (attention skew toward background, semantically incoherent prediction errors) that are grounded in prior CLIP interpretability work (Li et al., 2023; Shu et al., 2023). Figure 1 provides a clear visual motivation, and the diagnosis that standard full fine-tuning cannot fix these issues is supported by prior findings.

- **Semantic Label Smoothing is a clean, well-specified contribution.** Using the pre-trained text encoder's class similarity matrix to soften labels (replacing the uniform distribution in standard LS) is intuitively motivated, straightforward to implement, and requires no architectural changes at inference. The ablation (Figure 3a) confirms it contributes positively, and the qualitative example (Figure 4, cat→dog→computer) illustrates the desired behavior.

- **Synergistic effect of AAL + SLS is demonstrated.** The ablation study (Figure 3a) shows that each component individually improves performance and that their combination yields the best results on both domain-shift and domain+unseen-class scenarios. This supports the paper's claim that the two losses address complementary problems.

- **Comprehensive evaluation scope.** The paper tests across three distinct open-world scenarios (domain shift on DomainBed + ImageNet variants, unseen classes on 11 datasets, and the combined setting on Office-Home + DomainNet). This is more thorough than many CLIP fine-tuning papers, and improvements are directionally consistent across settings.

- **Practical insight from layer-wise analysis.** Figure 3c shows that aligning only the first four layers is optimal because deeper layers have very sparse attention distributions. This is actionable for reducing computational cost and is not a trivial finding.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The D-V structure is incompletely specified, undermining reproducibility of the core AAL contribution.** The paper states that the D-V structure works by "replacing the softmax-normalized product of Q and K^T with a diagonal matrix" (line 87) but never specifies what the entries of this diagonal matrix are — are they all 1 (identity), learned, or derived from some other signal? The D-V structure is central to producing the target distribution G_{D-V} used in the AAL loss (Equation 6), yet the mechanism is left ambiguous. Without this detail, the method cannot be independently reproduced, and the claimed advantage over the V-V-V structure (CLIP Surgery) cannot be assessed. This is not a missing appendix issue; it is an absent specification of the method's own key operation.

- **Beta Moving Average is not ablated, confounding the unique contribution of AAL and SLS.** AlignCLIP incorporates a BMA strategy from Shu et al. (2023) (Algorithm 1, line 111–112) that is applied at inference. The ablation study (Figure 3a) tests AAL and SLS but never removes BMA. If BMA alone provides a meaningful portion of the observed gains, the paper's claimed contribution from AAL and SLS is overstated. This is especially important because BMA is an additional inference-time technique not standard in the compared baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, and possibly CLIPood). A controlled experiment running AlignCLIP without BMA is needed.

- **Missing variance reporting and statistical significance.** The paper states that "reported AlignCLIP results include both the average and standard deviation from three separate runs" (line 144), but the tables are embedded as figures and it cannot be verified from the text extraction whether standard deviations are actually displayed. Beyond this, no statistical significance tests (e.g., paired tests across datasets, confidence intervals) are reported for any results. Since many improvements over strong baselines are in the 1–2% range (e.g., Table 3 harmonic mean: AlignCLIP 74.81 vs. CLIPood 73.67), these margins could plausibly fall within run-to-run variance. The paper needs to demonstrate that the reported gains are statistically meaningful.

### Minor

- **Modest improvement margins relative to method complexity.** The reported gains over strong baselines (especially CLIPood) are often 1–2 percentage points on average (Table 3 harmonic mean, Table 1 DomainBed average), and even smaller on ImageNet variants (0.4% over zero-shot on unseen classes). Given that AlignCLIP adds a Siamese architecture, two additional loss terms, and BMA, the practical benefit over simpler alternatives is incremental. This does not invalidate the contribution but tempers the claim of "markedly superseding existing techniques."

- **The final loss formulation requires clarification.** The total loss is given as L = L_AAL + λL_SLS (line 126). L_SLS = (1-ε)L_CE + ε L_S (Equation 8), which already incorporates cross-entropy. This is technically correct and avoids double-counting, but the paper never explicitly states that the cross-entropy term is subsumed within L_SLS, making it easy to misread as missing. Explicitly writing L = L_AAL + λ[(1-ε)L_CE + ε L_S] would prevent confusion.

- **Baselines' experimental conditions (re-run vs. reported) are unclear.** The paper does not state which baseline numbers were re-produced under the same conditions vs. taken from published tables. This matters for fairness, especially given hyperparameter sensitivity in fine-tuning.

## Removed Points
These points were examined against the paper and removed as invalid or misdirected; readers should treat them with caution.

- **"Inconsistent definition of the Attention Alignment Loss" (Criticism 2).** The reviewer argues that aligning the "problematic" distribution G toward G_{D-V} is incoherent. This misunderstands the Siamese self-distillation setup: G is the current (misaligned) distribution from the original branch, and G_{D-V} is the target distribution from the clean D-V branch. Aligning the current output toward a cleaner target is standard practice (analogous to knowledge distillation). The paper's logic is coherent.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the diagonal matrix explicitly.** State whether it is the identity matrix, a learned diagonal, or some other construction. If identity, acknowledge this directly. If learned, describe the parameterization and training procedure.

2. **Add a BMA ablation.** Run AlignCLIP without BMA (i.e., using the final model parameters θ_T rather than θ_T^{BMA}) and report the result alongside the main tables. This isolates the contribution of AAL and SLS from the inference-time averaging.

3. **Report standard deviations or confidence intervals prominently in all tables** (including ablation figures) and, given the small margins, perform a paired significance test (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank) across datasets to demonstrate that AlignCLIP reliably outperforms CLIPood.

4. **Clarify the loss formulation** by writing the total loss as L = L_AAL + λ[(1-ε)L_CE + ε L_S] and noting explicitly that cross-entropy is included within L_SLS.

5. **State which baselines were re-run** under the same experimental conditions and which were taken from published numbers.

## Score and Decision

This paper tackles a real problem with a largely sensible two-component solution. Semantic Label Smoothing is clearly described and well-motivated. The evaluation scope is commendably broad, and the improvements are directionally consistent. However, the paper has two significant weaknesses that prevent full acceptance in its current form: (a) the D-V structure — central to the AAL contribution — is incompletely specified, making the method irreproducible at its most critical point, and (b) the BMA technique is not ablated, leaving the unique contribution of AAL and SLS unclear. Combined with missing variance reporting for small-margin results, the evidence for the claimed improvements cannot be fully evaluated without revision. A major revision addressing these issues could substantially strengthen the paper.
