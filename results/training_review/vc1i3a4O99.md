## Summary

This paper proposes a method for interpreting sparse autoencoder (SAE) features in LLMs using a fixed vocabulary and mutual information-based objective to mitigate what the authors term the "frequency bias" — the tendency of existing explanation methods (TopAct, N2G) to produce lexical-level patterns rather than discourse-level semantic explanations. The paper further introduces two runtime steering strategies (Amplification and Calibration) that leverage these explanations to modify LLM representations, and evaluates on a jailbreak defense task for Mistral-7B-Instruct.

## Strengths

- **Novel, theoretically motivated explanation objective**: The paper identifies a genuine problem — existing SAE explanation methods produce explanations dominated by frequent linguistic patterns rather than semantic content — and proposes a principled solution based on mutual information over a fixed vocabulary. The derivation in Section 3.1 connects text generation dynamics (topic model random walk) to the challenge of separating discourse topics from linguistic patterns, providing a clear rationale for the method design. This is an advance over the ad-hoc activation-maximization approaches used in prior work.

- **Quantitative and qualitative evidence of improved explanation quality**: Table 2 shows the proposed method achieves a substantially higher explanation rate (54.8% on the activated subset vs. 36.5% for TopAct and 26.9% for N2G). Table 1 provides interpretable qualitative examples where the proposed method produces concise, topical word sets (e.g., "disrupt, operation, quickly, repair, failure, vehicle, performance, fleet, maintenance, schedule") while baselines yield repetitive lexical patterns (e.g., "used to; used to; used to; used to; used to"). These examples directly support the paper's claim of producing more discourse-level explanations.

- **Downstream utility demonstrated through steering**: The paper shows that the proposed explanations enable effective runtime steering for a real downstream task (jailbreak defense). The Aware Security strategy reduces ASR from 81.6 to 72.8 on Salad-Bench with only minor helpfulness degradation (MT-Bench 6.5→6.0). Critically, Figure 3 shows that explanations from TopAct and N2G fail to achieve similar safety improvements when used with the same steering strategy, directly linking explanation quality to practical steerability.

- **Efficient and practical design**: Both steering strategies monitor only a small subset of identified features (141 for safety awareness) rather than the full 65,536, adding negligible latency (normalized time 1.0) compared to perturbation-based baselines (e.g., Random Patch at 6.8×). This makes the approach practical for real-world deployment.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper's core contributions are substantiated, albeit with limitations.

### Major

- **The frequency bias claim is theoretically motivated but not empirically validated independently.** The paper derives (Section 3.1) that SAE features should be biased toward linguistic patterns over discourse topics, but never directly tests this on the trained SAE. The only evidence is that existing explanation methods (TopAct, N2G) produce lexical explanations — but this could be a shortcoming of those *explanation methods* rather than reflecting a property of the SAE features themselves. An experiment showing that SAE feature activations correlate more strongly with frequent linguistic patterns than with discourse-relevant content would substantially strengthen the paper's motivation. Without it, the contribution of the explanation method stands on its own (it produces better explanations), but the motivating theory remains unverified.

- **The steering results are modest and the "strong jailbreak defense" claim is overstated.** The proposed AS strategy achieves 72.8 ASR, which is slightly *worse* than Self-Reminder (71.3), a simple prompting baseline. The absolute improvement over the no-defense baseline (81.6→72.8) is an 8.8 percentage point reduction. While the comparison to TopAct/N2G explanations (Figure 3) shows the proposed explanations enable steering where others fail, the overall performance claim of "strong jailbreak defense ability" (line 186) does not match the evidence relative to existing lightweight defenses.

### Minor

- **The explanation evaluation lacks human validation.** The paper follows the established protocol of using GPT-4o as an annotator (Bills et al., 2023; Rajamanoharan et al., 2024b), and uses a separate thread to avoid contamination. However, it does not report human agreement rates or any calibration of the machine annotator against human judgments. Given the format difference between baselines (text spans) and the proposed method (individual words from a curated vocabulary), there is a legitimate concern about format bias in the GPT-4o judgments. Reporting human-machine agreement on a sample of features would significantly strengthen the validity of Table 2.

- **No ablation on layer selection for steering.** The choice of layer 8 (≈24% of layers) is motivated by analogy to Lieberum et al. (2024), but no experiment shows that intervening at this layer propagates to the output or that other layers would perform differently. Given that later layers could overwrite the steering intervention, the paper would benefit from measuring the output probability change induced by steering at layer 8 versus other layers.

- **Feature selection for safety is not validated.** The paper selects 141 "Aware Security" and 48 "Erase Harmful" features out of 65,536 (0.29%) by prompting GPT-4o to classify features based on their explanations. However, there is no check showing that these features actually activate discriminatively on harmful vs. harmless inputs, nor whether steering modifies the features it targets. Computing average activation of selected features on harmful vs. harmless prompts from Salad-Bench would address this gap.

- **Hyperparameter β for AS appears to be tuned without a clear held-out validation set.** The paper states "grid search some numbers" to find β=2.5 but does not specify whether this tuning was performed on a held-out split of Salad-Bench or on the test set itself.

- **The 5000-word vocabulary size is not justified.** The paper uses the 5000 most common words from the training set but does not ablate this choice or discuss how rare but semantically important words are handled.

## Suggestions

1. **Validate the machine annotator against human judgments** on a sample of 100–200 features, reporting agreement rates (e.g., Cohen's κ). This is the single most impactful addition to address concerns about evaluation validity, and it follows the precedent set in the prior work the paper already cites.

2. **Directly test the frequency bias claim** by computing, for a sample of SAE features, the correlation between feature activation strength and the frequency of the top-activating tokens/patterns. This would empirically ground the theoretical motivation in Section 3.1.

3. **Conduct a layer ablation for steering** — compare steering at layers ~4, 8, 16, 24, and 30 to show that the choice matters and that the intervention persists through later layers. Report the change in output token probabilities to verify the mechanism.

4. **Validate feature selection for safety** by computing the average activation of the 141 AS features on harmful vs. harmless prompts. Show that they fire discriminatively, and that steering actually modifies their activation levels as intended.

5. **Use a held-out validation set for hyperparameter tuning** of β to avoid overfitting to the test set.

6. **Tone down the claim of "strong jailbreak defense ability"** — the method performs comparably to Self-Reminder (72.8 vs. 71.3 ASR) and the framing should reflect this honestly.

## Score and Decision

The paper identifies a real problem, proposes a novel and well-motivated solution, provides both qualitative and quantitative evidence of improved explanation quality, and demonstrates practical utility in a downstream steering task. The weaknesses — lack of human validation of the evaluation, modest steering results, unvalidated frequency bias claim, and absent ablations — are real but none are fatal. The core contribution (the mutual information-based explanation method) stands on its own empirical merit and is not invalidated by any single criticism. With the suggested improvements (particularly human validation of the annotator and feature selection validation), the paper would be significantly stronger.
