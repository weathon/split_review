## Summary

The paper presents DSPart, a large-scale synthetic dataset of ~100K images with part annotations across 90 object categories (50 rigid + 40 animal), generated by combining 3D part-annotated CAD models with the 3D-DST diffusion-based image synthesis pipeline. The key claim is that diffusion-generated textures and backgrounds produce more realistic synthetic training data, reducing the synthetic-to-real domain gap for part segmentation. Experiments show that training on DSPart improves performance in unsupervised domain adaptation settings over prior synthetic datasets (UDAPart, CC-SSL) by 7–12 mIoU on PartImageNet.

## Strengths

- **Scale and diversity**: DSPart provides 100K images with 24.5K 3D part annotations across 90 categories, far exceeding existing synthetic part datasets like UDAPart (vehicles only) and CC-SSL (2 animal species). The inclusion of 40 animal species via SMAL models is a notable extension over prior work with <10 species. Table 1 documents this clearly.

- **Consistent empirical gains in UDA settings**: When used as source data for unsupervised domain adaptation with DAFormer, DSPart-Rigid improves mIoU over UDAPart by 7.08 (car), 11.72 (airplane), and 11.88 (bicycle) on PartImageNet (Table 2). DSPart-Animal similarly outperforms CC-SSL in UDA settings (Tables 3–4). The gains are substantial and consistent across multiple categories and settings.

- **Ablation isolating diffusion contribution**: Table 6 (Section 4.4) ablates diffusion-generated textures and diffusion-generated backgrounds separately, providing direct evidence that both components contribute to the performance improvement. This strengthens the attribution of gains to the diffusion pipeline rather than other factors.

- **PRF filter design rationale**: The paper clearly explains why the existing KCF filter fails for animal images (trained on noisy data, uses rotation error unsuitable for articulated objects) and proposes PRF with animal-specific pose metrics (PA-MPJPE, S-MPJPE, 2D PCK). The design motivation is sound.

- **Thorough multi-setting evaluation**: The paper evaluates under synthetic-only, UDA, and sequential real+synthetic settings, on two real benchmarks (PartImageNet, PascalPart), providing a comprehensive picture of where the dataset helps.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The core claim — diffusion-generated textures reduce domain gap — is confounded by uncontrolled dataset size/diversity differences.**  
   The paper attributes UDA improvements (+7–12 mIoU in Table 2) to the realism of diffusion-synthesized images, but does not control for dataset size. DSPart-Rigid contains ~48K images. The size of UDAPart is not stated, but given that UDAPart is a specialized vehicle dataset from Liu et al. (2022), it may be substantially smaller. The paper uses "iterations rather than epochs for fairer comparison" (Section 4.1), but this controls for the number of gradient steps, not for the number of unique training samples or the diversity of viewpoints/shapes. A simple ablation — subsampling DSPart-Rigid to match UDAPart's image count and re-running the UDA experiment — would cleanly separate the effect of dataset scale from the effect of diffusion realism. Without it, the central contribution claim is not convincingly demonstrated.

2. **PRF filter's quantitative effectiveness is not established relative to alternatives.**  
   While the paper describes PRF's design and states that Section 4.3 demonstrates its effectiveness (lines 85, 92), the parsed paper content for Section 4.3 is missing (likely stripped by parser). Based on what is available, the paper does not report: (a) how many of the 39K images PRF retains vs. rejects, (b) a direct comparison of downstream part segmentation performance when training on KCF-filtered vs. PRF-filtered animal images, or (c) agreement between PRF and the human-filtered seed set on a held-out validation partition. Since PRF is listed as a contribution, its effectiveness needs quantitative validation against a baseline.

### Minor

1. **Part definition mapping between DSPart and evaluation datasets is underspecified.**  
   The paper states that PartImageNet's "category and class mapping aligns most closely with DSPart among existing real part datasets" (line 99), but provides no detailed mapping of part taxonomies. DSPart defines parts per super-category (car, airplane, bicycle, boat, tool) with specific templates, while PartImageNet has its own part taxonomy. If the mapping is inexact, reported mIoU differences could partly reflect annotation mismatch rather than domain gap reduction. A table of part correspondences for one or two super-categories would suffice.

2. **Claim about "physically realistic interactions with the ground plane" is stated but not validated.**  
   The abstract claims that "prior knowledge of diffusion models enables the object to exhibit more physically realistic interactions with the ground plane and other spatial contexts" (line 4). However, no experiment compares diffusion-generated images against rendered images with real backgrounds to isolate this effect. The ablation in Table 6 separates textures from backgrounds, but the text describing this is truncated, and no explicit ground-plane analysis is provided.

3. **No quantitative annotation quality metrics for the 3D part annotation process.**  
   The annotation pipeline (Section 3.1) describes two iterations of inspection but reports no inter-annotator agreement scores, number of annotators, or per-category annotation time. While the process sounds reasonable, the absence of quality metrics makes it hard to assess potential annotation noise that could affect downstream results.

## Novel Insights

The reviewers converge on an important tension: the paper's main evidence for the value of diffusion-generated realism comes from comparing DSPart against older rendering-based datasets, but this comparison is confounded by differences in dataset scale. This is the single most impactful issue. A deeper insight emerges from the paper's own data: in the Syn-only setting, DSPart-Rigid performs comparably to UDAPart, and the paper attributes this to residual noisy samples. The improvement only manifests in UDA, where pseudo-labeling mitigates noise. This suggests that diffusion-generated textures may be more valuable for learning transferable features than for learning high-quality features in-domain — a distinction the paper does not explicitly draw, but that could guide future synthetic data design.

## Suggestions

1. **Run a controlled experiment that matches dataset sizes.** Subsample DSPart-Rigid to the same image count as UDAPart and repeat the UDA evaluation. If the mIoU advantage persists at matched size, the core claim is firmly supported. If it shrinks substantially, the contribution needs reframing around dataset scale rather than realism.

2. **Provide quantitative PRF validation.** Report the retention rate of PRF on the 39K images and compare downstream part segmentation performance when training on KCF-filtered vs. PRF-filtered images (vs. the 13K human-filtered seed set as a gold standard).

3. **Document the part taxonomy mapping** between DSPart super-categories and PartImageNet part definitions for at least one rigid and one animal category.

4. **Add failure case analysis** — show examples where DSPart images still produce incorrect part predictions and discuss limitations of the diffusion pipeline for particular categories or poses.
