## Summary

This paper introduces Ego-Foresight (EF), a self-supervised method for disentangling agent from environment in visual RL by predicting future frames from proprioceptive sequences. The insight is that movement provides a cue: content predictable from the agent's own motor commands can be identified as "self." The method integrates a reconstruction-based video prediction model with DDPG (following Denton & Birodkar's similarity loss for scene representations) and evaluates on DMC, Distracting DMC, and Meta-world. The key claimed advantage is removing the need for supervisory segmentation masks (e.g., SEAR) while maintaining or improving sample efficiency and final performance.

## Strengths

- **Self-supervised disentanglement without segmentation masks**: Unlike SEAR (Gmelin et al., 2023), which requires agent masks obtained from simulation IDs or trained segmentation models, EF learns to separate agent from environment using only proprioceptive states (joint angles, end-effector positions) available on any robot. This is a genuine practical advantage for real-world deployment. The Distracting DMC results (Figure 5) provide indirect evidence that the model does not collapse on background distractors.

- **Clear win on high-DOF embodiment (Quadruped)**: On the DMC Quadruped task (16 DOF), EF outperforms all four baselines (DrQ-v2, SEAR, Dreamer-v3, TD-MPC2) in both convergence speed and asymptotic reward (Figure 4). This result directly supports the claim that self-prediction of the agent's body is particularly beneficial for controlling complex morphologies, and it holds even counting the 50k babbling steps against EF's sample budget.

- **Practical scope of applicability**: Across 16 tasks total (4 DMC, 2 Distracting DMC, 10 Meta-world), EF achieves competitive results (best or matching in ~9/16) while requiring no task-specific supervision or segmentation masks. The use of a single set of hyperparameters across all tasks (noted in Section 4.3) further supports robustness.

- **Qualitative evidence of tool integration**: The prediction visualizations in Figure 7 show that EF correctly predicts the hammer's motion as part of the agent after grasping, while treating a static door as environment. This illustrates a body-schema adaptation capability absent from supervised disentanglement methods.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Motor babbling confounds sample-efficiency comparisons**: The 50k-step motor babbling stage (Section 3.3) uses random actions and counts toward EF's total environment steps. All curves in Figures 4–6 include these steps, yet baselines (DrQ-v2, SEAR, Dreamer-v3, TD-MPC2) begin task-directed learning from step 0. This means EF's sample efficiency is measured against an inflated budget: tasks solvable within 50k steps (e.g., Cartpole) show EF performing worse during babbling, then catching up afterward. The paper acknowledges this ("the first 50k steps of the Cartpole task perform worse than the baselines") but does not provide a controlled comparison (e.g., giving all baselines the same 50k random steps) or replot curves excluding the babbling phase. Without this, claims of improved sample efficiency are confounded. *Note: on Quadruped, EF outperforms baselines despite this overhead, making that specific result credible.*

- **SEAR baseline reproduction failure invalidates SEAR comparisons**: The paper states (Section 4.1): "For SEAR, despite using the publicly available code, we failed to match the results reported in the original paper. We note however, some inconsistencies in the original plots such as success rates above 1." Since SEAR is identified as "the most direct baseline" and "an oracle baseline when compared to ours," the inability to reproduce it means every comparison against SEAR in Figures 4 and 6 is against an unvalidated implementation. The paper cannot determine whether EF matches, exceeds, or underperforms the actual SEAR method, which directly undermines the experimental core of the paper. The authors should either resolve the reproduction discrepancy (through author correspondence or hyperparameter search) or remove SEAR from the comparisons.

- **No direct validation of agent-environment disentanglement**: The core methodological claim is that EF achieves "motion-based agent-environment disentanglement" (Section 3.2). Yet no experiment quantitatively measures this. There is no evaluation of: (a) whether the scene representation $h_s$ is actually invariant to agent pose or episode-specific variation, (b) prediction accuracy for agent pixels vs. environment pixels separately, or (c) correlation between predicted frames and ground-truth agent masks (available in simulation). Without such validation, the RL improvements could plausibly come from the extra loss terms, the LSTM proprioceptive encoder acting as an augmented feature extractor, or the random exploration from babbling—rather than from genuine disentanglement. The qualitative results (Figure 7) and Distracting DMC results are suggestive but not sufficient to verify the claimed mechanism.

### Minor

- **Ablation study does not isolate the core contribution**: The ablation in Section 4.4 varies prediction horizon $H$ and similarity weight $\alpha$. It does not test: (a) EF without the reconstruction loss (feeding raw encoder features into RL without the prediction objective), (b) DrQ-v2 with the same 50k babbling steps to control for extra exploration, or (c) whether the LSTM proprioceptive encoder alone (without frame prediction) provides the benefit. Without these, the contribution of the specific Ego-Foresight prediction objective cannot be isolated from the general benefits of random exploration or multi-task feature learning. The partial $\alpha=0$ ablation (Figure 9, 2 tasks) is a step in the right direction but insufficient.

- **Unequal number of random seeds across baselines and experiments**: The paper states (Section 4.1) "due to time constraints, not all baselines and experiments use the same number of random seeds." This reduces the reliability of pairwise comparisons, especially when EF shows higher variance attributed to fewer seeds. The paper should provide a clear table of seeds per condition.

- **Dreamer-v3 and TD-MPC2 results not visually compared**: The paper mentions results for Dreamer-v3 and TD-MPC2 are "provided in tables, available at each project's webpage" rather than being plotted alongside EF, DrQ-v2, and SEAR in Figures 4–6. Given that Dreamer-v3 and TD-MPC2 are state-of-the-art model-based methods, their omission from the main comparison figures weakens the paper's empirical support.

### Trivial

- The paper's claim that the method is "self-supervised" is technically correct—the prediction target (future frame) is derived from the data itself, and proprioception is an input modality, not a label—but a brief clarification that "self-supervised" here means no manual labels (as opposed to using no sensory data beyond pixels) would prevent confusion.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Quantitative disentanglement metrics**: Measuring mutual information between $h_s$ and agent pose, or per-pixel prediction error for agent vs. background, would directly validate the claimed mechanism.
- **DrQ-v2 + babbling control**: Running DrQ-v2 with the same 50k random steps would cleanly separate the benefit of the prediction loss from the benefit of extra exploration.
- **Adaptive babbling termination**: The paper mentions this as future work; implementing it would strengthen the method's practicality.
- **Statistical significance testing**: Reporting whether performance differences are significant given the number of seeds would strengthen the claims.

## Suggestions

1. **Provide a controlled babbling comparison**: Run DrQ-v2 and the authors' best-effort SEAR with the same 50k random babbling steps, then plot sample-efficiency curves from the end of babbling onward. This would resolve the primary experimental confound.
2. **Resolve or remove SEAR comparisons**: Either contact the SEAR authors to resolve the reproduction discrepancy, or drop SEAR from comparisons and reposition EF relative to DrQ-v2 (which was successfully reproduced).
3. **Add a direct disentanglement experiment**: In simulation, ground-truth agent masks are available at no cost. Measure prediction MSE for agent vs. background pixels separately, or compute the correlation between predicted frames and ground-truth masks. This would directly support the claimed mechanism.
4. **Add an ablation of the reconstruction loss**: Remove $\mathcal{L}_{rec}$ while keeping the scene and proprioception encoders feeding into the RL agent. If performance drops significantly, the prediction objective is driving improvement; if not, the benefit comes from other components (babbling, LSTM encoder, extra parameters).
5. **Standardize random seeds**: Report results for each condition with at least 5 seeds to improve reliability and enable meaningful variance comparisons.
