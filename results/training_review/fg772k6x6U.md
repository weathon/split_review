## Summary

This paper proposes CariNet, a deepfake detection framework with two main contributions: (1) a semi-supervised Artifact Attention module trained on human annotations that improves classifier performance, and (2) a Caricature Generation module that amplifies artifacts in deepfake videos to create "Deepfake Caricatures" — visually exaggerated versions that make deepfakes more detectable by humans. The classifier achieves competitive or state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks, and a user study shows caricatures improve human detection by 14–43 percentage points across presentation times from 300ms to 5 seconds.

## Strengths

- **Caricatures dramatically improve human deepfake detection in a well-designed user study.** Figure 3 (top) shows detection gains of 14–43 percentage points over standard deepfakes across all presentation times (300ms to 5s), all significant at Bonferroni-corrected α=0.0083. The improvement is substantial and holds even at very brief exposures, directly supporting the paper's core claim that amplifying artifacts aids human detection.

- **The Artifact Attention module trained on human annotations is a creative and well-motivated approach.** The paper collects over 11K human annotations across 1,000 videos and uses them to supervise attention map generation. The ablation study (Table 5) cleanly demonstrates that the full CariNet18 (86.04%) outperforms the version without modulation (83.72%) and without any attention mechanism (80.78%), confirming that human-guided attention contributes to classifier performance.

- **Strong cross-dataset generalization.** CariNet34 achieves the highest overall AUC (87.4%) across four unseen benchmarks (CelebDFv2, DFDCp, FaceShifter, DeeperForensics), outperforming all baselines including LipForensics (86.8%). On DFDCp and DFo, the gains are 4+ points over prior methods. The model also shows competitive cross-forgery detection (93.8% on FaceSwap, best among compared methods).

- **Robustness to unseen perturbations is competitive or state-of-the-art.** CariNet34 achieves the best AUC on Contrast (99.8%) and Pixelation (96.3%), and ties LipForensics on Noise (74.5%), suggesting the human-guided attention framework helps maintain performance under distribution shift.

- **Ecologically valid user study design.** The inclusion of multiple presentation times (300ms to 5s) and engagement probes provides practical insight into how caricatures would perform under realistic viewing conditions, including distracted or inattentive users.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The human experiment does not report false-positive rates for real videos, leaving a critical gap for a decision aid.** The study includes 200 real and 200 fake videos, but analysis focuses entirely on "detectability of fake videos." The paper does not clarify whether caricatures were shown for real videos. If caricatures are only shown for fake videos, participants could learn this mapping, inflating detection accuracy. If caricatures are shown for all videos, false-alarm rates (real videos incorrectly called fake) are essential but unreported. For any decision aid, not measuring whether it increases false alarms is a significant omission — the aid could worsen overall decision accuracy even if it improves fake detection.

- **The contribution of attention map guidance in caricature generation is not validated.** The Caricature module is trained on synthetic motion magnification data *without* attention maps; attention maps are only applied as a multiplicative modulation at inference. The paper does not ablate this guidance — there is no comparison of caricatures generated with learned attention maps vs. uniform maps vs. simple motion magnification without any modulation. The human study only compares standard deepfakes vs. full-pipeline caricatures. It is therefore unclear whether the artifact attention maps are necessary for the observed human improvement, or whether generic motion amplification would suffice. This undermines the claimed mechanism of artifact-specific amplification.

### Minor

- **No direct comparison to existing visual indicators (text warnings, heatmaps).** The paper motivates caricatures as an alternative to text-based prompts and heatmaps (citing prior work showing low compliance), but the human experiment only compares caricatures to *no indicator*. While the 40% improvement over no aid is meaningful, the paper does not test whether caricatures outperform or even match existing signaling methods. The central framing positions caricatures as a superior visual indicator, but this claim is not directly supported by the experiments.

- **Framing of the "more than 5%" improvement from human supervision inflates the contribution.** The ablation table shows that the full CariNet18 (86.04%) improves over the model without any attention mechanism (80.78%) by 5.26%. However, isolating the contribution of human-guided *modulation* specifically (full vs. w/o modulation: 86.04 vs. 83.72) yields ~2.3%. The paper's claim in the abstract selects the most generous baseline that conflates the addition of self-attention architecture with the human-guided modulation, making the benefit of human supervision appear larger than the controlled comparison suggests.

- **Classifier gains over prior state of the art are modest in several settings.** CariNet34's overall AUC of 87.4% edges LipForensics' 86.8% by 0.6%, and CariNet34 trails LipForensics on FaceShifter (93.9% vs. 97.1%). Cross-forgery average AUC (99.0%) is slightly below LipForensics (99.5%). The human-guided attention framework is competitive but the margin of improvement is not large across all benchmarks.

## Suggestions

1. **Report false-alarm rates from the existing human experiment.** Since real videos were already collected (200 in the stimulus set), re-analyzing the data to report how often participants incorrectly flagged real videos as fake (separately by caricature/standard condition if applicable) would directly address the most critical gap. Even if caricatures were only shown for fakes, reporting overall false-alarm rates and clarifying the experimental design would substantially increase trust in the results.

2. **Add an ablation of the attention map guidance in the caricature module.** Compare human detection performance for caricatures produced with (a) learned attention maps, (b) uniform amplification across the frame, and (c) random attention maps. This would validate whether artifact-specific amplification is necessary for the observed human improvement.

3. **Tone down the "more than 5%" claim** to reflect the controlled comparison (with vs. without human modulation, ~2.3%) and separately note the full system's improvement over a no-attention baseline.

4. **Add a small-scale comparison to text or heatmap indicators** to substantiate the claim that caricatures are a superior signaling method relative to existing approaches.

## Score and Decision

This paper has two genuinely novel contributions — the human-guided artifact attention module that improves classifier performance, and the caricature-based visual indicator that dramatically improves human deepfake detection. The user study results are striking and ecologically valid. The classifier results are competitive with the state of the art.

The major weaknesses are the missing false-positive analysis in the human study and the unvalidated role of attention maps in the caricature module. Both are addressable with additional analysis from existing data (false-alarm rates can be computed from the current experiment) or focused new experiments. The minor weaknesses (no baseline comparison to existing indicators, inflated framing of the 5% claim) are standard issues for a paper with a strong core contribution.

The weaknesses are substantive but not fatal — they do not invalidate the core contribution; they point to gaps that can and should be closed. With the false-positive analysis and an attention map ablation for caricatures, this would be a strong paper.
