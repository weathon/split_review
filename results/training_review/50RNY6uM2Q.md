## Summary

MG-LLaVA extends the LLaVA architecture by adding a high-resolution visual encoder (ConvNeXt) fused via Conv-Gate, plus object-level features extracted from an offline open-vocabulary detector pipeline (RAM + OWL-ViT). The model is instantiated with LLMs from 3.8B to 34B parameters and evaluated across 11 image and video benchmarks. The core architectural contributions are the multi-granularity vision flow and the fusion of object-level tokens.

## Strengths

- **Novel integration of object-level features via an open-vocabulary detection pipeline.**  
  The paper introduces object-level tokens (generated by RAM + OWL-ViT v2, extracted via RoIAlign) into a vision-language model, showing consistent improvements in the controlled ablation: +1.0–1.7% on MMBench-Dev and +0.4–0.9% on SEED from object-level features alone (Table 3). The ablation also demonstrates that open-vocabulary RAM tags outperform fixed COCO80 categories (69.2 vs 68.3 on MMBench-Dev, Table 4), validating the design choice.

- **Conv-Gate fusion efficiently merges low- and high-resolution features with minimal overhead.**  
  The fusion module keeps visual token count at the low-resolution level while incorporating high-resolution details. It outperforms alternative fusion designs (Resampler, Channel Concat, Patch Info Mining) on MMBench-Dev (69.8 vs next best 68.9) and MMStar (Table 4). Computational cost increases by only 0.45 TFLOPS (+7.8%) over the baseline (Table 3) while TextVQA improves by +2.8%.

- **Comprehensive evaluation across 11 benchmarks with multiple LLM backbones (3.8B–34B).**  
  The model is tested on perception, VQA, and video benchmarks, and results are reported for 5 different LLM instantiations. The ablation studies (Tables 3–4) systematically isolate the impact of each component and compare alternative fusion and merging strategies, providing useful empirical insights.

- **Scalability across model sizes.**  
  The method is demonstrated with LLMs spanning 3.8B (Phi3), 7B (Vicuna), 8B (LLaMA3), 13B (Vicuna), and 34B (Yi1.5), with the 34B variant achieving competitive scores against GPT-4V on MMBench and SEEDBench.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Data confound in main results conflates method gains with data scaling.**  
  The headline results (Tables 1–2) compare MG-LLaVA against LLaVA-1.5 and other open-source baselines that were trained on substantially different (and smaller) data mixtures. MG-LLaVA uses 1.26M pretraining + >1.3M instruction-tuning samples (including ALLaVA, ShareGPT4V, DocVQA, AI2D, DVQA), while LLaVA-1.5 uses 558K + 665K samples. The claimed +5.1% average improvement over LLaVA-1.5-7B is a composite of method gains and data scaling. The controlled ablation (Table 3, using only LLaVA-1.5's data) shows method gains of only 1–3%. This means the paper's central claim — "outperforming existing MLLMs of comparable parameter sizes" — is not properly supported by data-controlled experiments. The main results table should be clearly caveated, and ideally baselines should be retrained on the same data mixture.

- **Object-level features provide marginal benefit given the added complexity.**  
  The ablation (Table 3) shows that adding object-level features alone yields +1.0% MMBench, +0.4% SEED, and -0.2% TextVQA (Vicuna-7B); similarly modest gains for Phi3 (+1.7%, +0.9%, +0.2%). The larger gains come from the Conv-Gate high-resolution fusion (+2.8% TextVQA). Given the offline detector pipeline (RAM + OWL-ViT, NMS filtering, up to 100 extra tokens), the marginal contribution of object-level features is not convincingly justified. Furthermore, the paper does not control for the effect of simply adding more visual tokens (e.g., random patches), so we cannot rule out that the gains partly stem from token quantity rather than objectness.

### Minor

- **Video QA improvements are marginal.**  
  Gains over Video-LLaVA are +0.8% on MSVD-QA and +0.6% on MSRVTT-QA (Table 5). No statistical significance is reported, and the margins are small enough to be within noise.

- **The Vicuna-13B variant underperforms the LLaMA3-8B variant on several metrics** (MMB-D: 72.2 vs 76.5, SEED: 70.8 vs 71.5, MMStar: 34.1 vs 36.9), suggesting LLM quality matters more than raw parameter count. This observation is not discussed in the paper, which claims "pronounced scalability" without qualification.

- **The offline detector pipeline is not systematically characterized.**  
  While the paper ablates the tagging model (RAM vs COCO80), it does not analyze detector recall/precision on evaluation tasks, sensitivity to the maximum number of boxes (fixed at 100 without analysis), or failure cases where the detector misses or introduces irrelevant boxes. This makes it difficult to assess the robustness and generalizability of the pipeline.

- **Object-level features hurt TextVQA when added without high-resolution fusion** (-0.2% on Vicuna-7B, Table 3). The paper acknowledges this is due to "suboptimal detection of textual content" but does not provide a deeper failure analysis.

### Trivial

- The paper claims "none of the existing MLLMs have integrated multi-granularity features" (line 57), which is somewhat overstated given that prior works incorporate subsets of these features (e.g., region features in Shikra, high-resolution in Mini-Gemini/LLaVA-HR). The novelty lies specifically in the *combination* of all three granularities.

## Novel Insights

A genuinely novel observation emerges from the interplay of the three criticisms above: the paper reveals an interesting tension in multi-granularity MLLM design — the object-level features that conceptually seem most important (fine-grained recognition) deliver the smallest empirical gains, while the high-resolution fusion (which is architecturally simpler and adapted from prior work) drives most of the improvement. This suggests that for current MLLMs operating on benchmark tasks, improving the *density and fidelity of the base visual representation* (through high-resolution inputs) may matter more than injecting explicit object-level tokens. The marginal value of object-level features might be higher on tasks requiring relational reasoning or fine-grained discrimination that current benchmarks do not adequately stress, or it could be that the offline detector's imperfect recall limits its utility. This is a useful finding for the community, though the paper does not articulate it.

## Suggestions

1. **Major revision needed for fairness of comparison**: Retrain key baselines (at minimum LLaVA-1.5) on MG-LLaVA's full training data mixture, or clearly separate the data scaling effect from the method effect in the main results.
