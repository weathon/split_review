## Summary

This paper introduces Structure-aware Domain Knowledge Injection (SCPT+SSFT), a two-stage training paradigm that injects domain knowledge into LLMs by preserving and utilizing the inherent knowledge structure of textbooks. The method automatically extracts knowledge taxonomies from corpora, conditions language modeling on knowledge mindmap paths (SCPT), and generates structure-aware QA pairs for fine-tuning (SSFT). On MMedBench, using 76M tokens (0.3% of the 25.5B used by MMedLM), the method achieves accuracy within 2–4 points of full-data training, and on LongBench closed-book QA, SCPT+SSFT improves recall from 24.2% (vanilla CPT+SFT) to 31.5%.

## Strengths

- **Dramatic improvement in data efficiency with competitive performance.** Table 3 shows that using 0.3% of the training corpus (76M vs 25.5B tokens), Ours achieves 63.05% vs. 67.30% on InternLM2-7B and 65.36% vs. 67.75% on Llama3-8B. Relative to the base models, Ours captures ~50% of MMedLM's improvement (e.g., +4.46 vs. +8.71 on InternLM2-7B). On Llama3-8B, Ours actually exceeds MMedLM on the English subset (66.77% vs. 66.06%). This efficiency result is consistent across all three model families tested.

- **Well-motivated two-stage architecture (SCPT+SSFT) that cleanly isolates contribution of structure awareness.** The core idea — conditioning language modeling on extracted knowledge paths during pre-training and eliciting structured reasoning during SFT — is directly validated by the ablation study (Table 5): SCPT alone (46.50%) improves marginally over CPT+SFT (46.27%), but adding SSFT (49.96%) yields a substantial gain, confirming that both structure-aware training and structure-aware reasoning are necessary.

- **Extensive evaluation across model architectures and scales.** The method is tested on Llama2-7B/13B, InternLM2-7B, and Llama3-8B (Tables 2–4) on two distinct tasks (closed-book QA on LongBench, multi-choice QA on MMedBench), consistently outperforming vanilla CPT+SFT. The generalization results on Llama2-7B (+8.78%) and Llama2-13B (+6.17%) further demonstrate robustness across model sizes.

- **Evidence of cross-lingual knowledge transfer.** In Table 5, adding 8K English structure-aware QA syntheses (SSFT*) improves performance on non-English subsets (e.g., French 17.52%→27.14%, Russian 51.17%→57.42%), indicating that knowledge injected in one language transfers to others — an advantage over traditional SFT.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper's core claims — that structure-aware injection improves over vanilla methods and is more data-efficient — are supported by the experimental results, even if some of the more ambitious framing is not.

### Major

1. **The scaling law extrapolation (Eq. 1) is derived from only 3 data points and used for unsupported headline claims.** The curves fitted across 30M, 76M, and 132M tokens constitute a quadratic fit with 3 points, which is trivially overdetermined. The conclusion that "comparable performance (100%) with only 5% of the total training corpus" (line 388) is pure extrapolation with no empirical support. While the paper acknowledges this limitation in line 390 ("We have not empirically verified those predictions"), this does not justify using the 5% figure as a prominent claim in the abstract ("reducing the training costs to 5%") and introduction. A fourth data point at a higher ratio (e.g., 500M+ tokens) is needed before this can be treated as a finding rather than speculation.

2. **A critical pipeline component — the "specifically developed 7B model" for knowledge structure extraction — is insufficiently described.** The paper (line 121) references a specialized 7B model trained to identify knowledge hierarchies, claiming it matches stronger LLMs at lower cost (lines 123-126). However, the model's training data, architecture, and quantitative comparison against alternatives (e.g., Llama3-70B prompting, GPT-4) are not provided in the main text. Without this component, the entire structure extraction pipeline — and thus both SCPT and SSFT — is not reproducible. This needs to be addressed or the method scoped as using an external system whose characteristics are specified.

3. **The non-monotonic ablation result in Table 5 is not explained.** SSFT* (18K samples) has *lower* English accuracy than SSFT (10K samples) — 49.10% vs. 49.96%. The paper notes this only in passing without explanation. This pattern contradicts the claim that more structure-aware data monotonically helps on the primary evaluation language and invites questions about data quality, overfitting, or distribution mismatch.

### Minor

1. **The abstract's framing conflates absolute performance and relative improvement.** The abstract states "50% of traditional knowledge injection performance" and the introduction says "50% improvement." In context these refer to the ratio of improvements over base models (4.46/8.71 ≈ 51%), which is explained in the main text (lines 330-331). The phrasing is not technically wrong but risks misinterpretation, as a reader could infer the method achieves 50% of MMedLM's absolute accuracy (~33.7%) rather than 50% of the improvement margin.

2. **The LongBench OBQA baseline and the "surpassing" claim warrant more careful framing.** The OBQA baseline uses the prompt "Answer the question and explain why" with the passage in context, and results are reported via recall rather than the F1 that is standard on LongBench. The claim that CBQA (31.5%) surpasses OBQA (26.9%) could be explained by the model's ability to handle long-passage attention drift, but this comparison is not directly like-for-like — OBQA evaluates passage comprehension while CBQA evaluates memorization. The setup is valid but the framing overstates the comparison.

3. **Test set leakage prevention is somewhat weak.** The paper filters generated SSFT samples by removing those with >0.5 F1-similarity to test samples (line 262). An F1 threshold of 0.5 on lexical overlap is permissive — paraphrases with different surface forms can convey identical answers. Some analysis of the overlap distribution or manual verification would strengthen confidence, especially since Llama3-70B may have seen medical QA data during pre-training.

### Trivial
- The two SCPT training steps (content prediction under structure, mindmap recall) are described in one sentence (line 180: "These two steps iteratively alternate throughout training epochs."). A clearer specification of how loss is applied in the mindmap recall step would aid reproducibility.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A direct comparison against MMedLM2's published results using the same backbone (e.g., Llama2-7B) would make the "SOTA" framing verifiable, rather than relying on retrained "+MMed" baselines on newer model families.
- Adding a fourth data point at a higher ratio to validate the scaling law extrapolation.
- Ablating the quality of the knowledge structure extractor (specialized 7B vs. Llama3-70B vs. GPT-4), with human evaluation of structure quality, to understand this control variable.

## Removed Points

- **Criticism that the LongBench OBQA baseline is "invalid."** The paper describes the OBQA setup (CoT prompt with passages in context, line 273) and explains why CBQA can surpass OBQA (attention drift on long inputs, line 286). The setup is standard and interpretable. This is a misreading by the reviewer.
- **Criticism about "50% of traditional knowledge injection performance" being contradictory.** The paper clearly explains that this refers to the improvement ratio (4.46/8.71 vs. 2.57/4.96) in the main text (lines 330-331). The phrasing is somewhat ambiguous in the abstract but not factually wrong.
- **Criticism about the SCPT formulation being "not implementable from the text."** The conditioned language modeling formulation (Eq. 1, lines 161-166) and the mindmap recall step (Eq. 2, lines 172-176) are clearly specified. The design choice of zero loss on the prepended mindmap is intentional and explained.
- **Criticism about missing comparison to MMedLM2's published numbers.** This is a suggestion for improvement, not a weakness — the paper uses retrained baselines on comparable backbone models.
- **Strength from Strength Finder about scalability analysis with quantitative scaling law.** This conflicts with the verified weakness that the scaling law is derived from only 3 data points and extrapolated beyond what the data supports.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting finding is that conditioning language model training on knowledge structure paths during pre-training alone (SCPT without SSFT) provides minimal benefit (+0.23% over CPT+SFT on English MMedBench), but the combination with structure-aware SFT (SSFT) yields a large jump (+3.46% over SCPT+SFT). This suggests that the structure serves primarily as an organizational scaffold for the model's retrieval and reasoning during fine-tuning, rather than being memorized during pre-training. The cross-lingual transfer effect (English-only SSFT* improving non-English subsets by up to ~10 points) further indicates that the model learns to reason along knowledge structures in a language-agnostic way, which is a genuinely non-obvious result worth deeper investigation.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the headline claims** to match what the data actually supports: report the 50% improvement ratio accurately, and either remove the 5% cost claim from the abstract or add an explicit caveat that it is a projected extrapolation from a limited fit that has not been empirically verified.
2. **Provide a concrete specification of the structure extraction 7B model** — training data, architecture, and quantitative comparison to alternatives — or replace it with an off-the-shelf method that can be fully described within the paper's scope.
3. **Explain the non-monotonic English accuracy drop** in SSFT* (49.96% → 49.10%) or acknowledge it as a sign of overfitting or data quality degradation that future work should address.
4. **Add overlap distribution statistics** for the F1-based test leakage filter to justify the 0.5 threshold.

## Score and Decision

The paper proposes a sensible and well-motivated method for structured knowledge injection. The core empirical finding — that 0.3% of data can match ~50% of the improvement of full-data training — is supported and consistent across multiple models. However, the paper's presentation suffers from overclaiming on the basis of an unvalidated extrapolation (the 5% cost claim in the abstract), insufficient description of a critical pipeline component (the 7B structure extractor), and an unexplained non-monotonicity in the ablation. These issues are addressable but reduce confidence in the paper's strongest claims.
