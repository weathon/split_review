## Summary

This paper introduces Video Active Perception (VAP), a training-free method for long-form video question answering that selects key frames by comparing real video frames with frames generated by a lightweight video generation model (CogVideoX) conditioned on the question and answer set. The core idea, drawing from active perception theory, is to identify frames that "surprise" the model — those where the real frame diverges most from the generated expectation — and feed those to a VLM. VAP achieves strong zero-shot results on EgoSchema (68.1%), NExT-QA (81.4%), ActivityNet-QA (64.6%), and CLEVRER (40.5%), while using substantially fewer frames than standard uniform sampling (e.g., 32 vs. 180 on EgoSchema, yielding a 5.6× frames-per-question improvement).

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated conceptual framework.** Using a text-conditioned video generation model as a prior to identify "surprising" frames is an original idea grounded in active perception theory (Bajcsy, 1988). The connection between data acquisition and frame selection is clearly articulated (Section 1, Section 2).

- **Strong zero-shot results with substantially fewer frames across multiple datasets.** VAP achieves competitive or state-of-the-art zero-shot accuracy on EgoSchema (68.1%), NExT-QA (81.4%), ActivityNet-QA (64.6%), and CLEVRER (40.5%) while using only 32 frames (or 5 for CLEVRER) compared to 180, 44, and 90 frames respectively for standard uniform sampling baselines (Table 1, Section 3.4). The 5.6× frames-per-question improvement on EgoSchema is substantial.

- **Training-free and VLM-agnostic design.** VAP requires no fine-tuning of either the generation model or the VLM, and it is evaluated with three different VLM families (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, LLaVA-OV-72B) across four datasets (Table 1, Section 2.3), demonstrating generality.

- **Stronger performance on reasoning-heavy question types than caption-based methods.** VAP significantly outperforms VideoTree on CLEVRER explanatory (+154% relative), counterfactual (+71.43%), and predictive (+8%) questions (Section 3.5, Table 1). This highlights an advantage of using visual frames directly rather than captions for frame selection, especially for tasks requiring temporal and causal reasoning.

- **Informative ablations on frame count sensitivity.** The ablation varying the number of selected frames (Table 2) and initial frames (Table 3) provides practical guidance, showing that performance plateaus around n=32 — useful for practitioners deploying similar methods.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No accounting for the computational overhead of the generation pipeline in the efficiency analysis.** The paper's core efficiency claim (5.6× improvement) is measured solely by "frames per question" fed to the VLM. However, VAP's pipeline incurs significant additional computation: encoding all real frames through a 3D VAE (O(T) for T frames), running a 50-step diffusion process to generate latent frames, and performing RIFE frame interpolation. The paper does not report wall-clock time, GPU-hours, or total FLOPs for the full VAP pipeline vs. simply processing more frames directly with the VLM. Without these measurements, it is impossible to assess whether VAP is truly more *efficient* in practical terms, or whether the gain from using fewer VLM frames is offset by the generation overhead. This is acknowledged only in the limitations section ("changes in both models can directly impact the performance...") but never quantified.

- **No ablation against simpler selection heuristics.** VAP's core mechanism relies on cosine similarity between generated and real latents in the 3D VAE latent space. The paper does not compare against simpler, less expensive selection methods such as: (a) random frame selection, (b) CLIP-based question-frame similarity, (c) uniform selection with matched frame count, or (d) using a different (or no) generation model. Without these controls, it is unclear whether the specific "active perception via generation model" mechanism is responsible for the performance, or whether any question-aware selection method would perform similarly. This gap undermines attribution of the results to the specific method proposed.

### Minor

- **The "state-of-the-art zero-shot" claim is not fully verified against all baselines.** Some baselines in Table 1 (e.g., MoReVQA, VideoAgent variants) may involve fine-tuning or task-specific training, yet the paper presents them all under the "zero-shot video QA tasks" comparison without explicitly clarifying which are zero-shot vs. trained for each baseline. While the best results are indeed achieved by VAP in this comparison table, the "SOTA" claim would be strengthened by more clearly delineating which methods are truly zero-shot and which involve any form of training. Additionally, many recent zero-shot or few-shot methods for long-form video QA are not discussed in the comparison.

- **The generation model's latent space is not validated for task-relevant semantics.** VAP's selection criterion is cosine similarity in the 3D VAE latent space. The paper provides no analysis of whether this latent space preserves task-relevant information, whether the generated latents are sufficiently realistic for meaningful comparison, or whether the similarity scores correlate with actual frame relevance. This is a significant methodological gap that makes the mechanism somewhat of a black box.

- **Qualitative analysis is illustrative but lacks systematic grounding.** The case studies in Section 3.6 (Figure 4) show that VAP selects plausible frames, but there is no quantitative measure of selection quality (e.g., overlap with ground-truth keyframes, human evaluation of relevance) nor comparison against what other selection methods would pick for the same video and question. The examples appear cherry-picked.

- **Design choices are partially underspecified.** Several critical choices (why 50 denoising steps? why cosine similarity vs. other distance metrics? why CogVideoX specifically over other generation models?) are not justified experimentally. The paper states these as givens rather than tested decisions.
