## Summary

This paper proposes a computational model of reciprocally coupled continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs) to explain how place cells in hippocampus and grid cells in medial entorhinal cortex complement each other for spatial representation. The authors theoretically derive that the coupled CANN dynamics implement gradient-based optimization of the posterior (GOP), where reciprocal connections between place and grid cell networks encode the correlation prior between environmental and motion cues. Simulations demonstrate that the model achieves near-optimal Bayesian cue integration (decoding variance matches the optimal combination prediction across noise levels) and avoids the non-local error problem inherent to phase coding of grid cells.

## Strengths

- **Principled theoretical derivation linking attractor dynamics to Bayesian inference (Sec. 4.2, Eq. 19).** The paper analytically shows that the simplified dynamics of the coupled CANNs (projected onto the first two motion modes) match the gradient of the log-posterior, with the reciprocal connection strength \(J_{g i,p}\) directly encoding the correlation prior \(\sigma_{\phi^i}^2\). This provides a clean, mechanistic account of how reciprocal HPC–MEC connections could implement Bayesian cue integration.

- **Empirical validation that the network matches GOP (Fig. 3b).** The paper explicitly compares the coupled CANNs decoding results against direct GOP computation and shows they agree trial-by-trial. This directly confirms that the network dynamics converge to the same fixed points as the gradient-based optimization, not just the same final statistics.

- **Demonstration of near-optimal cue combination (Fig. 3c–d).** Across multiple noise levels, the coupled CANNs decoding variance when both cues are present matches the theoretical Bayesian prediction (\(1/\sigma_{\text{bayes}}^2 = 1/\sigma_{\text{env}}^2 + 1/\sigma_{\text{mot}}^2\)), and consistently outperforms either cue alone. This provides clear evidence that the model integrates sensory cues effectively.

- **Resolution of the non-local error problem (Fig. 4a–b).** When only the motion cue is available (simulating navigation in darkness), the coupled CANNs decoding error remains flat with increasing noise, while MAP decoding from grid cells alone suffers catastrophic non-local errors. The paper correctly explains this as a consequence of GOP's temporal continuity: the previous bump position serves as an initial condition, preventing jumps to distant local maxima.

- **Lyapunov function guarantees convergence (Eq. 20).** The paper shows that the negative log-posterior serves as a Lyapunov function for the simplified dynamics, ensuring the network always converges to a local maximum of the posterior. This provides a rigorous stability guarantee.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Grid cell inputs bypass path-integration dynamics entirely (Eq. 13, Sec. 4.1).** The external input to each G-CANN directly places a Gaussian bump at the true phase \(\psi^i(z_0)\) — i.e., grid cells are given the correct phase (plus noise) rather than integrating self-motion signals to update their phase. In biology, grid cells path-integrate velocity inputs to track position, and their phases accumulate drift over time. The model thus abstracts away the entire path-integration problem that defines grid cell function. While this simplification is reasonable for studying cue integration per se, it means the paper does not address the regime where the non-local error problem is most severe: continuous movement with accumulating path-integration error. The reported results may not transfer to settings where grid cell phases drift systematically over time rather than fluctuating around the true phase. This limitation is not flagged in Sec. 6.2.

### Minor

- **Bayesian optimality claim relies on an approximate test (Sec. 5.1).** The paper tests optimality by comparing the network's decoding variance to the formula \(1/\sigma_{\text{bayes}}^2 = 1/\sigma_{\text{env}}^2 + 1/\sigma_{\text{mot}}^2\), which assumes Gaussian decoding errors and a uniform prior. The full posterior (Eq. 6) includes the periodic correlation prior \(p(z,\phi)\) (Eq. 3), which introduces non-Gaussian structure. The paper does not show that the MAP of the full posterior reduces to this simple combination formula, nor does it numerically compute the true optimal posterior for comparison. The match in Fig. 3c–d is encouraging but does not constitute a proof of Bayesian optimality with respect to the actual posterior. The claim should be softened to "near-optimal" or "consistent with optimal cue combination under Gaussian approximations."

- **Wrap-around at periodic phase boundaries is not discussed (Secs. 3–4).** The GOP gradient equations (7–8) and the simplified CANN dynamics (17–18) involve terms like \(\|\phi^i - \psi^i(z)\|\) where \(\psi^i(z) = \text{mod}(z/\lambda_i, 1) \times 2\pi\). The gradient of this term is undefined at the wrap-around boundary where \(\psi^i(z)\) jumps from \(2\pi\) to \(0\). The paper does not address how the dynamics handle these points — whether the CANN's intrinsic dynamics smooth over the discontinuity, or whether the network can get stuck at phase boundaries. Given that the analytical derivation assumes a "linearized regime," this omission is worth addressing explicitly.

- **Projection approximation is validated only at fixed points (Sec. 4.2).** The derivation of the simplified dynamics (Eqs. 17–18) relies on projecting the full CANN equations onto the first two motion modes, assuming the bump retains its Gaussian shape under all conditions (external input, coupling). The paper validates that final fixed points match (Fig. 3b), but does not check whether the dynamics match the full posterior gradient at intermediate time steps. While this is unlikely to affect the core claims, it leaves a gap between the theoretical derivation and the empirical confirmation.

### Trivial
- The efficiency comparison in Sec. 2.1 assumes \(\Delta x\) is fixed as \(N\) increases, which is a reasonable simplification for estimation but deserves a brief caveat about how place field size may scale with environment size in practice.

## Novel Insights

The reviewer critiques collectively miss what makes this paper interesting. The key insight is not that coupling eliminates non-local errors per se, but that the *reciprocal connection structure* between place and grid cell CANNs provides a concrete neural substrate for the correlation prior in a Bayesian inference framework. This bridges two largely separate literatures: attractor network models of spatial representation and probabilistic (Bayesian) models of multisensory cue integration. Most prior coupled CANN models treat reciprocal connections as a generic mechanism for joint dynamics; this paper gives them a specific *epistemic* interpretation (encoding the correlation between sensory cues) and shows that this interpretation makes testable predictions about how decoding error should scale with noise. The empirical demonstration that the network matches GOP (Fig. 3b) is a stronger validation than simply showing that it has lower error than alternatives — it shows that the dynamics *implement a specific algorithm*. The non-local error result (Fig. 4) is clean and striking. The main limitation — abstraction of path integration — is real but does not undermine the paper's central contribution about the *integration* mechanism.

## Suggestions

1. **Acknowledge the path-integration abstraction explicitly in the limitations section.** Note that the model provides grid cells with direct phase inputs rather than modeling velocity integration, and discuss how incorporating accumulating drift would test the robustness of the non-local error result.

2. **Softening of the Bayesian optimality claim.** Change "optimal integration" to "near-optimal integration" or "consistent with Bayesian optimal integration," and note that the test relies on Gaussian approximations of the posterior.

3. **Add a brief discussion of how the dynamics handle wrap-around at phase boundaries.** Even a short paragraph explaining why this is not a practical issue (e.g., the CANN's continuous dynamics prevent the bump from straddling boundaries) would address this concern.

4. **Include a time-series visualization of bump dynamics** (e.g., \(z(t)\) and \(\phi^i(t)\) over a trial) to show that the convergence is smooth and to validate the gradient-based interpretation at intermediate times.
