## Summary

This paper presents Calgacus, a protocol that uses an LLM to hide a secret text inside a different, coherent text of exactly the same token length. The method is remarkably simple: tokenize the secret message, record the rank of each token in the LLM's probability distribution, then generate the stegotext by selecting the same-rank tokens under a different secret prompt. The protocol works, is clearly described, and achieves full-capacity steganography — a genuine property not featured in prior LLM steganography work. However, the evaluation is thin and the paper makes claims about human-level plausibility and opaqueness that are unsupported by the evidence presented.

## Strengths

- **Full-capacity steganography.** The protocol produces a stegotext that is exactly as long as the hidden message (equal token count), a property clearly distinguished from prior work (Section 2). This is a genuine and verifiable contribution.

- **Simple, efficient, and reproducible method.** The algorithm consists only of tokenization, rank extraction, and autoregressive rank-based generation. The paper convincingly shows encoding/decoding takes seconds on a laptop using an 8B model (Section 3). The simplicity means the method is immediately usable by anyone with access to an LLM.

- **Quantitative plausibility evidence using LLM log-probability.** Figure 4 shows that stegotexts generated by the method have log-probabilities that fall entirely within the distribution of 1000 real Reddit texts of the same length, while random strings do not. This provides some evidence that the generated texts are statistically similar to human-written text, even if it does not substitute for human judgment.

- **Theoretical analysis of the probability gap.** The "Low entropy token choices" analysis (Section 3) gives a clear, well-explained account of why stegotexts have lower average log-probability than originals despite preserving ranks. This strengthens the internal coherence of the evaluation.

- **Provocative discussion framing.** The discussion (Section 4) raises genuinely interesting questions about authorial intent, hallucination, and LLM knowledge that are stimulated by the protocol's existence. While speculative, this framing gives the paper a significance beyond a narrow technical contribution.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The technical method works as described and is not invalidated by any identified issue.

### Major

1. **No human evaluation to support claims of human-level plausibility.** The paper repeatedly asserts that stegotexts are "opaque to humans" (roadmap, Section 1), "coherent and plausible" (abstract), and that humans cannot distinguish originals from fakes ("This symmetry prevents one from establishing at first sight which text is authentic," Section 1). The sole evidence is LLM-assigned log-probability — a model-based proxy that is never validated against human perception. The paper provides no reason to believe log-probability correlates with human judgments of fluency or naturalness, nor does it cite prior work establishing such a correlation. For a steganographic protocol whose purpose is to produce text that *appears normal to human readers*, this is a critical gap. Without a human study (e.g., forced-choice detection, Likert ratings), the paper's strongest descriptive claims are unsubstantiated.

2. **Limited experimental scope.** The quantitative evaluation rests on:
   - **3 source texts** — selected at μ, μ−2σ, μ+2σ from one distribution (Reddit), all at exactly 85 tokens.
   - **1 generative model** — Llama 3 8B (Phi-3 is only used to verify the detection asymmetry, not to generate stegotexts).
   - **1 text type** — Reddit posts of a single fixed length.
   
   The paper's central quantitative result (Figure 4) is based on 300 generated stegotexts from three originals. No evaluation is performed on longer texts (the introduction mentions "an entire article"), non-English texts, domain-specific vocabulary, or texts with different model families as the generator. The method is simple enough that it likely generalizes, but the paper does not provide evidence for this, and its claims about "high-quality results" (abstract) would be strengthened by broader testing.

### Minor

3. **The AI-safety scenario is described but not rigorously evaluated.** The "underground LLM" scenario (Section 4) is presented as a theatrical play, and the paper references examples in Figures 11–12 (appendix). However, there is no quantitative evaluation of the feasibility constraints: whether the stegotext *s* can simultaneously be (a) a valid, coherent answer to the user's query, (b) long enough to encode the unfiltered answer *u*, and (c) practically usable on consumer hardware. The scenario is an interesting thought experiment but is not validated as a working application, weakening the claimed AI-safety implications.

4. **Security analysis is informal and some claims are unsubstantiated.** The paper explicitly avoids formal security models (which is appropriate given the difficulty), but then makes specific claims without evidence. For example, "inserting a simple random string in *k* is enough to nip [the key-search attack] in the bud" is stated without experimental verification. The deniability argument is based on observing that some stegotexts overlap in log-probability with originals, but this is not evaluated over a large set of keys or under a realistic threat model (e.g., an attacker who knows the model family and can fine-tune on observed stegotexts).

5. **The philosophical discussion conflates demonstrated results with speculation.** Section 4 mixes the paper's empirical findings with speculative reinterpretations of hallucination and LLM knowledge without clearly delineating what is supported by the experiments versus what is extrapolation. While provocative discussion is welcome, the conflation risks giving readers an inflated impression of what the paper has demonstrated empirically.

### Trivial

- The method involves choosing the *rᵢ*-th most probable token, but the paper does not specify how ties in the rank ordering are resolved (important for exact reproducibility).
- The paper's use of "plausible" oscillates between a statistical property (log-probability) and a human-judgment property without always making the distinction clear.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **A human evaluation** (even a small-scale crowdsourced naturalness rating or A/B detection test) would directly substantiate the paper's strongest claim about human opaqueness and is the single most impactful addition.
- **Testing on at least one additional model family** (e.g., Qwen2.5 or Mistral) as generator, and on texts of varying lengths (200+ tokens) and genres, would significantly strengthen confidence in generality.
- A simple ROC-style analysis quantifying the LLM-based detection accuracy (false positive / false negative rates when distinguishing originals from stegotexts via log-probability thresholding) would make the security discussion more concrete.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface one genuinely novel observation not fully articulated in the paper: the "low entropy token choices" analysis (Figure 5 and accompanying text) implies a fundamental limitation of any rank-preserving steganography — the probability gap between real and stegotexts is largely irreducible because even rank-1 tokens in the stegotext correspond to much lower absolute probabilities than rank-1 tokens in the original text. This has implications for the detectability of any such protocol and could be framed more centrally as a design constraint rather than an aside.

Beyond this, the paper's own contributions (full-capacity steganography, the protocol's simplicity, the conceptual discussion of intention) are well articulated in the paper itself.

## Suggestions

1. **Tone down the human-level claims** until human evidence is provided. Replace "opaque to humans" with "appear plausible according to an LLM-based plausibility proxy" or similar. The paper is stronger when it accurately describes what was measured.
2. **Expand the evaluation** to include at least 2 generative models, 10+ source texts, and texts of varying lengths (200+ tokens). The method is simple enough that this requires minimal implementation effort and would dramatically strengthen the paper.
3. **Implement and report a simple threshold-based detector** on the Reddit corpus to quantify the false-positive/false-negative rates for distinguishing originals from stegotexts. This would turn the informal detection observation into a concrete security measure.
4. **Separate the discussion section** more clearly into "demonstrated implications" and "speculative implications" to avoid conflating evidence with extrapolation.
