## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting RL rewards with a KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcome distributions, and introduces a Bayesian scheme for adapting the trade-off weight β online. The method is evaluated on a 2-state toy MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8, showing that a small positive β outperforms β=0. However, the paper's central claimed novel contribution—Bayesian β adaptation—is never actually specified, and no task-performance evidence is provided for it.

## Strengths

- **Fixed-β shaping consistently outperforms β = 0 in sparse-reward settings.** In the toy 2-state MDP (Table 1, Fig. 1), any β > 0 yields 69–74% higher final reward with far lower variance. On MiniGrid (Fig. 2), β = 0.01 reaches 98% success rate vs. 90.5% for β = 0. On MountainCar (Table 2), β = 0.1 gives an 18% improvement over baseline. These results are consistent across three environments.

- **Proposition 1 is mathematically correct.** For sufficiently small β, the optimal policy under the original MDP remains optimal under IncentRL. This is a basic continuity argument, not a deep result, but it is correctly stated.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None. The weaknesses below are collectively major enough to preclude acceptance but do not individually constitute a single fatal error that invalidates everything.

### Major

1. **The central claimed novelty—Bayesian β adaptation—is never specified.** The abstract and introduction call this the "central novelty" (lines 9, 21–22), and the conclusion asserts that "Bayesian adaptation of preference gaps removes the long-standing need for manual trade-off tuning" (line 289). Yet the Methods section (Section 3) contains no description of any Bayesian scheme: no prior distribution, no likelihood model, no update rule, no inference procedure. The algorithm description (Section 5.1) also omits it entirely. The only references are in Section 5.4, where the paper states "we tracked the posterior evolution of β using our Bayesian adaptation scheme" (line 230), without ever explaining what that scheme is. This is not an appendix-level detail—it is the core claimed contribution, and it is absent from the paper. The contribution cannot be evaluated, reproduced, or compared against alternatives. (Applies to Sections 1, 3, 5.1, 5.4.)

2. **No task-performance evidence is provided for the Bayesian β agent.** The paper says "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" (line 228). Results "not shown" cannot be evaluated. Figures 3 and 4 and Table 3 show only posterior diagnostics (posterior mean, variance, sampled β values)—these demonstrate that *something* converges, but without any task-success metric (success rate, cumulative reward, episode length), there is no evidence that the converged β value actually *works* for the agent. The claim that Bayesian β "eliminates manual tuning" requires showing that the agent using learned β performs at least as well as the best fixed β. This evidence is not provided. (Applies to Section 5.4, Figures 3–4, Table 3.)

3. **The predicted outcome distribution p is not concretely defined for the main experiments.** The paper defines p(o|s,a) formally for the 2-state toy (where it is hand-coded from transition probabilities), but for MountainCar and MiniGrid it provides no implementation details. For MountainCar, the paper says "q(o|s) is a preferred outcome assigning all probability to the goal (x ≥ 0.5)" and p is "the predicted outcome distribution" (line 197–199)—but what is the outcome space o? In a continuous state space, what does "outcome" mean? Is p a learned forward model? If so, what architecture, training procedure, and loss function are used? For MiniGrid, there is no description at all. Without these details the method is not reproducible and the experimental results are uninterpretable. (Applies to Sections 5.3, 5.4.)

4. **The only baseline is β = 0 (standard RL).** The paper cites curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al., Burda et al.), information gain (Houthooft et al.), and empowerment (Mohamed & Jimenez Rezende) in the related work section, but compares against none of them. The "fixed-regularization baselines" are just different β values within IncentRL itself. Showing that an intrinsic shaping bonus beats no shaping bonus does not calibrate whether the mechanism is competitive with established exploration methods (RND, ICM, etc.). In 2025, a new intrinsic motivation method needs to demonstrate that it adds value beyond what existing approaches already provide. This is a significant gap given the paper's claims about improving over "standard RL and fixed-regularization baselines." (Applies to Sections 5.2–5.4, Tables 1–2, Figure 2.)

5. **The theoretical analysis is too thin to constitute a meaningful contribution.** Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimality) and Proposition 2 (large β overrides rewards) are immediate continuity observations that follow directly from the form of the objective. They provide no insight into convergence properties, sample efficiency, robustness, or the dynamics of β adaptation. The "Limit Behavior" section (4.2) restates these without additional substance. (Applies to Section 4.)

### Minor

- **Only 3 seeds are used for MountainCar and MiniGrid** (Tables 2, Figure 2 caption states 3 seeds), with no confidence intervals or statistical testing. For the toy MDP, 7 seeds are used but the β=0 baseline has very high variance (CV ≈ 68%). Results from 3 seeds in high-variance settings are fragile and do not support the strength of the claims made.

- **The Bayesian β posterior is shown only for MiniGrid** (Figures 3–4, Table 3) with β converging to ~0.02, while the best fixed β used for comparison was 0.01. It is unclear whether this difference is meaningful. The claim of "comparable performance" (results not shown) is insufficient to establish that the adaptation recovers near-optimal performance.

- **Experimental protocol details are sparse.** Learning rates, network architectures, ε schedules, and hyperparameter tuning procedures are not provided. The paper says "same neural architecture and training hyperparameters across conditions" without listing them. While some details are standard for DQN in these environments, the absence of specifics hinders reproducibility.

- **Proposition 2 (β → ∞)** is not surprising and does not require proof. The statement that extremely large β makes the KL penalty dominate is obvious from Equation 1.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the Bayesian β scheme in full.** Provide the prior, likelihood, update rule, and inference procedure. Without this, the contribution cannot be evaluated.
2. **Show task performance for the Bayesian β agent** (success rate, cumulative reward, episode length) on MiniGrid and MountainCar, with error bars across seeds, alongside the fixed-β sweeps.
3. **Define p concretely** for MountainCar and MiniGrid. If it uses a learned forward model, describe its architecture, training procedure, and whether it is learned jointly or separately.
4. **Add at least one strong baseline** (e.g., RND-based DQN, curiosity-driven DQN) on MiniGrid to establish what the KL shaping term contributes beyond existing methods.
5. **Increase the number of seeds** (at least 10) and report confidence intervals or use the rliable protocol for comparative claims.
