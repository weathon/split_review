## Summary

This paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL rewards with a KL-divergence penalty between a predicted outcome distribution p(o|s,a) and a preferred outcome distribution q(o|s), weighted by a parameter β. The paper claims that treating β as a Bayesian random variable and adapting it online is its central novelty, eliminating manual tuning of the exploration–exploitation trade-off. Experiments are conducted on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey.

## Strengths

- **Genuinely distinct framing from policy-regularized RL (Section 2.2):** The paper correctly distinguishes its outcome-space KL penalty from TRPO/PPO's policy-space KL constraints and SAC's entropy regularization. The idea of shaping rewards via outcome-prediction alignment rather than policy divergence is underexplored and conceptually interesting.

- **Honest empirical demonstration of β-sensitivity (Section 5.3, Table 2):** The MountainCar sweep across β ∈ {0.0, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0} honestly shows the non-monotonic effect: β=0.1 improves goal reaches by 18%, while β=0.3 and β=1.0 degrade performance by 53% and 75%. This provides genuine motivation for the paper's claim that adaptive β is needed.

- **MiniGrid DoorKey results show practical gains (Section 5.4, Figure 2):** With β=0.01, the agent achieves 98% success rate (vs 90.5% baseline) and converges to 29-step episodes (vs 75-step baseline), a roughly 2.6× reduction in episode length on a standard sparse-reward benchmark.

- **Clean toy demonstration of the KL-shaping mechanism (Section 5.2, Table 1):** The 2-state MDP experiment isolates the effect of the KL penalty and shows consistent 69–74% improvement over standard Q-learning across all β > 0, with substantially reduced variance.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **The Bayesian adaptation mechanism — the paper's self-identified central novelty — is never described.** The abstract, introduction, contribution list (line 21-25), and conclusion all foreground Bayesian adaptation of β as the primary contribution. Yet the body of the paper contains no description of: the prior distribution over β, the likelihood function or performance metric that drives posterior updates, the update rule, or what constitutes a "round" of adaptation. The Methods section (Section 3) covers only the KL-based incentive shaping, not the Bayesian adaptation. Section 5.4 shows posterior concentration plots (Figures 3-4) and summary statistics (Table 3) but never explains how those posteriors were produced. The paper states "we tracked the posterior evolution of β using our Bayesian adaptation scheme" (line 230) without ever defining that scheme. Without this, the paper's headline claim is unverifiable and the method is irreproducible.

### Major

- **The KL divergence in the toy example is mathematically undefined under the paper's own formalism.** Section 5.2 specifies q(o|s₀) = {s₁: 1.0, s₀: 0.0} and p(o|s₀, a₁) = {s₁: 0.3, s₀: 0.7}. The KL divergence KL(p||q) includes the term 0.7 · log(0.7/0.0), which is infinite. The paper reports finite shaped rewards (Table 1), meaning the implementation must use a different computation than what is described (e.g., a smoothed q or a different divergence). Either the mathematical formalism or the reported results are incorrect for the paper's showcase example.

- **No comparisons to any intrinsic motivation or reward-shaping baseline.** The paper explicitly positions itself against the intrinsic motivation literature (Section 2.1) and claims to address "a core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods." Yet every experiment compares IncentRL only against standard RL (β=0). There are no comparisons to ICM, RND, VIME, empowerment-based methods, or potential-based reward shaping. Without such baselines, there is no evidence that IncentRL is better than existing approaches — only evidence that adding a hand-crafted shaping term helps over no shaping at all.

- **p(o|s,a) is never operationalized for MountainCar or MiniGrid.** Section 3.1 says p(o|s,a) "may be obtained from a forward model or from environment dynamics," but neither the MountainCar experiment (Section 5.3) nor the MiniGrid experiment (Section 5.4) specifies how this distribution is actually computed. In MountainCar (continuous state space), is there a learned dynamics model, a discretization, or oracle access? Without this specification, the experiments are not reproducible and the reader cannot assess whether gains are due to the IncentRL mechanism or to information leakage from whatever generates p(o|s,a).

### Minor

- **Confusing presentation of Bayesian adaptation results relative to fixed-β sweeps.** The MiniGrid fixed sweep uses β=0.01 (Section 5.4), yet the Bayesian adaptation plots show a reference line at β=0.1 and the text says "concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)" (line 239). In reality, the posterior converges to ~0.02 (Table 3, Round 4 mean = 0.0173), which is close to 0.01. The paper's narrative misdescribes its own results, creating an apparent inconsistency that is actually just poor presentation.

- **Small number of seeds and absence of statistical testing.** MountainCar uses 3 seeds with large standard deviations (e.g., ±908 for β=0.3). MiniGrid uses 3 seeds over 2M timesteps. No statistical tests are reported. The paper also references "additional runs (not shown)" (line 228) to support the Bayesian adaptation claim without presenting those results.

- **Theoretical analysis contributes little beyond definitions.** Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimal policy) is a continuity argument with no bound on ε. Proposition 2 (large β converges to KL-minimizer) follows directly from the objective definition. Section 4.3 on latent representations is purely speculative and untested.

### Trivial

- Results described as "not shown" (line 228) undercut the empirical evidence for the Bayesian adaptation claim.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Adding a comparison to at least one intrinsic motivation baseline (e.g., ICM or RND) would substantially strengthen the paper's positioning against the literature it cites.
- Specifying how p(o|s,a) is computed in each environment would make the experiments reproducible.
- Providing a bound on ε in Proposition 1 would strengthen the theoretical contribution.

## Suggestions

- **Describe the Bayesian adaptation mechanism in full.** This must include the prior, likelihood, update rule, and definition of a "round." Without this, the paper's central claim is unsupported. This is the single most important fix.
- **Fix the KL computation in the toy example** by either using a smoothed q, a different divergence (e.g., Jensen-Shannon), or clarifying how the implementation avoids the infinite KL.
- **Add at least one intrinsic motivation baseline** (ICM, RND, or VIME) to substantiate the paper's comparative claims.
- **Specify how p(o|s,a) is obtained** in MountainCar and MiniGrid.
- **Reconcile the textual description of Bayesian adaptation** (which says β ≈ 0.1) with the actual convergence values (~0.02) so the narrative matches the data.
