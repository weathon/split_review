## Summary

The paper presents *Calgacus*, a protocol that uses an LLM to hide a text of arbitrary meaning inside another coherent text of exactly the same length (in tokens). The method is elegantly simple: encode the rank of each token of the hidden message in the LLM's probability distribution, then generate a stegotext by picking tokens at those same ranks under a different prompt. The paper shows that the resulting stegotexts fall within the log-probability distribution of real Reddit posts (Figure 4), demonstrating plausibility. It then discusses profound implications for trust, AI safety, and the nature of LLM knowledge.

## Strengths

1. **Full capacity (same-length hiding) is a well-defined technical contribution.** The protocol guarantees that the stegotext is exactly as long (in tokens) as the hidden message. This is explicitly contrasted with prior LLM steganography methods that achieve statistical undetectability by sacrificing capacity (Section 2, "What we add to the field is Calgacus, a protocol with the notable property of having full capacity"). The method itself (Section 3) enforces this property by construction.

2. **Empirical evidence that stegotexts are plausible.** Figure 4 shows that 300 stegotexts (100 per source text, generated by Llama 3 8B from three Reddit posts) have LLM-assigned log-probabilities that fall entirely within the distribution of 1,000 real Reddit posts of the same length. The stegotexts are clearly separated from random baselines (ASCII strings, random English words). This is direct evidence that even modest open-source models can produce plausible cover texts while preserving the hidden message.

3. **Mechanistic analysis of why stegotexts are less probable than originals.** The "Low entropy token choices" paragraph and Figure 5 provide a clear explanation: rank-1 tokens from the hidden message are often "wasted" in low-entropy contexts where the LLM would almost always select a specific token, while the original text's rank-1 tokens appear in higher-entropy contexts where multiple tokens are plausible. This is the paper's most technically insightful contribution and usefully informs the security discussion.

4. **Elegant and practical method.** The protocol is remarkably simple (a few lines of pseudocode), requires only a single forward pass per token with no iterative modification, and runs on consumer hardware. This accessibility is a genuine strength — the method could be deployed by anyone with an open-source LLM.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **No empirical comparison against existing LLM steganography baselines.** The paper cites prior methods (Ziegler et al., 2019; Meteor/Kaptchuk et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024; Zamir, 2024) in the Related Work section, characterizing Calgacus's unique property as "full capacity." However, it provides no empirical comparison on any axis — statistical undetectability, fluency, steerability, or decoding accuracy. The reader cannot evaluate whether the full-capacity property comes at a meaningful cost in any of these dimensions. This is a critical gap because the paper's significance hinges on this trade-off being worthwhile.

2. **"While remaining opaque to humans" is asserted without evidence.** The paper states (in the introduction's roadmap): "While remaining opaque to humans, we show that LLMs can uncover a distinction between original texts and most of their encoded counterparts." This is a factual claim about human perception that is never tested. No human evaluation, user study, or perceptual experiment of any kind is conducted. The only evidence offered is an LLM-assigned log-probability, which is a proxy for statistical plausibility, not human indistinguishability. **If the paper's central significance narrative is about the "erosion of trust in written communication," the claim that humans cannot tell the difference is central and must be supported.**

3. **Evaluation scope is thin relative to the paper's broad claims.** The main experiment (Figure 4) uses:
   - Only 3 source texts (chosen at μ, μ-2σ, μ+2σ of the distribution)
   - Only one text length (85 tokens)
   - Only one LLM (Llama 3 8B) for stegotext generation
   - Only one text distribution (Reddit posts)
   
   For a paper that opens with "This marks a dramatic break in history—or perhaps the end of history itself" and discusses urgent AI-safety implications, this is an extremely narrow empirical base. The paper does not systematically study how the method scales with text length, model size, or domain, and does not characterize failure modes beyond a single hash example.

4. **Statistical detectability is acknowledged but its implications for the paper's central framing are not resolved.** The paper shows that stegotexts are systematically less probable than originals and that "generally the original text can be discerned from its stegotexts by picking the most probable one according to a LLM." It verifies this with a different LLM (Phi-3 3.8B) in Figure 14. This directly undercuts the "radical decoupling of text from authorial intent" narrative: if an automated procedure can reliably distinguish stegotexts from real text, the erosion of trust is bounded. The paper acknowledges this as a limitation but does not resolve the tension — it continues to use the dramatic framing as if the detectability were a minor side effect rather than a structural property of the method. The "deniability" argument (that some outlier prompts yield stegotexts with comparable probability) partially addresses this but is itself only supported anecdotally (Figure 15, in the appendix).

5. **The concrete AI safety scenario (trojan chatbot) is presented without validation.** The "Shibbolethian Theatre" scenario in Section 4 describes how a company could ship unfiltered LLM answers inside compliant responses. While the description is detailed, there is no end-to-end demonstration, no analysis of failure modes, and the strict requirements (identical LLMs, identical hardware, identical tokenizer behavior across machines) are mentioned but not tested. The scenario is presented as an urgent concern but remains a thought experiment.

### Minor

1. **Log-probability has well-known limitations as a measure of "soundness" or "meaningfulness."** The paper acknowledges this (Section 3: "This definition has a clear defect: longer texts are by construction less plausible") and mitigates it by comparing texts of equal length. However, log-probability conflates multiple factors — topic predictability, style, grammaticality — making it an imperfect proxy for human-judged quality. The paper does not triangulate with other metrics (e.g., perplexity, human ratings, grammaticality scores).

2. **The deniability argument is incomplete.** The paper argues that if an attacker tries the correct key, they still cannot be sure because other keys could produce plausible alternate messages. But this relies on the existence of "outlier prompts" that yield stegotexts with probability comparable to the original. The frequency of such prompts, how to find them, and the computational cost of doing so are not characterized. The claim "for some prompts the stegotexts can attain probabilities in the same ballpark as the original" is supported only by a single anecdotal example (Figure 15).

3. **The paper's genre is ambiguous.** The Discussion section (Section 4) is a philosophical essay on hallucinations, intention, and the nature of LLM knowledge that is largely disconnected from the empirical evidence. The paper does not clearly signal whether the primary contribution is the technical protocol or the philosophical interpretation. This makes it difficult to evaluate against standard scientific criteria — the philosophical claims are interesting but not empirically grounded, while the technical contribution is solid but underevaluated.

## Novel Insights

The low-entropy token analysis (Figure 5) is the most novel technical insight: it reveals that even when token ranks are preserved exactly, the stegotext is systematically less probable because rank-1 tokens are "used up" in low-entropy contexts where the LLM would almost always pick the same token anyway. This provides a principled explanation for why rank-preserving steganography cannot match the token-level probability distribution of natural text and places a fundamental bound on the statistical undetectability of this class of methods. This analysis goes beyond what is typically offered in steganography papers.

The philosophical connection drawn between LLM stegotexts, Oulipo constraints (Perec's *La Disparition* written without the letter 'e'), and the concept of hallucination as "lack of intention" rather than factual falsehood is a genuinely thought-provoking framing. However, it is presented as discussion rather than as an empirically supported contribution.

## Suggestions

1. **Add empirical baselines.** Compare Calgacus against at least one existing LLM steganography method (e.g., Meteor's variable-capacity scheme or Ziegler et al.'s token-sampling approach) on: (a) statistical detectability via a trained classifier or likelihood-ratio test, (b) fluency via perplexity or human evaluation, and (c) decoding accuracy. This would concretely characterize the capacity-detectability trade-off that is central to the paper's contribution.

2. **Either conduct a human study or retract the "opaque to humans" claim.** If the paper's significance rests on human inability to distinguish stegotexts, a human evaluation is essential. If not, the claim should be removed.

3. **Broaden the evaluation scope.** Test with multiple text lengths (e.g., 50, 100, 200 tokens), multiple LLMs (7B, 13B, 70B), and multiple domains (news, academic writing, dialogue) to establish the generality of the results.

4. **Either validate the trojan chatbot scenario or contextualize it as speculation.** An end-to-end demonstration with actual models and prompts would greatly strengthen the AI safety argument. Absent that, the paper should more clearly signal that this is a hypothetical scenario.

5. **Resolve the detectability tension explicitly.** A dedicated subsection examining "When does the detectability matter?" — comparing conditions where the log-probability gap is large vs. small, and discussing the practical implications for each regime — would turn the current weakness into a strength.
