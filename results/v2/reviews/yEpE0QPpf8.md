## Summary

The paper introduces Grounding-IQA, a new paradigm that integrates spatial grounding (bounding boxes) with MLLM-based image quality assessment. It defines two subtasks: GIQA-DES (quality descriptions with coordinates) and GIQA-VQA (local quality QA with referring/grounding). To support this paradigm, the authors construct GIQA-160K, a 167K-sample dataset via an automated pipeline (LLM-based object extraction + Grounding DINO detection + IQA-based box refinement). They also release GIQA-Bench, a 100-image/250-sample human-annotated benchmark evaluating description quality, VQA accuracy, and grounding precision. Experiments show that fine-tuning MLLMs on GIQA-160K yields models that outperform both general and task-specific MLLMs on the combined grounding+quality task.

## Strengths

1. **Novel grounding-IQA paradigm that genuinely extends existing IQA**: The paper is the first to systematically integrate multimodal referring and grounding with image quality assessment, defining two well-motivated subtasks (GIQA-DES and GIQA-VQA). This goes beyond prior MLLM-based IQA (Q-Instruct, DepictQA) which provides only text-level descriptions without localization. The difference is concretely illustrated in Fig. 2, where existing methods fail to pinpoint quality-relevant objects while Grounding-IQA does. (Section 3.1, Fig. 2)

2. **High-quality dataset generated by a principled automated pipeline**: The annotation pipeline (Section 3.2) is carefully designed with four stages, including a novel IQA-Filter + Box-Merge algorithm (Algorithm 1) that uses Q-Instruct to verify detected boxes against quality attributes. The refinement demonstrably brings the box area distribution much closer to human-annotated data (Fig. 6), and ablation results (Table 2a) show substantial gains from refinement (mIoU +0.0227, Tag-Recall +0.0452, BLEU@4 +2.70). The pipeline is the key enabler for creating a dataset at this scale for a new task.

3. **Comprehensive multi-aspect evaluation revealing systematic failures of existing methods**: GIQA-Bench evaluates three distinct capabilities (description quality, VQA accuracy, grounding precision) across eight metrics. Table 5 provides a revealing diagnostic: grounding MLLMs (Ferret, Kosmos-2) achieve decent grounding but poor quality assessment, IQA models (Q-Instruct) have good description quality but near-random VQA accuracy and no grounding, and general models are weak in all aspects. This systematic failure analysis is itself a valuable contribution, and Grounding-IQA models are the first to achieve competitive scores across all dimensions simultaneously.

4. **Broad compatibility with diverse MLLM architectures**: The GIQA-160K dataset improves performance across four different backbones (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, LLaVA-1.6-7B, mPLUG-Owl2-7B) in Table 4, with consistent gains in Tag-Recall, LLM-Score, and Acc(Total). This demonstrates the dataset's generality and the paradigm's adaptability beyond a single architecture.

5. **Multi-task synergy between description and VQA subtasks**: Ablation results in Table 3 show that joint training on both GIQA-DES and GIQA-VQA outperforms training on either alone. Notably, joint training boosts VQA Tag-Recall from 0.5577 (DES-only) or 0.4872 (VQA-only) to 0.7372, while maintaining high description quality (LLM-Score 63.00). This empirically validates the integrated paradigm design.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Asymmetric baseline comparison conflates task-specific training with paradigm advantage**: The central comparison in Table 5 contrasts models fine-tuned on GIQA-160K (the "Ours" group) with grounding models (Ferret, Shikra, GroundingGPT, Kosmos-2) and general MLLMs evaluated *without* fine-tuning on this data. The grounding models are pre-trained on generic grounding tasks but have never seen the GIQA dataset or its specific IQA-grounded format. This makes it impossible to separate the benefit of the proposed paradigm from the benefit of simply having more task-specific training data. For a rigorous comparison, the strongest grounding models should be fine-tuned on GIQA-160K itself. Without this, the paper's claim that "our method outperforms existing MLLMs" (Sec. 4.3) is overstated. While the "grounding MLLMs excel in grounding tasks but underperform on quality-related objects/areas" observation is supported by the zero-shot evaluation, the headline "outperforming" claim requires fairer baselines.

2. **Small benchmark with no significance testing limits reliability of rankings**: GIQA-Bench contains only 100 images and 250 test samples. Differences of 1-3 points in mIoU, Tag-Recall, or LLM-Score between methods (e.g., Ferret DES Tag-Recall 0.6778 vs. Grounding-IQA LLaVA-v1.6-7B 0.5981) could easily fall within sampling noise. No confidence intervals, error bars, or significance tests are reported. For a benchmark intended to establish rankings and serve as a community standard, this statistical grounding is essential. This is especially concerning for the description metrics (BLEU@4, LLM-Score) evaluated on only 100 samples, where LLM-based scoring can have high variance.

### Minor

3. **Description quality metrics measure textual style more than quality perception accuracy**: BLEU@4 and the LLM-Score compare generated descriptions (with coordinates excluded) against a single reference text. The reference text in GIQA-Bench is drawn from the same Q-Pathway source as the training data (with adjustments), meaning high scores could partially reflect stylistic mimicry of the training annotation style rather than genuine quality perception accuracy. The paper would benefit from human evaluation or reference-free metrics that directly assess whether the generated description correctly identifies quality-relevant regions and their severity, independent of lexical overlap with a particular reference. The VQA accuracy and grounding metrics are cleaner in this regard, which is why the VQA results are the paper's strongest evidence.

4. **20×20 coordinate discretization limits grounding precision despite "fine-grained" framing**: Bounding boxes are quantised to a 20×20 grid (~11×11 pixels on a 224×224 image). The ablation in Table 2b shows that continuous coordinates achieve higher mIoU (0.6046 vs. 0.5851). While the paper acknowledges this trade-off and the token-efficiency motivation is reasonable, the "fine-grained" characterization is somewhat at odds with such coarse discretization. The mIoU evaluation against continuous human-annotated boxes systematically penalizes the discrete representation. An analysis of the grid size trade-off (e.g., comparing n=m=10, 20, 50, continuous) would better justify the design choice.

### Trivial
- Some instances in Fig. 5 show bounding box coordinates with seemingly arbitrary ranges (e.g., [0, 0, 100, 100]), which may confuse readers about the actual coordinate system.

## Novel Insights

A genuinely novel insight that emerges across the reviews is that **existing models excel in one capability (grounding or quality assessment) at the expense of the other**, and this failure mode is not fixed by simply scaling or using more data in the existing paradigm. Table 5 reveals this cleanly: Ferret achieves 0.6458 mIoU but only 43.75 LLM-Score on GIQA-DES, while Q-Instruct achieves 62.00 LLM-Score but 0.5817 Acc(Total) on GIQA-VQA. The paper's key finding — that grounding and quality assessment require explicit joint training rather than emerging from either capability alone — is a stronger result than the raw performance numbers suggest. Also notable is that the multi-task ablation (Table 3) shows the two subtasks reinforce each other synergistically: joint training boosts VQA Tag-Recall from ~0.49-0.56 to 0.7372 while maintaining description quality, suggesting the paradigm captures complementary aspects of quality perception.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the narrative around capability enablement rather than outperformance**: The paper's strongest claim is that it enables a *new* capability (grounded IQA) that no existing method can perform well. The current framing of "outperforming existing MLLMs" invites unfair comparison critiques. Reframing to emphasize that grounding+IQA requires joint training and that existing paradigms fail at this combined task would better match the evidence.

2. **Add fair baselines by fine-tuning grounding models on GIQA-160K**: Even if the results show these baselines approach Grounding-IQA performance on some metrics, this would only strengthen the dataset's value while making the comparison cleaner.

3. **Report confidence intervals or statistical tests for GIQA-Bench**: Given the small size (N=100), even bootstrap confidence intervals would substantially improve the reliability story.

4. **Add a human evaluation study for GIQA-DES**: Have human raters assess whether generated descriptions correctly identify quality-relevant regions and their severity, independent of lexical overlap with the reference. This would directly address the description metric concern.

5. **Analyze the grid size trade-off**: Compare n=m=10, 20, 50, and continuous coordinates to map the Pareto frontier between token efficiency and grounding precision.
