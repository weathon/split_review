## Summary

This paper proposes TOKENCOUNT, a training-free object counting framework that repurposes SAM without architectural modifications by combining (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that iteratively samples prompts from probability distributions derived from SAM's image embedding, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method achieves the best MAE (16.25) among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147 and strong results on CARPK (MAE 4.68), outperforming all supervised methods on that dataset.

## Strengths

- **Best MAE among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147**: The method achieves MAE 16.25, a substantial improvement over the prior SAM-only method TFOC (19.95) — a 3.7-point gap. This is a meaningful advance within the SAM-only training-free category (Table 1).

- **Strong CARPK performance without training or auxiliary models**: MAE 4.68 on CARPK outperforms all supervised methods including SAFECOUNT (5.33), BMNet+ (5.76), and LOCA (9.97), achieved using only SAM without additional encoders (Table 1).

- **Well-designed ablation studies covering all key components**: Tables 3, 4, and 5 provide ablations on verification metrics (TS-SS vs. alternatives), iteration/prompt budgets (showing monotonic improvement with more prompts), and output token space vs. image embedding space, demonstrating consistent contribution of each proposed component.

- **Clean framework with practical efficiency advantage**: The method achieves 1.69 seconds per image on FSC-147 using a single A100 GPU and is reported as more than twice as fast as TFCAC on CARPK, since it avoids additional encoders and leverages SAM's native output tokens.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major

- **Misleading claims in the abstract about outperforming all methods**: The abstract states "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" (line 13), but on FSC-147, BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), LOCA (10.79), and TFCAC (12.26) all achieve better MAE. The body text (line 286) correctly qualifies this as "the best performance among training-free SAM only methods," but the abstract overclaims. Similarly, the CARPK claim of "superior performance, outperforming all supervised methods" omits that TFCAC (4.39) achieves better MAE — this is acknowledged only later in Section 4.2 (line 288). This inconsistency between the abstract's framing and the actual results weakens credibility.

- **Poor RMSE on FSC-147 unaddressed**: The method's RMSE of 133.11 on FSC-147 is worse than TFOC (132.16) and C-Anything (131.24) among SAM-only methods. The high RMSE relative to MAE indicates large variance in per-image errors — some images must have catastrophic failures. The paper never discusses this, not in the results section, not in the discussion. Section 5 mentions "performance degradation in scenarios with dense distributions of very small instances" but provides no quantitative characterization. Given that RMSE is the metric where the method performs least favorably, the silence is a significant omission.

- **No variance or statistical significance reported**: The method involves stochastic prompt sampling from multinomial distributions and random exemplar selection for CARPK ("we randomly selected 12 objects from the training set," line 232). Despite this inherent randomness, not a single standard deviation, confidence interval, or multi-run result is reported anywhere in the paper. The core ablation gap (output token MAE 16.25 vs. image embedding MAE 16.94, a difference of 0.69 points) is small enough that it could plausibly not be statistically significant, but this cannot be assessed.

### Minor

- **Core ablation margins are modest**: Table 5 shows output token verification (MAE 16.25) vs. image embedding verification (MAE 16.94) — a difference of 0.69. Table 3 shows TS-SS (16.25) vs. cosine similarity (16.48) — a difference of 0.23. While the consistent direction supports the paper's narrative, the margins are small and without significance tests, it is unclear whether these differences are reliable. The paper's central intellectual claim — that output tokens provide a categorically better verification signal — would benefit from stronger empirical support.

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not analyzed**: The token verification threshold of 300 and temperature scaling factor τ = 0.066 are described as set "based on validation experiments" (line 238), but no details of this validation or sensitivity analysis are provided. These are critical parameters that could significantly affect performance.

- **The decay mechanism for negative regions is underspecified**: Section 3.1 states that when a prompt is classified as negative, "the value of the probability distribution at that mask region is decreased" (line 206), but the exact decay rate or factor is never specified. This makes the method partially non-reproducible.

- **Figure 5 PCA analysis partially undermines the narrative**: The paper presents Figure 5 to show output tokens provide better separation than image embeddings. However, the paper itself acknowledges that for the top image, "in the case of image embeddings, the positive objects are distributed separately" (line 349), meaning the image embedding space also separates positive and negative well in that case. The bottom image is more supportive, but the mixed evidence weakens the claim.

## Novel Insights

The paper's genuinely novel observation is that SAM's output tokens — which jointly encode semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention in the decoder — provide a richer signal for counting verification than the commonly used image embeddings from SAM's encoder. This insight, combined with the probabilistic prompt generation strategy that adaptively explores the image space, offers a principled way to repurpose SAM for counting without any architectural modification. The PCA visualizations in Figure 5 and the ablation in Table 5 provide initial evidence for this claim, though stronger validation would be welcome.
