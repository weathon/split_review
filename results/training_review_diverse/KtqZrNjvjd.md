## Summary

This paper proposes Video Active Perception (VAP), a training-free method for long-form video question answering that selects key frames by comparing real video latents against latents generated by a lightweight text-conditioned video diffusion model (CogVideoX). Drawing inspiration from active perception theory, VAP retains frames that diverge most from the model's expected video dynamics. The method is evaluated on EgoSchema, NExT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, and CLEVRER, achieving strong zero-shot results while using substantially fewer frames than uniform sampling baselines.

## Strengths

1. **Novel and principled frame-selection approach**: The core idea — using a generative video model as a prior world model and selecting "surprising" frames via latent-space comparison — is novel, well-motivated by active perception theory, and clearly differentiates VAP from heuristic or caption-based selection methods (Section 1, Section 2).

2. **Training-free and model-agnostic**: VAP requires no fine-tuning of either the generation model or the VLM, and is demonstrated with three different VLM families (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, LLaVA-OV-72B), showing broad applicability (Sections 2.3, 3.3).

3. **Consistent accuracy improvements over uniform sampling of the same VLM**: This is the cleanest comparison in the paper. VAP with 32 frames outperforms uniform sampling with many more frames (180 for EgoSchema, 44 for NExT-QA, 90 for ActivityNet-QA) using the same VLM backbone — e.g., 68.1% vs. 64.5% for Gemini on EgoSchema, 81.4% vs. 75.3% for Gemini on NExT-QA (Table 1, Section 3.4). These are apples-to-apples comparisons and constitute the strongest evidence for the method's effectiveness.

4. **Strong reasoning performance on challenging tasks**: On CLEVRER, VAP achieves substantial relative gains over the caption-based VideoTree on explanatory (+154%) and counterfactual (+71.43%) questions, directly demonstrating the advantage of using visual frames rather than captions for temporal/causal reasoning (Section 3.5, Table 1).

5. **Comprehensive ablation on frame counts**: Systematic experiments varying the number of selected frames (Table 2) and initial conditioning frames (Table 3) demonstrate clear plateau behavior at 32 frames, providing practical guidance and validating the design choices. These ablations use a single smaller VLM (GPT-4o mini), which is a reasonable cost-saving choice.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Efficiency claim is incomplete: the computational cost of the generative prior is not measured.** The paper reports efficiency as "frames per question" (up to 5.6× savings), counting only the VLM's token-processing cost. However, Algorithm 1 requires running a full video diffusion model (50 denoising steps of a DiT transformer) plus RIFE interpolation to generate latents for all T frames of a long video before selection occurs. The paper describes CogVideoX as "lightweight" and the interpolation as "fast" but provides no end-to-end runtime comparison. If generating the full latent video — including 50 denoising steps over T frames (up to 5,400 for EgoSchema) — takes more time than simply encoding and processing the extra VLM frames directly, the claimed efficiency advantage is misleading. This is a structural gap in the paper's central claim of efficiency improvement.

2. **Answer conditioning for frame selection is not ablated, creating an unresolved methodological question.** VAP conditions the generation model on both the question *and all possible answers*, then selects frames that diverge from these answer-conditioned expectations. This means the frame selection process has access to the answer set, potentially guiding it toward frames that discriminate between answer options — going beyond selecting frames that are simply informative about the question. The paper does not ablate this: what happens if only the question (without answers) is used as conditioning? Without this ablation, it is unclear whether VAP's frame selection is doing active perception grounded in the question or serving as an answer-guided verification mechanism. The ethical/limitations statement also does not discuss this.

### Minor

1. **Comparisons with prior frame-selection methods are confounded by different backbones.** VAP feeds selected frames directly to a VLM (GPT-4o, Gemini, LLaVA-OV), while methods like VideoTree and VideoAgent caption frames and feed text to an LLM (e.g., GPT-4 text). The paper does not control for this — e.g., it does not run VideoTree with the same VLM and direct frame input, or clearly state why the comparison is still valid despite the backbone difference. The primary, clean comparisons (VAP vs. uniform sampling of the same VLM) are not affected, but the "SOTA" claim over these prior methods is on weaker footing.

2. **No statistical significance or variance reported.** Given that several margins are small (e.g., 64.6% vs. 63.5% on ActivityNet-QA, 40.5% vs. 39.6% on CLEVRER), the lack of confidence intervals or significance testing makes it impossible to judge whether these differences are reliable or due to noise. Single-run evaluation may be standard for large-scale benchmark experiments, but for fine-grained claims of superiority, some measure of variability is expected.

3. **Handling of open-ended (non-multiple-choice) QA is underspecified.** The method description and Algorithm 1 assume "question q and possible answers a" are fed as conditioning. ActivityNet-QA uses open-ended evaluation and NExT-QA includes open-ended questions. The paper does not specify how VAP handles cases where there are no predefined answer options — does it condition on the question alone? This should be clarified for reproducibility.

4. **The latent generation process is imprecisely specified.** Algorithm 1 says `˜h_{1:T} ← f({x_i}_{i∈S} | q, a)` — generating latents for all T frames — but the paper later describes chunking, memory banks, and RIFE interpolation. It is unclear how many frames are actually generated by the diffusion model (with 50 denoising steps) vs. interpolated, especially for very long videos. A precise breakdown of the generation pipeline would improve reproducibility.

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **End-to-end runtime comparison**: Reporting the total wall-clock time (generation + encoding + VLM inference) for a representative sample of videos would resolve the main efficiency concern. If the generation overhead is small relative to VLM savings, this would significantly strengthen the paper.
- **Answer-conditioning ablation**: Running VAP with question-only conditioning (no answer options) on a subset of data would clarify whether the answer set is driving frame selection.
- **Controlled comparison with prior methods**: Running VideoTree or VideoAgent with the same VLM and direct frame input would make the "SOTA" claim over these baselines cleaner.
- **Ablation on generation model choice**: Would a simpler flow-based predictor (without a diffusion head) work as the prior? This would test whether the specific choice of a full diffusion model is necessary or if the active perception idea is more general.
- **Failure case analysis**: The qualitative section only shows successes; discussing cases where the generation model produces poor latents and how this affects frame selection would add depth.

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a genuinely novel and well-motivated frame-selection method. The core idea — leveraging a generative video model as an active perception prior — is creative and the controlled experiments (VAP vs. uniform sampling of the same VLM) provide convincing evidence of effectiveness. However, two structural gaps significantly weaken the paper's central claims: (1) the efficiency metric ("frames per question") is presented as a cost savings but omits the substantial computational overhead of the generative prior, making the efficiency claim unverifiable; (2) conditioning frame selection on the answer set is not ablated, creating uncertainty about whether the method's gains come from principled active perception or answer-guided verification. These issues are addressable in revision but leave the paper in a state where its strongest claims are not fully supported by the evidence as presented.
