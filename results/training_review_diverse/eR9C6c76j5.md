## Summary

This paper presents ARGMINN, a mechanistically interpretable neural network for regulatory genomics where motifs and their syntax are directly encoded in the model's weights and activations, eliminating the need for complex *post hoc* analysis pipelines. The architecture uses a regularized convolutional layer to learn non-redundant motifs and a custom attention mechanism with an explicit memory stream to capture motif syntax. Extensive experiments demonstrate superior motif discovery, instance calling, robustness, and interpretable sequence generation validated by independent models.

## Strengths

**1. Direct encoding of motifs and syntax without *post hoc* pipelines.** ARGMINN's architecture (Section 3) is designed so that first-layer convolutional weights directly encode non-redundant motifs (via the filter-overlap regularizer, Eq. 2) and the modified attention mechanism (Eq. 3) reveals motif instances and syntactical rules from activations and attention scores in a single forward pass. This eliminates the need for fragile pipelines like DeepLIFTShap+MoDISco, which the paper demonstrates are slower, noisier, and less reliable.

**2. Demonstrated superior motif discovery accuracy and efficiency.** Across simulated and experimental datasets, ARGMINN recovered more relevant motifs with higher similarity to ground truth and fewer redundancies compared to standard CNN, ExplaiNN, and importance-score clustering (Figure 1b–d). For instance, on FOXA2 in HepG2, ARGMINN identified 4 motifs whose top match was a known relevant motif, versus the CNN identifying only 2 with weaker similarity. ARGMINN also consistently found the fewest extraneous motifs (Figure 1d).

**3. More accurate motif instance calling and syntax discovery in a forward pass.** ARGMINN's attention scores directly trace to filter activations, enabling motif instance calling with a single forward pass. On experimental REST binding data, ARGMINN recovered the unique spacing preferences of REST half-sites (Figure 2b). Quantitatively, ARGMINN's top-*k* called instances covered a higher fraction of ground-truth binding footprints than the FIMO+MoDISco pipeline, and even outperformed FIMO when scanning with ARGMINN's own motifs (Figure 2c).

**4. Robustness to background variation and adversarial attacks.** On simulated data with GC content ranging 5–95%, ARGMINN's performance degraded less than a standard CNN and ExplaiNN (Supplementary Figure S9a). Additionally, ARGMINN remained robust to adversarial sequences that fooled standard CNNs (dropping accuracy to 48–55%), because its filters encode biologically meaningful motifs rather than spurious patterns (Section 4.4).

**5. Interpretable generation of functional sequences validated by independent models.** After training on HepG2 accessibility, ARGMINN's discovered motifs and syntax were used to construct novel sequences whose predicted accessibility matched natural highly accessible sequences (p=0.75) and far exceeded shuffled backgrounds (p=1.78×10⁻³²). These results were confirmed with the independently trained Borzoi model (Figure 3b) and two additional oracles, demonstrating that ARGMINN's interpretations generalize beyond its own predictions.

**6. Effective QTL prioritization grounded in biological motif logic.** On known causal dsQTLs, ARGMINN more accurately distinguished causal from non-causal variants than baselines (Figure 2a), with causal mutations typically falling within ARGMINN-discovered motif instances and disrupting its prediction of accessibility.

**7. General applicability of the filter-overlap regularizer.** Applying the regularizer to a standard CNN improved the interpretability of its first-layer filters (Supplementary Figure S10c), indicating the regularizer can benefit existing genomic DNN architectures beyond ARGMINN.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

**1. Theorem 1 is substantially overclaimed relative to the evidence provided.**  
The paper states (Theorem 1) that "every configuration of motifs/subsequences which is definable by a sentence in first‑order logic (with positional variables) is recognizable by an ARGMINN classifier." The proof sketch (lines 191) is a single paragraph—one sentence about the motif-scanner encoding PWMs, and one sentence saying "each attention head learns one syntactical rule" built "up in complexity over layers." This does not constitute a proof. First-order logic over positions with motif predicates can express properties involving alternating quantifiers and arbitrary Boolean combinations, and the paper provides no construction, no bound on required layers, no discussion of what "recognizable" means (exact classification vs. approximation), and no treatment of quantifier alternation depth. This is not the paper's central contribution—the architecture and empirical results stand on their own—but the theorem as stated is not credible, and presenting it as a proven result weakens the paper's rigor. The authors should either supply a genuine proof for a meaningful restricted subclass (e.g., Boolean combinations of distance-constrained motif presence up to a fixed depth) or reframe the section as empirical observations about expressivity, which the paper's experiments already support.

### Minor

**1. No direct ablation study of the filter-overlap regularizer.**  
The regularizer (Equation 3) is central to ARGMINN's claim of monosemantic filters, but the paper does not include an explicit comparison of ARGMINN *with vs. without* this regularizer on the same architecture. The paper shows that applying the regularizer to a standard CNN improves its filter interpretability (Supplementary Figure S10c), which is helpful indirect evidence. However, a direct ablation on ARGMINN itself—measuring motif quality, redundancy, and the proportion of filters that match a single known motif under identical conditions—would cleanly separate the regularizer's effect from other architectural components. The paper acknowledges that the regularizer is "soft" and can be ignored (line 75), and that it still permits long motifs split across filters, but does not analyze how often such cases occur or whether the regularizer prevents polysemantic filters (a single filter learning multiple unrelated motifs). Adding this analysis would significantly strengthen the central interpretability claim.

**2. The syntax discovery via attention scores is not rigorously disentangled from motif occurrence patterns.**  
Because the syntax builder's keys and values are derived directly from motif activations, the attention scores may largely reflect motif match strength rather than independent syntax. The REST spacing preference shown in Figure 2b is consistent with what a standard CNN could learn from positional patterns alone. A controlled experiment that holds motif identity constant while varying only syntax (e.g., spacing, order) would more convincingly attribute syntax learning to the attention mechanism. The paper's empirical comparison to ExplaiNN (which cannot capture such syntax) partially addresses this, but a targeted synthetic experiment with controlled confounders would be cleaner.

**3. Predictive performance numbers are deferred to a supplementary table in the main text.**  
Line 203 states that "We then compared the predictive performance of ARGMINN to standard CNNs and ExplaiNN (Supplementary Table supptab:perf)" and acknowledges that ARGMINN "achieved competitive performance, but did not outperform its non-MI counterparts." Including these numbers (or a summary) in the main text would allow readers to directly assess the accuracy-interpretability trade-off without consulting supplementary material. This is a presentation issue, not a scientific one, but it matters given the paper's framing.

**4. QTL experiment tests only one dataset (DNase sensitivity).**  
The QTL prioritization result (Section 4.3, Figure 2) is shown for a single dataset. While the results are encouraging, demonstrating generalizability across additional QTL types (e.g., from different transcription factors or cell types) or including calibration of attention score magnitudes against mutation effect sizes would strengthen the claim that ARGMINN's predictions are grounded in biological causality rather than specific to this dataset.

## Novel Insights

Across the reviews, the most notable synthesized observation is that ARGMINN's architecture constitutes a genuine advance in how genomic interpretability can be achieved: rather than treating interpretability as a *post hoc* analysis applied to a black box, it builds interpretability into the model's inductive biases.
