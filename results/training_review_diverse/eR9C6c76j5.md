## Summary

The paper presents ARGMINN, a novel mechanistically interpretable neural network for regulatory genomics. The architecture embeds motif discovery directly into model weights via a filter-overlap regularizer that penalizes redundant filters, and uses a modified attention mechanism (the "syntax builder") to recover motif instances and their inter-motif syntax in a single forward pass. The paper provides empirical results on motif discovery, motif instance calling, QTL prioritization, robustness, and interpretable sequence generation, along with a theoretical expressivity claim.

## Strengths

- **Novel filter-overlap regularizer for monosemantic motif filters.** The regularizer (Eq. 4) penalizes different convolutional filters from attending to overlapping sequence positions, forcing each filter to encode a distinct motif. The paper shows that ARGMINN recovers known relevant motifs (e.g., FOX, HNF4, CEBP families on FOXA2 data) with fewer redundancies than standard CNN, ExplaiNN, or MoDISco, and that ARGMINN's motifs have higher similarity to ground-truth motifs (Figure 1).

- **Attention mechanism enables direct motif-instance and syntax discovery in a single forward pass.** The syntax builder derives keys/values directly from motif-scanner activations, so high attention scores trace back to specific motif positions. This is convincingly validated by the recovery of REST's known binding syntax (two half-sites with specific spacing and orientation, Figure 2b) and by the finding that ARGMINN's top-ranked instances cover more true binding sites than the MoDISco+FIMO pipeline (Figure 2c).

- **Demonstrated robustness to background variation attributable to biologically meaningful filter weights.** The paper reports that ARGMINN maintains tighter performance across a wide GC-content range (5%–95%) compared to standard CNN and ExplaiNN, and that adversarial sequences that fool a standard CNN have little effect on ARGMINN (Section 4.4). This supports the claim that ARGMINN's filters encode genuine motifs rather than spurious patterns.

- **Interpretable sequence generation validated by independent oracles.** Sequences constructed by sampling ARGMINN's discovered motifs and syntax rules are predicted to be as accessible as natural HepG2 sequences by Borzoi (Section 4.5), providing a mechanistic justification for the designed function.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The filter-overlap regularizer — the paper's core methodological contribution — is never properly ablated.** The paper shows that adding the regularizer to a standard CNN improves interpretability (Supplementary Figure), which is a useful one-directional test. However, the *inverse* ablation is missing: training ARGMINN *without* the regularizer (λ_o = 0, with and without the L1 penalty) and comparing motif quality and monosemanticity. Without this, we cannot attribute the claimed interpretability to the regularizer versus other architectural choices (the single convolutional layer, the modified attention mechanism, or simply the training procedure). The claim that filters "directly encode non-redundant motifs" due to the regularizer is weakened without this control.

- **Theorem 1 is presented without a proper proof, and the theoretical section is incomplete as a formal contribution.** Theorem 1 asserts that ARGMINN can recognize any first-order-logic-definable motif configuration. The "proof sketch" is three sentences of high-level intuition that do not define the formal language, specify what "recognizable" means in terms of ARGMINN's architecture, or establish any formal connection between attention layers and first-order logic. The corollary about ExplaiNN's limitations is similarly unsubstantiated. If the theoretical section is intended as a motivation, it should be reframed; if it is intended as a formal contribution, it requires a rigorous proof (or at minimum a clear statement of assumptions and formalism). The experimental REST result (90.9% vs. 73.4%) provides empirical support for ARGMINN's expressivity relative to ExplaiNN but does not substitute for a proof of the theorem's universal claim.

### Minor

- **The QTL prioritization section (Section 4.3) lacks specificity.** The paper states ARGMINN "was capable of classifying/prioritizing the causal dsQTLs much more accurately" but does not name the baseline models compared, report the evaluation metric, or describe the cross-validation scheme in the main text or figure caption. Figure 5a's caption refers only to "compared to other architectures" without specifying which ones. This makes the result difficult to evaluate.

- **The motif instance calling evaluation reports recall at top-k but does not report precision (false-positive rates) in the main text.** ARGMINN could achieve high recall by making many calls; precision is needed to assess specificity. The paper references a supplementary table ("motif-hit-pr") which may address this, but the main-text figure is incomplete without precision information.

- **The p = 0.75 claim of equivalence in the sequence generation experiment is statistically problematic.** The paper states that generated sequences are "just as accessible as natural sequences" based on p = 0.75 from what appears to be a null-hypothesis significance test. A non-significant p-value does not confirm equivalence — it simply fails to reject the null. The paper should report effect sizes (e.g., mean accessibility difference with confidence intervals) or use an equivalence test.

- **The attention-score threshold for calling motif instances (e.g., > 0.9) is stated as an example but no sensitivity analysis is provided.** The threshold choice could affect the motif-instance calling results, and its impact is unexplored.

- **The notation in the filter-overlap loss (Equation 4) is ambiguous.** The filter weights W have shape n_f × w × 4, making a single filter a w × 4 matrix. The indexing notation `W_a[α, β]` where both α and β range over [0, w-1] does not clearly distinguish between the width dimension and the nucleotide dimension. Clarification is needed to verify the correctness of the overlap computation.

### Trivial
- None that survive filtering.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Ablation of the attention mechanism.** Comparing ARGMINN's modified attention (single query, memory stream) to standard multi-head attention (with attention scores extracted similarly) would clarify whether the modified attention or the filter regularizer drives instance-calling improvements.
- **Computational cost comparison.** The paper criticizes existing pipelines (e.g., MoDISco) as "computationally expensive" but does not report training or inference time for ARGMINN versus baselines. Given the complexity of the filter-overlap loss (O(ℓ·n_f·w) per sequence per step), readers need to know whether interpretability comes at a significant computational cost.
- **Ledidi-based validation against actual experimental binding data.** The Ledidi results compare predicted binding strengths; validation against in vitro or in vivo binding measurements would further strengthen claims about filter interpretability.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface the following insight: the paper's architecture is genuinely creative in combining filter monosemanticity regularization with a memory-stream attention mechanism, but the evaluation design lacks the critical control of ablating the regularizer from ARGMINN itself. This is a recurring pattern in MI papers — the system as a whole works well, but the component responsible for the claimed property is not isolated. Validating ARGMINN requires not just showing it works, but showing *why* it works (regularizer vs. architecture). The REST syntax recovery experiment (Figure 2b) and the cross-model regularizer transfer (CNN + regularizer in Discussion) partially address this, but the direct ablation remains absent.

## Suggestions

1. **Ablate the filter-overlap regularizer.** Train ARGMINN with λ_o = 0 (and separately λ_l = 0) and report: (a) number of redundant/dispersed motifs, (b) motif similarity to ground truth, and (c) monosemanticity of filters. This directly validates the paper's central design choice and is the single most impactful improvement.
2. **Either provide a formal proof of Theorem 1 (in appendix) or downgrade it to a design motivation/conjecture.** The current pseudo-theorem does not contribute as a formal result; either prove it or reframe it honestly.
3. **Name the QTL baselines in the main text and report the evaluation metric.** State which architectures were compared and what metric (AUC, accuracy, etc.) was used.
4. **Add precision alongside recall for motif instance calling** (or report PR-AUC if supplementary data supports it). Also test sensitivity to the attention-score threshold.
5. **Report effect sizes with confidence intervals for the sequence generation equivalence claim** rather than relying on a non-significant NHST p-value.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a genuinely novel and well-motivated architecture, with broad empirical validation across multiple tasks. The writing is clear and the ambition is commendable. However, the evaluation has several gaps: the signature regularizer is never ablated, the theoretical expressivity claim is presented without a proper proof, and several experimental details are underspecified in the main text. These weaknesses are addressable and do not invalidate the core contribution, but they are substantive enough to require revision. The paper would be strengthened considerably by addressing these points, particularly the ablation.
