## Summary

This paper investigates attention entropy collapse (where attention probabilities concentrate on a single token) in Transformer training. Using a simplified model of stacked self-attention layers on an in-context linear regression task, the paper demonstrates empirically that softmax-based attention leads to entropy collapse and gradient explosion, while Lipschitz kernel attention (using ReLU or ELU+1 as kernel functions) maintains stable entropy and gradients. The paper argues that the Lipschitz property of the kernel function is responsible for this stability and that entropy collapse causes gradient explosion via the increased Frobenius norm of the attention probability matrix.

## Strengths

- **Empirical demonstration of divergent training dynamics between softmax and kernel attention:** Figure 1 clearly shows that softmax-based attention drives entropy to zero and gradient norms to explode around step 20, while ReLU and ELU+1 maintain high entropy and stable gradients throughout training. Figure 2 further visualizes that softmax attention matrices become one-hot vectors while kernel matrices remain diffuse. This is the paper's strongest concrete finding.

- **Controlled experiment isolating variance sensitivity:** Figure 3 presents a clean synthetic experiment where key variance σ² is varied independently of learning. Softmax entropy collapses sharply as variance increases (histograms concentrated near 0 for σ≥4), while Lipschitz kernel entropy stays stable. This directly demonstrates a key behavioral difference and is the paper's most methodologically rigorous contribution.

- **Empirical link between attention probability norms and gradient norms:** Figure 5 shows that softmax's ‖P‖_F rises to ≈√N (the theoretical maximum for one-hot distributions) and correlates with gradient explosion, while kernel attention's ‖P‖_F stays ≈1 and gradients remain small. The correlation, though not proof of causation, is a genuine empirical observation.

- **Shows QK-LayerNorm is insufficient:** Including QK-LayerNorm as a baseline (Figure 1 dashed purple) and demonstrating it also suffers entropy collapse strengthens the case that the softmax function itself — not just large logit magnitudes — is the root cause.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Confounded comparison undermines the causal attribution to "Lipschitzness":** The paper compares standard softmax attention (exp + row-wise softmax normalization) with kernelized attention (feature-map dot product + different denominator structure). These differ in three confounded aspects: (i) the similarity function (exp vs. φ(Q)·φ(K) feature dot product), (ii) the normalization scheme (row-wise Σ_j exp(Z_ij) vs. φ(Q_i)·Σ_j φ(K_j)), and (iii) the Lipschitz property (non-Lipschitz vs. Lipschitz). The paper attributes the observed stability entirely to the Lipschitz property of φ, but the evidence does not isolate this factor. A controlled test within the *same* attention framework — e.g., replacing exp with a Lipschitz activation (such as ReLU followed by L1 normalization) in standard softmax attention — would be needed to substantiate the causal claim. The paper mentions ReLUFormer (Shen et al., 2023) as doing exactly this but does not include it as an experimental baseline. The paper's central claim that "Lipschitzness of the attention plays an important role" is a reasonable hypothesis but remains unsubstantiated by the current experiments due to this confound.

- **The theoretical argument linking entropy collapse to gradient explosion is incomplete:** Section 3.5 uses Eqn (9) — an upper bound on the input-output Jacobian from Dasoulas et al. (2021) — and argues that as ‖P‖_F increases to √N, gradients explode. However, Eqn (9) is an *upper bound*, not a lower bound or equality. An increasing upper bound does not imply the actual gradient increases; the bound could simply become looser. The argument also conflates the input-output Jacobian of a single attention layer with the gradient of the loss w.r.t. parameters, which depends on the full chain rule across layers. The paper acknowledges this limitation implicitly by presenting correlations in Figure 5, but the text (e.g., "Therefore, the more extreme the distribution of attention probabilities is, the larger the gradient becomes") states the causal relationship as established rather than as a plausible mechanism supported by correlation. This gap weakens the paper's claimed theoretical contribution.

### Minor

- **Simplified model architecture limits generalizability to practical Transformers:** The experiments use a Transformer consisting only of self-attention layers, without residual connections, layer normalization, or MLPs. The paper justifies this choice (Section 3.1) by citing prior work using proxy models, and the simplification is defensible for isolating the attention mechanism. However, the paper does not discuss whether the observed gradient explosion would persist with residual connections (which add a direct gradient path bypassing the attention layer) or layer normalization (which controls activation scales). This is a scope limitation that should be explicitly acknowledged.

- **No comparison against existing entropy-collapse mitigation methods mentioned in Section 2.3:** The paper reviews NormSoftmax and σReparam as methods that address entropy collapse through variance control or spectral normalization, but does not include either as an experimental baseline. Comparing Lipschitz kernel attention against these would clarify whether it offers additional benefits beyond existing approaches, or whether the same stability can be achieved by controlling input variance alone. Including QK-LayerNorm is a partial concession, but QK-LayerNorm is not the strongest baseline for entropy collapse specifically.

- **Single-run results without statistical variability:** All figures show single training runs with smooth curves. Given the sensitivity of training dynamics near collapse, reporting variance across multiple seeds would strengthen confidence. This does not invalidate the findings but is a standard expectation for empirical work.

- **Experimental hyperparameters are underspecified:** The paper does not state the number of layers, hidden dimension, sequence length, optimizer, learning rate, or number of training steps for the main experiments (Figures 1–5). While the hard rules instruct me to remove trivial reproducibility nitpicks, these are core experimental parameters that a reader would need to assess or reproduce the work.

## Nice-to-Haves

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a genuine empirical observation — that kernelized attention maintains higher entropy than softmax attention across training — and provides useful synthetic experiments (Figure 3) isolating variance sensitivity. However, the paper's central causal claims (that Lipschitzness *causes* the stability, and that entropy collapse *causes* gradient explosion via ‖P‖_F) are incompletely supported: the first is confounded by architectural differences, and the second rests on an upper-bound argument plus correlation. The contribution is still real — the empirical phenomena are worth reporting — but the analysis as presented overclaims the causal mechanism and would need strengthening (particularly with a controlled Lipschitz-vs-non-Lipschitz comparison within the same architecture) to be a strong paper. As an empirical observation paper documenting the stability difference, it is borderline acceptable; as a paper claiming to have identified the causal mechanism, it falls short.
