## Summary

This paper proposes SeMv-3D, a general text-to-3D framework that aims to achieve both semantic and multi-view consistency simultaneously. The framework has two stages: a Triplane Prior Learner (TPL) that fine-tunes a pretrained T2I diffusion model (SD2.1) to produce a triplane prior (three orthogonal views: front, top, side) with spatial correspondences enforced by a novel Orthogonal Attention (OA) mechanism, followed by a Semantic-aligned View Synthesizer (SVS) that encodes the pixel-space triplane via DINO and aligns it with textual semantics through cross-attention and OA before NeRF-style rendering. A batch sampling and rendering strategy enables generating arbitrary views in a single feed-forward pass. Experiments compare against MVDream, Shap-E, Point-E, VolumeDiffusion, and 3DTopia on Objaverse.

## Strengths

- **Novel orthogonal attention for triplane spatial consistency.** The OA mechanism (Sec. 3.2.2, Eqs. 3–6) is a principled design that explicitly correlates pixels across orthogonal triplane axes (e.g., a pixel in the xy-plane attends to corresponding pixels in the xz- and yz-planes sharing the same coordinate). The qualitative ablation (Fig. 6) shows OA outperforms temporal attention (used in MVDream/SVD) at preserving fine-grained geometry and texture for the sparse triplane setting. This is the paper's strongest technical contribution.

- **Batch sampling and rendering enables any-view generation from a single T2I forward pass.** The method (Sec. 3.3.2) runs the diffusion model once to obtain triplane latents, then renders any number of views via batched NeRF-style ray marching. Table 1 quantifies this capability: the method supports "Any" views per step vs. 4 for MVDream and 1 for prior-based methods. While the rendering itself follows standard (EG3D/NeRF) practice, the coupling of a one-step triplane prior with arbitrary-view rendering is a meaningful capability advance over fine-tuning-based methods that must regenerate for each view set.

- **User study provides direct evidence for the paper's central claim.** The user study (Sec. 4.4, Table 1) with 48 users across 25 prompts reports SeMv-3D achieving 52.1% preference for semantic consistency and 55.8% for multi-view consistency, both winning over MVDream (33.3% and 14.6%). This is the most direct evidence that the method delivers on its stated goal of simultaneous consistency.

- **Generalization experiment demonstrates decoupling of structure and semantics.** The text reconstruction experiment (Sec. 4.5, Fig. 5b) — varying only the text prompt while keeping the triplane prior fixed — shows the framework can modulate appearance and texture while preserving 3D spatial structure. This goes beyond standard evaluation and validates the conceptual separation between TPL (3D structure) and SVS (semantic alignment).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No quantitative multi-view consistency metrics.** The paper's central claim is simultaneous semantic *and* multi-view consistency, yet the only objective metrics reported (Clip Score, Aesthetic Score, lines 227–228) are computed on a single front view. There are no LPIPS between rendered views, no PSNR on held-out views, no 3D reconstruction metrics (Chamfer distance, FID on rendered multi-view sets), and no evaluation of cross-view coherence. The user study partially fills this gap, but the absence of any quantitative multi-view metric is a significant omission for a paper whose main thesis rests on a consistency claim. The reported Clip Score advantage over MVDream (30.26 vs. 30.09) is small and comes without error bars or significance testing.

- **Ablation studies are entirely qualitative.** The ablations (Sec. 4.5, Figs. 6, 7a) show rendered images that visually improve as components (OR, TO, OA, CA) are added, but there are no corresponding quantitative metrics (Clip Scores, LPIPS, or user study results per ablation condition). Given that the ablations are central to attributing performance to each proposed module, the lack of numerical evidence makes it difficult to assess the contribution of individual components, and the risk of cherry-picked examples is non-negligible.

### Minor

- **Triplane representation justification is thin.** The paper defines the triplane prior as three pixel-space RGB images (front, top, side views) generated by a diffusion model, then encodes them via DINO into tokens for NeRF rendering. This departs sharply from the standard latent triplane formulation (EG3D, 3DTopia) where the triplane is a compact latent feature map decoded by a tiny MLP. The paper cites EG3D for the triplane concept but does not discuss why pixel-space orthogonal views are sufficient to encode full 3D geometry, nor why the circuitous route of generating RGB images → encoding to tokens → decoding to a radiance field is preferable to directly learning latent triplane features. A dedicated justification is needed.

- **OA / UNet integration is underspecified for reproducibility.** The OA operates on three planes (P_xy, P_xz, P_yz) simultaneously within a UNet designed for single-image generation. The paper states that OR/TO modules are "plugged into each level of the UNet network before upscale and downscale" (line 90) and that OA is appended after each OR module (line 102), but it never specifies how the UNet produces three output planes — whether via three separate decoders, concatenation as channels, or another mechanism. The tensor shapes, the resulting computation graph, and the modifications to the base SD2.1 UNet are absent, making the method difficult to reproduce.

- **"Any view" claim is not validated at non-canonical angles.** All qualitative results show views at canonical angles (0°, 90°, 180°, 270° for comparison with MVDream; 60° intervals for comparison with prior-based methods). No images are shown at e.g., azimuth 37°, elevation 15°, or other non-axis-aligned viewpoints. Since the "any view" capability is listed as a property in Table 1 and highlighted in the contributions, the absence of any demonstration at non-canonical angles weakens the claim. Given that the rendering follows standard NeRF/EG3D procedures, the claim is plausible but unsubstantiated by evidence in the paper.

- **Clip Score advantage over MVDream is marginal and lacks error bars.** The reported Clip Score (30.26 vs. 30.09) is a 0.17 difference. Without error bars, confidence intervals, or significance testing, this could easily be within noise. The Aesthetic Score is actually lower than MVDream's (4.43 vs. 4.84), which the paper rationalizes as "closer to the real situation" — a post-hoc argument without supporting evidence.

- **How ground-truth depth and masks are obtained from Objaverse is not described.** The rendering loss (Eq. 8) includes depth and mask terms (λ_D, λ_M), and the paper trains on Objaverse. Objaverse does not natively provide depth or mask annotations for all objects, yet the paper does not explain how these are rendered or computed. This is a missing implementation detail that affects reproducibility.

- **User study lacks methodological details.** The study (Sec. 4.4) reports 48 users, 25 prompts, and 6 groups per user, but does not state (a) whether the order of methods was randomized across users, (b) whether users saw the text prompt alongside each method's outputs, or (c) whether any inter-rater reliability metric (e.g., Fleiss' kappa) was computed. These details affect the reliability of the results.

### Trivial

- **The characterization of 3DTopia's "Views/One-Step = 1" in Table 1 is ambiguous.** 3DTopia generates a triplane from the diffusion model in one step, which can then be rendered from any view. The column header could be interpreted as "views directly generated by the diffusion model per step," but this is not clarified in the table caption or the comparison section. This does not affect the paper's conclusions but is a clarity issue.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Reporting LPIPS between random view pairs or FID on multi-view renders would strengthen the multi-view consistency claim.
- Quantitative ablation metrics (Clip Score and a multi-view consistency metric for each ablation condition) would replace the purely qualitative figure.
- Showing a few non-canonical views (e.g., azimuth 37°, elevation 15°) with the corresponding images would directly validate the "any view" claim.
- Including the 25 evaluation prompts (e.g., in supplementary material) would aid reproducibility.
- Reporting inference time / computational cost of generating N views would help assess the practical advantage of one-step inference.
- Showing representative failure cases (beyond the note about not converging well) would improve the paper's honesty and help readers judge limitations.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation that emerges from the reviews is that the paper's architecture implicitly separates *3D structure learning* (TPL enforces spatial correspondence via OA on orthogonal views) from *semantic alignment* (SVS injects text conditioning into triplane latents via cross-attention). The text reconstruction experiment (Fig. 5b) vindicates this design choice: varying the text prompt while holding the triplane prior fixed produces different appearances without breaking multi-view consistency. This suggests that the orthogonal attention mechanism genuinely captures view-invariant 3D properties that are separable from semantic modulation — a property that most end-to-end fine-tuning methods lack. If the evaluation gaps (quantitative multi-view metrics, quantitative ablations) were filled, this architecture could be a meaningful step toward controllable 3D generation where structure and semantics are disentangled.

## Suggestions

1. **Add at least one quantitative multi-view consistency metric.** The most impactful addition would be LPIPS between pairs of rendered views at random azimuths (excluding training views) compared against MVDream and the best prior-based methods. Reporting average LPIPS over 10–20 view pairs per prompt would directly substantiate the central multi-view consistency claim.

2. **Provide quantitative ablation results.** For each ablated component (OR, TO, OA, CA), report Clip Score and LPIPS between views on a held-out set of prompts. A small table with these numbers would dramatically strengthen the attribution of performance to individual modules.

3. **Show non-canonical views.** Include at least one qualitative example with renders at non-axis-aligned angles (e.g., azimuth 37°, elevation 15°) and report LPIPS over such views to demonstrate that quality does not degrade.

4. **Specify the UNet architecture modifications.** Add a brief description or a table showing how three output planes are produced in the UNet (three decoders? channel concatenation?) and where OA modules are inserted with tensor shapes. This is essential for reproducibility.

5. **Add error bars or confidence intervals** to the Clip Score and Aesthetic Score results.

## Score and Decision

The paper proposes a genuinely novel architectural idea (orthogonal attention for triplane consistency) and a well-motivated two-stage pipeline that separates 3D structure learning from semantic alignment. The user study provides direct evidence for the claimed benefits, and the qualitative results are visually compelling. However, the evaluation has significant gaps: the paper's central claim of multi-view consistency is not backed by any quantitative metric beyond the user study, ablation studies are qualitative only, the Clip Score advantage is marginal and unaveraged, and the "any view" property is not demonstrated at non-canonical angles. These weaknesses are addressable but, in the paper's current form, leave the contribution insufficiently supported.
