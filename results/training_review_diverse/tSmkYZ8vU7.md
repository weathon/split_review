## Summary

This paper makes two contributions to interactive image matting: (1) **COCO-Matting**, a large-scale dataset of 38,251 human instance-level alpha mattes derived from COCO by fusing accessory masks and converting binary segmentation masks into continuous alpha mattes via a pretrained trimap-based matting network (DiffMatte); and (2) **SEMat**, a SAM-based interactive matting framework that adds LoRA fine-tuning, learnable matting tokens, a matting adapter, a lightweight UNet-style decoder, regularization loss, and trimap loss. Experiments across seven diverse benchmarks show consistent improvements over prior methods (MatAny, MAM, SmartMat, InstMatt).

## Strengths

- **COCO-Matting dataset is a meaningful resource.** It is the largest human matting dataset with both "complex" (multi-instance, occlusion) and "natural" labels — a gap identified in prior work. Table (tab:dataset) shows it uniquely combines complex scenes and natural imagery. The Performance Improvement Analysis (Table tab:dataset_exp) demonstrates that training with COCO-Matting yields substantial gains over P3M-10K and RefMatte under the same method (MAM or SEMat), confirming its practical value.

- **Comprehensive and well-structured ablation study.** The ablation (Table tab:ablation) cleanly separates contributions into four dimensions: (a) COCO Mask quality, (b) Network components (LoRA, matting tokens/adapter, prompt enhancement), (c) Dataset construction steps (Accessory Fusion, Mask-to-Matte), and (d) Training objectives (regularization loss, trimap loss). Each component shows measurable improvement, with average SAD dropping from 29.91 to 6.29. This provides solid evidence for the design choices.

- **Strong empirical evaluation across diverse benchmarks.** The method is tested on seven datasets covering human, animal, object, synthetic, and instance matting. On all datasets, SEMat variants (SAM, HQ-SAM, SAM2) consistently outperform prior SOTA methods. The instance matting results on HIM2K (Table tab:Instance) are particularly notable, showing SEMat (SAM2) achieves 36.1% average improvement over the dedicated instance matting method InstMatt.

- **Generalization beyond human-centric training.** Although COCO-Matting focuses on humans, SEMat shows strong improvements on AIM-500 (object matting) and AM-2K (animal matting), demonstrating that the real-scene priors learned from human data transfer to other categories.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "Impro." (average relative improvement) metric in Table 1 is not explicitly defined.** The paper (§5) states it is "the average relative improvement on widely used five metrics" but does not specify what it is relative to. The baseline is inferable (MatAny has "-", suggesting it serves as the reference), but readers cannot independently verify the computation without an explicit formula. Moreover, the aggregate metric can mask individual failures: e.g., on AIM-500, SEMat (SAM) has SAD 11.46 vs. SmartMat's 11.19 — worse on this metric — yet its "Impro." (64.1%) surpasses SmartMat's (56.2%). While the individual metrics are reported separately, the paper's central summary column needs a clear definition.

- **The method-to-baseline comparison does not control for model capacity.** MAM and MatAny freeze the SAM backbone and train only a lightweight decoder, whereas SEMat introduces LoRA fine-tuning (rank 16), learnable matting tokens, a matting adapter, and a UNet decoder. The paper does not report parameter counts, FLOPs, or inference speeds, making it impossible to attribute the reported gains to specific architectural innovations rather than simply having a larger, more heavily tuned model. The ablation study (Table b - Network) partially addresses this by showing incremental gains within SEMat's framework, but the headline comparisons against MAM/MatAny conflate innovation with capacity.

### Minor

- **The COCO-Matting ground truth is generated by a pretrained model (DiffMatte) without independent quality validation.** The Mask-to-Matte step (§3.2) uses DiffMatte to convert binary masks into alpha mattes, meaning the "ground truth" is actually another model's predictions. The paper provides no human evaluation, comparison with hand-labeled mattes, or error analysis of the generated mattes. While downstream performance gains (training on COCO-Matting improves real-benchmark results) serve as indirect validation, a sample-level quality check would substantially strengthen the dataset contribution.

- **Dataset ablation does not control for size.** In Table (c - Dataset), replacing RefMatte (13,181) or P3M-10K (10,421) with COCO-Matting (38,251) yields improvements, but COCO-Matting is 2.9–3.7× larger. Some of the reported gains could stem from sheer scale rather than dataset quality differences.

- **The Accessory Fusion threshold (τ=0.8) and dilation parameters are set empirically without sensitivity analysis.** These choices (§3.2) could affect which accessories are merged and thus impact dataset quality. A brief sensitivity study would increase confidence.

- **No variance or confidence intervals reported for main results.** Given that many numbers are close across methods (e.g., SEMat variants on AIM-500, SIM), variance estimates would help assess statistical significance.

### Trivial
- The paper does not report computational cost (training time, inference speed, model parameters) relative to baselines, which is useful for assessing practicality.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A human evaluation or comparison with hand-annotated mattes on a small sample of COCO-Matting would validate the dataset quality directly.
- Training SmartMat (or another non-SAM method) with COCO-Matting vs. alternatives would help separate the value of the dataset from the value of SEMat's architecture.
- Controlling dataset size (e.g., subsampling COCO-Matting to match RefMatte's size) in the dataset ablation would cleanly separate quality from quantity.

## Removed Points

- **"Circular dependency" framing (Point 1 from harsh critic):** The critique that training on COCO-Matting is "training to predict what DiffMatte would predict" is overstated. The downstream experiments show that training on COCO-Matting improves performance on *independent real benchmarks*, which would not happen if the dataset only encoded DiffMatte's biases. The concern is real but the "fatal" severity is unwarranted; it has been moved to Minor.

- **"Never trained on purely natural data":** The reviewer claims the method is never trained on natural data. In fact, COCO-Matting uses real COCO images as backgrounds; the mattes are generated but the images are natural. The combined training uses synthetic + real-scene data. This is a misreading.

- **"Novelty relative to HQ-SAM is incremental":** This is a matter of judgment, not a factual error. The paper explicitly acknowledges the similarity (§4) and clearly distinguishes its multiple matting tokens (producing a trimap) from HQ-SAM's single HQ-token. Not a valid weakness.

- **"Missing ethical considerations about COCO biases":** This is a generic expectation better suited as a suggestion than a weakness. It does not affect the paper's core technical contribution.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface a genuine tension in the paper: the dataset contribution (COCO-Matting) is validated primarily through the method contribution (SEMat), and vice versa. The harsh critic correctly notes that the dataset's pseudo-label quality is unverified, while the method's gains are not cleanly disentangled from increased model capacity. However, the strength finder correctly notes that the paper's ablation structure does isolate individual contributions — just not the dataset-method interaction. A more incisive experiment would be to pair SEMat's architecture with different training datasets (already done) *and* pair MAM with COCO-Matting (also done in Table tab:dataset_exp), which the paper does provide: MAM + COCO-Matting achieves 80.7% relative improvement on HIM2K vs. MAM + P3M-10K, demonstrating the dataset's value independent of SEMat. This partially bridges the gap between the two critiques.

## Suggestions

1. **Define "Impro." explicitly** with the formula and baseline reference (MatAny). Consider also reporting per-dataset per-metric relative improvements in a supplementary table.
2. **Report parameter counts** for SEMat variants and baselines, and ideally run a controlled experiment where MAM/MatAny are also LoRA-fine-tuned to isolate architectural innovations.
3. **Add a small human validation study** for COCO-Matting: e.g., randomly sample 100 generated mattes, compare with hand-refined versions, and report quality metrics.
4. **Ablate dataset size** by subsampling COCO-Matting to match RefMatte's count to separate quality from quantity effects.

## Score and Decision

This paper makes two solid contributions — a large-scale real-scene matting dataset and an effective SAM-based matting framework — validated across extensive benchmarks with thorough ablations. The weaknesses are real but addressable: the "Impro." metric needs explicit definition, the model-capacity confound should be acknowledged/controlled, and the dataset would benefit from quality validation. These do not invalidate the core claims but should be addressed in a revision.
