## Summary

The paper introduces a principled method for inducing full covariance structure in internal-coordinate representations of protein backbones. Rather than estimating the complex (2M-5)² covariance entries from data, the method uses the Maximum Entropy principle with Lagrange multipliers to impose constraints on 3D atom fluctuations. This reduces the prediction space to just M Lagrange multipliers (one per atom). The approach is demonstrated in a VAE across unimodal (low-data: 25–400 structures) and multimodal (high-data: 500k+ structures) settings, showing meaningful density estimates on several protein systems.

## Strengths

- **Principled theoretical foundation.** The derivation from the Maximum Entropy principle with constraint-enforcing Lagrange multipliers (Eqs. 6–9) is clean and well-motivated. The connection between 3D atom-position constraints and internal-coordinate covariance via the G_m derivative matrices is mathematically sound.

- **Massive parameter reduction.** The method reduces the prediction space from (2M-5)² covariance entries to M Lagrange multipliers (one per atom). For 1pga (168 backbone atoms), this is a reduction from ~110,000 to 168 values — a practical enabler for full-backbone internal-coordinate models.

- **Demonstrated effectiveness in extremely low-data regimes.** The VAE with induced covariance produces Ramachandran plots and atom-position variance profiles that match NMR reference ensembles for 1unc (25 structures) and 1fsd (41 structures), where baselines (fixed κ-prior, standard estimator) produce much larger or unphysical fluctuations. This is a genuine contribution — no existing method scales internal-coordinate density models to full backbones with so few examples.

- **Rotational invariance by construction.** Because the model operates entirely in internal coordinates and constructs constraints from the predicted mean structure, it avoids SE(3) invariance requirements without needing data augmentation or equivariant layers.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The first-order (linear) approximation that underpins the entire covariance induction is not validated.** The derivation converts the constraint E[Δx_m²] = C_m into the quadratic form E[Δκᵀ G_m Δκ] by linearising Δx_m as a Taylor expansion (Eqs. 7–8, Section 3.3). The paper acknowledges this as a "small angle approximation" and even adds an auxiliary loss to keep Lagrange multipliers in a range where the approximation is valid, but it never measures the actual approximation error. For the multimodal cases (cln025, 2f4k) where fluctuations involve unfolding, the linearisation may miss substantial nonlinear components. Since every downstream result depends on this step, the omission of any validation — e.g., comparing exact Δx_m² vs. Δκᵀ G_m Δκ for random perturbations from the reference ensembles — leaves the method's foundation unverified. **Why it matters:** If the approximation errors are large, the induced covariance structure systematically misrepresents the true constraints, and the resulting density is misspecified regardless of how well the VAE is trained.

2. **The experimental evaluation does not isolate the contribution of induced covariance from the VAE architecture.** The paper lists "κ-prior (learned)" (a VAE with a learned diagonal covariance) as a baseline but the unimodal results (Fig. 2) compare the method primarily against "κ-prior (fixed)" and the standard estimator. Without seeing the learned-diagonal baseline in the main unimodal comparison, it is impossible to tell whether the improvement comes from the induced *full* covariance or simply from having *any* learned covariance (the learned diagonal could already capture much of the variance structure). For the multimodal setting, the constraints improve modestly over the fixed κ-prior for cln025 but barely for 2f4k, while a flow model (without constraints) outperforms both — raising the question of whether the constraints or the VAE's latent capacity drive the results. **Why it matters:** The paper's central claim is that *induced covariance from 3D constraints* improves density modelling. Without isolating this factor from the architecture, the claim is not cleanly supported.

### Minor

1. **The Lagrange multiplier prediction couples covariance to mean structure without discussion of alternatives.** The U-Net predicts λ from the pairwise distance matrix of the *decoded mean* structure (Section 3.4). This makes covariance a deterministic function of the mean for fixed U-Net weights — there is no mechanism to vary covariance independently for different structures sharing the same mean. The paper does not discuss whether this is restrictive, nor does it ablate alternatives (e.g., predicting λ from the latent code directly). This is a design choice, but its implications for multimodal ensembles (where distinct conformations could have similar mean structures) are unexplored.

2. **The multimodal evaluation shows the method underperforming a baseline without constraints.** The flow-based model (Köhler et al., 2023) captures more modes and produces more accurate TICA free energy landscapes. The paper honestly acknowledges the VAE is "not as expressive," but this leaves uncertainty about whether induced covariance helps in settings with large conformational changes. A controlled comparison — e.g., applying the induced covariance to a more expressive backbone model — would clarify the method's independent utility.

3. **No quantitative joint-distribution metric in the main text.** The in-text evaluation relies on Ramachandran plots (2D marginal), atom-position variance profiles (1D marginal), and TICA free energy landscapes (2D projection). While these are informative, the paper references quantitative tables (Tab. quant_uni, Tab. quant_multi) only in the appendix. Including at least one in-text quantitative measure (e.g., negative log-likelihood, Wasserstein distance between reference and sample distributions) would strengthen standalone credibility.

### Trivial

- The "low data regime" characterization for 1pga (400 samples for ~107 dimensions) could benefit from a brief discussion of sample complexity relative to dimensionality. This does not affect the conclusions but clarifies the framing.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface one genuinely insightful observation beyond the paper's own contributions: the tension between the linear approximation and the multimodal regime. The paper's Discussion acknowledges fluctuations are "too small," but neither the reviews nor the paper fully connect this to the linearisation's validity range. Specifically: the constraints force fluctuations to stay within the linear regime, which may be *why* the method produces "too small" fluctuations — the approximation itself limits the fluctuation amplitude the method can capture. This suggests that extending the method to larger motions may require either a higher-order expansion or a multi-step generative process (as the paper hints with diffusion models), rather than simply a more expressive VAE. This insight turns a stated limitation from apologetic to mechanistic and points to a concrete research direction.
