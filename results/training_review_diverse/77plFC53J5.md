## Summary

This paper identifies repeated feature computations across time steps in SNNs (Feature Overlapping Phenomenon) and proposes Temporal Differential Decoupling (TDD) to separate static from dynamic features, plus a Gradient Sensitivity Criterion (GSC) to filter insensitive features in the differential domain. The DDLA algorithm is introduced to reduce computational cost. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 and Spikedriven Transformer, the method reports up to 80.9% spike reduction per timestep and 57.8% total spike reduction.

## Strengths

1. **Temporal Differential Decoupling (TDD) provides a principled decomposition of SNN computation.** The derivation in Section 4.1 (Eq. 5–7 and Table 1) rewrites the SNN forward pass so that the output at time T is the sum of the previous output plus differential updates. This algebraic rearrangement is clean and directly reveals which feature computations are redundant across time steps.

2. **Gradient Sensitivity Criterion (GSC) for feature selection in the differential domain.** Using a first-order Taylor expansion (Eq. 8–10), the paper derives a measurable criterion ζ(O_{l,i}^t) = |δL/δO_{l,i}^t · O_{l,i}^t| (Definition 2) to identify which differential features matter most. This provides a principled, gradient-based metric rather than ad-hoc thresholding, and the Taylor-motivated derivation gives it theoretical grounding.

3. **Empirical demonstration of substantial spike reduction.** The paper reports up to 80.9% reduction in spikes per timestep and 57.8% total spike reduction on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 at T=6 (Section 7, Figure 5). These are non-trivial reductions that, if accuracy is indeed maintained, would represent a meaningful advance for efficient SNN inference.

4. **Theoretical error bounds for the approximation.** Theorems 1 and 2 provide formal error bounds showing that the approximation error decreases with more time steps and can be controlled via the filtering ratio k. This provides a theoretical grounding for the method's behavior, even though the bounds involve quantities not instantiated for the tested models.

5. **Observation of diminishing marginal utility of spikes.** The paper empirically notes that as maximum time steps increase, spike count per timestep decreases and accuracy gains plateau (Section 7). This observation supports the central thesis that computational redundancy grows with longer simulations.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **No accuracy numbers are reported anywhere in the paper.** The paper repeatedly claims "accuracy comparable to baseline models" (abstract, contributions, Section 7), but it never reports a single accuracy number — not for the baseline, not for the method, not in any table, figure, or paragraph. Table 2 is titled "Comparison of spike number and simulation length T" and contains only spike counts. The central trade-off of any efficiency method (reduction in cost vs. change in accuracy) cannot be evaluated. This is the single most critical gap: the paper's core claim is unverifiable as written.

2. **No comparison to any existing spike-reduction or temporal-redundancy method.** The paper discusses SEENN (Li et al., 2023) and other dynamic time-step methods in Section 2.3 but never compares against them or any other baseline. Without showing that DDLA outperforms simpler approaches (e.g., random spike dropout, threshold adjustment, or simply reducing time steps), the contribution's advantage over the state of the art is unsubstantiated.

3. **The method's computational mechanism is under-specified, making the efficiency claims unclear.** The paper does not specify whether GSC is applied as an offline calibration step (one-time on a validation set) or computed online at every inference step. If gradients must be computed at inference time to determine sensitivity, the backward pass overhead could negate the forward-pass savings. The description in Section 5.2 is high-level and lacks pseudocode or a precise algorithmic specification of how the forward pass is modified to avoid recomputing static features from scratch.

4. **Overclaimed novelty.** The "Feature Overlapping Phenomenon" as defined (Eq. 6: O_l^T = O_l^{T-1} + Δ_l^T) is a tautological identity that follows directly from defining Δ_l^T as the difference. Presenting this as a "first-of-its-kind study" and "first to discover" (Sections 1, 4) overstates the contribution. Temporal redundancy in SNNs — due to weight sharing and static inputs — has been acknowledged in prior work on temporal attention, sparse spikes, and dynamic time steps. The paper would be stronger if it positioned itself more modestly as proposing a specific technique to exploit a known property, rather than claiming to discover that property.

5. **Event-based datasets claimed but never evaluated.** Contribution 3 (line 27) claims "comprehensive testing on both static and event-based datasets," but Section 7 only evaluates on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (static datasets). No event-based dataset (e.g., DVS-CIFAR10, N-Caltech101) is used. This is a clear overclaim that needs to be corrected.
