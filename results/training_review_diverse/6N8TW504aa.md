## Summary

This paper proposes the Graphical Multioutput Gaussian Process (GMOGP), a framework for multi-output regression that replaces the symmetric covariance-based correlation of traditional MOGP (e.g., LMC) with an asymmetric, attention-weighted dependence structure. Each output is modeled as a Gaussian process conditioned on parent outputs, with parent assignments learned via a dynamic attention mechanism. Kernel hyperparameters are shared across outputs via multi-objective optimization with Pareto optimality, and the framework extends to non-Gaussian data through element-wise invertible normalizing flows (TGMOGP). Experiments on synthetic and real datasets (SARCOS, KUKA) show empirical improvements in RMSE and NLL over baselines including LMC, FICM, GPRN, and V-LMC.

## Strengths

- **Dynamic attention-based parent learning with asymmetric coefficients.** Section 3.2 introduces a scoring function with per-edge parameters (w_{ij}, c_{ij}) that achieves dynamic attention, addressing the "static attention" issue identified by Brody et al. (2022). The resulting asymmetric coefficients α_{ij} ≠ α_{ji} (verified in Table 3) provide an interpretable, directed measure of output dependence that goes beyond the symmetric covariance matrices used in classic MOGPs like LMC.

- **Pareto-optimal hyperparameter learning via multi-objective optimization.** Section 3.3 formulates kernel hyperparameter learning as a separable MOO problem with a weighted-sum method, grounded in established optimization theory (Marler & Arora, 2010). Each output has its own O(N³) marginal likelihood objective, which is much smaller than the O((SN)³) cost of a full MOGP. The MOO formulation enables information sharing through shared kernel parameters without requiring the heavy joint covariance of classic MOGPs.

- **Principled non-Gaussian extension with per-output normalizing flows.** Section 3.4 extends GMOGP to non-Gaussian data using element-wise invertible transformations (SAL flow). Unlike the single-latent approach of Maroñas & Hernández-Lobato (2023), GMOGP allows each output to have distinct transformations, which is more flexible. The improved performance on the negatively-skewed SARCOS data (Table 2) supports this design.

- **Strong empirical performance on synthetic and real benchmarks.** Tables 1 and 2 show GMOGP/TGMOGP achieving lower test RMSE and NLL than several baselines (LMC, FICM, GPRN, CMOGP, V-LMC, SGPRN). On synthetic data (Table 1), TGMOGP achieves the lowest RMSE (0.34) and NLL (0.32). On real data (Table 2), TGMOGP with 100 inducing points outperforms V-LMC with 100 inducing points while reducing time cost. Figure 5 further shows consistent gains across training sizes and insensitivity to the number of latent processes.

- **Distributed and scalable design.** Remark 3.2 and the "shadowed" results in Table 2 demonstrate that GMOGP can be learned with distributed computing units without requiring block-diagonal approximations, prediction fusion, or data segmentation — four typical complications of distributed GP methods.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Imprecise graphical model formalism incompatible with the claimed DAG structure.** The paper frames GMOGP as a "graphical model" / Bayesian network over outputs, but the construction does not define a proper joint distribution over all S outputs in a single DAG. Eq.(14) writes p(f_X^{(1)},...,f_X^{(S)}) = p(f_X^{(i)}|pa_i) ∏_{j∈pa_i} p(f_X^{(j)}), which is the factorization for *one* specific target node i whose parents are root nodes. The paper then states "alternately, we can treat each output as the target node" (line 119), meaning there are S different factorizations rather than a single DAG over all outputs. A genuine DAG would require ∏_{i=1}^S p(f_X^{(i)}|pa_i) with pa_i defined consistently across all nodes without cycles. The paper's initial assumption that pa_i contains *all other nodes* (line 119) creates a cyclic dependency structure (e.g., f_X^{(1)} depends on f_X^{(2)} and vice versa), which contradicts the paper's own statement that "the joint distribution only admits directed acyclic graphs" (line 111). The graphical model terminology is therefore used imprecisely — the actual model is S separate (but coupled through shared hyperparameters) conditional models, not a single probabilistic graphical model over all outputs.

- **Predictive inference does not condition on observed values of other outputs.** The predictive distribution for output i (Remark 3.2) uses only the target's own observations: k_G^{(i)}(x_*,X)(k_G^{(i)}(X,X) + σ_i² I_N)^{-1} y^{(i)}. Unlike standard MOGP methods (LMC, FICM, GPRN) that condition on all observed outputs jointly via a cross-covariance matrix, GMOGP's only cross-output information sharing during inference is through the shared kernel hyperparameters θ_j (learned via MOO) and the attention coefficients α_{i,j}. The paper's claims of "correlating data from multiple diverse sources" (abstract) and providing "dependent processes" (Section 3) are overstated relative to what the model actually achieves at inference time. The observed performance gains may stem from the flexible additive composite kernel structure rather than any genuine multi-output conditioning, and the paper does not empirically disentangle these two sources.

- **The marginal prior derivation assumes parents are independent.** Proposition 3.1 derives the marginal covariance of f_X^{(i)} as Σ α_{i,j}² k_θ_j(X,X) + k_θ_i(X,X), which implicitly assumes cov(f_X^{(j)}, f_X^{(k)}) = 0 for j≠k among parents. While this is a *mathematical assumption* the authors are free to make, it is inconsistent with the DAG interpretation they invoke. In a proper directed graphical model with linear Gaussian conditionals, parents are typically dependent through their own ancestors in the graph. The paper neither justifies this independence assumption nor discusses what graphical assumptions would make it valid (e.g., assuming all parents are root nodes with independent priors).

- **Missing essential ablations to support the multi-output contribution.** The paper does not include: (i) a comparison to a single-output GP that uses the same composite kernel (k_G^{(i)} = Σ α_{i,j}² k_θ_j + k_θ_i) but trained independently per output — this would isolate the benefit of the MOO-based multi-output hyperparameter sharing from the composite kernel structure itself; (ii) a variant with a fully-connected graph (all α's learned without pruning) vs. the attention-pruned graph, to verify that the graphical structure itself improves performance; (iii) a variant with a random graph structure. Without these, it is unclear whether the gains attributed to "graphical representations" come from the multi-output structure or simply from having a more expressive (additive) kernel.

### Minor

- **No error bars, confidence intervals, or significance tests on any reported metric.** Tables 1 and 2 report only point estimates. For synthetic experiments with known ground truth, standard deviations over multiple independent runs are expected. Given the modest reported differences between some methods, the reader cannot assess whether the improvements are statistically meaningful.

- **Attention mechanism uses label values y during initial learning without discussion of potential data leakage.** Line 168 states: "In practice, we substitute the observation values ⟨y^{(i)}, y^{(j)}⟩ at the initial learning phase." Using training labels to initialize the graph structure before GP training is a form of transductive learning. The paper does not discuss whether this risks overfitting or bias, nor how this interacts with the GP marginal likelihood.

- **The distributed learning scheme is under-specified.** The "shadowed" results in Table 2 are described as learned "with two distributed computing units," but the paper does not explain how the hyperparameters Θ are shared/communicated between units, how the MOO is implemented in the distributed setting, or whether the Pareto front is computed globally or per unit.

- **The paper does not evaluate uncertainty calibration.** A key claimed advantage of GP models is well-calibrated uncertainty. The paper reports only RMSE and NLL but does not assess predictive coverage or calibration, which would be particularly informative given the non-Gaussian extension and the use of variational inference.

### Trivial

- The paper references Figure 5(c) as showing per-output test error but the description mentions "the 3rd output" — a clearer breakdown would be helpful.
- Some mathematical notation is inconsistent (e.g., Z vs. T for the index set).

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface a genuine tension in the paper: the GMOGP is presented as a "graphical model" for multi-output regression, but its actual machinery is closer to a set of coupled single-output GPs with an additive, attention-weighted prior. The central insight from combining the two reviews is that the paper's technical contribution (attention-based asymmetric dependence + MOO-based hyperparameter sharing + per-output normalizing flows) is potentially valuable and empirically competitive, but the graphical model framing over-reaches and invites scrutiny under which the model's properties (no joint conditioning, assumed parent independence) appear as flaws. The paper would be stronger if it repositioned itself as a "conditionally structured multi-output GP with attention-based additive kernels" rather than as a graphical model / Bayesian network over outputs. A critical open question that neither review fully resolves is how much of the empirical gain comes from the multi-output hyperparameter sharing vs. the increased expressiveness of the additive composite kernel — this requires an ablation study the paper does not provide.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the contribution honestly.** Drop the claim of a full DAG-based graphical model over outputs. Instead, present GMOGP as a conditionally-structured MOGP where each output has a GP prior whose mean is an attention-weighted combination of other outputs' GP priors, with kernel hyperparameters shared via multi-objective optimization. This framing is accurate and avoids the DAG consistency issues.

2. **Add the critical ablation study.** Compare GMOGP to a single-output GP with the same composite kernel (k_G^{(i)}) but trained independently per output (no MOO-sharing of θ_j). This directly isolates the benefit of the multi-objective hyperparameter sharing from the additive kernel flexibility.

3. **Add error bars.** Report standard deviations over at least 5 random train/test splits for all metrics. Without these, the empirical claims are unverifiable.

4. **Clarify the inference mechanism.** Explicitly state that GMOGP's prediction for output i conditions only on y^{(i)} (not on other outputs' labels), and explain why this is still useful for multi-output regression (e.g., hyperparameters encode cross-output information, the attention graph provides interpretable structure).

5. **Discuss the label-based attention initialization.** Explain why using y^{(i)} values for initial graph construction does not cause overfitting or data leakage, or propose an alternative that avoids label dependence.

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a novel and potentially valuable approach to multi-output regression with attention-based output dependencies, MOO-based hyperparameter sharing, and non-Gaussian extensions. The core ideas (asymmetric attention weights, separable per-output inference, per-output normalizing flows) are interesting and the empirical results are promising. However, the paper's central framing as a graphical model / Bayesian network is imprecise to the point of being misleading — the actual model does not define a proper joint DAG over all outputs, and predictive inference does not condition on other outputs' observed values in the way that the MOGP literature standard expects. These are not fatal flaws (the model is still coherent and empirically competitive), but they are major gaps between claims and reality that prevent acceptance in the current form. The missing ablations and absence of error bars further weaken the empirical contribution.

The paper would require: (a) honest reframing of the model's relationship to graphical models, (b) ablation isolating the multi-output benefit, and (c) error bars on all results, to be acceptable. I rate this as borderline with potential.
