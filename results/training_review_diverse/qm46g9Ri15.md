## Summary

This paper identifies two misalignment problems that degrade CLIP's representation stability in open-world environments: (1) **attention misalignment** (background-biased attention in multi-head attention layers) and (2) **predictive category misalignment** (classifying into semantically unrelated categories). The authors propose AlignCLIP, which introduces Attention Alignment Loss (AAL) using a Siamese architecture with a D-V attention structure to correct attention bias, and Semantic Label Smoothing (SLS) to leverage pre-trained class similarity relationships. The method is evaluated across domain shift, unseen class, and combined open-world scenarios on multiple benchmarks, consistently outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods including CLIPood and CoCoOp.

## Strengths

- **Clear problem diagnosis with two distinct, well-motivated misalignment problems.** The paper pinpoints attention misalignment (background bias in ViT attention, supported by prior interpretability work) and predictive category misalignment (semantically incoherent errors) as specific failure modes of CLIP fine-tuning. Section 1 grounds these issues in both existing literature and the paper's own analysis (Figure 1), providing a concrete diagnostic contribution beyond "CLIP doesn't generalize well."

- **Novel Attention Alignment Loss with a Siamese D-V architecture.** AAL (Section 3.2, Figure 2) uses a diagonal-matrix-based D-V structure in a Siamese branch (frozen gradients, shared weights) to produce a cleaner similarity distribution \(G_{\mathrm{D-V}}\) that guides alignment of both the original attention distribution \(A_c\) and the original similarity distribution \(G\). The ablation (Figure 3b,c) validates that aligning both distributions and focusing on the first four layers yields optimal gains, and the case study (Figure 4, second row) visually confirms reduced background bias.

- **Semantic Label Smoothing that is simple and principled.** SLS (Section 3.3) replaces the uniform distribution in standard label smoothing with a class-similarity matrix derived from CLIP's pre-trained text embeddings. This is a clean, lightweight regularization that directly addresses predictive category misalignment. Figure 3d shows SLS outperforms standard label smoothing by 0.89%–3.21% on three datasets, and the qualitative example (Figure 4, first row) demonstrates that SLS prevents egregious misclassifications (e.g., cat→computer vs. cat→dog).

- **Comprehensive evaluation across three open-world scenarios.** The paper tests AlignCLIP on: (a) domain shift (Tables 1–2 on DomainBed and ImageNet variants), (b) unseen classes (Table 3 on 11 datasets), and (c) both co-occurring (Table 4 on Office-Home and DomainNet). In all settings, AlignCLIP consistently outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods (CLIPood, CoCoOp, CoOp), demonstrating robustness across diverse distributional challenges.

- **Ablation studies validating each component's contribution.** Figure 3a shows that removing either AAL or SLS degrades performance on both domain-shift and combined scenarios, confirming both components are necessary for the best results. Additional analyses (Figure 3b-d) dissect the contribution of attention alignment, layer selection, and the superiority over standard label smoothing.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
None.

### Minor

1. **The computation of \(G_{\mathrm{D-V}}\) is not specified with a concrete formula, impairing reproducibility.** The paper states that the D-V structure "replac[es] the softmax-normalized product of \(Q\) and \(K^{T}\) with a diagonal matrix" and that \(G_{\mathrm{D-V}}\) is "the normalized attention distribution obtained by calculating the cosine similarity distance between visual token features, using the D-V structure, and transposed text features" (line 87–88). How the diagonal-matrix attention output feeds into this similarity computation is left to the reader's inference. Since AAL is the paper's central contribution, a precise mathematical definition of \(G_{\mathrm{D-V}}\) is essential for reproducibility.

2. **The contribution of Beta Moving Average (BMA) relative to AAL and SLS is not disentangled.** BMA (from Shu et al., 2023) is used in all AlignCLIP experiments. The ablation study (Figure 3a) tests only the inclusion/exclusion of AAL and SLS *while keeping BMA fixed*. Since CLIPood also uses a momentum-moving-average strategy, it is unclear how much of AlignCLIP's margin over CLIPood stems from BMA versus from AAL+SLS. An ablation that removes BMA (or replaces it with a simpler EMA) while keeping AAL+SLS would cleanly attribute the gains. This does not invalidate the core contribution—the ablation *does* show that AAL+SLS contribute beyond BMA—but it weakens the precision of the claimed improvements.

3. **Baseline comparison documentation is incomplete.** The paper reports state-of-the-art results against MIRO, DPL, CLIPood, etc. (Tables 1–4) but does not state whether these numbers are taken from prior papers, reproduced in a shared codebase, or obtained from published checkpoints. Hyperparameters for baselines are not reported. For a paper making "markedly supersedes" claims, documenting baseline provenance is important.

4. **Whether the class similarity matrix \(\mathbf{S}_{ij}\) is row-wise normalized (e.g., via softmax) before use in \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S}}\) is not stated.** Equation 8 defines \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SLS}}=(1-\epsilon)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}+\epsilon\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S}}\) with \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S}}=\sum_i \mathbf{S}_{ij}\log(p(y_i|\mathbf{x}))\). If \(\mathbf{S}_{ij}\) is raw cosine similarity (which can be negative), using it directly as a target distribution would be unusual. Normalization details matter for both correctness and reproducibility.

5. **It is not stated whether the number of attention layers to align (Figure 3c, "first four layers") was selected on a validation set or based on test performance.** If chosen based on the test set, the analysis risks overfitting to the particular datasets used. This is a standard methodological safeguard that should be documented.

6. **How the harmonic mean is averaged over 11 datasets in Table 3 is not specified** (simple average vs. weighted). This is a minor documentation gap.

7. **Limitations are not discussed.** The method relies on CLIP's pre-trained text encoder to compute class similarities; if class names are semantically ambiguous or the textual embedding space is poorly structured for a given task, SLS could be detrimental. A brief limitations paragraph would strengthen the paper.

### Trivial

- The paper uses \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SSL}}\) in Algorithm 1 but \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SLS}}\) in the text (Equation 8 and surrounding prose). This notation inconsistency should be harmonized.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Reporting the computational overhead of the Siamese D-V branch (two forward passes through the image encoder per iteration) would help practitioners assess the cost of the improvement.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface two useful observations. First, the paper's identification of **two separate misalignment problems** (attention-level and prediction-level) and the demonstration that they require separate corrections (AAL for attention, SLS for predictions) is a genuinely useful decomposition that could inform future CLIP fine-tuning work. Second, the finding that **only the first four attention layers need alignment** (Figure 3c) is a practical insight that could reduce computational overhead in follow-up methods — it suggests that attention misalignment is primarily a shallow-layer phenomenon in ViT.

2. Add an ablation that removes BMA (or replaces it with a simple EMA) while keeping AAL+SLS, to isolate the contribution of each component.
3. State the source of all baseline numbers (original papers / reproduced / official checkpoints) in a footnote or appendix.
4. Clarify whether \(\mathbf{S}_{ij}\) is row-wise normalized before being used as a target distribution in \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S}}\).
