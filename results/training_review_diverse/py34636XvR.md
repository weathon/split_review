## Summary

This paper proposes SF-EUOT, a method for solving the Entropic Unbalanced Optimal Transport (EUOT) problem that achieves simulation-free training and one-step generation. The authors derive the dynamical formulation of EUOT and its dual, then leverage the reciprocal property of the Schrödinger bridge to parametrize the path measure via a static generator network. The method achieves FID 3.02 on CIFAR-10 with NFE 1 without pretraining—matching the best prior SB result (IPF, FID 3.01) which requires NFE 200 and diffusion model pretraining—and shows strong results on image-to-image translation tasks.

## Strengths

1. **Derivation of the dynamical dual formulation for EUOT (Theorem 1, Proposition 1).** The paper generalizes the Schrödinger Bridge framework to the unbalanced setting, deriving both a dynamical formulation (Eq. 8) and a dual form (Eq. 12) that provide the theoretical foundation for the algorithm. This extends the EOT/SB theory in a principled way.

2. **Simulation-free training via the reciprocal property.** By parametrizing the path measure through a static generator \(T_\theta\) and the reciprocal property (Eq. 22), the model avoids SDE simulation during both training and inference—a key bottleneck in prior SB methods (IPF, IMF, ENOT-diffusion). This is validated by the reported training time (~4 days on 2 RTX 3090Ti GPUs vs. >10 days for IMF/ENOT-diffusion).

3. **Competitive generative modeling without any pretraining.** Table 1 shows EUOT-Soft achieves FID 3.02 with NFE 1 on CIFAR-10. All other SB methods report FID ≥ 100 without pretraining, and the best prior SB result (IPF, FID 3.01) requires 200 NFE and diffusion model pretraining. This is a clear advance in SB scalability.

4. **One-step generation (NFE=1).** The reciprocal parametrization enables direct one-shot mapping from source to target, whereas IPF requires 200 NFE, IMF requires 100 NFE, and even diffusion models typically require 50–1000 NFE. This drastically reduces evaluation cost.

5. **Strong image-to-image translation results.** Table 2 shows the model outperforms both adversarial baselines (DiscoGAN, CycleGAN) and OT-based methods (NOT, DSBM) on Male→Female (FID 8.44 vs. next-best 11.96) and Wild→Cat (FID 14.59 vs. ≥25 for SB models).

6. **Ablation on entropic regularization (\(\sigma^2\)).** Figure 5 systematically studies the effect of noise level on FID and transport cost (MSE), providing insight into the trade-off between distribution matching and stochasticity.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The Laplacian computation is neither explained nor discussed.** The loss functions (Eqs. V_objective2, inner_loop) and Algorithm 1 (Line 204) include the term \(\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\Delta v_\phi(t,x)\). In high dimensions (CIFAR-10 images have \(d=3072\), 64×64 CelebA has \(d=12288\)), computing this Laplacian is non-trivial: the full Hessian diagonal costs \(O(d^2)\) per sample. The paper never mentions how this is approximated (e.g., via Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator, which costs two backward passes per sample, or some other method). This is a significant reproducibility gap. *However*, the method demonstrably *does* scale to these dimensions (the experiments ran), so the critic's claim that "the method as described cannot scale" is contradicted by the paper's own results. The actual problem is that the implementation detail is missing, not that the method is inherently unscalable. The authors should clarify this.

2. **Results are reported without variance across multiple seeds.** The CIFAR-10 FID, I2I results, and Gaussian benchmarks are single numbers. For a method with PDE-style losses (which can be sensitive to initialization and hyperparameters), reporting at least mean and std over 2–3 seeds would substantially increase confidence in the results.

### Minor

1. **Several important hyperparameters are not ablated.** The HJB penalty weight \(\lambda_D\), the generator loss weight \(\lambda_G\), the norm order \(p\) (1 ≤ p ≤ 2), and the number of time steps \(N\) are not studied. Given that these directly control the optimization landscape, their influence on the results is unclear.

2. **The value network loss in Algorithm 1 is technically correct but lacks explanatory annotation.** The loss \(- \frac{\alpha}{2} \|\nabla v_\phi\|^2 - v_\phi(1,\hat{y}) + \Psi^*(v_\phi(1,y))\) correctly implements the negation of the original sup objective (converting maximization to minimization), but the negative signs can mislead a reader into thinking there is a sign error. Adding a short justification (or splitting the loss into named terms as done in Eq. V_objective2) would improve clarity.

3. **The generator loss \(\lambda_G R\) is terse.** Minimizing \(R\) (the PDE residual) w.r.t. \(T_\theta\) is correct—the gradient flows through the sampled \(x_t\) and \(x_{t+\Delta t}\) which depend on \(\hat{y}=T_\theta(x,z)\)—but the pseudocode does not make this dependence explicit. A clarifying sentence referencing Eq. (20) would help reproducibility.

4. **The high-dimensional Gaussian results (Table 3) show the method underperforms DSBM-IPF and RF on 2 of 3 metrics.** The paper honestly acknowledges this and attributes it to the difficulty of PDE-based learning, but this does weaken the claim that the method accurately learns the EUOT solution.

### Trivial
- In Eq. (V_objective2), the running cost term appears as \(\|v_\phi\|^2\) instead of \(\|\nabla v_\phi\|^2\)—this appears to be a notation artifact (\(v_\phi\) likely should be \(\nabla v_\phi\)).

## Nice-to-Haves

- Adding a Hutchinson trace estimator reference for the Laplacian computation, or a note that the Laplacian is avoided through some reparametrization, would resolve the main reproducibility concern.
- A study of the linear time distribution vs. uniform time sampling would validate the design choice.
