## Summary

This paper proposes a method for explaining sparse autoencoder (SAE) features learned from LLM hidden states, using a fixed vocabulary and a mutual-information-based objective to produce more discourse-level explanations. The authors also introduce two steering strategies (Amplification and Calibration) that leverage these explanations to modify LLM behavior during runtime, and demonstrate their application to jailbreak defense on Mistral-7B-Instruct. The paper is an **empirical methods paper** with a theoretical motivation section — it contributes a new explanation method and an application pipeline, not a pure theory or dataset contribution.

## Strengths

- **Novel mutual-information-based explanation objective with clear empirical advantage.** The proposed method (Equations 3–5) normalizes word-feature activations across all learned features, which is a principled way to identify feature-specific vocabulary. Table 2 shows a substantial gap over baselines: 57.8% explainable rate on activated features vs. 30.5% for TopAct and 19.6% for N2G. The qualitative examples in Table 1 are genuinely compelling, showing that the proposed method produces topic-coherent word sets (e.g., "contact, phone, unit, accuracy, exact, burning, location, precise, details, smoke") while baselines yield rigid lexical phrases.

- **Demonstrated that discourse-level explanations enable a downstream use case where baseline explanations fail.** The steering experiments (Table 3, Figure 3) show that the proposed method's explanations can be used to select safety-related features for jailbreak defense, reducing Salad-Bench ASR from 81.6 to 72.8 while maintaining reasonable helpfulness (MT-Bench 6.0). Critically, applying the same procedure with TopAct and N2G explanations yields negligible defense improvement (Figure 3), supporting the claim that discourse-level explanations are more useful for steering.

- **Efficient explanation collection avoids the activation-coverage problem of prior methods.** By using a fixed 5000-word vocabulary built from the training data, the method does not require collecting activation spans over a large validation corpus. This addresses a practical limitation: Table 2 shows that 34.0% of features never activate on the validation set for TopAct and 42.0% for N2G, while the proposed method explains every feature.

- **Theoretical identification of frequency bias as a structural challenge.** Section 3.1's topic-model random walk derivation (Equation 2) provides a formal framework for understanding why SAE features trained on text tend to encode both discourse topics and linguistic patterns, with the latter dominating. Even though not experimentally validated, this analysis usefully frames the problem.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The core contributions — a new explanation method and its application to steering — are supported by evidence, even if the evaluation has gaps.

### Major

- **Explanation evaluation lacks human validation and has uncontrolled asymmetry.** The paper uses GPT-4o as a machine annotator to judge whether raw explanations are "relevant" to summaries. This follows an established paradigm (Bills et al., 2023; Rajamanoharan et al., 2024b), but those works validated against human judgments, which this paper does not. The paper also does not specify how summaries are generated for each explanation method — GPT-4o may be generating the summaries it then evaluates, creating a risk of circularity. Additionally, the comparison is asymmetric: TopAct and N2G produce 5–10 token text spans, while the proposed method produces word lists, which are inherently easier to match to a short summary. Without human validation or inter-rater agreement, the quantitative advantage in Table 2 cannot be confidently attributed to genuine semantic interpretability rather than evaluation artifacts. This directly affects the paper's answer to RQ1.

- **Steering evaluation lacks critical controls and statistical rigor.** The steering experiment (Section 4.3) does not report how many features were selected for TopAct and N2G (only 141 and 48 are reported for the proposed method's AS and EH strategies), making it unclear whether the comparison is fair. There is no random-feature baseline to establish that improvement comes from the *explanations* rather than from the mere act of intervening on features. The performance gap between the proposed AS method and the Self-Reminder baseline (ASR 72.8 vs. 79.9, MT-Bench 6.0 vs. 6.5) is reported without variance estimates or significance testing. The claim that "the key to preventing jailbreak attacks is not to forget harmful knowledge, but to enhance safety awareness" (based on EH's failure) is plausible but confounded — EH may have failed because the 48 features identified as "harmful" were poorly selected, not because erasing harmful knowledge is fundamentally wrong.

- **Feature selection pipeline has two layers of unvalidated machine annotation.** The pipeline selects features for steering by: (1) having GPT-4o judge explanations as "yes"/"probably", then (2) prompting GPT-4o to label features as harmful or safety-related using a crafted taxonomy. The accuracy of neither step is reported, and the taxonomy itself is not provided. This means the entire steering pipeline rests on machine annotations that are not independently verified.

- **Only one model (Mistral-7B-Instruct) and one layer (8th) are tested.** This limits generalizability. The choice of layer is motivated by prior work's scaling (24% of total layers), but claims about discourse-level explanations and steering utility would be significantly stronger with at least one additional model family or layer.

### Minor

- **The theoretical frequency-bias analysis is not experimentally validated.** Section 3.1 presents a derivation and concludes that SAEs learn both discourse topics and linguistic patterns, with the latter dominating due to frequency. However, no experiment directly measures this bias — e.g., by quantifying what proportion of features' top-activating words are function words vs. content words, or by comparing the proposed method against an ablation that simply uses a controlled vocabulary without the mutual information objective. The method could be effective for reasons other than the frequency-bias story (e.g., the fixed vocabulary naturally excludes lexical patterns).

- **Strong assumption in the objective derivation.** The simplification from Equation 3 to 4 assumes p(c = W_c) ≈ 1 — i.e., that each SAE feature encodes exactly one unique concept. The paper acknowledges this is an idealization, but does not discuss how violations (multi-concept features, feature superposition) would affect the method. This is a standard approximation in the SAE literature, but its consequences are unexplored.

- **Several methodological choices are not ablated or justified.** The vocabulary size (5000 words), the K value for top-K words per feature, the feature count C = 2^16 (based on a scaling law from GPT-2/GPT-4, not validated for Mistral-7B), and the calibration hyperparameter β (set to 2.5) are all design decisions with no ablation study. The paper's conclusions would be strengthened by showing that results are robust to reasonable variations.

- **The calibration steering strategy's side effects are not analyzed.** The calibration formula (Section 3.3) replaces feature-specific activations with a constant mean vector for all tokens, which is a strong intervention. The paper reports the aggregate MT-Bench score (6.0), but does not analyze whether particular response dimensions (coherence, specificity) are disproportionately affected.

### Trivial
- The random walk parameter σ in Equation 2 is a modeling constant in the derivation; it is correctly not "set" in experiments, but the paper could note this to avoid confusion.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A small human evaluation (e.g., 50–100 features rated by 3+ annotators) to validate the GPT-4o judgments and confirm that the proposed method's advantage holds under human evaluation.
- A controlled steering baseline using randomly selected features of the same size, to attribute improvement to explanation quality rather than feature count.
- Statistical significance testing or variance estimates for the steering results (e.g., multiple runs with different SAE seeds).
- Release of code, feature IDs, and selected feature explanations for reproducibility.
- An ablation comparing the mutual-information objective against a simpler TF-IDF baseline over the same fixed vocabulary.

## Removed Points
- The criticism that the paper "conflates tokens with feature types" when claiming an N:1 ratio of linguistic patterns to discourse topics. The paper's random walk model has N noise vectors (ε₁…ε_N) and 1 discourse vector (c₀) per sentence — the N:1 ratio is correct for the model's feature instances, and the reviewer's concern reflects a misunderstanding of the derivation.
- The criticism about the "reproducibility" concern regarding missing code/checkpoints as a "major obstacle" — this is a standard reproducibility concern common to most ML submissions and does not threaten the paper's claims. Moved here because it's not specific enough to be a core weakness of the paper's methodology.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviews surface a clear tension: the paper's strongest contribution (a new explanation method that genuinely produces more discourse-level explanations) is evaluated with a methodology that lacks the validation it needs to fully support the claim. The most interesting unresolved question is whether the mutual-information objective itself drives the improvement, or whether most of the gain comes from the simpler choice of a controlled, fixed vocabulary — an ablation the paper does not include.

## Suggestions
1. **Add a human validation study** for the explanation evaluation (50–100 features rated by 3+ annotators). Report agreement between human and machine judgments, and show that the proposed method's advantage holds under human evaluation. This directly addresses the most serious evidential gap.
2. **Add a random-feature steering baseline** and report the number of features selected for each baseline explanation method. This controls for the possibility that improvement is due to feature count or selection noise rather than explanation quality.
3. **Include an ablation of the mutual-information objective** against a simple top-K by dot-product baseline over the same fixed vocabulary, to isolate the effect of the normalization across features.
4. **Add at least one more model** (e.g., Gemma-2B or Llama-3-8B) and one more layer to demonstrate generalizability.
5. **Report variance** for the steering results across multiple runs.

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses an important problem with a well-motivated method. The qualitative results are compelling, and the idea of using a fixed vocabulary with mutual information is novel and sensible. However, the evaluation of the central claim (that the proposed method produces better discourse-level explanations) is compromised by the lack of human validation and the asymmetric comparison format. The steering experiment shows promise but lacks the controls needed to attribute improvement to explanation quality. These gaps are substantial enough that the paper in its current form does not meet the evidence bar for acceptance at a competitive venue. A major revision focused on evaluation rigor could make this a strong paper.
