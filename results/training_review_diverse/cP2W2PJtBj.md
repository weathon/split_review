This paper proposes AntifakePrompt, a novel approach that adapts a vision-language model (InstructBLIP) for deepfake detection by formulating it as a visual question answering task and applying soft prompt tuning. The method tunes only 4,864 parameters (the embeddings of a pseudo-word appended to the prompt "Is this photo real ___?") while freezing the entire backbone. The authors curate a comprehensive benchmark of 23 testing datasets spanning text-to-image generation, image editing, face swapping, and adversarial attacks. Results show that prompt tuning yields a large improvement over the untuned VLM (from 36.53% to 91.81% average accuracy) and achieves competitive or leading average cross-domain accuracy against many baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel VQA formulation with prompt tuning for deepfake detection.** The paper is the first to cast deepfake detection as a VQA task on a pretrained VLM and apply soft prompt tuning. This is well-supported by the 55-percentage-point average accuracy gain over the same untuned InstructBLIP backbone (36.53% → 91.81% in Table 2), and by matching or exceeding most non-VLM baselines on held-out data.

- **Comprehensive benchmark of 23 testing datasets.** The authors meticulously construct held-out datasets spanning six generative scenarios (text-to-image, stylization, inpainting, super-resolution, face swap, and three adversarial attacks) plus multiple real-image sets. This provides a standardized evaluation framework that future work can directly build upon.

- **Exceptional parameter and data efficiency.** AntifakePrompt tunes only 4,864 parameters (vs. 23M for ResNet-50 and 11M for DE-FAKE). Data-efficiency experiments (Table 1) show that with 1.5K training images (0.2% of Wang2020's training data), the method outperforms pretrained Wang2020 on every fake dataset. With 15K images, it matches or exceeds DE-FAKE on most held-out datasets.

- **Robustness to adversarial attacks.** AntifakePrompt (+LaMa) achieves strong performance on all three attack scenarios (adversarial 97.83%, backdoor 96.83%, data poisoning 94.63%), substantially outperforming most baselines on these challenging test sets.

- **Thorough ablation studies justifying design choices.** The paper systematically ablates pseudo-word position (replace/prefix/postfix), tuning target (Q-Former only / LLM only / both), and training data size, providing clear evidence for the chosen configuration.
