## Summary

VEditBench introduces a large-scale, multi-task benchmark for text-guided video editing (TGVE), curating 420 real-world videos (300 short 2–4s, 120 long 10–40s) across six categories and six editing tasks (object insertion/removal/swap, scene replacement, motion change, style translation). It evaluates ten models across nine dimensions spanning semantic fidelity and visual quality. The paper's core contributions are the dataset scale, the expanded task taxonomy, and the multi-dimensional evaluation framework.

## Strengths

1. **Largest and most diverse TGVE benchmark with long-video coverage**: VEditBench's 420 videos (including 120 long videos of 10–40s) significantly surpass existing benchmarks like LOVEU-TGVE-2023 (76 videos) and BalanceCC (100 videos) in scale and duration diversity. Six content categories (Animals, Food, Scenery, Sports Activity, Technology, Vehicles) provide broader coverage.

2. **Expanded editing task taxonomy**: The six defined tasks (object insertion, removal, swap, scene replacement, motion change, style translation) offer substantially broader coverage than prior benchmarks that focused on only foreground/background/style modifications. This enables more nuanced analysis of model capabilities.

3. **Multi-dimensional evaluation framework with nine metrics**: The framework separates semantic fidelity (spatial text alignment, spatio-temporal text alignment, structural similarity, motion similarity) from visual quality (image quality, image aesthetics, motion smoothness, temporal quality, video quality), enabling fine-grained comparison of model trade-offs.

4. **Novel motion similarity metric**: The trajectory-based motion similarity metric using CoTracker and Hungarian matching (Section 4.1) provides a dedicated measure for motion preservation, addressing a gap in prior evaluation approaches.

5. **Systematic empirical analysis revealing model-task interactions**: The paper demonstrates that no single model dominates across all dimensions, that performance varies significantly across tasks, and that there is a tension between semantic fidelity and visual quality — findings supported by the multi-dimensional evaluation.

6. **Long-video evaluation exposes unique challenges**: The VEditBench-Long subset reveals that models struggle with flickering, temporal artifacts, and computational constraints on longer videos, motivating a direction for future research.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **No validation that automatic metrics correlate with human perception.** The paper introduces a new Motion Similarity metric and uses several automatic metrics to rank models, but provides no human evaluation to validate whether these scores reflect perceived editing quality. For a benchmark that aspires to be a "standard benchmark," this is a significant gap. While most adopted metrics (CLIP, ViCLIP, Q-Align, Content-Debiased FVD) have been validated in their original contexts, their suitability and combination for TGVE evaluation has not been demonstrated. The Motion Similarity metric (with its arbitrary α=0.5 weighting, reliance on CoTracker on edited videos, and Hungarian matching that does not account for tasks where trajectories genuinely have no counterpart) is entirely unvalidated. A human correlation study (even small-scale) is necessary to establish that the benchmark's rankings are meaningful.

2. **No statistical analysis of results.** All quantitative results in Tables 2 and 3 are reported as point estimates without variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Given that CLIP-based metrics on short videos can have high inter-sample variance, claims like "TokenFlow and VidToMe emerge as more well-rounded models" cannot be assessed for statistical reliability. This weakens the confidence in cross-model comparisons.

3. **SSIM applied uniformly across tasks where structure should intentionally change.** The paper computes SSIM between source and edited frames for all six tasks, including scene replacement and style translation where the structure *should* differ substantially. A single SSIM score averaged across tasks conflates task-appropriate preservation with task-appropriate transformation. Per-task reporting or a task-adaptive structural metric would be more informative.

### Minor

4. **Data curation pipeline is under-specified.** The paper mentions manual review of videos, captions, and prompts but does not report: the number of human reviewers, their instructions, inter-annotator agreement, criteria for judging video quality (beyond "blurry, shaking, ghosting"), or how many prompts/videos were discarded or modified. This limits reproducibility. The paper notes "More details about edit prompt generation can be found in the supplementary material," but core curation rigor metrics are missing from the main text.

5. **Motion Similarity metric's limitations for non-preservation tasks not discussed.** The Hungarian matching step assumes trajectories should correspond between source and edited videos. In object addition/removal, many trajectories will legitimately appear or disappear. The paper does not discuss how this affects the score or whether the metric is appropriate for all six task types.

6. **No per-category counts or per-task metric breakdowns in tabular form.** The paper states videos are "balanced across and diversified within six categories" but does not report per-category counts. The radar charts (Figure 5) hint at per-task patterns but are difficult to read; a table of task-level scores would be more informative and is standard practice for benchmarks.

7. **Hyperparameter/inference settings for evaluated models not reported.** The paper does not specify whether each model was run with default settings or tuned on a development subset. For a benchmark intended as a standard, this information is important for reproducibility and fair comparison.

### Trivial

8. **Naming inconsistency**: The abstract and introduction refer to "Object Insertion" while Section 3.2 uses "Object Addition" for what appears to be the same task. These should be harmonized.

9. **Duration inconsistency**: The abstract states long videos are "10-20 seconds" while the introduction and Section 3.1 state "10-40 seconds." This should be corrected.

10. **Qualitative analysis limited to one example (Figure 6).** While acceptable for space reasons, the single example ("changing the cat to a rabbit") limits the qualitative insight into model behavior across tasks.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison of model rankings on VEditBench vs. existing benchmarks (LOVEU-TGVE-2023, BalanceCC) would directly demonstrate whether VEditBench reveals different or new insights, strengthening the claim of added value.

- Reporting confidence intervals via bootstrapping or multiple runs would significantly strengthen the reliability of cross-model comparisons without being standard-norm-violating for this type of paper.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a human evaluation study** — even 50–100 edited videos rated by 3–5 annotators on the paper's own evaluation dimensions would establish that the automatic metrics correlate with human judgment and justify the benchmark's claim to be a "standard" evaluation tool.
2. **Report variance** — bootstrapped confidence intervals or standard deviations across videos for each metric-model combination in Tables 2 and 3.
3. **Add per-task metric breakdowns in tabular form** alongside the radar charts.
4. **Harmonize terminology** (Object Addition → Object Insertion) and fix the duration range inconsistency between abstract (10–20s) and body (10–40s).
5. **Report curation quality statistics** — number of human reviewers, inter-annotator agreement, count of videos/prompts discarded or modified during manual review.
6. **Discuss limitations of the Motion Similarity metric** — particularly how the Hungarian matching handles tasks where trajectories appear/disappear, and whether α=0.5 is robust across tasks.

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a genuine and timely need — a large-scale, multi-task, multi-metric benchmark for TGVE. The dataset and evaluation framework represent a clear improvement over existing resources. However, the paper's ambition to be a "standard benchmark" is undermined by the absence of human validation for its evaluation metrics, lack of statistical analysis, and under-specified curation quality assurance. These are real but fixable issues. The core resource (the dataset and task framework) is valuable, and the analysis of model trade-offs is well-grounded in the data presented.
