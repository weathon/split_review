## Summary

This paper introduces a confidence decision boundary visualization method for active learning using Voronoi tessellation. The key idea is to partition a 2D feature space into Voronoi cells, identify "predicted ridges" (edges between cells with different predicted labels), and assign each ridge a "predicted ridge confidence" metric (1 − Σ_k P(ŷ_i=k)P(ŷ_j=k)) to highlight boundary segments closest to the true class boundary. The authors demonstrate the approach by visualizing eight AL query strategies on MNIST and CIFAR-10, making qualitative observations about strategy-specific sampling behaviors including "high-risk boundary-crossing," two types of uncertainty (insufficient samples vs. noisy regions), and class imbalance in KCenterGreedy.

## Strengths

1. **Novel confidence-based decision boundary visualization via Voronoi tessellation**: The paper proposes a genuinely new approach to visualizing decision boundaries that quantifies varying certainty along different boundary segments, going beyond prior Voronoi-based work (Migut et al., 2015) that treated multi-class boundaries uniformly. The ridge confidence metric is clearly defined and Figure 4 provides preliminary evidence that higher-confidence ridges align better with ground-truth boundaries, with the number of high-confidence ridges increasing as model accuracy improves.

2. **Reveals interesting AL strategy-specific behaviors**: The qualitative observations — entropy sampling's "high-risk boundary-crossing" (Section 4.1, Fig. 5), the distinction between uncertainty from insufficient samples vs. noisy regions (Section 4.1, Fig. 6), the shift in margin sampling's focus as accuracy improves (Section 4.2, Fig. 7), and KCenterGreedy's class imbalance (Section 4.3, Fig. 8e) — are genuinely interesting and demonstrate the visualization's potential to generate hypotheses about query strategy behavior.

3. **Holistic visualization framework with three complementary views**: The design of Confidence Decision Boundary, Cumulative Sampling over 5 Rounds, and Error Detection panels (Section 4, Fig. 5) provides a multi-faceted look at model behavior — decision boundary quality, spatial-temporal distribution of sampling, and error correction patterns — that is more informative than any single view.

4. **Transparency about key design choices**: The paper clearly states (Section 3.3) that a separate "visualization model" trained on the full pool is used to produce the fixed 2D feature space, and the t-SNE dimensionality reduction limitations are acknowledged with mention of quantitative evaluations (though results are not reported).

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **The visualizations use a fixed oracle-derived embedding, not the AL model's evolving feature space, limiting what the findings reveal about AL dynamics.** Section 3.3 states that a separate visualization model was trained on the entire pool to near-perfect accuracy (0.9907 on MNIST, 0.9831 on CIFAR-10) to produce a fixed 2D projection used for all main visualizations (Figures 3–8a–d). The paper's rationale — "facilitating a consistent comparison" — is reasonable for a visualization tool paper, but the consequence is that the Voronoi tessellation, ridge geometry, and even the ground-truth boundary are computed on a feature space the AL model never sees. The AL model's own representation is presumably noisier and less separable; insights about where the model samples, makes errors, or is uncertain are projected onto oracle coordinates rather than visualized in the model's own feature space. The paper claims to "visualize the model's understanding" and "capture the cumulative effect of the model in the active learning process" — these claims are undercut by the embedding being decoupled from the model under study. Notably, Figure 8e *does* use the AL model's features (caption: "KCenterGreedy on CIFAR-10 drawn by the feature extracted from AL model"), suggesting the authors could have done more analysis in the model's own space. The paper does not discuss this disconnect or provide evidence that insights from the oracle projection transfer to the actual AL setting.

   *Why it matters: This is the single most significant limitation. It does not invalidate the visualization method itself (which could be applied to any feature space), but it substantially weakens the paper's strongest claims about revealing AL mechanisms.*

2. **The findings are purely qualitative, with no replication, error bars, or quantitative backing.** The AL experiments (Figure 1) show single lines per strategy with no mention of multiple seeds, random seeds, or any uncertainty quantification. Given that AL outcomes vary considerably with the initial labeled set, this is a significant methodological gap. The core qualitative observations (two types of uncertainty, boundary-crossing, class imbalance) are supported only by pointing to specific figure panels. There is no operational definition of "noisy regions," no quantification of the proportion of samples each strategy draws from such regions across rounds, and no measurement of whether the claimed patterns are robust to random seeds or hyperparameters. For a paper claiming to "enable a much deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms," the evidence is thin.

   *Why it matters: This is a methods paper introducing a visualization tool, so standard ML rigor (error bars, multiple seeds) is expected for the experimental demonstration. Without it, readers cannot assess whether the observed patterns are genuine or artifacts of a particular initial condition.*

3. **The dimensionality reduction quality evaluations are mentioned but results are not reported.** Section 3.3 states that "two quantitative evaluations: Correlation Test and Local Structure Preservation" were conducted on the t-SNE embedding, but no results are provided. Since all visualizations depend on the quality of the 2D projection, the reader cannot assess whether the embedding preserves meaningful neighborhood structure — a well-known concern for t-SNE.

   *Why it matters: The entire visualization pipeline depends on the fidelity of dimensionality reduction. Without these results, the spatial arrangement of points, Voronoi cells, and ridges may be misleading.*

### Minor

1. **Ridge confidence validation is suggestive but not compared to simpler alternatives.** Figure 4 shows that ridges with higher confidence intervals have higher accuracy and that high-confidence ridges increase as the model improves. This is correlational evidence that the metric relates to boundary quality. However, the paper does not compare the proposed metric to simpler alternatives (e.g., prediction margin on points, distance to decision hyperplane) to establish whether the Voronoi-based aggregation adds value beyond what standard uncertainty estimates already provide. The metric is also validated on a single configuration (dataset/model combination) based on what can be inferred from Figures 3–4.

2. **The definition of predicted ridge accuracy (A_ridge) is ambiguous.** The paper defines A_ridge = |B_pred ∩ B_gt| / |B_pred|, where B_pred and B_gt are sets of ridges (line segments). How the "intersection" of line segments is computed (tolerance-based matching? exact overlap?) is not specified, making Figure 4 difficult to fully interpret.

3. **No analysis of sensitivity to the dimensionality reduction method.** t-SNE is used exclusively. Decision boundaries derived from projections are projection-dependent. The paper acknowledges this concern (Section 3.3) but provides no robustness check with other methods (UMAP, PCA, parametric embeddings) or argument for why the choice is not critical.

4. **The distinction between "two types of uncertainty" is identified but not quantitatively validated.** The observation that concentrated sampling reduces uncertainty from insufficient samples while noisy-region uncertainty is harder to resolve is interesting, but no quantitative evidence is provided that these two types correspond to different data distributions or that strategies differ in how they address them.

### Trivial

- The color-scheme descriptions for the Error Detection visualizations are explained in the text (Section 4) but a standalone legend in each figure would improve readability.
- The paper does not include a limitations or future work section, which would help contextualize the oracle embedding choice and qualitative nature of findings.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A validation showing the same qualitative patterns appear when the Voronoi tessellation is computed on the *current* AL model's features (like Figure 8e), not just the oracle projection.
- Quantitative operationalization of the "two types of uncertainty" (e.g., define "noisy regions" by cells that are consistently misclassified, measure the fraction of samples drawn from such regions per round).
- A small user study or structured comparison against alternative visualizations (scatter plots with iso-contours) to support the claim that the proposed approach makes strategy differences easier to identify.
- Extension of the ridge confidence validation (Figure 4) to multiple strategies and datasets.

## Suggestions

1. Report the results of the Correlation Test and Local Structure Preservation evaluations mentioned in Section 3.3, and add a sensitivity analysis using UMAP or a parametric embedding as a robustness check.
2. Run the AL experiments with at least 3–5 random seeds and report mean ± std accuracy curves (Figure 1). This is essential for establishing that the qualitative observations are robust.
3. Include an analysis using the AL model's *own* evolving feature space (as in Figure 8e) for at least one key observation (e.g., the margin sampling shift in noisy regions from Section 4.2) to demonstrate that the patterns are not artifacts of the oracle projection.
4. Clarify how "intersection" of line segments is defined for the ridge accuracy metric A_ridge.
5. Add a limitations section that candidly discusses: (a) the implications of using a fixed oracle embedding rather than the AL model's evolving features, (b) the projection dependence of the decision boundary, and (c) the qualitative nature of the current findings.
6. Operationally define "noisy regions" (e.g., Voronoi cells where predictions are inconsistent across rounds) and quantify the fraction of samples each strategy draws from such regions over time to support the two-types-of-uncertainty claim quantitatively.

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a genuinely novel visualization approach for active learning with a clean formulation of ridge confidence. The method is clearly described and the qualitative observations, while preliminary, are interesting. However, the combination of (a) decoupling the visualization from the AL model's own feature space without discussing limitations, (b) single-run experiments with no error bars, and (c) missing results for the dimensionality reduction quality evaluations means the paper currently falls short of its stronger claims about revealing AL mechanisms. The core method has clear value, but the experimental demonstration needs substantial strengthening. The paper is borderline: the contribution is real but the evidence is thin.
