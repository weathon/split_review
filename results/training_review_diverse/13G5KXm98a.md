## Summary

This paper introduces a confidence decision boundary visualization method based on Voronoi tessellation for analyzing active learning query strategies. The approach decomposes the decision boundary into "predicted ridges" evaluated by a ridge confidence metric (C_pred), enabling more granular observation of how different AL strategies sample points and how the model's understanding evolves. Using this method, the authors conduct qualitative analyses of eight AL strategies across MNIST and CIFAR-10, yielding observations about entropy-based methods, least confidence/margin sampling, and diversity-based methods including K-Center Greedy's class imbalance behavior.

## Strengths

- **Novel predicted-ridge confidence metric for granular boundary analysis.** The paper formalizes a ridge-level confidence score (Section 2.2, Eq. 1) that quantifies how distinct the model's predictions are on either side of a Voronoi ridge, moving beyond uniform treatment of all boundary sections. Figure 4 validates this by showing that ridge accuracy (A_ridge) consistently improves with higher confidence intervals across rounds, directly supporting the claim of more granular partitioning.

- **Identifies two distinct uncertainty types from visualization.** Using the Error Detection visualization, the paper discovers that uncertainty from insufficient training samples can be reduced by concentrated sampling, while uncertainty in noisy regions persists due to mixed class features (Section 4.1, Figure 6a–c). This insight is genuinely derived from the proposed visualization and is not obtainable from accuracy curves alone, demonstrating practical value.

- **Reveals hidden class-imbalance behavior in K-Center Greedy.** The visualization shows that K-Center Greedy's sampling becomes severely imbalanced across classes because it is influenced by the model's biased understanding of data distribution (Section 4.3, Figure 8d–8e). This provides visual evidence for a known limitation (Yehuda et al., 2022) that is invisible in aggregate accuracy numbers, supporting the paper's claim that the visualization enables deeper understanding of query strategies.

- **Empirical validation of high-confidence ridge alignment with ground truth.** Figures 3a–3d and Figure 4 demonstrate that predicted ridges with higher confidence more closely follow the true decision boundaries, and that ridge accuracy improves as the confidence threshold increases and as the model improves over rounds. This establishes credibility for the core metric.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Missing results for the t-SNE fidelity evaluations.** Section 3.3 states that "we conducted two quantitative evaluations: Correlation Test and Local Structure Preservation … on the t-SNE method used in this study" to assess dimensionality-reduction distortion, but no results, summary statistics, or even a qualitative assessment are reported. Since the entire visualization pipeline depends on the fidelity of this 2D embedding, the reader cannot assess whether the Voronoi boundaries and observed patterns reflect genuine data structure or projection artifacts. This is a straightforward evidential gap — the evaluations were performed but their results are absent.

2. **Separate visualization model is used without sufficient discussion of its implications.** The paper trains a separate "visualization model" on the entire pool dataset to optimal accuracy (~99% on MNIST, ~98% on CIFAR-10) and uses its feature space for the main visualizations (Figures 3, 5, 6, 7, 8a–d). While this provides a stable coordinate system for comparison, the Voronoi cells are defined by spatial proximity in this oracle model's feature space, not the AL model's evolving feature space. The paper acknowledges this choice transparently (Section 3.3) and includes Figure 8e using the AL model's own features for one comparison, but it does not discuss how patterns might differ in the AL model's feature space or whether the visualization model's oracle-quality separability might exaggerate or distort certain behaviors. A limitations paragraph addressing this would substantially strengthen the work.

### Minor

3. **Qualitative observations would benefit from more systematic support.** The paper makes several interesting claims (e.g., "high-risk boundary-crossing" in entropy sampling, the shift in margin sampling from "easy" to "noisy" uncertainty, two uncertainty trends) that are supported by single figures with hand-circled regions. The paper acknowledges this is qualitative analysis, which is perfectly valid for a visualization paper, but the number of rounds examined and whether patterns persisted across different random seeds is unspecified. This does not invalidate the contributions, but the claims would be more convincing with a brief statement about how extensively the patterns were verified.

4. **The ridge-confidence metric has edge cases that could be discussed.** C_pred = 1 − Σ_k P(ŷ_i=k) P(ŷ_j=k) can produce high confidence scores when both representatives are uncertain but predict different classes (e.g., near-uniform but different modes). The intuition linking metric value to "reliability of the predicted ridge" in such cases is not discussed. An ablation comparing alternatives (e.g., margin or entropy on each side) would clarify the design rationale. That said, the empirical validation in Figure 4 does show the metric works as intended in practice.

5. **No direct visual comparison with prior decision-boundary visualization methods.** The paper argues that prior methods (Rodrigues et al., Somepalli et al., Migut et al.) "treat all sections of the decision boundary uniformly" but never shows what those methods produce on the same AL data. A side-by-side comparison would make the contribution concrete and immediately convincing. This is more of a completeness suggestion than a flaw.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface an interesting tension: the paper uses a separately trained oracle model for visualization features precisely because the AL model's features are too noisy in early rounds for clean visual comparison (Figure 2 shows this evolution). This pragmatic compromise — using a fixed high-quality feature space for comparative visualization while recognizing the AL model's own space is the ground truth of interest — is a recurring challenge in interpretability research. The paper's partial solution (Figure 8e) of showing at least one comparison in the AL model's space offers a template other visualization papers could follow. The reviewer's demand for t-SNE fidelity quantification also highlights a broader issue: Voronoi-based boundary analysis in reduced dimensions is becoming more common, yet the field lacks standardized diagnostics for when such embeddings are "good enough" for faithful geometric analysis.

## Suggestions

1. **Report the t-SNE fidelity evaluation results** (Correlation Test, Local Structure Preservation) that were conducted — even a brief table of numbers would resolve the most serious evidential gap.
2. **Add a limitations paragraph** explicitly discussing: the use of a separate visualization model, reliance on t-SNE projection, oracle treatment of pool labels, and the qualitative nature of the analysis.
3. **Include at least one primary analysis using the AL model's own features** (even for one key comparison, e.g., Entropy vs. Random) to demonstrate that the main qualitative patterns are not artifacts of the visualization model's feature space.
4. **Add a brief statement** about how many rounds/runs were examined for the qualitative claims, to help readers gauge the robustness of the observations.

## Score and Decision

The paper proposes a genuinely useful visualization tool and produces interesting, non-trivial observations about AL strategy behavior. The core method is well-motivated and the validation in Figure 4 is sound. However, the missing t-SNE fidelity results — evaluations the paper itself claims to have performed but never reports — create an unnecessary evidential gap that prevents full trust in the pipeline. Combined with the insufficiently discussed separate visualization model and the absence of limitations discourse, the paper falls short of the standard for acceptance in its current form. The contributions are real and the approach is promising; addressing these gaps would make a strong resubmission.
