## Summary

This paper introduces Rationality of Thought (RoT), a zero-shot prompting method that injects structured rational-thinking steps (inspired by Kahneman's System 2) into LLMs to reduce cognitive biases and improve reasoning. The authors construct a 464-question cognitive bias dataset spanning 29 bias types, and test RoT on GPT-4, GPT-3.5, and LLAMA2-13B, reporting 18.7% accuracy improvement on the bias task for GPT-4 and modest gains across arithmetic/commonsense reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, SVAMP, AQUA-RAT, ARC).

## Strengths

1. **Cognitive science framing with practical instantiation.** The paper draws on a well-motivated theoretical foundation (dual-process theory, System 1/System 2) and translates it into a concrete multi-step prompting strategy (identification, decomposition, execution, reflection, computation, assessment). The ablation study confirms that each step contributes, with step 3 (data/rational analysis) causing the largest 9% drop — evidence that the framework is more than an arbitrary concatenation of instructions.

2. **Cognitive bias dataset fills a gap.** The systematic collection from 93 bias types → 5 filtering criteria → 29 measurable biases → 464 questions (116 seed questions from authoritative psychology literature + 348 GPT-4 generalized and manually screened) is constructive. Even if the dataset has validity concerns (see Weaknesses), the curation methodology and the 5-criteria framework are a useful template for future work evaluating rational thinking in LLMs.

3. **Ablation-validated contribution of each step.** The stepwise ablation on GPT-4 quantifiably demonstrates that removing any of the six steps degrades performance, with step 3's removal causing the sharpest drop. This provides internal validity that the method's structure matters, not just that a verbose prompt was used.

4. **Honest reporting of model-capability dependence.** The paper explicitly notes that RoT degrades performance on LLAMA2-13B and GPT-3.5 (the latter under CoT) and only benefits GPT-4. This negative finding is presented rather than hidden, and the offered explanation (rational thinking is an advanced capability requiring a sufficiently capable base model) is plausible and practically informative.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Dataset contamination threatens the central cognitive-bias result.** 75% of the test questions (348 of 464) are generated by GPT-4 based on seed questions and construction rules, then GPT-4 is the primary model tested on them. Although manual screening was performed, the evaluation has circularity: RoT may simply align GPT-4's responses with patterns it already produced during dataset generation. No held-out human-authored portion, human baseline, or independent validation set is used. This substantially weakens the claim that the 18.7% improvement reflects genuine bias "correction" rather than in-distribution optimization. The 97.4% ceiling performance of GPT-4 + RoT further suggests the test set may not be sufficiently discriminative.

2. **Headline comparison against CoT-fewshot is uncontrolled.** The abstract and Section 4.3 claim that zero-shot RoT "outperforms inference enhancement techniques such as CoT using few-shot" on GSM8K (+0.4), AQUA-RAT (+4.8), and ARC-c (+0.7). However, the CoT-fewshot numbers are taken from prior papers (Wei et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2022) without re-running them under the same model version, API endpoint, decoding parameters, or experimental conditions. The paper does run its own CoT-zero-shot baseline, which IS a fair comparison, but the paper's most eye-catching claim rests on numbers the authors never produced. Given the tiny margins (often < 1 point), these differences could easily be artifacts of API drift or configuration mismatch.

3. **The paper overclaims "general reasoning enhancer" while selectively reporting negative results.** RoT *degrades* GPT-4's accuracy on SVAMP relative to both Direct and CoT-zero-shot (per the reviewer's reading of Table 3, which the paper does not discuss). The paper reports a 3% average improvement across tasks, but this average masks the SVAMP degradation and is primarily driven by gains on a few datasets. A method that actively hurts performance on a standard benchmark should be analyzed and explained, not glossed over.

### Minor

4. **RoT method is underspecified for replication.** The six steps are described at a high level, but no concrete prompt template is provided in the paper (and no appendix section referencing one exists in the parsed text). Steps are named "Identification, Decomposition, Execution, Reflection, Computation, Assessment" in Section 3.4 but the ablation descriptions refer to different functional labels (e.g., "research-backed solution analysis" for step 2, "data analysis support" for step 3). This ambiguity makes it difficult for readers to understand exactly what is being removed and to reproduce the method.

5. **The 97.4% ceiling suggests insufficient dataset difficulty.** GPT-4 + RoT achieves near-perfect accuracy on the cognitive bias test set. While this showcases RoT's effectiveness, it also raises the question of whether the dataset is challenging enough to discriminate among capable models, limiting its utility as a benchmark for future work.

6. **No cost/benefit analysis.** RoT likely incurs substantially higher token usage and latency than Direct or CoT-zero-shot, yet the paper reports no average generation length, token count, or wall-clock time. For practitioners weighing a 1–6% improvement against added cost, this information is necessary to assess practical significance.

7. **Alternative explanation for RoT's effect on weaker models.** The paper attributes LLAMA2-13B/GPT-3.5 degradation to these models lacking "advanced capability" for rational thinking. An equally plausible alternative is that the RoT instruction's length and complexity simply overwhelm smaller models' instruction-following capacity — a confound not controlled for. A prompt-length-matched control would disentangle these explanations.

### Trivial

8. The step-naming inconsistency between Section 2/3.4 (list) and the ablation descriptions (functional labels like "data analysis support") should be harmonized.
9. The paper uses "Rational of Thought" (Introduction line 1) and "Rationality of Thought" (title) inconsistently.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Prompt template disclosure:** Providing the exact RoT instruction used across all tasks would be the single highest-value addition for reproducibility and for disambiguating whether the benefit comes from the rationality framing or simply from structured multi-step instructions.
- **Re-run CoT-fewshot baselines** under the same API version and temperature=0 to remove the uncontrolled comparison concern.
- **Control for prompt length/complexity:** Compare RoT against a zero-shot prompt with the same number of steps but without the "rationality" framing to isolate the effect of the cognitive-science framing.
- **Error analysis** on reasoning tasks: where does RoT fail relative to CoT?
- **Example questions** from the cognitive bias dataset (even 1–2 per bias type) would help readers assess dataset quality.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation from the review is that the paper's strengths and weaknesses mirror each other: the cognitive-science framing gives the work a principled foundation, but the dataset constructed from that same framing (GPT-4 generating its own test stimuli) creates a circular evaluation that undermines the strongest reported result. The ablation result (step 3 removal = 9% drop) is the most robust piece of evidence, as it survives concerns about test-set contamination. The negative result on SVAMP, if confirmed, suggests RoT may be harmful on simpler, more straightforward reasoning problems — a failure mode that would be worth investigating and could sharpen the paper's claims about when rational thinking instructions help vs. hurt.

## Suggestions

1. **Fix the dataset evaluation.** Hold out a portion of human-authored seed questions for validation, or test on an independent set of cognitive bias questions from psychology literature. This would break the circularity.
2. **Re-run CoT-fewshot baselines** yourself under identical conditions (same model snapshot, temperature=0) and report the numbers alongside yours.
3. **Disclose the exact RoT prompt template** in an appendix or supplementary material.
4. **Acknowledge and analyze the SVAMP degradation** explicitly. Either explain why RoT hurts on this dataset or adjust the scope claims.
5. **Harmonize step names** between the method description and ablation study.

## Score and Decision

The paper tackles an interesting question with a well-motivated approach. The cognitive bias dataset is a genuine contribution, and the ablation study provides useful internal validation. However, the two major weaknesses — (1) the circular evaluation of the headline 18.7% improvement due to GPT-4 generating its own test set, and (2) the uncontrolled CoT-fewshot baseline comparison that the paper's central claim depends on — prevent the current evidence from convincingly supporting the claimed advantages. These are fixable in a revision, but in its present form the paper's empirical case is too brittle to accept.
