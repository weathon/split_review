## Summary

This paper investigates mechanistic universality between Transformer (Pythia-160M) and Mamba (130M) language models by (1) using Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to extract features from both architectures and quantifying feature correspondence via Max Pairwise Pearson Correlation (MPPC), and (2) performing path-patching-based circuit analysis to identify an induction circuit in Mamba and compare it with the known Transformer induction circuit. The paper finds that most SAE features have similar activation patterns across architectures (mean MPPC 0.74), that features match at proportionally equivalent depths (roughly 2:1 layer mapping), and that Mamba's induction circuit—relying on its SSM state and local convolution—is functionally analogous to the Transformer's attention-based induction mechanism but with an "Off-by-One" nuance where token information is written into the SSM state at the next position.

## Strengths

- **First systematic use of SAEs for cross-architectural feature similarity (Section 4).** The paper introduces SAEs as an architecture-agnostic decomposition tool to compare internal representations between Transformers and Mamba. This is a principled advance over neuron-level comparisons, and the paper empirically validates that SAEs capture meaningful cross-arch similarity while neuron baselines produce near-zero signal (Section 3.3, Figure 3a). The approach is well-motivated and could be adopted by future work.

- **Controlled comparison framework with two skylines (Section 3.3).** The paper proposes a hierarchy of variation (SAE variables → LM variables → architectural variables) and instantiates two skylines—model seed variant and SAE seed variant—to bound expected similarity under different sources of variation. This structured decomposition is conceptually useful even if the subtractive interpretation has limitations.

- **Discovery of an induction circuit in Mamba with structural analogy to Transformers (Section 6).** Using path patching, the paper identifies that Mamba's induction mechanism relies on the SSM state at layer 17 and the local convolution aggregating information from tokens A and B into the B+1 position. This is a novel and non-trivial finding that extends circuit-level understanding to SSM-based architectures, which are relatively under-explored in mechanistic interpretability.

- **Depth specialization finding (Section 3.4).** The observation that features match at proportionally equivalent depths (layer l in Pythia ↔ layer ~2l in Mamba) extends prior findings on neuronal depth specialization to architecture-agnostic features and supports the conjecture of a "stretched" universal hierarchical structure across architectures.

- **Complexity-based interpretation of MPPC differences (Section 5).** The paper identifies and quantifies a meaningful confound: simpler features (single-token patterns) naturally achieve higher MPPC regardless of architecture. Using GPT-4o-based auto-interpretation to label complexity and monosemanticity, the paper shows that the MPPC gap between cross-arch and model-seed comparisons partially reflects differing complexity effects. This provides useful nuance for interpreting the metric.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The subtractive decomposition (cross-arch MPPC minus model-seed MPPC) as the "marginal effect of architectural changes" is not adequately justified (Section 4.2, Figure 3b).** The paper interprets $\rho^{p→m} - \rho^{p→p'}$ as isolating architecture, but this requires two unvalidated assumptions: (a) that the model-seed skyline captures *all* non-architectural variation, and (b) that effects add linearly. In practice, the two compared conditions differ along many entangled dimensions simultaneously—depth (12 vs 24 layers), parameter count (160M vs 130M), effective capacity, training dynamics (different optimizer schedules, data curricula), and optimizer specifics—not just "architecture" in a controlled sense. The paper acknowledges that "model variables, especially ones related to initialization and training, are implicitly included in architectural changes" (line 211), but then proceeds to use the subtractive interpretation anyway without quantifying how much of the gap these confounds explain. The later complexity-based interpretation (Section 5) reframes the subtraction as complexity cancellation rather than architecture isolation, which partially mitigates this concern but also introduces ambiguity about what the difference actually measures. **This matters because the paper's claim of a "novel metric" to quantify architectural universality rests on this decomposition being valid.**

- **The model-seed skyline is not a clean baseline for cross-architectural comparisons.** The seed variant changes only initialization and training data order *within the same Transformer architecture*. Mamba differs from Pythia in fundamentally more ways: depth (24 vs 12 layers, a 2× difference), parameter count (130M vs 160M actual LM parameters), optimizer settings, training data curriculum, and the architectural inductive biases of SSMs vs attention. A more meaningful baseline would be a third model that shares one architecture but differs along a specific dimension (e.g., a deeper Pythia variant, or a Mamba variant with different depth). Without such controls, the paper cannot attribute the MPPC gap specifically to "architectural change" rather than these other confounded factors.

### Minor

- **GPT-4o-based auto-interpretation lacks human validation (Section 5).** The paper relies entirely on GPT-4o for complexity and monosemanticity scoring without any human-rated calibration subset. While auto-interpretation is standard practice for large-scale feature analysis, the claims about complexity-driven MPPC differences would be substantially stronger with even a small (100–200 feature) human validation study establishing inter-rater reliability with the LLM judge.

- **SAE training hyperparameters are underreported.** The paper states the expansion factor (32×, dictionary size 24576) but does not report learning rate, batch size, optimizer, number of training tokens, or whether the encoder/decoder are tied/untied. This hinders reproducibility.

- **Circuit analysis scope is too narrow to support broad claims about circuit universality (Section 6).** The induction circuit identification is limited to one model size (130M), one layer (17 out of 24), one specific prompt format ([A][B]...[A]), and one type of prompt (arbitrary token patterns). The paper does not test whether the identified circuit generalizes to other model sizes, different types of induction patterns (e.g., multi-token [B]), or more naturalistic text. While the finding is plausible and methodologically sound, the paper's claims about "structural analogy" would benefit from ablation experiments (e.g., bypassing the convolution to test whether the off-by-one motif is causal) and testing on a more diverse prompt set.

- **No statistical uncertainty quantification.** The paper reports means and distributions over ~300k features but provides no standard errors, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Given the large sample size, even trivial differences would be statistically significant; effect sizes and uncertainty measures would help readers assess the meaningfulness of the reported gaps (e.g., 0.74 vs 0.76 mean MPPC).

- **The complexity-based cancellation interpretation assumes equal complexity effects across skylines without verification (Section 5).** The argument that subtracting model-seed MPPC from cross-arch MPPC "cancels out" feature complexity assumes that the complexity–MPPC relationship is identical in both comparisons. Figure 4a suggests this is approximately true, but the paper does not quantify the similarity of these slopes or test whether the assumption holds for the tail of the distribution (complex features with score 4). For complex features where both MPPC values are low, the difference could be small due to floor effects rather than genuine architecture-independence.

- **Mamba mathematical description is imprecise (Section 2.2, Eq. 5).** The statement that "$F_a$ and $F_b$ are some kinds of operation function" (line 104) is too vague to be informative. While the paper cites the original Mamba paper for exact details, the description should at minimum specify that $F_a$ and $F_b$ are data-dependent linear transformations parameterized by the input $c_i^{(l)}$ (the discretization step), which is a standard part of the Mamba formulation.

### Trivial

- **Figure 3 caption says "Matched pairs mainly fall on near depth, i.e. on the diagonal"** (line 241). The actual depth mapping is approximately 2:1 (layer $l$ in 12-layer Pythia ↔ layer ~$2l$ in 24-layer Mamba), which is a proportional diagonal, not the literal matrix diagonal. The caption's phrasing is ambiguous.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Shared dictionary / cross-validation experiment.** Training a single SAE on concatenated activations from both models (or training on one and testing decoder reconstruction on the other) would provide a more direct test of whether the same features can represent both models' activations, complementing the current MPPC analysis.

- **Larger-scale circuit analysis.** Testing the induction circuit on multiple model sizes (e.g., Pythia-410M vs Mamba-390M), additional prompt variants, and other known circuits (e.g., IOI, docstring completion) would substantially strengthen the universality claims.

- **Explicit verification of the off-by-one hypothesis via convolution ablation.** The paper's suggestion that the local convolution causes the off-by-one effect (line 399, "We are still unclear why such mechanism forms") could be directly tested by intervening on the convolution weights (e.g., setting them to identity on the current token) and measuring whether the induction behavior changes.

## Novel Insights

The most novel synthesis emerging from the reviews is that the paper's central contribution faces a tension between its clean quantitative framework and the messy reality of cross-architectural comparison. The hierarchy of invariance (SAE→LM→architecture) and the two-skyline design are genuinely useful conceptual tools, but the paper's attempt to quantify the "marginal effect of architecture" via simple subtraction is undermined by the fact that the model seed variant and the cross-arch comparison differ on many more dimensions than architecture alone (depth, capacity, training dynamics). The complexity-based interpretation in Section 5 partially addresses this by reframing the subtraction as controlling for a known confound (feature simplicity/complexity), but this is not the same as isolating architecture. This suggests that the field may need more principled approaches to cross-architectural comparison—perhaps shared dictionaries, canonical correlation on SAE feature spaces, or intervention-based measures—rather than attempting to strip away confounds post-hoc via subtraction. Separately, the circuit analysis for Mamba induction is interesting precisely because it reveals a mechanistic divergence (off-by-one) that the feature-level analysis would miss, highlighting that representational similarity and mechanistic similarity are distinct dimensions of universality.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify what the subtractive metric actually measures.** The paper should either (a) provide a more careful decomposition that isolates specific architectural differences (e.g., by comparing Mamba to a deeper Transformer or controlling for parameter count), or (b) explicitly reframe the subtraction as removing complexity-driven similarity rather than isolating architecture, and remove the "marginal effect of architecture" language.

2. **Add human validation for auto-interpretation scores.** Even 100–200 features rated by 2–3 human annotators would substantially strengthen the complexity and monosemanticity claims in Section 5.

3. **Report SAE training hyperparameters** (learning rate, batch size, optimizer, training steps/tokens, tied/untied decoder) in the main text or appendix.

4. **Add ablation experiments for the circuit analysis** to test whether the identified components (convolution, layer 17 SSM state) are causally necessary for induction behavior.

5. **Include confidence intervals or standard errors** for the reported mean MPPC values and MPPC differences. Given ~300k features, bootstrap-based intervals would be straightforward.

6. **Fix the Figure 3 caption** to say "proportional depth" rather than "diagonal."
