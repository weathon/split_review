## Summary

This paper introduces KaSA (Knowledge-aware Singular-value Adaptation), a PEFT method that combines SVD truncation of the base model (removing minor singular components) with an SVD-structured adapter whose learnable singular values are regularized via L2 and orthogonality losses. The method is evaluated across NLU (GLUE with RoBERTa and DeBERTaV3), NLG (E2E with GPT-2), instruction following (LLaMA3 8B, Mistral 7B, Gemma 7B, LLaMA2 13B on 4 synthetic datasets and MT-Bench), and commonsense reasoning (8 datasets with LLaMA2 7B and LLaMA3 8B). KaSA consistently outperforms LoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA, and in many settings full fine-tuning, with particular strength in commonsense reasoning where gains over MiLoRA reach +2.3–2.7% average accuracy.

## Strengths

- **Strong and consistent empirical results across diverse settings.** KaSA outperforms FFT and all compared PEFT baselines on average across NLU (e.g., RoBERTa-large: 89.0% vs. 88.2% FFT), NLG (GPT-2 Large: best on all 5 metrics), instruction following (beats FFT on multiple models/datasets), and commonsense reasoning (LLaMA2 7B: 81.5% average vs. 79.2% MiLoRA; LLaMA3 8B: 84.6% vs. 81.9% MiLoRA). The consistency across model sizes and task types is impressive.

- **Thorough component ablation and parameter scalability analysis.** Figure 3 (ablation) isolates the contribution of each of the four components (SVD truncation, knowledge-aware adaptation, L2 regularization, orthogonality loss), showing monotonic improvement from LoRA baseline to full KaSA, with performance drops of 2.05–3.25% when excluding any component. Figure 4 shows KaSA outperforms LoRA, PiSSA, and MiLoRA across all rank settings (r = 1–128), with largest relative gains at low parameter budgets.

- **Practical deployment advantage.** KaSA preserves LoRA's zero-inference-latency property (adapter can be merged into the base model) and supports task-switching with a single frozen base model. The authors also release high-quality synthetic instruction datasets generated by GPT4o.

- **Transparent significance reporting for instruction following.** The paper reports standard deviations and p-values (α=0.05) for all instruction-following experiments, allowing readers to assess reliability.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "knowledge-aware" mechanism claim is not supported by the evidence provided.** The paper's central framing — that the learnable singular values "dynamically activate knowledge based on its relevance to the task" — is supported only by a heatmap (Figure 6) showing that learned singular values vary across layers. This observation is automatic for any learnable diagonal matrix and does not demonstrate that the values correspond to *knowledge relevance* in any testable sense. The paper does not: (1) correlate learned singular values with any external measure of task-relatedness, (2) ablate whether de-activating certain learned directions harms performance on irrelevant tasks, or (3) compare against a non-adaptive baseline where singular values are fixed (e.g., all ones). The method works empirically, and the gap is primarily in framing rather than results — but the stated motivation is significantly stronger than what is actually shown. The paper would be better served by either providing mechanistic evidence or recalibrating the claims to match what is demonstrated (that the SVD-structured adapter + regularizers empirically outperform prior methods, regardless of whether the mechanism is "knowledge-awareness").

### Minor

- **Missing AdaLoRA in instruction following and commonsense reasoning experiments.** AdaLoRA is the most direct methodological competitor (SVD reparameterization with learnable singular values + orthogonality constraints). It is included in NLU (where KaSA outperforms it) and NLG (GPT-2 Medium), but omitted from instruction following (Table 4) and commonsense reasoning (Table 5), weakening the claim of broad superiority across all settings. Given that KaSA outperforms PiSSA and MiLoRA in these settings, including AdaLoRA would strengthen the empirical contribution.

- **No variance reported for NLU and NLG results.** NLU results (Tables 1–2) report only median values from 5 runs with no standard deviations or confidence intervals. NLG results (Table 3) report means from 3 runs with no variance. This makes it difficult for readers to assess whether the observed gains (e.g., +0.2 BLEU on GPT-2 Medium) are statistically reliable. The paper does report variance for instruction following — this should be extended to all experiments.

- **SVD truncation approximation (dropping W_noise + ΔW_noise) is assumed without verification.** The method in Equation (4) drops both the truncated minor components and their task-specific updates as negligible. While this follows assumptions in prior work (cited), the paper does not verify empirically that the update on the truncated subspace is indeed small or harmful. An ablation measuring the impact of including vs. excluding ΔW_noise would clarify whether this step is critical or incidental.

- **Some p-values indicate non-significant differences.** The paper's claim that "9 out of 12 experimental settings on MT-Bench" are significant at α=0.05 is accurate (verified). However, for the synthetic datasets, several baseline-vs-KaSA comparisons are not significant (e.g., Gemma 7B summarization PiSSA p=0.730; Mistral 7B classification PiSSA p=0.103, coding MiLoRA p=0.158). The paper should present a more balanced account of which differences are and are not significant, rather than selectively highlighting the MT-Bench statistic.

### Trivial

- **The "16 benchmarks" in the abstract are not clearly enumerated.** Counting across experiments yields a number in that range, but a reader cannot easily verify which 16 are meant without cross-referencing all tables.

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity for β and γ is not reported.** The ablation shows both regularizers contribute, but no analysis of how performance varies with different values of β, γ, or the truncation rank r (vs. adaptation rank) is provided.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Computational cost of SVD on large models.** The paper would benefit from briefly reporting the time and memory overhead of performing SVD on all weight matrices of 7B–13B models. This is relevant for practitioners considering adoption.

- **Non-adaptive baseline for singular values.** Comparing KaSA against a variant where ΔΣ is not learned (set to identity, or fixed to uniform values) would isolate the benefit of making singular values learnable, separate from the SVD-structure of the adapter itself.

- **WinoGrande result for LLaMA3 8B.** KaSA's 85.5 is slightly below MiLoRA's 85.6 on WinoGrande. This minor drop (and its significance) is worth noting.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface a genuine tension: KaSA's empirical strength likely stems from the combination of SVD truncation + SVD-structured adapter + regularizers rather than a proven "knowledge-awareness" mechanism. This suggests the method could be reframed as a principled low-rank adaptation in a shared spectral space — which may be a more defensible and equally compelling contribution than the current knowledge-activation narrative. The ablation study actually supports this re-interpretation: the SVD truncation step and the structured adapter each contribute independently, and their combination works well without requiring a "relevance" interpretation.

## Suggestions

1. **Tone down or validate the "knowledge-aware" mechanism.** Either provide intervention experiments (freeze learned singular values, compare against fixed-Σ baseline, correlate values with task-specific importance scores) or reframe the narrative to emphasize the SVD-structured adaptation + regularization combination as the source of gains. The empirical results are strong enough to stand without the mechanistic claim.

2. **Add AdaLoRA to instruction following and commonsense experiments.** This is the most important missing baseline and would make the comparison comprehensive at low experimental cost (given the infrastructure is already set up).

3. **Report standard deviations for NLU and NLG tables.** Readers need to assess reliability. The paper already computes multiple runs; just report the variance.

4. **Provide a more complete discussion of statistical significance.** Rather than highlighting the MT-Bench statistic selectively, report how many total comparisons (across all tasks and baselines) reach significance.

## Score and Decision

This is a solid empirical paper with a well-defined method, extensive evaluation across diverse settings, and consistent improvements over strong baselines. The main gap is the overclaimed mechanistic interpretation ("knowledge-awareness"), which is a framing issue rather than a fatal methodological flaw. The missing AdaLoRA baseline in two settings weakens but does not invalidate the results. With revisions addressing the mechanistic framing and completeness of baseline comparisons, the paper makes a clear contribution to the PEFT literature.
