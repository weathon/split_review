## Summary

This paper proposes Ego-Foresight (EF), a self-supervised method for agent-environment disentanglement that uses motion-based visuomotor prediction to learn separate representations for the agent and scene without requiring the supervisory masks needed by prior work (e.g., SEAR). The method uses a dual-encoder architecture (scene encoder with a slow-feature similarity loss + proprioceptive LSTM) to predict future frames conditioned on future proprioception, and integrates these learned representations with DDPG for model-free RL. Experiments on DMC, Distracting DMC, and Meta-World show that EF matches or exceeds supervised baselines on several tasks, with advantages that grow with agent complexity.

## Strengths

1. **Removes the need for costly supervisory masks while maintaining competitive RL performance on several tasks.** The key practical advantage of EF over SEAR is eliminating the need for ground-truth agent segmentation masks. On the Quadruped task (16 DOF), EF outperforms all baselines including the supervised SEAR and the strong model-free DrQ-v2 (Fig. 4), showing that self-supervised motion prediction can replace costly supervision for high-DOF control. On Distracting DMC (Fig. 5), EF maintains competitive performance despite random backgrounds, supporting robustness to visual distractions.

2. **Scalable advantage with increasing agent complexity.** Results across DMC tasks (Cartpole 2 DOF, Walker 7 DOF, Cheetah 8 DOF, Quadruped 16 DOF) show a trend where EF's relative benefit grows with agent complexity (Sec. 4.2). This is a non-trivial finding that aligns with the paper's central motivation — that modeling one's own embodiment becomes more valuable as the body becomes more complex.

3. **Qualitative evidence of learned agent-environment separation and body-schema adaptation.** Figure 7 provides illustrative evidence of the model's behavior: in the Door Open task, only the arm is predicted while the door remains static (showing scene-background understanding); in the Hammer task, the hammer moves once integrated into the agent's body. These visualizations, while not quantified, demonstrate the model's emergent ability to distinguish agent from environment without supervision.

4. **Systematic ablation of design parameters.** The paper ablates prediction horizon \(H\) (Fig. 8) and similarity loss weight \(\alpha\) (Fig. 9), providing practical guidance. The finding that longer horizons (\(H=40\)) yield better efficiency and performance is actionable for practitioners.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The core claim of "self-supervised agent-environment disentanglement" is not directly validated, and the observed RL improvements could arise from other components.** The paper's central contribution is that motion-based prediction enables disentangled agent/environment representations. However, no experiment directly measures whether the learned representations are actually disentangled — e.g., testing whether the scene encoder is invariant to agent pose, or whether the proprio encoder captures motion patterns absent from the scene encoding. The evidence rests entirely on:
   - Downstream RL performance (which could benefit from the auxiliary reconstruction loss acting as a regularizer, the diverse motor-babbling data, or the additional parameters of the LSTM/encoder).
   - Qualitative results (Figure 7) that are illustrative but not quantified.
   
   The paper lacks the most critical control ablation: comparing against a version of the same architecture that predicts future frames *without* proprioceptive conditioning (i.e., standard video prediction as an auxiliary loss). Without this, it is impossible to attribute the gains to proprioceptively-conditioned disentanglement rather than to the auxiliary prediction loss alone. This gap directly undermines the paper's primary claimed contribution.

2. **The SEAR baseline comparison is weakened by acknowledged reproduction failures.** The paper states (Sec. 4.1) that despite using SEAR's public code, the authors "failed to match the results reported in the original paper" and note "inconsistencies in the original plots such as success rates above 1." Since SEAR is the most directly comparable baseline (supervised agent-environment disentanglement vs. EF's self-supervised version), the comparison is made against a potentially suboptimal re-implementation. The paper also admits that "not all baselines and experiments use the same number of random seeds" — while seed count details exist in the appendix, the acknowledged asymmetry in replication quality raises concerns about whether SEAR is being compared under fair conditions.

3. **The ablation study omits the most important control — isolating the core disentanglement architecture.** The paper ablates prediction horizon \(H\) and similarity loss weight \(\alpha\), but these tests probe peripheral design choices rather than the central mechanism. The critical missing ablation is: what happens if you remove the proprioceptive-conditioning pathway from the architecture entirely (i.e., predict future frames without any conditioning on future proprioception)? Without this control, the paper cannot distinguish between the benefits of (a) having an auxiliary video prediction loss vs. (b) having a proprioceptively-conditioned prediction loss that explicitly forces agent-environment separation. The \(\alpha=0\) ablation removes the similarity loss but retains the full dual-encoder structure with proprioceptive conditioning, so it does not address this gap.

### Minor

4. **Motor babbling confounds the sample-efficiency comparison in ways the paper does not fully control.** EF requires 50k steps of random actions (motor babbling) before task-relevant learning begins, while baselines start learning from step 0. The paper includes these 50k steps on the x-axis of all learning curves, which is transparent but conflates the cost of babbling with the benefits of the learned representation. More importantly, motor babbling provides diverse exploratory data that baselines do not receive — it is unclear how much of EF's later advantage comes from this extra data versus from representation quality. A control (e.g., providing the same 50k random steps to baselines before training, or comparing EF against a version with a much shorter babbling phase) would strengthen the conclusions.

5. **Meta-World results are mixed and the framing slightly overstates consistency.** EF achieves the best success rate in only 3 of 10 Meta-World tasks, matches baselines in 3, and underperforms in 4 (Sec. 4.3). While the paper honestly reports these numbers and the "matches or improves" claim is technically true, the overall picture is that EF provides uneven benefits across manipulation tasks. This limits the generality of the claimed advantages.

6. **The DOF-based trend claim rests on only four tasks without statistical testing.** The observation that EF's advantage grows with agent complexity is based on the DMC results from Cartpole (2 DOF), Walker (7 DOF), Cheetah (8 DOF), and Quadruped (16 DOF). While the pattern is suggestive and consistent with the paper's motivation, four data points with different task types (swing-up vs. locomotion) are insufficient to establish a reliable trend — the confound between DOF count and task difficulty is not disentangled.

## Novel Insights

The reviews converge on a key observation that goes beyond the paper's own narrative: the paper claims to demonstrate "self-supervised agent-environment disentanglement" but provides no direct evidence that the representations are actually disentangled. The RL improvements — while real on several tasks — could be attributed to any combination of the auxiliary reconstruction loss, the diverse motor-babbling data, the additional model parameters, or the proprioceptive conditioning itself. The most incisive insight from the reviews is that the paper's evaluation treats downstream RL performance as a proxy for disentanglement without isolating the mechanism. This gap is structural — it could be addressed by simple diagnostic probes (e.g., testing scene encoder invariance to agent pose) or by the missing ablation (removing proprioceptive conditioning). Absent such controls, the paper's central claim remains a plausible hypothesis rather than a demonstrated result.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a direct disentanglement diagnostic:** Train a linear probe to predict agent joint angles from the scene encoder's output. If disentanglement succeeds, this probe should perform at chance. Conversely, verify that the proprio encoder contains sufficient information to reconstruct agent pose. This would provide direct evidence for the claimed property.
2. **Add the critical ablation:** Compare against a version of EF that predicts future frames *without* conditioning on future proprioception (i.e., standard video prediction as an auxiliary loss, using the same architecture). If EF outperforms this variant, the advantage comes from the proprioceptively-conditioned prediction rather than just having an auxiliary loss.
3. **Control for motor babbling:** Either (a) start the x-axis of learning curves after the babbling phase to show clean sample-efficiency comparison, or (b) run baselines with the same 50k random-step pre-training before their RL begins, to isolate the effect of the learned representation from the extra data.
