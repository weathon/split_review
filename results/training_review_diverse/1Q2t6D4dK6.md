## Summary

This paper presents the first systematic benchmark evaluating adversarial defenses for Image Quality Assessment (IQA) metrics. It compares 25 defense strategies (17 purification, 6 adversarial training, 6 certified robustness) against 14 attack algorithms in both non-adaptive and adaptive settings, using 7 NR IQA metrics and 1,000 images from KONIQ-10K. The benchmark generates over 215,000 adversarial images and provides a public leaderboard.

## Strengths

1. **First systematic benchmark bridging the IQA adversarial-defense gap.** The paper fills a clear void: while classification has RobustBench and similar resources, no prior work provides a unified framework for evaluating defenses on IQA metrics. The coverage of 25 defenses × 14 attacks × 7 metrics is genuinely comprehensive for a first-of-its-kind study. (Sec. 1, Sec. 2, Table 1)

2. **Large-scale, reproducible experimental pipeline.** The evaluation uses 1,000 diverse images from KONIQ-10K (stratified by SI, colorfulness, and MOS), generating over 215,000 adversarial images. All code is released via GitLab CI/CD with ~5,000 GPU hours of computation on standard hardware, enabling full reproducibility. (Sec. 3.4, Sec. 3.6)

3. **Novel adaptive-attack evaluation for IQA defenses.** This is the first benchmark to systematically compare adaptive vs. non-adaptive scenarios for IQA defenses, integrating differentiable defenses into the attacked metric so gradient-based attacks can access defense gradients. The results reveal stark contrasts — e.g., Flip ranks best in non-adaptive yet worst in adaptive (Table 1) — demonstrating a critical, previously unstudied dimension of vulnerability. (Sec. 3.2, Table 1, Fig. 1)

4. **Multi-faceted evaluation metrics.** The paper defines four criteria (D_score, D_score^(D), Q_score, SROCC_adv, SROCC_clear) plus certification-specific metrics (radius, abstention, relative delta), enabling comparison along multiple axes rather than a single score. (Sec. 3.5, Tables 1–3)

5. **Inclusion of the full defense landscape.** The benchmark spans adversarial purification (17 methods), adversarial training (6 configurations), and certified defenses (6 methods), allowing cross-category comparisons. (Sec. 3.3, Tables 2, 3)

6. **Public leaderboard and dataset release.** The online leaderboard (https://videoprocessing.ai/benchmarks/iqa-defenses.html) and planned adversarial-image dataset release enable community contributions and independent verification. (Abstract, Conclusion)

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Very small sample sizes undermine the reliability of black-box and certified defense results.** The paper uses only **50 images** for black-box attacks and **10 images** for certified defenses (Sec. 3.4, line 185), citing computational complexity. The confidence intervals in Table 3 are wide (e.g., D_score for DMS: 12.57±11.39%, SROCC_adv for RS: 0.59±0.16), making it difficult to draw reliable conclusions about relative method performance. For a benchmark aiming to guide future work, these sample sizes are too small to support the presented rankings and comparisons. This is a significant evidential gap for these sub-evaluations.

### Minor

2. **No statistical significance testing.** The paper reports 95% confidence intervals but never tests whether differences between defenses are statistically significant. Many intervals overlap substantially (e.g., DISCO, JPEG, and Color Quantization in the non-adaptive SROCC_adv column), so readers cannot determine which differences are meaningful. The paper acknowledges this in the Limitations (Sec. 5, "more rigorous statistical framework is needed"), but the omission weakens the core comparative claim.

3. **Certified metrics reported alongside attack-based evaluation without clarifying their independence.** The certified radius (l2 guarantee) and certified relative delta are theoretical properties computed independently of the attack suite. However, they appear in Table 3 alongside empirical attack-based metrics (D_score, SROCC) without explicitly noting that the certified guarantees do not apply to the l∞/l0/semantic attacks used in the empirical evaluation. This creates potential for misinterpretation. The empirical metrics (D_score, SROCC) are valid; the certified metrics are meaningful on their own terms — but the connection should be clarified.

4. **D_score^(D) metric interpretation gap for spatial transformations.** For defenses that heavily transform the image (Flip, Crop, Rotate), D_score^(D) measures the disparity between *purified attacked* and *purified original* images. If both are heavily transformed, the metric may be artificially low because both are "broken similarly" — not because the attack was removed. The paper acknowledges this issue for Q_score (Sec. 3.5, line 201) but not for D_score^(D).

5. **Adversarial training evaluation covers only 2 of 7 IQA metrics.** The paper acknowledges computational constraints (Sec. 3.4, line 186), but this limits cross-defense-type comparisons and weakens the claim of a fully unified framework. Conclusions about adversarial training relative to other defense types should be caveated more prominently.

6. **Ten-class division for certification not validated.** The paper states "ten segments showed optimal correlations with subjective scores" (line 159) but provides no ablation data or reference. Since the choice affects certification metrics (class-based radius vs. regression delta), this design decision needs stronger justification.

7. **Adaptive attack entries missing for non-differentiable defenses.** JPEG and Color Quantization have no adaptive entries ("---" in Table 1). The paper implies that only differentiable defenses are used in adaptive attacks (Sec. 3.2, "We integrate differentiable defenses"), but this should be stated explicitly when discussing the adaptive results.

8. **Certification–attack mismatch and small sample issues not listed in Limitations.** The Limitations section (Sec. 5) discusses dataset bias, single-parameter attacks, transferability, and ranking methodology, but omits the small-sample problem for certified/black-box evaluations and the disconnection between certified guarantees and the attack threat model.

### Trivial
- Defense parameters are selected for the non-adaptive case and reused for adaptive (Table 1 caption). This is stated but could be acknowledged earlier in the methodology as a design choice that may understate adaptive defense performance.
- Eq. 2 (adversarial purification formulation) is a conceptual sketch that does not reflect the actual optimization procedures of the listed purification methods — a brief note clarifying this would help.

## Nice-to-Haves
- **Increase sample sizes for certified defenses and black-box attacks** in future releases of the benchmark. Even 50 images would substantially improve statistical reliability.
- **Add bootstrap-based pairwise significance tests** or effect-size analysis to support the comparative rankings.
- **Ablation on the number of quality classes** (e.g., 5 vs. 10 vs. 20) for the classification-based certified methods.
- **Include peak memory usage** alongside mean inference time in Table 1 for practitioners with resource constraints.

## Removed Points

These points were identified by reviewers but are removed from the main weakness list with brief justification:

- **Certification as a "structural flaw" / "does not test what it purports to test"**: The paper evaluates defenses empirically against the attack suite (D_score, SROCC) AND separately reports certified radii/deltas as theoretical guarantees of the defense. The empirical evaluation is valid regardless of threat model; the certified metrics are independent properties. The paper makes no claim that the certified radius applies to the specific attacks. This is a presentation clarity issue, not a structural flaw, and the concern is downgraded to Minor (point 3 above).

- **Eq. 2 mismatch with actual implementations**: The equation is a conceptual formulation of the defense goal, not an implementation recipe for each specific method. This is standard practice for benchmark papers that evaluate pre-existing methods. No actual flaw.

- **"Adversarial training conclusions are incomplete" gripe**: The paper explicitly states (line 186) that only 2 metrics are used due to computational cost. The conclusions in the relevant section are appropriately scoped to the evaluated metrics.

- **Pure formatting/style nitpicks**: None identified beyond those already addressed.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The key novel finding — that simple spatial transforms dominate non-adaptive defense while stochastic methods dominate adaptive, and that no defense is universally effective — is a genuine result of the benchmark, but it is the paper's own empirical discovery rather than a meta-insight from the reviews.

## Suggestions

- Expand the certified-defense and black-box evaluations to at least 50–100 images in a benchmark update; the current 10/50 sizes are too small for statistically reliable rankings.
- Add a brief clarification that certified radii/deltas are properties of the defense method under l2 perturbations and are independent of the attack-based empirical evaluation.
- Report pairwise significance tests (bootstrapped confidence intervals on differences) to support the comparative claims, or at least flag overlapping intervals as statistically indistinguishable.
- Provide the ablation data for the 10-class division choice for certified methods.
- Explicitly note why certain defenses lack adaptive entries (non-differentiability).

## Score and Decision

This paper makes a solid and timely contribution — the first comprehensive benchmark for adversarial defenses on IQA metrics. The core purification results (1,000 images) are the main contribution and are empirically sound. The weaknesses (small samples for sub-evaluations, lack of significance testing, and several clarification issues) are real but not fatal: they affect the reliability of specific sub-results without invalidating the overall contribution. With expanded sampling and some clarifications, this benchmark would be even stronger.
