## Summary

This paper introduces CAD-Editor, the first text-based CAD editing system. It formulates the task as sequence-to-sequence using an LLM-friendly CAD sequence representation, fine-tunes LLaMA-3-8B on a large synthetic dataset (generated by combining design-variation models SkexGen/Hnc-CAD with GPT-4o multi-level captioning), and then further fine-tunes on a small selective dataset built from the model's own outputs (chosen via D-CLIP score or human feedback). The method achieves 91.4% valid ratio, 0.21 D-CLIP score, and 31.1% human evaluation success rate — significantly outperforming GPT-4o baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel task formulation and first working system for text-based CAD editing.** The paper formulates text-based CAD editing as a seq2seq problem with an LLM-adapted CAD sequence representation (variable-length textual tokens for categorical/numerical values). This is the first work to bridge natural-language instructions and parametric CAD editing, addressing an important gap between design-variation generation and user-controllable editing.

- **Effective synthetic data pipeline validated by ablations.** The combination of design-variation models (SkexGen, Hnc-CAD) for paired CAD generation and GPT-4o with multi-level captioning (describe → identify differences → compress) produces a high-quality training set. Ablations (Table 2) show that multi-level captioning dramatically improves over basic captioning across all metrics (e.g., Valid Ratio 63.4% vs. 42.1%, D-CLIP −0.06 vs. −0.28).

- **Two-stage fine-tuning with selective data boosts instruction alignment.** The key insight that sampling the initial model multiple times and selecting good outputs (via D-CLIP or human feedback) for a second fine-tuning stage yields measurable improvements. Table 2 shows D-CLIP improving from 0.11 (no selective data) to 0.20 (with DCF) and human eval improving correspondingly. This is a practical strategy for injecting human intention without costly annotation.

- **Comprehensive evaluation.** The paper reports Valid Ratio, D-CLIP, COV/MMD/JSD, and a human evaluation with 2,500 ratings by five crowd workers each. CAD-Editor achieves 91.4% Valid Ratio and 0.21 D-CLIP, far exceeding GPT-4o (basic: −0.65; 3-shot: −0.01), and is the only method with a non-trivial human evaluation score (31.1% vs. 0% and 16.6%). Qualitative results (Figures 4–8) convincingly demonstrate diverse editing capabilities.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Test set is generated by the same pipeline as the synthetic training data, limiting generalization claims.** The test set (2,000 examples) is constructed by taking CAD models from the DeepCAD test split, generating variants using SkexGen/Hnc-CAD, and captioning them with GPT-4o — the exact same pipeline used to produce the synthetic training data (Section 4.1: "get the initial version following Section 3.2"). While manual verification ensures correctness, the distribution of edits and language in the test set mirrors that of the training data. The paper claims to solve "text-based CAD editing" broadly, but the evaluation only supports performance on the distribution of edits produced by the specific variation-generation + captioning pipeline. The human evaluation is conducted on the same generated examples, so it does not provide independent evidence of generalization to naturally-occurring or human-written instructions. The conclusion acknowledges dependence on the quality of GPT-4o and design variation models, but the test set limitation is not discussed explicitly. Including a small evaluation on human-written instructions (even 50–100 examples) would substantially strengthen the generalization claim.

### Minor

- **31.1% human evaluation success rate is low, and the paper does not characterize what "unsuccessful" means.** While the paper reports the number honestly and shows it beats baselines (16.6%, 0%), the conclusion describes the performance as "compelling." Given that roughly two-thirds of generated edits are judged unsuccessful, a failure-mode analysis (e.g., what fraction are invalid shapes vs. wrong edits vs. unchanged outputs?) would substantially strengthen the paper and help readers understand where the model falls short. This is addressable in a rebuttal and does not threaten the core contribution.

- **Selective dataset size and statistics not reported.** The paper describes constructing a selective dataset from the initial model's outputs but never states its size, how many samples are generated per input, or what fraction of outputs are selected by D-CLIP/HF. Without these numbers, it is impossible to assess how much human or automatic selection effort is required and how dependent the method is on the initial fine-tuning quality.

- **No error bars or variance reported.** Results in Table 1 are described as "average scores across 3 runs" but no standard deviation, min/max, or per-run values are shown. For a small number of runs, variance could be meaningful, particularly for D-CLIP and MMD/JSD.

- **Data filtering thresholds without justification.** The cosine similarity thresholds (0.95 for SkexGen, 0.9 for Hnc-CAD) are presented without sensitivity analysis or rationale. While not a fatal gap, this makes it hard to assess how robust the data pipeline is to threshold choices.

- **Ablation studies use a 10k-example mini version, not the full 92k dataset.** The paper is transparent about this ("restricted computing resources"), but it leaves open the question of whether the gains from multi-level captioning and selective data hold at full scale.

### Trivial

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Failure-case analysis.** Categorizing human evaluation failures (invalid geometry, instruction not followed, shape correct but wrong location, unchanged output) would clarify where the model struggles and guide future work.
- **A small evaluation set with human-written instructions** (e.g., 50–100 manually authored edits on existing CAD models not generated by SkexGen/Hnc-CAD) would provide the strongest evidence of generalization beyond the synthetic pipeline.
- **Sensitivity analysis on data filtering thresholds** would help assess pipeline robustness.

## Suggestions

1. **Explicitly discuss the test set limitation** and add a small evaluation on human-written instructions. This is the single most impactful addition the authors could make.
2. **Add a failure-mode analysis** (categorizing unsuccessful human eval cases) to clarify where the model falls short.
3. **Report selective dataset size** and how often the initial model produces a "good" sample.
4. **Add error bars or per-run values** for the reported metrics.

## Score and Decision

This paper presents a credible first solution to the under-explored problem of text-based CAD editing. The method is well-motivated, the ablations validate design choices, and the evaluation is comprehensive by the standards of a new task. The main weakness — test set distribution overlap with training data — is significant but not fatal; it is a transparency issue that can be addressed with an explicit limitation discussion and/or an additional small-scale evaluation on human-written instructions. The paper is largely sound with bounded weaknesses.

**Overall assessment:** This is a solid contribution that should be published, with revisions addressing the evaluation limitation and reporting omissions.
