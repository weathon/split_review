## Summary

This paper investigates compositionality failures in text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion variants). It identifies two error sources: (i) erroneous attention contributions in the CLIP text encoder (minor), and (ii) sub-optimality of CLIP's output space for compositional prompts (major). The core contribution is WiCLP (Window-based Compositional Linear Projection), a lightweight learned linear transformation applied to CLIP token embeddings that maps them to a space better suited for the UNet. The method is trained on a curated set of VQA-filtered images generated by other models. The paper also introduces SWITCH-OFF, which applies WiCLP only during early denoising steps to preserve FID on non-compositional prompts.

## Strengths

- **Empirical proof that CLIP's output space is the primary error source (Section 4, Figure 5):** By per-prompt optimization of text embeddings while keeping the UNet frozen, VQA scores jump from ~0.36 to ~0.75 on color prompts. This directly demonstrates that the UNet is fully capable of compositionality when given suitable embeddings — the bottleneck is CLIP's output space, not the UNet. This is the paper's strongest and most cleanly controlled result.

- **Parameter-efficient method with measurable compositional gains:** WiCLP (a single linear layer with a window of 5 tokens) improves VQA by 16.18% (color), 15.15% (texture), and 9.51% (shape) over the base SD v1.4, while maintaining competitive FID (27.40 on MS-COCO vs. 30.54 for GORS). The method requires orders of magnitude fewer parameters than UNet fine-tuning approaches and adds negligible inference cost.

- **Diagnostic attribution of erroneous attention in CLIP vs. T5 (Section 3.1, Figure 4):** Using attention-contribution analysis across 780 color and 582 texture prompts, the paper quantitatively shows CLIP has significantly more "unintended attention" between mismatched attribute-object pairs compared to T5, providing a concrete measurable explanation for T5's superior compositionality in models like DeepFloyd.

- **SWITCH-OFF technique for practical deployment (Section 5.3):** The paper identifies that applying WiCLP at all timesteps raises FID and introduces a simple control (only apply WiCLP when t ≥ τ) to retain compositional gains while recovering clean FID. This is a practical contribution that addresses a real concern in the space.

- **Zero-shot attention reweighting as a controlled diagnostic (Section 3.2):** Hand-crafting an attention reweighting matrix yields only 2.93% VQA improvement, confirming that erroneous attention is a *minor* source of compositional failures — which reinforces the paper's main thesis about CLIP output space being the dominant issue.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Training data construction confounds the quantitative comparisons against fine-tuning baselines (Section 2.3).** The training set for WiCLP is constructed by generating 210 images per prompt from three models (SD 1.4, DeepFloyd, SynGen) and selecting the **top 30 with the highest VQA scores** — the same VQA metric used for evaluation. This introduces a confound: WiCLP is fine-tuned on a dataset explicitly curated to score well on VQA. The baselines (especially GORS, which also fine-tunes) were trained on different data (the original T2I-CompBench training set, which involves real images without this per-prompt VQA filtering). The paper does not control for this, making it impossible to tell how much of WiCLP's reported advantage over GORS comes from the data curation versus the projection method itself. This does **not** affect the per-prompt optimization experiment (Section 4) or comparisons against zero-shot methods (Attn-Exct, Structured Diffusion, Composable Diffusion), but it weakens the central quantitative claim that WiCLP "outperforms existing methods."

- **No discussion of limitations.** The paper does not acknowledge that the training data is synthetic and VQA-filtered, that the method was only tested on three attribute categories (color, texture, shape), or that the VQA metric is a learned proxy, not a perfect measure. This omission makes the claims sound stronger than the evidence supports at face value.

### Minor

- **SWITCH-OFF configuration for Table 1 is unclear.** Section 5.3 discusses τ = 800 as offering "competitive VQA score" and "competitive FID similar to that of the clean model," but the paper never explicitly states whether the VQA/FID numbers reported in Table 1 use τ = 800, a different τ, or the full projection at all timesteps. This ambiguity prevents readers from connecting the trade-off analysis to the headline results.

- **Window size s = 2 is chosen without ablation.** WiCLP's only hyperparameter (beyond the learned weights) is the window size. The paper uses s = 2 without any study of how VQA/FID vary with s. Since WiCLP's improvement over CLP (s = 0) is a central claim, showing this dependency would strengthen the contribution.

- **No explicit quantitative comparison between CLP and WiCLP in text.** The paper states "WiCLP improves over CLP" (Section 5.2) and this may be visible in Table 1, but no numerical VQA/FID comparison of CLP vs. WiCLP is given in running text. The marginal benefit of the window over the token-wise projection is unclear from the prose alone.

- **The "number of layers with unintended attention" metric is coarse (Section 3.1).** Counting layers without considering attention magnitude or downstream effect reduces precision. This does not undermine the paper's conclusion (the T5 vs. CLIP gap is large and directionally clear), but the metric choice lowers the rigor of this diagnostic.

- **CLIP vs. T5 comparison is confounded by architecture differences.** The two text encoders differ in layer count, head dimension, and pretraining data. The paper's observed gap in "unintended attention" could partially reflect these architectural differences rather than inherent encoder quality. This weakens the implied causal claim about why T5-based models compose better.

- **Per-prompt optimization experiment (Section 4) does not fully rule out UNet memorization.** Since the embedding is optimized on the same images used to compute VQA, the very high scores (~0.75) may partly reflect overfitting rather than a reachable embedding space. The experiment is still valuable as an existence proof, but this nuance is not discussed.

## Suggestions

## Score and Decision

This paper makes a genuine analytic contribution by isolating CLIP's output space as the primary bottleneck for compositional generation in Stable Diffusion, and the per-prompt embedding optimization experiment (Section 4) is clean and convincing. The WiCLP method is simple, parameter-efficient, and shows real improvements. However, the headline quantitative comparisons against fine-tuning baselines (especially GORS) are compromised by the VQA-filtered training data construction — the WiCLP training set is curated using the same evaluation metric, while baselines were trained on different data. This confound is significant enough that the claimed "state-of-the-art" performance cannot be taken at face value. The paper needs a major revision (retraining on unfiltered data or providing controlled comparisons) to make the quantitative claims credible. The diagnostic analysis and SWITCH-OFF idea remain valuable, but the central experimental evidence for WiCLP's superiority over existing methods is not yet convincing.
