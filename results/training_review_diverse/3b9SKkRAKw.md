## Summary

This paper proposes LeFusion, a lesion-focused diffusion model for synthesizing lesions into lesion-free medical images. The key innovations are: (1) a lesion-focused training loss that computes error only within lesion regions while preserving backgrounds through inpainting-style forward-diffusion, (2) histogram-based texture control for handling multi-peak lesions (e.g., distinct lung nodule types), (3) multi-channel decomposition for joint multi-class lesion generation (e.g., MI and PMO in cardiac MRI), and (4) DiffMask, a controllable lesion mask diffusion model. Validation on lung nodule CT (LIDC) and cardiac lesion MRI (Emidec) shows consistent downstream segmentation improvements for nnUNet and SwinUNETR.

## Strengths

1. **Lesion-focused training objective with guaranteed background preservation**: The paper redesigns the diffusion loss to compute error only within the lesion mask (Eq. 4), and the inpainting-style mixing step (Eq. 3) overwrites non-lesion areas with forward-diffused real background. This ensures backgrounds are never generated by the model and remain faithful to the original scan — a principled advantage over conditional diffusion methods that must also model backgrounds.

2. **Histogram-based texture control for multi-peak lesions**: The method conditions the diffusion process on the lesion's grayscale histogram via cross-attention, enabling explicit control over lesion types (e.g., ground-glass vs. solid nodules) without requiring additional class annotations. This addresses a genuine challenge where models otherwise collapse toward healthy-appearing lesions (Fig. 6).

3. **Multi-channel decomposition for joint multi-class lesion generation**: Generating each lesion class in a separate channel and combining through masks allows the model to capture inter-class correlations. On Emidec, the joint version (LeFusion-J) improves both MI and PMO Dice over separate modeling and all baselines (Table 2).

4. **Significant downstream segmentation improvements**: LeFusion-generated synthetic data consistently improves nnUNet and SwinUNETR on both datasets. Improvements of ~5% Dice on LIDC and substantial gains on Emidec (e.g., +7.41% PMO Dice) demonstrate practical value.

5. **Controllable lesion mask generation via DiffMask**: The diffusion-based mask generator enables control over size, location, and boundary (Sec. 3.3). Synthetic masks from DiffMask further improve downstream performance, demonstrating diversity beyond hand-crafted alternatives.

6. **Addresses a real and well-motivated clinical problem**: The paradigm of starting from abundant normal scans to generate pathological cases directly targets data scarcity and long-tail imbalances, with validation across two modalities (CT, MRI) and two segmentation architectures.

### Minor

1. **No variance or significance reporting for downstream results**: Tables 1–2 report single-point Dice/NSD estimates. Downstream training involves stochasticity, and some reported differences are small (e.g., ~0.2% Dice between Cond-Diffusion and LeFusion on one subset). Without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multi-run experiments, it is impossible to assess which improvements are statistically reliable. This is the most significant gap in the evaluation.

2. **Lesion-focused loss is not cleanly ablated**: The core contribution is the lesion-focused training objective (Eq. 4), but the paper never compares LeFusion to an otherwise-identical model trained with the standard full-image loss. The existing comparison to RePaint is not a clean ablation — RePaint may differ in other training details, not just the loss function. An ablation isolating the lesion-focused loss would directly validate the central design claim.

3. **Histogram-based texture control evaluation is largely qualitative**: The quantitative PSNR/SSIM diversity numbers for histogram control are embedded in a figure (Fig. 6) rather than reported in a dedicated table. The downstream results (LeFusion vs. LeFusion-H) provide indirect evidence, but a direct evaluation — e.g., histogram matching metrics, or classification of generated nodules by type — would substantially strengthen the claim.

4. **Several implementation details are absent from the main text**: The conditioning mechanism for DiffMask (concatenation vs. cross-attention), the exact construction of the multi-channel input during training, the hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, noise schedule) for both LeFusion and baseline methods, and how normal ROIs were selected on LIDC ("regions where lung nodules typically appear") are not specified. While the paper provides an anonymous code repository, these details should be present for self-contained evaluation.

5. **Cond-Diffusion (L) comparison conflates autoencoder with methodology**: The paper acknowledges that "image-space diffusion outperformed latent diffusion due to constraints imposed by the autoencoder," meaning the underperformance of Cond-Diffusion (L) may stem from autoencoder quality rather than the conditional diffusion approach itself. This is a known limitation, but it weakens the comparison.

### Trivial

- Eq. 4 uses `\|\epsilon-p_\theta(\hat{x}_t,t)\|_2` without the squared notation; standard diffusion training uses the squared L2 norm (MSE). This is a notational imprecision.

## Suggestions

1. **Add error bars**: Run each downstream experiment at least 3 times with different seeds and report mean ± std for all tables. For nnUNet, leverage its internal 5-fold cross-validation to report variance.
2. **Add a clean ablation of the lesion-focused loss**: Train the same model with full-image noise-prediction loss (removing the mask from Eq. 4) while keeping architecture and inference identical. Compare downstream performance.
3. **Move the PSNR/SSIM diversity numbers to a proper table**: Report the quantitative histogram-control evaluation in a dedicated table rather than embedding in a figure.
4. **Provide key hyperparameters in the main text**: Learning rate, batch size, number of diffusion steps, noise schedule, and number of U-Net parameters. This costs little and aids reproducibility.
5. **Clarify the DiffMask conditioning mechanism**: Specify whether the control sphere is concatenated as an input channel or integrated via cross-attention.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a well-motivated, methodologically coherent approach to a significant clinical problem. The core ideas — lesion-focused loss, histogram-controlled texture synthesis, multi-channel decomposition, and mask diffusion — are each novel and well-justified. The evaluation covers two modalities, two segmentation architectures, and multiple synthetic data configurations, with consistent improvements across the board.

The weaknesses are genuine but minor: lack of error bars, missing clean ablation of the loss, and somewhat limited quantitative rigor for the histogram control analysis. None of these threaten the core claims or invalidate the contributions. The paper's strengths — principled background preservation, explicit texture control, downstream gains of ~5% Dice — are solid and well-demonstrated.

This is a solid paper that would benefit from the suggested improvements but is already a clear contribution. I recommend acceptance.
