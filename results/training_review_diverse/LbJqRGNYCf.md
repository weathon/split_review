## Summary

This paper proposes JoMA (Joint MLP/Attention dynamics), a theoretical framework that derives a first integral (invariant) relating the self-attention logits to the MLP lower-layer weights during training. The key predictions are: (1) under linear activation, attention becomes increasingly sparse (winner-take-all), recovering prior results; (2) under nonlinear activation, attention first becomes sparse (focusing on salient/co-occurring tokens) and then densifies (incorporating less frequent co-occurrences); (3) this two-phase behavior qualitatively explains how multilayer Transformers learn hierarchical feature structures when data is generated by a latent hierarchical model. Experiments on Wikitext2/Wikitext103 and pre-trained OPT/Pythia models are provided as validation.

## Strengths

1. **Novel joint dynamics framework (JoMA invariant)**. Theorem 1 provides a first integral that merges the self-attention layer into the MLP lower-layer dynamics for linear and exp attention. This goes beyond prior work that omitted residual connections or nonlinear MLP activation. The invariant is clearly stated and represents a genuinely new theoretical tool.

2. **Prediction of two-phase attention behavior under nonlinear activation**. The paper predicts that attention entropy first drops then rebounds under nonlinear activation (while only dropping under linear activation). This is a nontrivial and testable prediction that distinguishes nonlinear from linear dynamics. Theorem 4 quantifies a convergence speed advantage for salient components.

3. **Recovery of prior linear-case results**. Under linear activation, JoMA recovers the winner-take-all sparsity behavior from Scan&Snap (Theorem 2), showing consistency with established findings and that the framework generalizes prior work.

4. **Experimental effort across multiple settings**. The paper tests its predictions on models trained from scratch (Wikitext2/103) and on multiple pre-trained model families (OPT-2.7B, Pythia-70M/1.4B/6.9B), including analysis of both attention entropy and stable rank of MLP layers.

5. **Verification of hidden node–latent alignment on synthetic data**. Table 1 shows that MLP hidden nodes achieve high normalized correlation (up to 0.99 for layer 0) with ground-truth latent variables in synthetic HBLT data, supporting the assumption that MLP layers learn latent features.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **The nonlinear dynamics analysis (Section 4) is heuristic, not rigorous**. The derivation of the central equation $\dot\vv \propto (\vmu - \vv) \circ \exp(\vv^2/2)$ (Eqn. 13) is presented as "Similar to Eqn. (7), we use closed-form simplification of JoMA to incorporate self-attention," but this is not systematically derived from Theorem 1 or Theorem 3. Scalar terms are omitted, the analysis restricts to $\vv$ close to $\vmu$, and Theorem 4's regime condition depends on constants $C_{jk}$ that depend on initial conditions but are not quantified in terms of interpretable model parameters. The overall argument for the "sparse-then-dense" pattern is a suggestive calculation rather than a rigorous derivation. This significantly weakens the paper's central theoretical claim.

2. **The multilayer hierarchical analysis (Section 5) is qualitative and not formally connected to the proven dynamics**. The paper explicitly says it "analyze[s] qualitatively," but the core claim about how attention dynamics explain hierarchical learning in multilayer Transformers amounts to a verbal story without formal theorems connecting the single-layer dynamics to the multilayer setting. The experimental validation (Table 1) shows correlation between hidden nodes and latents but does not test whether the attention dynamics exhibit the predicted sparsity changes across layers or demonstrate causation. Given that the paper's title promises to "Demystify Multilayer Transformers," this gap between the title's ambition and the actual theoretical support is substantial.

3. **Experimental evidence provides only partial and ambiguous support for the central prediction**. For pre-trained models (Figure 4, OPT/Pythia), the paper acknowledges that "the attention patterns show less salient drop-and-bounce patterns" and pivots to stable rank dynamics, which also do not consistently show the predicted pattern (e.g., Pythia-70M layers show flat or decreasing stable rank). The paper does not provide quantitative measures (e.g., time of minimum entropy, statistical tests for U-shapedness) or a controlled synthetic experiment that isolates the prediction. The empirical evidence for the core theoretical claim is therefore weaker than would be expected to fully validate it.

4. **Mismatch between theoretical derivations and experimental setup**. Theorem 1 for the nonlinear case is cleanly proven only for exp attention (the softmax case requires strong unverified assumptions), and Section 4's nonlinear analysis uses exp attention. However, all real-world experiments use softmax attention, as do the pre-trained models. The paper does not demonstrate that the qualitative behavior (sparse-then-dense) proven for exp attention provably transfers to softmax attention under the same assumptions, leaving a gap in the claimed theory-experiment connection.

### Minor

1. **Softmax case of Theorem 1 rests on strong, unverified assumptions**. The softmax invariant requires (i) $\bar\vb_m$ constant over time and (ii) a rank-1 structure in a second-moment term. Assumption (i) is at odds with the fact that attention scores change significantly during training. The verification in Figure 2 only covers the linear-activation case, not the nonlinear setting where the main claims are made. While the paper acknowledges these as assumptions, their plausibility is not discussed.

2. **Orthonormal embedding assumption limits practical relevance**. The paper assumes orthonormal embeddings ($U_C^\top U_C = I$), which is unrealistic when $d < M$. The Discussion mentions almost-orthogonal embeddings via the JL lemma but this is not incorporated into any theorem or analysis. The theoretical results therefore apply to a simplified setting whose distance from practice is not quantified.

3. **Theorem 4's regime condition involves opaque constants**. The condition $|\delta_k(t)| \ll |\delta_k(0)|\exp[-C_{jk}\exp(\mu_k^2)]$ depends on $C_{jk}$ that "only depends on $\delta_j(0)$ and $\delta_k(0)$" but is not given a closed form or estimated from data, making it hard to verify when the regime holds in practice.

### Trivial

None. The paper is generally well-structured and clearly written given the complexity of the material.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A controlled synthetic experiment (e.g., a 1-layer Transformer with known co-occurrence statistics) that directly tests the sparse-then-dense prediction and measures the time of minimum entropy against the prediction of Theorem 4.

- A more honest framing in the title and abstract — replacing "Demystifying Multilayer Transformers" with something like "A Framework for Joint MLP/Attention Dynamics" would better match the actual contribution.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation that emerges across the reviews is that the paper's core value — the JoMA invariant itself — is actually its cleanest and best-supported contribution, while the downstream claims (sparse-then-dense, hierarchical learning) are where the rigor degrades. This suggests the paper would be stronger if it centered the invariant as the primary contribution, presented the nonlinear dynamics as a conjecture with suggestive evidence rather than a "prediction," and saved the hierarchical story for a follow-up. The reviews collectively reveal that the paper is trying to do too much: prove an invariant, analyze linear and nonlinear dynamics, explain hierarchical learning, and validate on real models — all in one paper. The invariant alone is a publishable contribution; the rest dilutes it.

## Suggestions

1. **Separate rigorous results from heuristic ones**. Clearly demarcate what is proven (Theorem 1 for linear/exp attention, Theorem 2) from what is derived heuristically (the sparse-then-dense prediction in Section 4) and what is qualitative (Section 5). This would give readers clarity about the paper's actual theoretical backing.

2. **Add a controlled synthetic experiment**. A simple 1-layer Transformer trained on data with known co-occurrence statistics, with attention entropy measured over training and compared to the prediction of Theorem 4, would significantly strengthen the empirical validation.

3. **Tone down the title and overarching claims**. "Demystifying Multilayer Transformers" vastly overstates what is accomplished. A title like "JoMA: Joint Dynamics of MLP and Attention in Transformer Training" is more accurate and would better prepare readers for the paper's scope.

4. **Either prove the hierarchical claims or explicitly frame them as speculation**. The current framing ("show qualitatively," "implicitly learned") sits uncomfortably between rigorous theory and empirical observation. An honest discussion of what would need to be proven to make the hierarchical story rigorous would be more constructive than the current presentation.
