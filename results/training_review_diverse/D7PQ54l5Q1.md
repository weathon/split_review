## Summary

The paper proposes Diffusion Posterior MCMC (DPMC), which combines DDIM-based diffusion proposals with annealed MCMC sampling at each intermediate noise level to improve inverse problem solving. The key idea is to run multiple MCMC correction steps per noise-level distribution to reduce the accumulated approximation error that limits DPS. The paper demonstrates empirical improvements over DPS across super-resolution, deblurring, inpainting, and phase retrieval tasks on FFHQ and ImageNet.

## Strengths

1. **Consistent empirical improvement over DPS across multiple tasks with fewer function evaluations.** Tables 1 and 2 (lines 152, 163) show that DPMC achieves lower LPIPS and FID than DPS on both FFHQ and ImageNet for super-resolution, Gaussian deblurring, motion deblurring, and inpainting, using ~700 NFE versus DPS's 1000 NFE. This directly supports the paper's core claim.

2. **Ablation study confirms monotonic improvement with more MCMC steps.** Table 4a (line 175) shows that increasing K from 1 to 8 yields progressively lower LPIPS (e.g., from 0.451 to 0.413) and FID. This validates the central intuition that running multiple sampling steps at each intermediate distribution reduces accumulated error, and is the strongest evidence for the method's mechanism.

3. **Weighting schedule ablation (Table 4c) provides practical hyperparameter guidance.** The paper tests multiple schedules for ξ_t ∝ f(ᾱ_t) and finds ξ_t ∝ ᾱ_t³ gives the best performance, offering a principled default for future users.

4. **Transparent treatment of baseline evaluation conditions.** The paper explicitly notes (line 144-145) that RED-Diff and ΠGDM are sensitive to additive noise and reports both noisy (σ=0.05) and noiseless versions for reference. This candor about an asymmetry that could favor DPMC is a strength rather than a weakness.

5. **Demonstrated effectiveness on a nonlinear inverse problem (phase retrieval).** Table 3 (line 182) shows DPMC improves LPIPS over DPS (0.497 vs 0.532) with similar FID, extending beyond linear tasks.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **MCMC kernel is never specified.** The paper's central methodological contribution is "MCMC exploration steps" (line 107, Figure 1 caption), yet it never defines what MCMC kernel is used. No update equations are given, no transition kernel is specified, no pseudocode is provided, and the text does not state whether the method uses Langevin dynamics, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, Metropolis-adjusted Langevin, or a custom proposal. The only mentions of specific MCMC algorithms are in related work (Langevin dynamics in prior methods, line 16) and as future work (HMC, line 189). The hyperparameters η_t and ξ_t are introduced (line 129) but without connecting them to any MCMC update. For a paper whose title and primary contribution center on MCMC, this is a critical reproducibility gap — a reader cannot implement the method from the description provided.

2. **Source of DPS baseline numbers is ambiguous.** The paper states (line 134) that results for FPS, DiffPir, CCDF, etc. are taken from their papers, and that ΠGDM, RED-Diff, and DDNM are reproduced. DPS numbers appear in the same tables but the paper never states whether they were reproduced under the same protocol or taken from Chung et al. (2023a). Given that DPS is the primary baseline (the paper's main claim is improvement over DPS), this ambiguity weakens the comparison.

### Minor

1. **No standard deviations or confidence intervals reported for any metric.** All tables report point estimates without error bars. Given that the claimed improvements over DPS are sometimes modest (e.g., phase retrieval FID: 34.67 vs 35.24 in Table 3), uncertainty quantification would substantially strengthen the evidential value.

2. **Ablation study limited to one task (Gaussian deblur on FFHQ).** The paper acknowledges (line 159) that the ablation covers only a single setting. The sensitivity of the method to K, T, and ξ_t could differ substantially across tasks (e.g., phase retrieval with a non-Gaussian likelihood vs. linear deblurring).

3. **The NFE comparison needs clarification.** The paper reports ~700 NFE for DPMC vs. 1000 for DPS. However, it does not clarify whether the K=4 MCMC steps require ε_θ (score network) evaluations. Even in the worst case (each MCMC step does require ε_θ), the total is 60 (first 30%) + 120×(1+4) (middle 60%) + 20 (last 10%) = 680, still below 1000 — so the claimed advantage holds — but the paper should state this explicitly to avoid ambiguity.

4. **Heuristic for applying MCMC to only the middle 60% of steps lacks justification.** The paper states (line 129) that proposal-exploration steps are applied only in the middle 60%, reverting to DPS elsewhere, with one exception for box-mask inpainting. No analysis is provided to justify this specific range — e.g., measuring where the DPS approximation error is largest along the diffusion trajectory.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the MCMC kernel explicitly** — provide the update equations for the exploration step, state whether an acceptance step is used, and relate η_t and ξ_t to the kernel's step size parameters. Add a pseudocode algorithm box.
2. **Clarify the NFE accounting** — explicitly state whether each MCMC step requires a score-network (ε_θ) evaluation, and provide a per-step breakdown of the NFE count.
3. **State the source of DPS numbers** — clarify whether they are reproduced or cited, and if reproduced, confirm identical settings (same pretrained models, random seeds, step counts).
4. **Add error bars** — report standard deviations or confidence intervals over multiple runs for at least the primary comparison (DPMC vs. DPS).
5. **Add an ablation across tasks** — even an abbreviated ablation on one additional task (e.g., motion deblur or phase retrieval) for the key parameter K would substantially strengthen claims about generality.

## Score and Decision

**Overall assessment**: The paper identifies a genuine limitation of DPS and presents a plausible remedy with promising empirical results. The core empirical claim — DPMC improves over DPS with fewer NFE — is supported by the data. However, the paper's central methodological component is critically underspecified: the MCMC kernel is never defined, the NFE accounting is incomplete, and the primary baseline's provenance is ambiguous. These are not execution flaws that a rebuttal can fix — they require a revision of the manuscript. In its current form, the paper does not meet the reproducibility standard required for acceptance at a top venue.
