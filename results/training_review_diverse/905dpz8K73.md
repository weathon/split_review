## Summary

This paper develops a computational model of reciprocally coupled continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs) for place cells and grid cells. It theoretically derives that the coupled dynamics implement gradient-based optimization of the posterior (GOP), demonstrating Bayesian-optimal integration of environmental and motion cues, and shows that the model avoids non-local decoding errors inherent to grid cell phase coding.

## Strengths

**S1. Theoretical derivation linking coupled CANNs to Bayesian inference.** The paper analytically shows (Eq. 19) that the dynamics of the bump centers in the coupled place-cell and grid-cell CANNs are proportional to the gradients of the log-posterior, and that the reciprocal connection strength encodes the correlation prior between sensory cues (Eq. 9 vs. Eq. 3). This provides a rigorous formal connection between a biologically plausible attractor network and Bayesian cue integration.

**S2. Empirical validation of Bayesian-optimal cue integration.** Fig. 3c–d shows that the model's decoding variance when both environmental and motion cues are presented matches the theoretical Bayesian prediction (1/σ²_bayes = 1/σ²_env + 1/σ²_mot) across multiple noise levels. This directly confirms that the network integrates the two cues optimally, which is the paper's central quantitative claim.

**S3. Demonstrates that the coupled system avoids non-local errors in phase coding.** Fig. 4 shows that when only the motion cue is available (a setting that causes large non-local errors in standard grid-cell phase decoding), the coupled CANNs produce decoding errors that remain small and stable even at high noise levels. This illustrates the practical benefit of the coupled architecture over grid-cell-only decoding.

**S4. Tractable mode-projection analysis.** The paper employs a projection onto the first two motion modes of the CANNs to derive simplified dynamics for the bump centers (Eqs. 17–18), making the analysis mathematically clean and connecting directly to the gradients of the posterior.

### Minor

**W1. The GOP equivalence requires parameter matching — robustness is not analyzed.** The derivation establishing the equivalence between network dynamics and GOP (Eq. 19) requires the network parameters to satisfy specific relationships (e.g., σ²_ϕⁱ = (8πA_gτ_g)/(λᵢJ_{gi,p}ρ_pR̂_p)). The paper presents this as demonstrating that the model "implements GOP," but the equivalence is a design condition for a specific parameter set. The paper does not analyze how robust the integration quality is when parameters deviate from these conditions. This is not fatal — the paper transparently states the parameter relationships — but it limits the generality of the claim. A brief robustness analysis (e.g., decoding variance as a function of parameter mismatch) would strengthen the work considerably.

**W2. The non-local error claim relies on initialization at the true location without characterizing the basin of attraction.** In the motion-only simulation (Fig. 4), the place cell bump is initialized at the true location z₀ (stated in Fig. 3a caption). The paper argues that the coupled system avoids non-local errors because GOP starts from the previous bump position, reflecting "the continuity of the animal movement." However, the simulations are run under a static condition (fixed location), not a dynamic tracking scenario. The paper would benefit from either (a) characterizing the basin of attraction — how far from the true location can the initial bump be while still converging correctly — or (b) running a continuous tracking simulation to demonstrate the temporal-continuity argument directly. As it stands, the gap between the theoretical argument (temporal continuity) and the static simulation (initialization at z₀) leaves the non-local error claim less fully supported than it could be.

**W3. The claim that "GOP tends to outperform MAP" is not cleanly tested.** In Sec. 3.2, the paper states that "GOP tends to outperform MAP, as it avoids large non-local errors." But the experiment in Fig. 4 compares the coupled CANNs (which implement GOP on the full posterior) against MAP based *only on grid cell activity* (maximizing p(r_g|ϕ)). This comparison conflates two factors: (1) using the full posterior (with place cells + correlation prior) versus grid-only information, and (2) gradient-based search versus global maximization. A direct comparison between GOP on the full posterior and MAP on the full posterior would isolate the claimed advantage of gradient-based search over global maximization for phase coding. The paper's main claim about the coupled system avoiding non-local errors is still supported, but the secondary claim about GOP specifically outperforming MAP needs a cleaner test.

**W4. Biological implausibility of congruent connections without discussion.** The reciprocal connections between place cells at x and grid cells at θⁱ are set to peak when θⁱ = ψⁱ(x) (Eq. 9), which assumes the brain knows the exact mapping from location to phase for each grid module. The paper does not discuss how such precise connections could be established (e.g., through learning during development or via hardwiring). This is a common simplifying assumption in computational models, but acknowledging it would strengthen the paper.
