## Summary

This paper proposes a computational model of reciprocally coupled continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs) to study how place cells in hippocampus and grid cells in medial entorhinal cortex complement each other for spatial representation. The core theoretical contribution is the derivation that the coupled network dynamics implement gradient-based optimization of the posterior (GOP), a form of Bayesian inference. Simulations demonstrate Bayesian optimal cue integration and show that the model avoids the non-local error problem that plagues phase coding of grid cells.

## Strengths

1. **Rigorous theoretical derivation linking coupled CANN dynamics to Bayesian inference (GOP):** The paper explicitly derives (Section 4.2, Eqs. 17–19) that the reduced dynamics of the coupled place-cell and grid-cell CANNs correspond to gradient ascent on the log-posterior. This establishes a formal connection between attractor neural network dynamics and optimal Bayesian inference that goes beyond heuristic use of attractor models in prior work. The Lyapunov function analysis (Eq. 20) further guarantees convergence to a local posterior maximum.

2. **Quantitative validation of Bayesian optimal cue integration across noise levels:** Section 5.1 demonstrates that the decoding variance of the coupled model matches the theoretical Bayesian prediction (1/σ²_bayes = 1/σ²_env + 1/σ²_mot) across multiple noise levels (Fig. 3c–d). This directly supports the central claim that the model integrates environmental and motion cues optimally, and the noise sweep shows robustness.

3. **Biologically principled reciprocal connections encoding the correlation prior:** The congruent connectivity between place and grid cells (Eq. 9) is derived from the joint distribution p(z,φ) (Eq. 3), providing a concrete neural substrate for conveying the correlation prior between sensory cues. The analytical relationship σ²_φi ∝ 1/J_{gi,p} (line 216–218) directly links connection strength to prior uncertainty, which is a clean and novel mapping.

4. **Analytical convergence guarantee via Lyapunov function:** The proof that the network dynamics monotonically decrease the negative log posterior (Eq. 20) provides theoretical stability assurance not typically given in coupled attractor models.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Non-local error experiment (Section 5.2) lacks specification of initial conditions and does not fully instantiate its own explanatory mechanism.** The paper states that in the coupled CANNs, "the previous bump position of place cells serves as the initial point, which restricts the bump to evolve to a local maximum of the posterior, reflecting the continuity of the animal movement" (Section 5.2). However, the simulation in Figure 4 is a *static* animal at fixed location x=0 (Section 5.1), not a moving animal. The paper does not specify how the place cell bump is initialized when I_p = 0 in this static setting. If initialized at the true location (z=0), the result is informative — the coupled dynamics maintain the correct location despite noisy grid-only input — but this needs to be stated explicitly. If initialized randomly, the analysis of how the network avoids being pulled to wrong periodic modes would require explanation. The temporal-continuity argument invoked to explain *why* non-local errors are avoided is not directly tested, since the simulation involves no movement and thus no "previous bump position" from a prior time step. This gap between the claimed mechanism (temporal continuity / history-dependence) and the actual experiment (static single-trial convergence) undermines the strength of the paper's central applied claim. A moving-animal simulation with sequential time steps would directly substantiate the explanation.

### Minor

1. **Sensitivity of the two-mode projection approximation to coupling strength not examined.** The theoretical derivation in Section 4.2 relies on projecting the CANN dynamics onto the first two motion modes (height and position), assuming the bump shape remains Gaussian under reciprocal coupling. The paper validates this approximation against GOP numerically in Figure 3b, but only for one set of parameters. The robustness of the approximation to different coupling strengths J_{gi,p} is not tested. While the match is likely to hold across a range due to the well-known dominance of these modes in CANNs, an explicit check (or an analytical condition for when the approximation holds) would strengthen the theoretical claim.

2. **Simulation parameters and trial numbers not reported in the main text.** Key experimental details — the number of neurons per module, exact parameter values used (e.g., J_p, J_g, a_p, a_{gi}, ρ_p, ρ_g, τ_p, τ_g, k_p, k_g), number of trials per condition, and how results are aggregated — are absent from the main text. Even if these appear in the appendix (which was not available for review), a summary in the main body is essential for reproducibility and for assessing the statistical significance of results like the variance matching in Figure 3c–d and the error gap in Figure 4b.

A direct comparison at matched noise levels would sharpen the claimed advantage.
- **Error bars on the variance estimates in Figure 3d:** Reporting confidence intervals or standard errors across trials would help assess the reliability of the match to the Bayesian prediction, especially at low noise levels where the curves nearly overlap.

## Novel Insights

The reviewer's critiques sharpen one insight not fully articulated by the paper: the non-local error avoidance in the coupled CANNs is better understood as a consequence of GOP dynamics (gradient ascent from a starting point) operating on a *posterior that combines a likelihood with a prior* — rather than as a consequence of temporal continuity or a moving-animal prior. The temporal continuity explanation is a secondary intuition: the "previous bump position" provides the initial condition for GOP, but the fundamental reason non-local errors are avoided is that the coupled dynamics solve a well-posed Bayesian inference problem (with a proper prior) rather than attempting to invert a periodic likelihood alone. The paper would benefit from foregrounding this distinction: GOP avoids non-local errors because the posterior (with its prior from the reciprocal connections) is unimodal in the neighborhood of the true location, not because the animal is moving.
