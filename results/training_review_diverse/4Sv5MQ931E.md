## Summary

This paper proposes MSR-ViR, a modular VideoQA framework that integrates a Modularized Spatial-Temporal Grounding (MoST-Grounding) module with a multimodal LLM, along with an Alternate Self-reflection Training strategy that alternately applies SFT to the multimodal LLM and DPO to the question parser LLM using the multimodal LLM's loss as feedback. Experiments on NExT-QA, STAR, and NExT-GQA show improvements in both QA accuracy and temporal grounding quality over several baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel Alternate Self-reflection Training strategy**: The paper introduces a principled method for training the question parser LLM via DPO using the multimodal LLM's cross-entropy loss as the preference signal, without requiring human-annotated preference pairs. The ablation study (Table 3) confirms this is essential, with a 1.5-point accuracy drop on NExT-QA when removed.

- **Strong empirical results across benchmarks**: MSR-ViR_L achieves best overall accuracy on NExT-QA and STAR-sub (Table 1), surpassing grounding-based methods (LSTP, SeViLa) particularly on temporal questions (+3–5 points). On NExT-GQA (Table 2), MSR-ViR achieves state-of-the-art mIoP and mIoU among comparably-sized methods, demonstrating both improved QA accuracy and more precise temporal grounding.

- **Well-designed modular composition with off-the-shelf modules**: MoST-Grounding dynamically assembles 7 types of small modules (e.g., UniVTG for zero-shot temporal grounding, YOLO-World for open-vocabulary detection) via structured JSON policies, providing explicit reasoning paths that contrast with black-box end-to-end alternatives.

- **Thoughtful robustness design**: The inclusion of a global video representation (average-pooled frames) and a guiding prompt mitigates cases where MoST-Grounding localizes inaccurately, and both design choices are validated in the ablation study (Table 3).

## Novel Insights

The most interesting methodological insight from the reviews is the recognition that the Alternate Self-reflection Training strategy effectively replaces human preference annotation with the downstream LLM's own loss, creating a closed-loop optimization where the question parser is directly rewarded for policies that reduce the multimodal LLM's confusion. This is a neat instance of using a non-differentiable pipeline's output loss as an implicit reward signal — an approach that could generalize beyond VideoQA to other modular pipeline settings where intermediate modules need to be trained end-to-end despite non-differentiable components.
