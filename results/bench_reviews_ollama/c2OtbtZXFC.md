## Summary

This paper proposes a retraction-free, penalty-parameter-free optimization method for the Stiefel manifold that uses a landing update (projected gradient plus a quadratic penalty gradient) with an explicit penalty parameter $\mu=1/3$ and a constant step size, proving exact convergence to a stationary point. The key theoretical ingredients are a restricted secant inequality (RSI) for the quadratic penalty function on the Stiefel manifold and a linear convergence result for the penalty gradient descent. As an application, the paper introduces Manifold-LoRA, which constrains one factor $B$ of the LoRA decomposition $\Delta W = BA$ to the Stiefel (or oblique) manifold, uses the landing update for $B$ and standard Euclidean updates for $A$ within the AdamW framework, and demonstrates improved performance on GLUE, SQuAD, and E2E benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **Explicit penalty parameter and exact convergence with constant step size**: Unlike prior retraction-free methods (e.g., Ablin & Peyré, 2022) that require an unspecified "sufficiently large" penalty parameter and only converge to a neighborhood, this paper establishes $\mu = 1/3$ as an explicit sufficient penalty (Lemma 2) and proves exact convergence to a stationary point with an $\mathcal{O}(1/K)$ rate (Theorem 1). The RSI condition (Lemma 1) for the quadratic penalty near the Stiefel manifold is a self-contained and useful technical contribution.

- **Lower per-iteration computational cost**: The landing update has cost $2dr^2$ compared to $4dr^2 + \frac{40}{3}r^3$ for the popular Cayley retraction (Section 3.1), and avoids matrix inversion, relying instead on scalable BLAS3 operations. This is a practical advantage that is clearly articulated.

- **Manifold geometry for LoRA is a meaningful reframing**: Constraining $B$ to the Stiefel manifold to reduce representation redundancy in $\Delta W = BA$ and interpreting $A$ as coordinates under an orthonormal basis is a conceptually clean formulation (Section 4.1). The experimental results (Tables 1–3) demonstrate that Manifold-LoRA at rank $r=8$ can match or exceed LoRA at $r=16$ on several tasks (e.g., RTE: 83.0 vs. 81.2; STS-B: 91.9 vs. 91.4).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Theory-practice gap for the main application**: The convergence theory (Section 3, Theorem 1) analyzes a gradient descent iteration on a single Stiefel manifold $\text{St}(d,r)$ with a constant step size and the explicit landing update. The actual Manifold-LoRA algorithm (Algorithm 1) operates on the product space $\text{St}(d,r) \times \mathbb{R}^{r \times m}$, uses AdamW with learning-rate scheduling and a spectral-norm-based scaling heuristic (Eqs. 14–15), and jointly updates $B$ and $A$. None of these features (product manifold structure, adaptive optimizer, varying step sizes, interaction between Stiefel-constrained $B$ and unconstrained $A$) are covered by the theory. The paper provides no formal or even informal argument that the landing mechanism's convergence properties transfer to this setting. This gap is significant because the paper's main selling point is combining the landing theory with the LoRA formulation, yet the theory only validates one component in isolation. While theory-practice gaps are common, the breadth of this one — optimizer, step-size scheme, and problem geometry all differ — makes it substantially harder to trust that the empirical improvements are attributable to the theoretical mechanism rather than the regularizing effect of the Stiefel constraint alone.

### Minor

- **Overstated redundancy elimination motivation**: The paper motivates the Stiefel constraint by arguing that $\Delta W = BA$ has redundancy because $BA = (BC)(C^{-1}A)$ for any invertible $C$, and that constraining $B$ to the Stiefel manifold addresses this by "optimizing the subspace generated by $B$ instead of $B$ itself" (Section 4.1). While the column space of $B$ is indeed fixed under the Stiefel constraint, the representation retains an orthogonal ambiguity $BA = (BO)(O^\top A)$ for any $O \in O(r)$, so the redundancy is reduced but not eliminated. The paper's framing as "addressing the redundancy" overstates the effect; it would be more accurate to say the constraint reduces redundancy from an infinite-dimensional group to a finite-dimensional one. This does not invalidate the approach (fixing the subspace is the important part), but the motivation section should acknowledge the remaining ambiguity.

- **"Half the parameters" comparison conflates rank reduction with the manifold constraint**: The paper's headline comparison (Abstract, Section 5.1) pits Manifold-LoRA at $r=8$ against LoRA at $r=16$, attributing the improvement to the manifold approach. This confounds reduced rank (which halves parameters) with the Stiefel constraint. The same-rank comparisons (Manifold-LoRA $r=8$ vs. LoRA $r=8$) are available in the tables but show smaller improvements. The "half the parameters" framing is accurate in terms of parameter count but misleading about what drives the improvement.

- **No variance or statistical significance reported**: The experimental results show single numbers for each metric. For tasks like CoLA where seed-to-seed variance can be 5% or more, and for the claim that Manifold-LoRA "surpasses full fine-tuning" on SQuAD (differences within 0.2 F1 points), the absence of error bars makes it difficult to assess whether reported improvements are robust.

- **"2× speedup" claims based on epochs, not wall-clock time or FLOPs**: The paper claims Manifold-LoRA converges "twice as fast" (Section 5.2), but this refers to epoch counts only. The per-iteration overhead of computing projected gradients and penalty terms (plus spectorm-norm-based learning-rate scaling) is not accounted for. While the per-iteration FLOP comparison of the landing update vs. Cayley retraction is favorable ($2dr^2$ vs. $4dr^2 + \frac{40}{3}r^3$), the relevant comparison for the practical algorithm is against standard LoRA (which uses neither), and wall-clock timings are not provided.

- **Initialization condition not discussed for practical use**: Lemma 2 and Theorem 1 require $\|X_0 - \bar{X}_0\| \leq 1/8$ (close initialization to the manifold). For randomly initialized $B$ matrices in LoRA, this condition may not hold (the distance would typically be $O(\sqrt{d})$), and the paper does not discuss how or whether this is satisfied in practice.

## Novel Insights

The paper's key insight — that the landing mechanism on the Stiefel manifold naturally pairs with the Gram-matrix penalty to both enforce the manifold constraint and reduce LoRA's representation redundancy during training — is architecturally elegant. However, the most important observation from this review synthesis is that the empirical benefits of Manifold-LoRA may be largely attributable to the regularizing effect of the Stiefel constraint on $B$, rather than to the convergence guarantees of the landing method. Disentangling these contributions (e.g., via orthogonal regularization baselines) remains an open question that the paper does not address.

## Evaluation

**Originality**: The theoretical contribution (explicit $\mu = 1/3$, RSI condition, exact convergence) is solid and improves on Ablin & Peyré (2022). The application to LoRA via manifold constraints is novel. However, the theory-practice gap is substantial.

**Importance**: The LoRA application is timely and practically relevant. The Stiefel landing method contribution is meaningful for the manifold optimization community.

**Claim support**: The theoretical claims (Theorem 1) are well-supported. The empirical claims ("2× speedup," "surpasses full fine-tuning") are overstated relative to the evidence. The "half the parameters" framing is misleading about what drives improvements.

**Soundness**: The Stiefel optimization theory is sound. The bridge from theory to Manifold-LoRA is informal and heuristic.

**Value**: The theory contribution stands on its own as an incremental but meaningful improvement. The LoRA application is promising but empirically driven without formal backing from the theory.
