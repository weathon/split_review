## Summary

The paper proposes TIDY, a universal fake image detection method that leverages the discrepancy between CLIP image and text embeddings (|**I** − **T**|) rather than relying on visual-only features. Captions are generated by BLIP-2, and a global-to-local scheme uses GLIP to compute per-object discrepancies. An MLP head is trained on these discrepancy vectors for binary real/fake classification, achieving improved generalization across 20 generators compared to visual-only baselines.

## Strengths

- **Strong empirical generalization across diverse generators**: Tables 1–3 demonstrate consistent improvements over UniFD (1.69% AP average) and NPR (8.02% AP average) across 20 generators spanning GANs, diffusion models, and deepfakes. The cross-generator training experiment (Fig. 6) further supports the "universal" claim, showing that training on GAN data generalizes to diffusion data and vice versa.

- **Robustness to image perturbations**: Figure 3 shows that TIDY maintains near-plateau AP under Gaussian Noise and Blur where DIRE and UniFD degrade substantially, supporting the claim that the joint visual-language space is more robust than visual-only representations.

- **Robustness across caption models**: Figure 8 shows consistent performance across BLIP, BLIP-2, and LLaVA, reducing concerns about over-reliance on a single captioner.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Missing ablation of global vs. local discrepancy**: The global-to-local scheme (§3.2) is presented as a key contribution, but no ablation compares D_g alone vs. D_l alone vs. D_g + D_l. The weights are set to w₁ = w₂ = 1.0 "empirically" with no justification or exploration. Without isolating the local component, it is impossible to determine whether the global-to-local scheme adds any value beyond the global discrepancy alone. This directly undermines the second claimed contribution.

- **Modality ablation does not validate that *discrepancy* is the key signal**: The ablation in Figure 4 compares (i) text-only, (ii) image-only, and (iii) |I − T| (TIDY). A concatenation baseline [I; T] that also uses both modalities but does not compute discrepancy is absent. Since |I − T| is a fundamentally different computation than simply having access to both embeddings, without a [I; T] baseline, the comparison only shows that multi-modal information helps—not that *discrepancy specifically* is the right way to combine it. This is critical because the paper's central claim is that discrepancy is the discriminative signal, not just that two modalities are better than one.

- **Theoretical framing is partially confounded by caption model quality**: Captions are generated by BLIP-2 (trained predominantly on real images). Fake images are OOD inputs to BLIP-2, which may produce lower-quality or less-aligned captions for them, systematically inflating the image-text discrepancy for fakes. The ablation across different caption models (Fig. 8) partially addresses this, but since all tested caption models are trained primarily on real images, the confound applies to all of them equally. The observed discrepancy may reflect "images that confuse caption models" rather than an inherent semantic misalignment property of fake images in CLIP space. While this does not invalidate the method's practical utility, it undermines the theoretical interpretation—that fake images are semantically misaligned in joint vision-language space—versus the simpler explanation that caption models fail on OOD inputs.

### Minor

- **The |I − T| design discards directional information**: Section 3.1 claims that D = |I − T| "could provide higher dimensional information [...] such as direction, which should also contain informative clues." However, the absolute value operation discards the sign of each dimension, removing bidirectional information. The paper does not compare against I − T (without absolute value) or against simpler scalar metrics like cosine similarity, leaving the design choice unjustified beyond architectural convenience.

- **Robustness evaluation is in-distribution**: The perturbation experiments (Fig. 3) are conducted only on ProGAN images—the same generator used for training. This tests in-distribution robustness but not cross-distribution robustness under perturbations, which is the more realistic threat model.

None.

## Removed Points

- **Complaint about UniFD using CLIP being "visual-only"**: The paper accurately characterizes UniFD as using only the visual encoder of CLIP—i.e., extracting only image embeddings. This is a fair description, not a mischaracterization, since UniFD does not leverage the text modality.

- **t-SNE visualization criticism**: While t-SNE can create misleading impressions, the visualization is supplementary evidence and not a core claim. This is standard practice and not a substantive flaw.

- **Computational cost of BLIP-2 + GLIP**: This is a practical consideration but not a methodological flaw. The paper is about method design, not deployment optimization.

- **Missing analysis of GLIP failure modes on fake images (number of objects detected, etc.)**: This is a reasonable suggestion but falls into nice-to-have territory; the method works empirically and the ablation across caption models provides some robustness evidence.

## Novel Insights

The paper's insight that the *difference* between CLIP image and text embeddings carries useful signal for fake image detection is interesting and empirically supported. However, the key question left unanswered is whether the signal stems from genuine semantic misalignment of fake images in joint vision-language space or from systematic caption degradation on OOD inputs. Resolving this would clarify whether the approach generalizes because of a fundamental property of fake images or because of a useful (but incidental) artifact of caption model behavior on generated images.

## Suggestions

- Add a direct ablation: D_g alone, D_l alone, D_g + D_l with varying w₁/w₂. Document whether local discrepancy adds meaningful value over global alone.
- Add a [I; T] concatenation baseline to the modality ablation to establish whether discrepancy per se is the key signal, or whether simply having both embeddings suffices.
- To address the caption-quality confound, run a control where real images also receive BLIP-2 captions (current setup) vs. human captions, showing that the discrepancy gap persists even with higher-quality captions for real images.
