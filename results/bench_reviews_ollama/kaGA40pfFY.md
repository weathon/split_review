Let me now write the final review.</think>## Summary

The paper proposes "Rationality of Thought" (RoT), a zero-shot prompting method inspired by dual-process (System 1/System 2) theory from cognitive psychology. RoT structures the model's reasoning into six steps (Identification, Decomposition, Execution, Reflection, Computation, Assessment) and is evaluated on a newly curated cognitive bias dataset (464 questions across 29 bias types) and standard reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, SVAMP, AQUA-RAT, ARC). The headline results are an 18.7% improvement for GPT-4 on the cognitive bias dataset and marginal improvements over CoT-few-shot on reasoning benchmarks (+0.4 on GSM8K, +4.8 on AQUA-RAT, +0.7 on ARC-c).

## Strengths

- **Novel evaluation lens via cognitive biases**: The paper builds a structured cognitive bias dataset for LLMs by filtering 93 bias types down to 29 via clear criteria (Section 3.1), which is a distinctive and psychologically grounded evaluation direction that goes beyond standard reasoning benchmarks.
- **Zero-shot approach with competitive results**: RoT achieves zero-shot performance that is competitive with or slightly exceeds CoT-few-shot on several reasoning benchmarks (e.g., +4.8 on AQUA-RAT for GPT-4), which is a meaningful practical advantage since it requires no example engineering.
- **Ablation study provides some insight**: The stepwise ablation (Table 2) shows that removing Step 3 (Execution/Data Analysis) causes the largest drop (9%), providing some evidence that the structured decomposition—rather than merely prompt length—contributes to performance.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **GPT-4 generated 75% of the cognitive bias test set, then is evaluated on it.** Section 3.1 explicitly states that 348 of the 464 questions were generated by GPT-4 from seed questions (with manual screening). The headline 18.7% improvement claim and the near-perfect 97.4% accuracy of GPT-4+RoT are therefore uninterpretable as evidence of genuine cognitive bias mitigation. A model that generates its own test questions can recognize its own generation patterns, inflating measured performance. This contaminates the entire cognitive bias evaluation—the paper's primary and most novel contribution.

### Major

- **Unfair comparison on the cognitive bias task: RoT (a long structured prompt) vs. CoT ("Let's think step by step").** On the cognitive bias dataset, the only CoT baseline is zero-shot with the 5-word prompt. The RoT prompt contains six detailed cognitive-science-structured steps. Without a prompt-length/detail-matched control, the cognitive bias results (even setting aside contamination) cannot be attributed to the specific cognitive-science structure rather than simply to providing more detailed instructions. The ablation in Section 3.4 removes entire steps (shortening the pipeline), which conflates step content with prompt length.
- **RoT consistently hurts smaller/less capable models, undermining generality.** LLAMA2-13B-chat performance drops by 12.1% with RoT, and GPT-3.5-turbo's performance barely improves (1.5%). The authors' explanation—"rational cognition is an advanced capability"—is circular: the method works only on models where it works, which says little about whether the cognitive-science framing is the cause or whether GPT-4 simply follows longer instructions better. The method's utility is effectively limited to the strongest models.
- **Small, statistically unsupported improvements on reasoning benchmarks.** The claimed improvements over CoT-few-shot on GSM8K (+0.4) and ARC-c (+0.7) are within noise for single-run evaluations at temperature 0. No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multiple runs are reported. The conclusion that RoT "has the potential to serve as a key technology in the transition towards AGI" (Section 4.3) is a massive overclaim given the marginal and unverifiable gains.

### Minor

- **The ablation design conflates removing a step's content with removing its token budget.** As noted, removing a step shortens the pipeline and reduces prompt tokens. A more informative ablation would replace a step's instructions with generic filler text of equivalent length, which would separate the contribution of cognitive-science content from the contribution of additional computation/tokens.
- **The "no manual annotation" claim is misleading.** The conclusion states RoT "does not require additional manual annotations, thus avoiding the pitfall of feature engineering." However, the RoT prompt itself is a manually engineered, six-step structured prompt—this is feature engineering at the prompt level rather than the example level. The distinction from CoT-few-shot is that RoT uses a manually designed *instruction template* rather than manually designed *demonstration examples*, but both involve manual engineering.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A length/detail-matched control prompt (e.g., "read carefully, check your work twice, consider alternatives, compute precisely, verify the answer") would allow disentangling the cognitive-science structure from the effect of simply providing more detailed instructions. If such a control performed similarly to RoT, it would weaken the cognitive-science claim; if RoT outperformed it, the claim would be substantially strengthened.
- Per-bias-type analysis of where RoT helps and doesn't (e.g., "Representativeness" and "Reflection Effect" underperform across all models—why?) would enhance the cognitive-science contribution.
- Testing RoT on open-weight models of varying sizes (beyond LLAMA2-13B) would help clarify whether the method's effectiveness is a function of model capability or instruction-following ability.

## Removed Points

- **"GPT-4 near-perfect score is consistent with recognizing its own generated patterns."** This is a restatement of the contamination issue already captured in the Fatal weakness. The contamination concern itself is valid; the speculative mechanism (pattern recognition) is inferred rather than proven, so I keep the contamination concern but weaken the stated certainty about mechanism.
- **"Equation 2 provides no theoretical insight."** This is true but trivial—most prompting papers have similar formalizations. This is a presentation nitpick, not a substantive flaw.
- **"Perplexing result that zero-shot CoT outperforms few-shot CoT on GSM8K."** The author's speculation about this is reasonable and well-documented in the literature. Flagging it as a weakness is overstated.
- **"No inter-annotator agreement reported."** This is a minor reproducibility nitpick; the dataset underwent manual screening. Not a substantive flaw.
- **"Missing experiments on open-weight models."** This overlaps with the generality concern already captured above; requesting more models is a nice-to-have, not a weakness.
- **Strength Finder claim: "Zero-shot outperformance over Few-shot CoT" as a *core* strength.** This is partially undercut by the small margins (+0.4 on GSM8K) and lack of statistical testing. Demoting from core strength to supporting nuance.

## Novel Insights

The observation that RoT systematically hurts weaker models (LLAMA2-13B drops 12.1%) while benefiting GPT-4 is underexplored. This pattern is consistent with findings in the prompting literature where complex prompts degrade performance on models with weaker instruction-following capabilities, suggesting that the benefit of RoT may be more about GPT-4's ability to comply with elaborate instructions than about activating a "System 2" reasoning mode. The cognitive bias dataset construction—while contaminated—introduces an evaluation paradigm that bridges psychology and LLM assessment, which could be a valuable direction if reconstructed with independent question generation.

## Suggestions

- Reconstruct the cognitive bias dataset using human annotators exclusively (no GPT-4 involvement in question generation) and re-evaluate. This is the single most critical fix.
- Add a detail-matched but non-cognitive-science-structured control prompt to isolate whether the gains come from the specific six-step cognitive framework or from simply providing more detailed instructions.
- Report means and standard deviations across multiple runs for reasoning benchmark results, or use bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Tone down the AGI-related claims; they are unsupported by the marginal improvements on reasoning benchmarks.

## Score and Decision

The paper has a fundamental data contamination issue: the model evaluated (GPT-4) generated 75% of the test set on which the headline result (18.7% improvement) rests. On the uncontaminated reasoning benchmarks, the improvements over CoT-few-shot are marginal (+0.4 on GSM8K, +0.7 on ARC-c) and lack statistical validation. The cognitive-science framing is interesting but not yet empirically validated as the cause of improvement—without a length-matched control, the gains could simply come from more detailed instructions. RoT's failure to help (and active harm to) weaker models further limits generality. The conceptual contribution of connecting dual-process theory to prompting is worthwhile, but the empirical validation is too flawed to support the claims made.
