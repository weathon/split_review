# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a novel approach to safe offline reinforcement learning by utilizing a flow-based latent action manifold that focuses on empirically safe regions. This method addresses the challenges of reconciling soft penalty designs with hard safety requirements and avoiding out-of-distribution actions during policy deployment. The proposed framework is constraint-free and employs a lightweight refiner stage for small, ordered updates in the latent space, effectively decoupling reward, safety, and out-of-distribution control. This design allows for stable multi-objective optimization without explicit constraints or online interaction. The approach is validated across various safe offline benchmarks, demonstrating lower violation rates while maintaining or improving baseline performance in return, highlighting its potential for practical and effective safer offline policy learning.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors have done a great job in presenting their ideas in a clear and concise manner.
2. The authors have provided detailed proofs and explanations for their approach, which adds to the credibility and reliability of their work.
3. The authors have conducted extensive experiments to validate their approach. The results are presented in a clear and concise manner, and the authors have provided detailed explanations and discussions of their findings.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method involves several hyperparameters, such as expert loss weights and prior shaping temperatures, which can make it challenging to tune and potentially impact the performance and robustness of the method.
2. The authors have not provided code for their approach, which makes it difficult for others to reproduce and validate their results.
3. The authors have not provided a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and resource requirements of their approach, which could be a limitation for practical applications.

## Questions
1. The authors mention that the shared expert is always performed last. Can you provide more insights into why this is the case? How does the order of the experts affect the performance of the method?
2. How does the proposed method handle the trade-off between safety and reward? Is there a way to adjust the balance between safety and reward optimization?
3. How does the proposed method handle different types of safety constraints? Is it limited to specific types of constraints or can it handle a variety of safety requirements?
