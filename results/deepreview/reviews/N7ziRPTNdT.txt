# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the role of generation in achieving human-level visual perception, specifically focusing on data efficiency and compositional generalization. It explores whether generative (decoder-based) or non-generative (encoder-based) methods are more effective in achieving this goal. The authors provide theoretical analysis and empirical evidence suggesting that generative methods, particularly those employing inversion techniques, offer significant advantages over non-generative approaches in terms of compositional generalization and data efficiency.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The problem is well-motivated and the theoretical analysis is interesting. The authors provide a new perspective on the importance of generative models in achieving human-level visual perception, particularly in terms of data efficiency and compositional generalization. The paper provides both theoretical analysis and empirical evidence to support its claims, making the findings more convincing.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper presents interesting ideas, the theoretical analysis is limited to specific function classes (e.g., $\mathcal{F}_{\text{int}}$ and $\mathcal{G}_{\text{int}}$), which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world data. The findings are based on the PUG datasets, which feature relatively simple and controlled scenes. It is unclear how well the results would generalize to more complex and diverse datasets, such as those encountered in naturalistic environments. Moreover, the paper's focus on specific function classes and datasets may limit the generalizability of the findings to other scenarios and data types.
2. The paper does not extensively compare its approach with other recent works in the field, such as those using advanced non-generative methods like SigLIP2 or DINOv2. A more comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art methods would provide a better understanding of the advantages and limitations of the proposed approach. Moreover, the paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the potential limitations and challenges of the proposed method, such as scalability to larger datasets or more complex scenes.
