# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a robust evaluation framework for heterogeneous treatment effect (HTE) estimators, focusing on relative error to compare the performance of two HTE estimators. The authors derive theoretical conditions for robust relative error estimation, introducing new loss functions and a neural network design to estimate nuisance parameters. This framework provides reliable evaluations and enables a new learning algorithm for HTE estimation, demonstrating good performance in experiments.

## Strengths
The paper introduces a robust evaluation framework for heterogeneous treatment effect (HTE) estimators, focusing on relative error to compare the performance of two HTE estimators. The authors derive theoretical conditions for robust relative error estimation, introducing new loss functions and a neural network design to estimate nuisance parameters. This framework provides reliable evaluations and enables a new learning algorithm for HTE estimation, demonstrating good performance in experiments.

## Weaknesses
The paper lacks a clear and compelling motivation for the study. The authors do not adequately explain the limitations of existing methods or the specific problems they aim to address. Additionally, the theoretical results are relatively straightforward, and the experimental section is limited in scope, which further weakens the overall impact of the paper.

## Questions
1. What is the motivation of this paper? Why is it important to estimate the relative error of different HTE estimators? What are the limitations of existing methods that this paper aims to address? What are the main problems this paper seeks to solve? 

2. The theoretical results in this paper are relatively straightforward. Could the authors elaborate on any technical challenges encountered in deriving the theoretical properties of the proposed estimator?

3. The experimental section of this paper is limited. Could the authors include additional experiments to validate their findings?
