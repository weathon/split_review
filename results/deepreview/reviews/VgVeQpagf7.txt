# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a method for differentially private dataset distillation. The method is based on D3S, a dataset distillation method that matches the statistics of a pretrained model trained on a private dataset and a model trained on synthetic data. The authors propose to use a publicly pretrained model instead of a model trained on private data, and then privatize the statistics of the private data. They also propose several tricks to improve the performance of the method. The paper shows that the proposed method can achieve better accuracy than DP-SGD on CIFAR10 and CIFAR100.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well written and easy to follow.
2. The authors conduct a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, including ablation studies, visualizations, and applications to federated learning and continual learning.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is a straightforward application of D3S in the DP setting, which limits its technical novelty.
2. The experiments are only conducted on CIFAR10 and CIFAR100, which are relatively small datasets. It would be more convincing to see results on larger datasets such as ImageNet.
3. The comparison with DP-SGD is not entirely fair, as the proposed method uses a publicly pretrained model while DP-SGD trains a model from scratch. The performance gap may be partially attributed to the use of a weaker baseline.

## Questions
1. The authors mention that the use of SAM at validation time does not incur additional privacy cost since it falls under the post-processing property. However, the post-processing property only applies to the final output of the algorithm, while SAM is used during the training process. Could the authors clarify whether this affects the privacy guarantee of the proposed method?
2. The authors mention that the cost of generating these images is relatively heavy. Could they provide a detailed analysis of the computational and memory requirements of the proposed method, and compare them to DP-SGD?
