## Strengths
The paper studies an interesting problem, namely how to watermark LLM outputs without access to the model. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors propose four different watermarking strategies and evaluate them empirically.

## Weaknesses
The paper has several weaknesses, which I detail below. 

1. The paper makes several incorrect claims about watermarking. The authors claim that most watermarking methods require access to the decoding process. This is incorrect; in fact, the large majority of watermarking methods do not require access to the decoding process. For instance, see [1,2,3]. 

2. The authors claim that watermarking methods are not useful in practice because they require model access. This is also incorrect; in fact, model owners can (and do) watermark their models. For instance, OpenAI has admitted to watermarking their models (see https://openai.com/learn/techniques/watermark/). 

3. The authors propose several watermarking strategies, but none of them are novel. For instance, the initial strategy is the same as Kirchenbauer et al. The authors claim that their approach is different because they use words instead of tokens. However, this is a trivial change. The other strategies are also not novel. 

4. The authors do not compare to any of the existing watermarking methods. In fact, there are several watermarking methods that do not require model access (see [1,2,3]). The authors should compare to these methods. 

5. The authors use the term "prompt injection" but never define it. 

[1] https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043
[2] https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10769
[3] https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19534
