# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a Hessian-informed zeroth-order (ZO) method for federated learning (FL). The proposed method, HiSo, utilizes global diagonal Hessian approximations to accelerate convergence while maintaining scalar-only communication. This approach avoids the need to transmit any second-order information, preserving the benefits of dimension-free communication. Theoretical analysis shows that HiSo achieves an accelerated convergence rate independent of the Lipschitz constant and model dimension under certain Hessian approximation assumptions. Empirical results demonstrate that HiSo delivers a 1-5x speedup in communication rounds over existing state-of-the-art ZO-FL baselines across diverse large language model (LLM) fine-tuning benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel approach that leverages Hessian information to accelerate zeroth-order optimization in federated learning without increasing communication costs.
2. The proposed method is supported by both theoretical analysis and empirical results, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency.
3. The paper addresses a significant challenge in FL by enabling faster convergence and reducing communication overhead, which is crucial for large-scale distributed learning tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks clarity in several sections, particularly in explaining the notation used in the algorithms and theoretical analysis. For example, the meaning of $r_l$ in Algorithm 1 is not clearly defined, and the relationship between $r$ and $r_l$ needs further clarification.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the memory requirements for storing historical states and client participation information. This is an important consideration for practical FL implementations, especially on resource-constrained devices.
3. The paper does not compare the proposed method with other Hessian-informed optimization techniques that have been proposed in the literature.

## Questions
1. What is the memory overhead associated with storing historical states and client participation information? How does this affect the scalability of the proposed method for large-scale FL scenarios?
2. How does the proposed method perform compared to other Hessian-informed optimization techniques in terms of convergence speed and accuracy?
3. What are the potential limitations or failure cases of the proposed method? How does it handle scenarios where the Hessian approximation is poor or the low-effective rank assumption does not hold?
