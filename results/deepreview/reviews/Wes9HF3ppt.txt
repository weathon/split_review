# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes insertion language models (ILMs), which generate sequences by inserting tokens at arbitrary positions. ILMs overcome the limitations of autoregressive models (ARMs), which struggle with complex constraints and out-of-order dependencies, and masked diffusion models (MDMs), which have variable-length generation issues. Trained with a tailored network parameterization and a simple denoising loss, ILMs perform well on planning tasks and are comparable to ARMs and better than MDMs in text generation and infilling.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. 
2. The idea of insertion language models is novel and interesting. 
3. The experiments are comprehensive and well-designed. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of ILMs in planning tasks and text generation/infilling.

## Weaknesses
1. The training objective of ILMs is similar to that of masked language models (MLMs), which also predict masked tokens. What are the key differences between ILMs and MLMs? Can we convert an MLM into an ILM by simply changing the masking strategy?
2. ILMs perform slightly worse than ARMs trained for the same number of steps on text data. It would be helpful to provide the results of ARMs trained for more steps to see if ILMs can eventually match their performance.
3. Can you provide the results of MDMs and ILMs with longer sequences (e.g., 512, 1024, 2048) on text data? This would help us better understand the performance of different models on longer sequences.
