# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces ASPEC, a framework for automated agent design that addresses the limitations of existing approaches by creating stateful teams of specialist agents that accumulate knowledge over time. ASPEC manages the full agent lifecycle through evolutionary discovery of specialist archetypes and experiential cultivation of expertise. It employs a "retain-then-escalate" policy, balancing efficiency and adaptability. The framework demonstrates performance improvements on expert-level scientific benchmarks like GPQA while maintaining efficiency and adaptability.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel framework, ASPEC, that addresses the limitations of existing automated agent design approaches by creating stateful teams of specialist agents that accumulate knowledge over time. This approach mimics human expert learning through practice and reflection, which is a significant advancement in the field.
2. The paper provides a thorough explanation of the ASPEC framework, including its evolutionary discovery process and the cultivation of expertise. The diagrams and case studies help in understanding the framework's components and their interactions.
3. The experimental results demonstrate that ASPEC leads to substantial performance improvements on expert-level scientific benchmarks like GPQA. The framework's ability to match or exceed the performance of state-of-the-art methods while maintaining efficiency is a significant strength.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a rigorous theoretical framework to model the convergence properties of the specialist discovery process. This limits the understanding of how the framework will perform in different domain scenarios.
2. The evaluation of ASPEC is primarily focused on scientific benchmarks. The applicability and performance of the framework in more diverse environments, such as real-world software engineering tasks, are not thoroughly explored.
3. The paper identifies the risk of specialists amplifying training biases through memory cultivation but does not propose mitigation strategies. This is a critical concern for the robustness and generalizability of the framework.

## Questions
1. How does ASPEC handle the risk of specialists amplifying training biases through memory cultivation? Are there any proposed mitigation strategies?
