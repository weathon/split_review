# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a new image quality assessment (IQA) task paradigm called grounding-IQA, which integrates multimodal referring and grounding with IQA to enable more fine-grained quality perception. The authors propose two subtasks: grounding-IQA-description (GIQA-DES) and visual question answering (GIQA-VQA). To support this new task, the authors construct a dataset, GIQA-160K, and develop a benchmark, GIQA-Bench, to evaluate the grounding-IQA performance.

## Strengths
1. The introduction of the grounding-IQA task paradigm is a novel contribution that enhances the fine-grained quality assessment of images.
2. The creation of the GIQA-160K dataset and the GIQA-Bench benchmark provides valuable resources for the research community.
3. The paper provides a thorough explanation of the methodology, including the automated annotation pipeline and the evaluation criteria.
4. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in facilitating more fine-grained IQA applications.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing IQA methods to highlight the advantages of the proposed approach.
2. The paper could provide more insights into the limitations of the proposed method and potential directions for future research.
3. The paper could include more visualizations to aid in understanding the concepts and results.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed grounding-IQA approach compare with other state-of-the-art IQA methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency?
2. What are the potential applications of the grounding-IQA task beyond image quality assessment?
3. How does the proposed method handle biases in the dataset, and what steps are taken to ensure the robustness of the results?
