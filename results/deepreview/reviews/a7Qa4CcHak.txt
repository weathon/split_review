# Review

## Summary
This paper presents Terminal-Bench 2.0, a benchmark for evaluating AI agents on challenging, real-world tasks in command line environments. It includes 89 tasks inspired by professional workflows, with each task featuring a unique environment, human-written solution, and comprehensive tests for verification. The authors demonstrate that current frontier models and agents achieve less than 65% success on these tasks, with smaller models scoring around 15%. The paper also provides an error analysis to identify areas for model and agent improvement, publishing the dataset and evaluation harness to assist developers and researchers in future work.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured and easy to follow.
2. The benchmark covers a wide range of tasks, providing a comprehensive evaluation of agent capabilities.
3. The error analysis offers valuable insights into the limitations of current models and agents, guiding future improvements.

## Weaknesses
1. The tasks in the benchmark are manually created, which may introduce subjective biases and inconsistencies.
2. The benchmark only considers the success or failure of tasks, without evaluating the efficiency or cost of the solutions.

## Questions
1. How can the benchmark ensure that the tasks are representative and unbiased?
2. How does the benchmark assess the efficiency and cost of different solutions?
3. How does the benchmark ensure that the tasks remain relevant and challenging as AI agents evolve?
