# Review

## Summary
This paper focuses on the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in Graph Continual Learning (GCL) scenarios. The authors propose a benchmark called LLM4GCL to evaluate the performance of LLMs in GCL. They also identify a flaw in the existing experimental setup and propose a new one to avoid task ID leakage. Finally, the authors propose a new method called Simple Graph Continual Learning (SimGCL) and conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate its effectiveness.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the current experimental setup used in GCL, identifies a flaw, and proposes a new experimental setup to avoid task ID leakage. This analysis is valuable for the research community and helps to make the evaluation of GCL methods more reasonable.

2. The paper conducts extensive experiments to evaluate the performance of various methods in GCL scenarios. The experiments are well-designed and cover different types of datasets and settings, providing a thorough comparison of different approaches.

3. The paper proposes a new method called Simple Graph Continual Learning (SimGCL) that leverages the power of LLMs to address catastrophic forgetting in GCL. The method is simple yet effective, as demonstrated by the experimental results.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks novelty in its proposed method. The use of LLMs and prototype classifiers in continual learning scenarios has already been explored in previous works. The paper does not introduce any new techniques or innovations to improve the existing methods.

2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method. Given that LLMs are known for their high computational requirements, it would be important to evaluate the scalability of the approach when applied to large-scale graph datasets.

3. The paper does not consider the impact of different LLM architectures on the performance of the proposed method. It would be valuable to investigate how various LLM models, such as GPT-3 and GPT-4, affect the results.

4. The paper does not compare the proposed method with other state-of-the-art approaches that also leverage LLMs for continual learning on graphs. A comparison with these methods would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed approach.
