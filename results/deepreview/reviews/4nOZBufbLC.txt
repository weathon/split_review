# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces Count Bridges, a novel framework for generative modeling and deconvolution of integer-valued biological data. The authors propose a stochastic bridge process on the integers using Poisson birth-death dynamics, which provides an exact and tractable analogue of diffusion-style models for count data. They extend this framework to enable direct training from aggregated measurements via an Expectation-Maximization-style approach that treats unit-level counts as latent variables. The methods are evaluated on synthetic benchmarks and real-world biological applications, including modeling single-cell gene expression data at the nucleotide resolution and deconvolving bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomic data.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important problem in biological data analysis, namely the challenge of modeling and deconvolving integer-valued count data.
2. The proposed Count Bridges framework is novel and well-motivated, providing a principled approach to generative modeling and deconvolution of count data.
3. The methods are thoroughly evaluated on synthetic benchmarks and real-world biological applications, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in integer distribution matching and deconvolution tasks.
4. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the proposed methods and their application to biological data.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with existing methods for deconvolution of biological data, such as those based on deep learning architectures like self-attention mechanisms. It would be beneficial to include such comparisons to better evaluate the performance of Count Bridges in the context of existing literature.
2. The scalability of Count Bridges to large-scale datasets is not thoroughly explored. While the methods are shown to perform well on synthetic and real-world datasets, a more extensive analysis of scalability to even larger datasets would strengthen the paper.
3. The paper could provide more details on the computational efficiency of Count Bridges, including training time and resource requirements. This information would be valuable for assessing the practical applicability of the methods.

## Questions
1. How does the performance of Count Bridges compare to existing deep learning-based methods for deconvolution of biological data?
2. Can you provide more details on the scalability of Count Bridges to large-scale datasets? Have you evaluated the methods on datasets with millions of cells or larger?
3. What are the training time and resource requirements for Count Bridges? How do these compare to existing methods for deconvolution and generative modeling of biological data?
4. The paper mentions that Count Bridges can handle unit-level generation from aggregated observations. Can you provide more details on this aspect? How does the method perform when only aggregated count data is available for training, and how well can it recover individual cell-level profiles?
