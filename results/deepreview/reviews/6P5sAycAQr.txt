# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces Defined Taxonomic Stratification (DefNTaxS), a framework that enhances the performance of generalized vision-language models like CLIP in zero-shot image classification tasks. DefNTaxS addresses the challenge of label ambiguity by leveraging large language models (LLMs) to automatically cluster related classes into hierarchical subcategories, thereby enriching the text prompts with taxonomic context. This approach improves the differentiation between semantically similar classes, leading to significant accuracy gains across seven benchmarks, including ImageNet and EuroSAT, without the need for retraining or manual prompt alteration.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The idea is clearly illustrated.
2. The proposed method is simple and easy to implement. It leverages the power of LLMs to improve the performance of VLMs.
3. The experiments show the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The motivation is not clear. The authors claim that "lateral relationships between classes" and "classification confidence" are important, but they do not show the evidence. Besides, the authors do not explain how the proposed method addresses these issues.
2. The proposed method is not novel. It seems that the method only uses LLMs to generate some descriptions for the classes and the subcategories of the classes. The descriptions include some common sense, which may improve the performance. However, this practice is widely used in LLMs-based applications. It seems that the proposed method is a straightforward application of LLMs.
3. The authors claim that the proposed method addresses the issue of label ambiguity. However, the experiments do not show the improvement on ambiguous labels.
4. The authors claim that the proposed method is a fully automated, procedural, training-free framework. However, the method requires the use of LLMs to generate descriptions, which may be time-consuming and expensive.
5. The authors do not show the failure cases of the proposed method.

## Questions
1. What is the motivation of the proposed method?
2. What is the novelty of the proposed method?
3. Does the proposed method address the issue of label ambiguity?
4. Is the proposed method practical in real-world applications?
