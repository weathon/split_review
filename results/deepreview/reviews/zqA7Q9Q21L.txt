# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a method for computing worst-case robust pursuit strategies in pursuit evasion games (PEGs) under partial observability. The proposed method extends a dynamic programming (DP) algorithm for solving Markov PEGs to asynchronous moves by the evader and incorporates a belief preservation mechanism to estimate the evader’s possible positions. The method is then integrated into an existing framework called Equilibrium Policy Generalization (EPG) to learn a real-time pursuer policy through cross-graph reinforcement learning. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed method through experiments on various graph structures and shows that it outperforms existing methods in terms of robustness and generalization capabilities.

## Strengths
1. The paper proposes a novel approach to worst-case robust pursuit strategies in PEGs under partial observability, which is an important and challenging problem.
2. The paper extends the DP algorithm to asynchronous moves by the evader and incorporates a belief preservation mechanism to handle partial observability, which are reasonable and practical considerations.
3. The paper integrates the proposed method into the EPG framework and demonstrates its effectiveness through experiments on various graph structures, showing improvements over existing methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity or runtime performance of the proposed method, which would be important considerations for real-time applications.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with existing methods for robust pursuit strategies in PEGs, particularly in terms of computational efficiency and generalization capabilities.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed method to different parameters or settings, such as the observation range or the number of pursuers.

## Questions
1. How does the computational complexity of the proposed method compare to existing methods for robust pursuit strategies in PEGs?
2. Can the proposed method be extended to other types of PEGs or game-theoretic problems beyond the ones considered in the paper?
3. How does the proposed method perform under different observation ranges or other sensing capabilities beyond binary detection?
4. How does the proposed method handle dynamic or adversarial graph structures, where the graph topology may change over time?
5. How does the proposed method perform against strategies other than the DP evader policy, such as non-optimal or adaptive strategies?
