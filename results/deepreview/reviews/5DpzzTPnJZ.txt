# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a theoretical analysis of plasticity loss in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and proposes a novel algorithm, Sample Weight Decay (SWD), to address this issue. The authors identify two primary factors contributing to plasticity loss: the non-stationarity of data distributions and the non-stationarity of targets induced by bootstrapping. They attribute the loss of plasticity to two mechanisms: the rank collapse of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Gram matrix and the 1/t decay of gradient magnitude. The paper focuses on the second mechanism and proposes SWD as a solution to restore gradient magnitude. The authors evaluate SWD on TD3, Double DQN, and SAC algorithms across various environments, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving learning performance and alleviating plasticity loss.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a comprehensive theoretical analysis of plasticity loss in DRL, offering a new perspective on the problem.
2. The proposed SWD algorithm is well-motivated and grounded in theory. It is designed to address the gradient attenuation issue, providing a novel solution to plasticity loss.
3. The authors conduct extensive experiments across multiple environments and algorithms, demonstrating the effectiveness of SWD in improving learning performance.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper evaluates SWD on a limited set of algorithms (TD3, Double DQN, SAC) and environments (MuJoCo, ALE, DMC). While the results are promising, it would be beneficial to see how SWD performs in a wider range of scenarios, including more complex environments and real-world applications.
2. The authors do not provide a thorough comparison with existing methods for addressing plasticity loss, such as Network Reset, Neuron Recycling, and Noise Injection. It would be valuable to see how SWD compares to these methods in terms of performance and computational efficiency.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational overhead introduced by SWD. It would be helpful to know how much additional computation SWD requires compared to the base algorithms, and whether this overhead is significant enough to impact practical deployment.

## Questions
1. How does SWD compare to existing methods for addressing plasticity loss in terms of performance and computational efficiency?
2. Can the authors provide more details on the computational overhead introduced by SWD, and discuss its potential impact on practical deployment?
3. Have the authors considered evaluating SWD on more complex environments and real-world applications beyond the scope of the current experiments?
