# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a brain-inspired dual-stream network, HiTNet, for multimodal sentiment analysis under missing data. The network consists of an intra-modal enhancement stream inspired by hippocampal memory systems and an inter-modal regulation stream inspired by thalamic perceptual regulation. The authors evaluate HiTNet on three benchmark datasets and demonstrate its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, especially under extreme missing conditions.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow.
2. The idea of using brain-inspired mechanisms to handle missing data is interesting.
3. The experimental results are promising, especially under extreme missing conditions.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity or resource requirements of HiTNet, which could be a concern for practical applications.
2. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing methods, including a discussion of the limitations of current approaches and how HiTNet addresses these limitations.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of different types of missing data (e.g., missingness in different modalities or at different frames) on the performance of HiTNet.

## Questions
1. How does HiTNet handle different types of missing data (e.g., missingness in different modalities or at different frames)? Are there any limitations or challenges in dealing with such data?
2. Can the authors provide more details on the computational complexity and resource requirements of HiTNet, and how it compares to other state-of-the-art methods?
3. How does HiTNet integrate with existing multimodal sentiment analysis pipelines or frameworks? Are there any specific requirements or constraints for the data or model?
4. How does HiTNet generalize to other types of sentiment analysis tasks or domains? Have the authors tested the model on any such datasets or scenarios?
5. How does HiTNet compare to other brain-inspired or biomimetic approaches for sentiment analysis or related tasks? Are there any specific advantages or disadvantages compared to these methods?
