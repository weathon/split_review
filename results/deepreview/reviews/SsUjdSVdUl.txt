# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces Critique-RL, a two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework for developing critique models that provide feedback on language model outputs. The authors propose this method to improve the discriminability and helpfulness of critique models without relying on strong supervision. The first stage focuses on optimizing discriminability using direct reward signals, while the second stage emphasizes helpfulness with indirect rewards based on actor refinement. Extensive experiments demonstrate significant performance gains, with Critique-RL achieving notable improvements in both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks. The paper also highlights the compute efficiency of the approach, showing better scalability compared to traditional methods.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel two-stage RL approach for developing critique models, which is a significant contribution to the field of language model supervision. The method addresses the critical challenge of optimizing both discriminability and helpfulness in critique models, which has not been effectively tackled in previous work.

2. The paper provides a thorough analysis of the limitations of indirect reward signals and proposes a well-reasoned solution with Critique-RL. The empirical findings and behavior analysis are presented clearly, with detailed experimental results and ablation studies that validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

3. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, making it easy to follow the methodology and results. The figures and tables are informative and support the claims made in the text.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's focus on mathematical reasoning tasks raises concerns about the generalizability of the proposed method to other types of tasks, such as open-ended text generation or complex decision-making scenarios. The reliance on a rule-based verifier may limit the applicability of Critique-RL to tasks where such verifiers are available.

2. The paper does not adequately address the scalability of the two-stage RL approach. While the method shows promising results in the experiments, it is unclear how it would perform with larger models or in more diverse settings. The computational requirements and potential limitations in training such models should be discussed in more detail.

3. The paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods for training critique models. While it compares with several baselines, a more extensive comparison with the latest approaches in the field would strengthen the paper's claims and provide a clearer picture of its contributions.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed two-stage RL approach scale with larger models or more complex tasks? Are there any computational limitations or bottlenecks that need to be addressed for practical deployment?

2. Can the method be extended to other types of tasks beyond mathematical reasoning, such as open-ended text generation or complex decision-making scenarios? If so, how would the verifier need to be adapted?

3. How does Critique-RL compare with other recent state-of-the-art methods for training critique models? A more comprehensive comparison would help to better position this work in the context of existing approaches.
