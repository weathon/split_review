# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a two-stage framework to parse raster-based scientific diagrams into structured representations. In the first stage, the authors collect a high-quality TiKZ-30K dataset, where they pay special attention to the drawing order and comment annotations. In the second stage, they use reinforcement learning to further improve the model. Experiments show that the proposed method outperforms existing open-source MLLMs and even surpasses leading commercial models like GPT-5 and Claude-Sonnet-4.

## Strengths
1. The proposed method is technically sound. The two-stage framework is reasonable, and the design of the hybrid reward function is interesting.
2. The experiments are comprehensive, and the results are promising. The proposed method outperforms existing open-source MLLMs and even surpasses leading commercial models like GPT-5 and Claude-Sonnet-4.
3. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors should provide more details about the TikZ-30K dataset, such as the data distribution and the number of comments in the code.
2. The authors should provide more details about the reward function, such as the weights used to combine different rewards.
3. The ablation study is incomplete. The authors should also provide an ablation study for the reward function.

## Questions
1. In Section 3.2, the authors mention that they split the filtered samples into two parts: 30,000 for SFT and 28,000 for RL. Why do the authors use more samples for SFT than for RL?
2. In Section 3.3, the authors mention that they do not set special weights for each reward component. How do the authors combine these rewards, and what is the final reward used in the experiments?
3. In Section 4.5, the authors provide an ablation study for code reordering and comment injection. Why do the authors not provide an ablation study for the reward function?
