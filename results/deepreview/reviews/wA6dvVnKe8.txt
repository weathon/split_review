# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates adversarial robustness in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which are crucial for robot learning, enabling robots to execute tasks from natural language instructions. The authors propose a model-agnostic adversarial patch attack called Embedding Disruption Patch Attack (EDPA) that can be applied across different VLA models without prior knowledge of the model architecture, action space, or robotic manipulator. EDPAs aim is to maximize discrepancies in latent representations between clean and adversarial visual inputs and disrupt the semantic alignment between visual and textual representations. This causes the VLA model to misinterpret visual information, leading to incorrect actions and task failure. To counter this, the authors introduce an adversarial fine-tuning scheme for the visual encoder, optimizing it to produce similar latent representations for both clean and adversarially perturbed visual inputs. Evaluations on the LIBERO robotic simulation benchmark show that EDPAs significantly increase task failure rates in state-of-the-art VLA models, while the proposed defense effectively mitigates this degradation.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a model-agnostic adversarial patch attack (EDPA) that can be applied to different VLA models without prior knowledge of the model architecture, action space, or robotic manipulator. This makes the approach highly versatile and applicable to a wide range of VLA models, enhancing its practical relevance.

2. The authors conduct extensive evaluations on the LIBERO robotic simulation benchmark, demonstrating the effectiveness of EDPAs in increasing task failure rates across various state-of-the-art VLA models. This thorough evaluation provides strong empirical support for the proposed approach.

3. The paper proposes an adversarial fine-tuning scheme for the visual encoder to mitigate the effects of adversarial attacks. By optimizing the visual encoder to produce similar latent representations for both clean and adversarially perturbed visual inputs, the authors effectively enhance the robustness of VLA models against adversarial attacks, improving their reliability in real-world applications.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a comparison with non-patch-based adversarial attacks, such as those involving noise added to the entire image. It would be beneficial to evaluate how the proposed method performs against such attacks to provide a more comprehensive assessment of its effectiveness.

2. The evaluation of the proposed defense is limited to the OpenVLA model. To better demonstrate the generalizability and robustness of the defense strategy, it would be advantageous to test it against a broader range of VLA models, such as the $\pi_0$ model, which has a different architecture and potentially different vulnerabilities.

3. The paper does not provide sufficient detail about the hyperparameters used for the OpenVLA model in the experiments. Specifically, it is unclear whether the authors used the default settings or if they adjusted the hyperparameters. Providing more transparency about the experimental setup would enhance the reproducibility and credibility of the results.

4. The proposed adversarial fine-tuning scheme is only tested against the original OpenVLA model. To validate the effectiveness of this defense strategy, it should be tested against fine-tuned versions of OpenVLA as well. This would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of its robustness and general applicability.

## Questions
1. How does the performance of the proposed method compare to non-patch-based adversarial attacks, such as those involving noise added to the entire image? It would be valuable to include such comparisons to provide a more comprehensive assessment of the proposed method's effectiveness.

2. Can the authors provide more detailed information about the hyperparameters used for the OpenVLA model in the experiments? Specifically, did the authors use the default settings or did they adjust the hyperparameters? Providing this information would enhance the reproducibility and credibility of the results.

3. Has the proposed adversarial fine-tuning scheme been tested against fine-tuned versions of the OpenVLA model? It would be beneficial to include such experiments to validate the effectiveness and general applicability of the defense strategy.
