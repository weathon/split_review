## Summary

The paper presents *Calgacus*, a rank-based steganographic protocol that uses an LLM to hide a secret text within a different, coherent text of exactly the same length (same token count). The method is simple: extract token ranks from the secret message, then generate a stegotext by selecting tokens at those ranks following a different prompt (the key). The paper demonstrates that stegotexts from Llama 3 8B have log-probabilities within the range of real Reddit posts, and wraps this in an extended philosophical discussion about LLM intentionality, hallucinations, and the decoupling of text from authorial intent.

## Strengths

1. **Full-capacity steganography.** The protocol produces a stegotext exactly as long as the secret message, a property the authors explicitly contrast with prior variable-length methods (Section 3, Related Work). This is a concrete and measurable advantage.

2. **Quantitative plausibility evidence.** Figure 4 shows that stegotexts generated by Llama 3 8B have log-probabilities falling within the distribution of 1000 real Reddit posts, while random ASCII and random-word strings do not. This provides at least one piece of evidence that the fake texts are not obviously incoherent.

3. **Clear, reproducible method description.** Section 3 gives a step-by-step recipe (tokenize → record ranks → generate stegotext from a different prompt using those ranks) that is illustrated in Figure 3 and requires only standard LLM inference. A public demo repository is provided.

4. **Concrete AI-safety application scenario.** Section 4's "shipping unfiltered LLMs without really shipping them" play connects the technical result to a pressing policy concern, giving the paper stakes beyond a purely technical exercise.

5. **Well-written and thought-provoking discussion.** Sections 4–5 raise genuinely interesting questions about LLM knowledge, hallucinations as a failure of intention attribution, and the erosion of trust in text. The Oulipo parallel and the Tacitus/Calgacus framing are effective literary devices.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **No human evaluation for the "opaque to humans" claim (evidential gap).** The paper asserts that stegotexts are "opaque to humans" (Section 1) but provides no human-subject study to support this. A text can have good LLM log-probability and still exhibit subtle disfluencies, topic drift, or unnatural transitions that a human reader would notice. For a steganography paper, the claim that the hidden text is undetectable by human inspection is central, and it is unsubstantiated as presented. A forced-choice detection experiment would directly address this.

2. **No comparison to prior LLM-based steganography (methodological gap).** The paper cites several prior LLM-based steganography methods (Ziegler et al., 2019; Kaptchuk et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024; Zamir, 2024) and claims "full capacity" as the distinguishing property, but provides no experimental comparison — neither qualitative examples nor quantitative metrics (bits per token, text quality, etc.) — against any of these. Without such comparison, the reader cannot assess whether the method represents an improvement or is simply a rediscovery of known rank-based approaches.

3. **Extremely limited evaluation scope.** The main experiment (Figure 4) uses only 3 source texts, each 85 tokens, from a single dataset (Reddit), evaluated primarily with one LLM (Llama 3 8B). No experiments vary text length, explore failure modes systematically (beyond the hash example), or measure success rates across diverse inputs. The Phi-3 cross-check (Figure 14) is mentioned but is not a central analysis. The histograms lack confidence intervals, making it hard to assess statistical reliability. This evaluation is insufficient to support the paper's broad claims.

4. **Security analysis is entirely qualitative and unverified.** Section 3.1 discusses brute-force bounds and deniability but provides no experiments — no key-recovery attacks, no steganalysis detection tests, no adversary model with quantifiable success rates. The "deniability" claim rests on a single exemplary observation (an outlier prompt producing comparable log-probability), without statistical analysis. For a paper presenting a steganographic protocol, this is a significant omission.

### Minor

1. **Low technical novelty for a top-tier venue.** The method is acknowledged by the authors as "so simple it could be seen as a mere variation of the standard algorithm used to generate text with LLMs" (Section 5). Rank-based steganography has been known at least since Ziegler et al. (2019). The "full capacity" property is a direct consequence of rank-preserving generation, not a new algorithmic idea. The paper's contribution is primarily a demonstration and discussion piece, not a novel technical method. This is not inherently disqualifying, but the framing as a major result overstates the technical novelty relative to the evidence provided.

2. **Practical limitation understated.** Sender and receiver must run exactly the same LLM under identical software/hardware conditions to obtain identical logits and ranks. The authors acknowledge this but its impact in real-world deployment scenarios is substantial.

3. **The discussion, while interesting, is speculative interpretation of a simple technical artifact.** Sections 4–5 extrapolate broad claims about "the radical decoupling of text from authorial intent" and "eroding the historical pact between intent and the written word" from a method whose human-indistinguishability has not been demonstrated. The philosophical reflections would be more compelling grounded in a validated technical result.

### Trivial

- The histograms in Figure 4 would benefit from bootstrapped confidence bands or error bars.
- The paper would be strengthened by reporting failure rates (e.g., proportion of generated stegotexts judged incoherent by human raters or by an LLM quality metric).

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison of bits-per-token capacity against prior methods (Meteor, Wu et al., etc.) under matched conditions.
- Evaluation across multiple text lengths (e.g., 50, 100, 200 tokens) and genres (news, fiction, technical writing).
- A simple automated steganalysis baseline (e.g., a classifier trained to distinguish stegotexts from real text).

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface one genuinely novel observation: the paper's framing of hallucinations as a "void of intention" rather than a failure of factuality is insightful, but the reviews collectively observe that this framing is logically independent of the specific steganographic protocol — it would apply to any sufficiently fluent LLM-generated text, regardless of whether the generation was constrained by steganographic ranks. This disconnect between the technical method and the philosophical conclusions is not acknowledged in the paper, and addressing it would strengthen the discussion.

## Suggestions

1. **Run a human detection experiment.** The single most impactful addition would be a forced-choice experiment (e.g., MTurk workers asked to distinguish real Reddit posts from stegotexts). If accuracy is at chance, the "opaque to humans" claim is supported; if not, it must be retracted or qualified.

2. **Include at least one prior steganography baseline.** Reproduce one existing method (e.g., Kaptchuk et al., 2021 or Wu et al., 2024) under matched conditions and compare on text quality and capacity metrics.

3. **Expand the evaluation scale.** Use more source texts (50+), multiple lengths (e.g., 50–200 tokens), and at least 2–3 LLMs for encoding. Report failure rates and show confidence intervals.

4. **Add a basic security experiment.** Even a simple attack — e.g., generate plausible key candidates and check whether decoding yields coherent English — would be informative. A negative result (the attack fails) would strengthen the security claims.
