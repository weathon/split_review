## Summary

This paper identifies the "semantic imbalance" problem in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant temporal components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality). It introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) as a diagnostic metric to quantify this imbalance, and an Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW) mechanism that dynamically reweights component-specific contrastive losses in the CoST framework. The core idea — diagnosing and correcting representation imbalance via a directional metric — is plausible and well-motivated. However, the experimental validation and presentation have significant issues that prevent the paper from substantiating its claims.

## Strengths

- **Validated diagnostic for semantic imbalance.** Table 1 shows that on synthetic data with controlled amplitude ratios, the SDE metric and asymmetry factor Δ clearly capture the degree to which one component (trend or seasonal) dominates in TS2Vec's representations. This provides a concrete, measurable characterization of a real problem.

- **Forecasting improvements on a subset of benchmarks.** On ETTm1, CoST+APW achieves lower MSE than vanilla CoST across all five forecasting horizons (e.g., 0.012 vs 0.015 at horizon 24; 0.132 vs 0.154 at horizon 672). On ETT2 and ETT1 the method also shows wins on multiple horizons. These results indicate the approach can be effective under certain data regimes.

- **Negative result ablation.** Table 2 shows that directly adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec does not help and sometimes hurts performance. This ablation clarifies why a diagnostic metric cannot simply be converted into a training loss, supporting the paper's case for the multi-view + weighting approach.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Claims are overstated relative to the experimental results.** The abstract claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and Section 4.4.4 claims "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy." However, CoST+APW is *worse* than vanilla CoST on 3 of 5 Electricity horizons (168: 0.566 vs 0.425; 336: 0.677 vs 0.576; 720: 1.010 vs 0.911) and on multiple Weather horizons (e.g., 336: 0.266 vs 0.256; 720: 0.299 vs 0.278). The method's advantage is dataset-dependent, not consistent. Moreover, SDE values are *never reported* for CoST+APW (Table 3 only contains MSE/MAE), so the claim of "consistently lower SDE values" is unsubstantiated.

2. **No ablation isolating the APW mechanism from the fusion MLP.** The proposed method introduces both (a) a learned fusion MLP (Section 4.4.2) that maps concatenated trend and seasonal embeddings to a composite embedding, and (b) the asymmetric weighting (Section 4.4.3). These two changes are confounded: any performance gain could come from the MLP alone, the weighting alone, or their interaction. Without an ablation comparing CoST+MLP (without APW) against CoST+APW (with APW), the contribution of the claimed core mechanism (the weighting) is not isolated. The paper acknowledges this gap only in the conclusion as future work, which is insufficient for a main experimental contribution.

3. **Inconsistent SDE terminology and underspecified computation in the CoST+APW pipeline.** The acronym is expanded as "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title/abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3) — three different names for the same concept. More critically, the SDE formula in Section 3.2 assumes a single encoder v(·) that processes both the composite and the individual components. In the CoST+APW pipeline, v(a) comes from the trend encoder, v(b) from the seasonal encoder, and v(a+b) from a separate fusion MLP. The paper never specifies how these different encoders' outputs are reconciled for cosine similarity computation (dimension matching, alignment of latent spaces). Without this, the Δ computation that drives the entire weighting mechanism is not reproducible.

4. **Contradiction between "pluggable without architectural changes" and the fusion MLP.** The abstract states the approach "can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes." However, Section 4.4.2 introduces a non-trivial architectural addition — a learned fusion MLP — that is not part of the CoST baseline. While the APW loss-weighting itself may not require architectural changes, the paper presents the MLP+APW as an integrated method, so the "no architectural changes" claim is misleading.

### Minor

5. **Missing SDE values for the proposed method.** Table 3's caption says it "reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" but shows only MSE and MAE columns. Reporting SDE values for CoST+APW versus CoST would directly validate whether the weighting mechanism actually reduces representation imbalance.

6. **"Isotropic embedding collapse" is diagnosed as a limitation but never experimentally analyzed.** The introduction identifies isotropic embedding collapse as one of three core limitations, and Section 2.4 discusses it in related work. However, the paper provides no measurement of isotropy, uniformity, or embedding collapse for any method, and does not show that APW alleviates collapse. This thread is dropped entirely.

7. **Key hyperparameters γ, γ' are never specified.** The paper defines the weighting as (1+γ·Δ)L_season + (1+γ'·(−Δ))L_trend but does not report what values of γ and γ' are used in any experiment, nor any sensitivity analysis.

8. **No standard deviations or statistical significance.** Results are reported as point estimates with no variance across runs. Given the mixed results (APW is worse than CoST on several settings), standard deviations or significance tests are essential to determine whether the observed improvements are reliable.

### Trivial

9. The paper uses three different expansions for SDE (Disentanglement / Separability / Decomposition). This should be unified.

10. Figure 1 caption uses inconsistent notation: it gives Δ = SDE_{(B,A)} - SDE_{(A,B)} but the text defines Δ = SDE_{b,a} - SDE_{a,b}. The subscripts (A,B) vs (a,b) should be consistent.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Comparison to a simple static reweighting baseline (e.g., always upweight the seasonal loss by a fixed ratio) would clarify whether dynamic weighting adds value over a fixed heuristic.
- Visualizations of learned embeddings (e.g., t-SNE/PCA of trend vs seasonal components) would strengthen the qualitative argument that APW produces more balanced representations.

## Removed Points

- The harsh critic's claim that "The proposed method does not outperform the baseline it claims to improve" as a fatal flaw is partially overbroad: the method does outperform CoST on ETTm1 (all horizons) and ETT2 (most horizons). The real issue is overclaimed *consistency*, not complete failure. Retained as a major weakness (point 1 above) but reframed.
- "The SDE that drives the asymmetric weighting is never formally defined for the CoST+APW pipeline" — the paper does provide enough description to infer the computation (v(a) = pool(V_T), v(b) = pool(V_S), v(a+b) = MLP([v(a)||v(b)])), but it is underspecified on dimension alignment. Retained as a major weakness (point 3) but softened.
- "The method is not reproducible because SDE is undefined" — this is too strong. The computation is inferable even if not fully specified. Retained as a clarity/missing-detail issue.
- Strength Finder's claim that "Table 3 shows CoST+APW consistently outperforms vanilla CoST and TS2Vec in forecasting MSE across multiple horizons on ETTm1 and Electricity" — this is inaccurate for Electricity (APW loses on 3 of 5 horizons). Removed as a misreading of the results.

## Suggestions

1. Report SDE values for CoST+APW alongside CoST on real datasets, to directly show that the weighting reduces the asymmetry Δ.
2. Add an ablation that separates the fusion MLP from APW: compare CoST (baseline) → CoST+MLP (MLP only, no weighting) → CoST+APW (MLP + weighting). This would isolate the contribution of the weighting mechanism.
3. Temper the language in the abstract and conclusion: replace "consistent gains" with more precise characterizations of where and when the method helps.
4. Standardize the SDE name and specify the full computation pipeline for SDE in the CoST+APW setting, including dimension matching.
5. Report γ, γ' values and provide sensitivity analysis.
