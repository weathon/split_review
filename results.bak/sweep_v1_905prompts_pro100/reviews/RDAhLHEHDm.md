## Summary

This paper challenges the prevailing sequence-centric paradigm in Scientific LLMs by proposing a "context-driven" approach: instead of feeding raw protein sequences to LLMs, it uses bioinformatics tools (InterProScan, BLAST, ProTrek) to generate structured textual context that is then provided to a general-purpose LLM. Through a systematic comparison across 7 models (3 specialized Sci-LLMs, 4 general LLMs) and 3 input modes (sequence-only, context-only, sequence+context), the paper finds that context-only consistently achieves the highest accuracy and that adding raw sequence to context degrades performance. The authors argue this reveals a "tokenization dilemma" and advocate reframing Sci-LLMs as reasoning engines over structured knowledge rather than sequence decoders.

## Strengths

- **Systematic, broad empirical comparison**: Table 1 evaluates 7 models across 3 input modes and 3 task categories (function, pathway, subcellular location), providing a clear and consistent picture. Context-only yields the highest accuracy for every model tested, a pattern that holds across both specialized Sci-LLMs and general-purpose LLMs.

- **Multi-dimensional analyses**: The paper goes beyond a single accuracy table with complementary analyses — layer-wise representation tracing through Evolla (Figure 3, showing ARI degradation from 0.945 → 0.916 → 0.809 across encoder/alignment/decoder stages), temporal degradation analysis across publication years (Figure 4), and practical cost-efficiency quantification (Table 2). This triangulation strengthens the central argument.

- **Wet-lab validation on truly novel sequences** (Section 5.6): The test on unpublished Rhodopsin and PETase sequences absent from all major databases provides the strongest evidence against memorization as an alternative explanation. The context-driven method achieves 100% and 97.3% accuracy respectively.

- **Clear, well-articulated framing**: The "tokenization dilemma" (weak representation vs. semantic misalignment) is a crisp conceptual framework that organizes the experimental narrative effectively and gives the paper a strong thesis.

- **Practical efficiency demonstration**: The cost analysis (Table 2) showing the context-driven method is ~23× cheaper than Evolla for single queries and ~154× faster in batch mode provides a concrete practical argument for the approach.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Task asymmetry undermines the "reasoning" claim**: The paper's central framing — that context-driven LLMs function as "reasoning engines over expert knowledge" — is not adequately disentangled from the simpler explanation that the models are performing reading comprehension over context that already contains the answer. The context is constructed from tools that detect conserved domains (InterProScan) and retrieve GO annotations from homologs (BLAST). The structured prompt template (Section 4) shows that context includes explicit GO terms and domain descriptions. For many proteins, this context will contain the answer or a strong paraphrase, making the task one of information extraction rather than biological reasoning. The paper acknowledges the label leakage concern but addresses only *identity-based* leakage (not retrieving the query protein's own annotations), not the more fundamental issue that context often provides sufficient information for the answer without requiring synthesis. The wet-lab validation partially addresses this but is limited to binary classification on two families — far simpler than the open-ended functional QA in the main benchmark. No analysis (adversarial perturbations, context ablation, reasoning probes) attempts to separate retrieval from synthesis.

- **LLM-Score judge is unvalidated**: The entire quantitative comparison in Table 1 relies on an automated LLM judge ("LLM-Score") with no reported validation against human expert annotations. If the judge exhibits systematic biases — for instance, preferring answers that resemble the structured language of the context — the apparent superiority of the context-only condition could partially reflect judge preference rather than true answer quality. No calibration study, inter-rater agreement, or human correlation analysis is presented.

### Minor

- **Embedding comparison in Section 5.2 is methodologically problematic**: The paper compares final-layer embeddings from Sci-LLMs against embeddings generated by Qwen-embedding applied to the context text. These are different embedding models applied to different data types, making the claimed superiority of the context-driven approach (ARI 0.958 vs. 0.492–0.809) uninterpretable as a comparison of *paradigms*. The high ARI for the context-driven approach may primarily reflect the determinism and information density of the context text rather than any property of the approach itself. The layer-wise analysis in Section 5.3 (tracing Evolla's internal representations) is a more principled comparison and should carry more weight.

- **"Sequence as noise" claim rests on small effect sizes without statistical grounding**: The degradation from context-only to sequence+context is consistent but small — e.g., Intern-S1 drops from 86.15 to 84.03, Evolla from 74.02 to 70.53, Gemini 2.5 Pro from 87.19 to 86.98. No confidence intervals, statistical tests, or per-example analysis of where the degradation occurs are reported. While the directional consistency across models is suggestive, the paper's strong language ("actively detrimental," "informational noise") is not proportionally supported by the magnitude of evidence. Alternative explanations (e.g., increased input length causing distraction) are not ruled out.

- **Ambiguity in last decoder embedding extraction** (Section 5.3): The paper does not specify whether the "last decoder embedding" for Evolla is extracted from the sequence representation before decoding or from the generated answer tokens. If it includes decoded text, the ARI could be confounded by output quality rather than purely reflecting internal representation degradation.

- Validating the LLM judge against human expert annotations on a subset of model outputs.
- Reporting bootstrap confidence intervals or statistical tests for the context-only vs. sequence+context differences in Table 1.

## Novel Insights

The layer-wise representational analysis tracing Evolla's internal processing (Figure 3) provides a genuinely novel mechanistic view: the protein encoder (SaProt) produces well-structured functional representations (ARI 0.945), but these degrade through the Q-Former alignment module (ARI 0.916) and further through the LLM decoder (ARI 0.809). This directly visualizes the "semantic misalignment" horn of the tokenization dilemma and provides evidence that the bottleneck in sequence-as-modality approaches is not in encoding biology but in translating between biological and linguistic semantic spaces.

## Suggestions

- The most impactful addition would be a context ablation experiment: test whether models can answer functional questions when the context provides only structural/domain descriptions without explicit GO terms or functional keywords. This would directly address whether the LLM is reasoning or reading.
- For the sequence-as-noise claim, compute per-example score differences between context-only and sequence+context conditions, categorize by protein properties (sequence length, domain complexity), and report confidence intervals to assess whether the effect is systematic or concentrated in edge cases.
- Validate the LLM-Score judge against even a modest set of human expert annotations and report correlation and any systematic judge biases.
