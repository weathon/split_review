## Summary

DeCodec proposes a neural audio codec that disentangles speech and background sound in the representation domain via a Subspace Orthogonal Projection (SOP) module and a Representation Swap Training (RST) procedure, with additional semantic-paralinguistic decomposition within the speech branch via Semantic Guidance (SG). The architecture is cleanly motivated by human auditory processing, and the paper demonstrates the model's utility across reconstruction, speech enhancement, one-shot voice conversion, and downstream ASR/TTS tasks—all from a single codec front-end.

## Strengths

- **SOP+RST joint design is necessary and sufficient for decoupling, validated by ablation.** Table 4 cleanly shows that neither SOP alone (SDR‑B = –13.15 dB) nor RST alone (SDR‑B = –10.67 dB) achieves decoupling, but their combination (Ablation‑3) yields SDR‑B = 0.49 dB and SDR‑S = 7.90 dB. This is a well-structured ablation that directly supports the paper's core architectural claim.

- **Competitive speech enhancement from a codec, without a dedicated SE module.** On the DNS Challenge Without‑Reverb set, DeCodec (non‑causal) achieves OVL=3.39, SIG=3.64, BAK=4.13, surpassing dedicated SE models including SELM (3.26, 3.51, 4.10) and StoRM (3.21, 3.51, 3.94). The fact that this emerges from representation recombination rather than time‑domain separation is a genuine advantage of the disentanglement approach.

- **Improved one-shot VC on noisy speech relative to cascaded denoising+tokenization.** DeCodec achieves WER=50.46% and SIM=0.83 on noisy speech, compared to StoRM‑SpeechTokenizer's 52.73%/0.83 (Table 3). The lower WER suggests that representation‑domain decoupling introduces less distortion than cascaded time‑domain separation followed by separate tokenization.

- **Hierarchical disentanglement (speech‑BGS + semantic‑paralinguistic) within a single model.** Adding SG to SOP+RST reduces downstream ASR WER* from 41.9% (Ablation‑3) to 25.8% (DeCodec‑c), showing that the semantic‑paralinguistic decomposition is effective even under noisy conditions.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Reconstruction comparison is confounded by bitrate differences that are not discussed.** DeCodec operates at 4.0+4.0 = 8 kbps total (sum of both parallel RVQ branches), while baselines use lower bitrates: EnCodec 6 kbps, HiFi‑Codec 2 kbps, DAC 4.5 kbps, SpeechTokenizer 4 kbps. Higher bitrates mechanically yield higher SDR, so the claim that "DeCodec achieves the highest SDR for speech reconstruction" (Sec. 4.2.1) is partially an artifact of a larger bitrate budget. The paper does not acknowledge this or provide controlled comparisons (e.g., a version of DeCodec at 6 kbps total, or an ablation showing the decoupling cost at matched bitrate). This does not invalidate the decoupling contribution—DeCodec at 8 kbps with decoupling is still a meaningful achievement—but the reconstruction numbers as presented cannot be interpreted in isolation without bitrate control.

2. **The core decoupling metric (SDR‑B / SDR‑S) is underspecified.** The paper states these are "signal distortion ratios for decoupled background sound and decoupled speech" but never explains *how* the "decoupled" signals are obtained—are they generated by decoding only the BRVQ branch (with SRVQ zeroed), or some other procedure? Without this detail, the numbers in Table 4 cannot be independently interpreted or reproduced. Furthermore, SDR‑B for the full DeCodec is –0.36 dB (non‑causal) and –1.11 dB (causal), meaning the decoded background signal is worse than silence. While Ablation‑3 (SOP+RST without SG) achieves positive SDR‑B (0.49 dB), the full model's negative SDR‑B shows that SG comes at a measurable cost to background fidelity. The paper should clarify the computation procedure and report standard source separation metrics (SI‑SDRi) for both components.

### Minor

3. **The theoretical "proof" of RST decoupling (Sec. 3.6) is not rigorous and should be reframed.** The argument uses the mean value theorem for vector functions applied to a deep neural network decoder, which is not mathematically valid: the existence of ξ where a linear approximation holds does not force independent additive decomposition across the decoder's entire domain. The conclusion that "Zs1 must be independent of n1" does not follow from the premises presented. The authors should either remove or substantially weaken this claim, presenting it as intuitive motivation rather than a formal proof. The empirical results (ablation study, SE, VC) are the real evidence for decoupling.

4. **The SE comparison may have a training-data confound, though the evidence is inconclusive.** DeCodec is trained on mixtures with DNS‑Noise and tested on the DNS Challenge test set. The SE baselines' results are taken from their papers, which may have been trained on different data distributions. However, the DNS Challenge provides standardized training data that most SE baselines also used, so the confound is not clearly one-sided. DeCodec also performs competitively on the "Real Recordings" subset (OVL=3.13, SIG=3.45, BAK=3.99), where domain match is less of a concern. This issue warrants caution but does not undermine the results.

5. **Model size, FLOPs, and number of quantizers per branch are not reported.** The paper lists bitrate as 4.0+4.0 kbps but does not state the number of RVQ layers per branch (K_s, K_n, K_r) or the codebook size explicitly. These details are needed for reproducibility and practical deployment assessment.
