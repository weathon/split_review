## Summary

TbLTA proposes the first weakly-supervised framework for dense long-term action anticipation (LTA) that uses only video transcripts — ordered action lists without timing or duration information — during training, eliminating the need for expensive frame-level annotations. The architecture combines a temporal alignment module (ATBA, from prior work) to generate frame-level pseudo-labels with cross-modal attention for semantically grounding video features, a CTC loss for transcript-level supervision, and a CRF-augmented decoder. On the Breakfast benchmark the deterministic variant achieves 29.03 avg MoC, outperforming the best fully supervised method (ActFusion, 28.45); on 50Salads the stochastic variant matches fully supervised performance (28.51 Top-1 vs. ActFusion 28.39).

## Strengths

- **First weakly-supervised LTA model trained exclusively on video transcripts.** The paper's core claim — that dense LTA is feasible using only ordered action lists without any frame-level timing, boundaries, or durations — is genuinely novel and well-supported. Section 3 clearly defines the problem setting, and Section 4.2 demonstrates competitive results that make this a new benchmark direction. This is the paper's strongest contribution.

- **Competitive or superior performance on Breakfast vs. fully supervised methods.** Table 1 shows TbLTA (deterministic) achieves 29.03 avg MoC vs. the best supervised method ActFusion at 28.45 on Breakfast; at Obs 30% / 10% horizon it reaches 40.28 vs. 35.79 (ActFusion). This directly supports the claim that transcript-only supervision can match dense annotation.

- **Stochastic variant matches supervised methods on 50Salads.** TbLTA* Top1 reaches 28.51 avg MoC on 50Salads, matching ActFusion's 28.39, showing generalization to a dataset with denser action distributions and weaker temporal regularities.

- **Ablation studies confirm individual component contributions.** Section 4.3 shows that removing CTC supervision degrades performance by ~0.6-0.8 points; removing cross-attention drops average MoC by 1.3 points (50Salads) and 5.7 points (Breakfast); removing CRF causes 5.3-point drops on 50Salads and 4.1-point drops on Breakfast at longer horizons. These ablations are clean and informatively designed.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Ablation experiments are conducted on the stochastic (Top-1) variant, while the main comparisons use the deterministic model.**  
  *Verification:* The TbLTA row in Table 4 (ablation) reports 33.8 for 50Salads Obs 20% 10%, matching the stochastic Top-1 entry in Table 1 (33.76), not the deterministic entry (24.90). The paper explains this choice (Section 4.3: "For clarity, we adopt this choice Top-1 MoC for ablations as it provides a stable reference point"), and the component contributions are likely similar across variants. Nevertheless, the evidence for which components are critical for the deterministic model — the one compared against fully supervised methods — is indirect, weakening the support for the headline conclusions. The authors should either re-run ablations on the deterministic model or provide a stronger justification for why the stochastic proxy is sufficient.

- **Pseudo-label quality is never quantitatively analyzed.** The entire training pipeline depends on pseudo-labels generated by ATBA (Section 3.1), and the cross-modal attention mask is built from these pseudo-labels (Section 3.1, Eq. 1-2). Yet the paper provides no evaluation of pseudo-label accuracy (e.g., frame-wise agreement with held-out ground truth, boundary quality). Without this, it is unclear whether the model succeeds because pseudo-labels are accurate or despite their noise. Given that poor pseudo-labels could misguide the attention mechanism (creating a circular dependency), the lack of analysis is a significant gap.

### Minor

- **"Self-supervised" claim for the duration loss is overstated.** Section 3.2.3 describes the duration loss as "self-supervised," but the class-wise priors $\hat{d}$ are computed from observed-segment pseudo-labels generated by ATBA, which are themselves noisy. This is more accurately described as a weak supervision signal derived from the alignment module, not a self-supervised objective. The dependency on pseudo-label quality should be discussed.

- **Hyperparameters $\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \gamma_3$ (loss weights) are not reported in the main paper.** These weight the alignment, CTC, and duration losses (Section 3.2.1-3.2.3). While they may appear in the supplementary (stripped by the parser), at least the chosen values or selection procedure should be stated in the main text.

- **Evaluation is limited to kitchen activity datasets.** Breakfast, 50Salads, and EGTEA are all kitchen-centered, highly procedural activities with strong temporal structure. The paper would benefit from at least a discussion of how the approach might generalize to less structured scenarios, where transcript-based alignment is more challenging.

- **ATBA is adopted from prior work with minimal adaptation.** The paper is transparent about this (Section 3.1: "we adopt the ATBA module proposed in Xu & Zheng (2024)"), and the novelty lies in the overall framework rather than the alignment component itself. However, the writing could more clearly delineate which components are novel (cross-modal attention, multi-loss combination for LTA) vs. reused.
