## Summary

This paper proposes Critique-RL, a two-stage reinforcement learning approach for training language models to critique (assess and provide feedback on) model outputs, without relying on human annotation or commercial API supervision. The key innovation is identifying that standard RL training with indirect reward signals (based on actor refinement) fails to optimize the critic's discriminability (ability to judge correctness), causing either overly conservative or overly aggressive behavior. Critique-RL's Stage I directly optimizes discriminability via rule-based correctness verification rewards, and Stage II optimizes helpfulness while regularizing to preserve discriminability. Experiments on Qwen2.5-3B/7B across math reasoning benchmarks show consistent and sometimes large gains over SFT, STaR, Retroformer, and CTRL baselines.

## Strengths

- **Insightful motivating analysis of discriminability collapse.** Section 4.1 and Figure 3 provide a clear diagnosis: indirect reward signals (r_refine, r_correction, r_Δ) fail to jointly optimize the critic's ability to judge both correct and incorrect responses, leading to conservative or aggressive behavior patterns. This analysis is independently valuable and convincingly motivates the two-stage design.

- **Consistent and substantial gains across all benchmarks and model sizes.** Table 1 shows Critique-RL achieves the highest Acc@Refine and Acc@Dis on all three in-domain datasets (MATH, GSM8K, AQuA) for both 3B and 7B models. The gains are large (e.g., 58.40% vs. 54.06% on MATH-7B for Acc; 85.20% vs. 71.42% for Acc@Dis), and the pattern holds across all nine task–model combinations.

- **Ablation studies cleanly validate the two-stage design.** Table 3 demonstrates that removing Stage I or Stage II degrades performance, and critically, removing the discriminability regularization (r_dis + KL) in Stage II causes a sharp drop in Acc@Dis (82.8→77.7 on MATH-3B), confirming that the regularization is necessary to prevent discriminability collapse during helpfulness optimization.

- **Iterative training and inference-time compute scaling show practical benefits.** Table 2 shows a second iteration of two-stage training further improves performance (48.6→51.0 Acc on MATH-3B). Figure 1 shows Critique-RL improves the performance ceiling with more inference samples and is more compute-efficient than parallel response sampling.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The evaluation scope is too narrow to support the broad claims about "scalable oversight" and "generalization to unseen tasks."** All main experiments are on math reasoning (MATH, GSM8K, AQuA). The OOD tasks (SVAMP, TheoremQA) are also math reasoning datasets. A claim about scalable oversight — a problem spanning reasoning, coding, factuality, open-ended generation — needs evidence from at least one diverse task family beyond math. The summarization experiment (CNN/DailyMail) is relegated to the appendix and lacks clarity on how rule-based rewards apply there. The paper's claims should either be tempered (e.g., "generalization to unseen math tasks") or supported with non-math evaluations (e.g., coding with unit tests, or fact verification).

- **The "without stronger labeling" framing is imprecise and risks misleading readers.** The paper states it trains critics "without stronger labeling" (abstract, introduction), but the SFT initialization uses critique data generated by Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct — an instruction-tuned model that is indeed stronger than the base Qwen2.5-3B model the critic is initialized from. The paper does acknowledge this choice is *rather than using GPT-4o* (Section 4.1), which is a valid and useful distinction from prior work. However, the abstract-level claim should be more precise (e.g., "without human annotation or commercial model supervision") to avoid the appearance of claiming the method is fully self-supervised.

### Minor

- **No variance or statistical significance measures are reported.** All main results (Tables 1, 2, 4) are reported as single values with no error bars, confidence intervals, or multi-seed runs. While the evaluation uses temperature=0 for greedy decoding and fixed test sets, the RL training itself has stochasticity across seeds. Reporting standard deviations over at least 3 seeds for one key configuration would substantially improve confidence in the reported gains.

- **The judgment extraction mechanism (function \(f(x,y,c)\) in Eq. 7) is underspecified.** The paper states that critics "include correctness judgments for each step and also provide a judgment for the final answer" (Section 4.2) but does not describe the parsing procedure to extract \(f(x,y,c)\) from natural language. If extraction relies on fragile regex or formatting conventions, the method may not transfer reliably. The prompt format and parsing details should be specified.

- **No discussion of total training cost (GPU-hours) compared to baselines.** The two-stage RL requires sequential training phases, which imposes additional computational cost. Reporting training wall-time or GPU-hour comparisons would help practitioners assess the trade-off.

### Trivial

- In Figure 1's legend, "w/o Critique-RL (3B)" appears duplicated, and the figure is difficult to read at its printed size.
- The hyperparameter \(\beta_2\) (KL coefficient in Stage II) is introduced in Eq. 9 and Algorithm 1 but its value is not specified in Section 5.1 where \(\beta_1=0.2\) is given.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An analysis of what kinds of judgment errors the trained critic still makes (e.g., breakdown by problem difficulty or answer magnitude) would strengthen the claim that discriminability training genuinely improves calibration rather than just boosting aggregate accuracy.
- A direct evaluation showing that the SFT initialization could be replaced with weaker (e.g., base-model self-generated) critique data without collapsing performance would strengthen the "no stronger labeling" narrative.
- Comparing against self-critiquing approaches (Self-Refine, Critic-CoT) in the main paper rather than only in the appendix would help readers understand when a separate critic is preferable to a single-model self-critique setup.

## Suggestions

1. **Broaden evaluation scope.** Add at least one non-math reasoning task (e.g., coding with unit-test-based correctness, or fact verification) to support the scalable-oversight framing. This is the most impactful change the authors could make.

2. **Reframe the contribution honestly.** Replace "without stronger labeling" with more precise language such as "without human annotation or commercial API supervision." This removes the tension between the abstract and the actual procedure (which uses Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct for SFT data).

3. **Report error bars.** Run at least 3 seeds for one key configuration (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B on MATH) and report standard deviations alongside the point estimates.

4. **Specify the judgment extraction procedure.** Publish the exact prompt format used to elicit step-by-step correctness judgments and the parsing logic for \(f(x,y,c)\).

5. **Specify \(\beta_2\) value.** Include the Stage II KL coefficient in Section 5.1 alongside the \(\beta_1=0.2\) specification.
