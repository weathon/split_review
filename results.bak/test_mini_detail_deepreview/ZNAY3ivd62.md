## Summary
2-3 sentence summary of the paper's contribution.

The paper introduces GUI-Spotlight, a visual grounding model that iteratively invokes three specialized tools (crop, extract, find_color) to progressively narrow focus on GUI elements. The model is trained via a three-stage pipeline (SFT → RL with a modified GSPO objective → high-resolution refinement) using only 18.5K curated samples, achieving 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro — surpassing all prior 7B models.

## Strengths

- **State-of-the-art accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro with high data efficiency.** GUI-Spotlight (init. UI-TARS-1.5-7B) achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, surpassing prior 7B models (best: UI-Venus-7B 50.8%, V2P-7B 50.6%) using only 18.5K training samples — orders of magnitude fewer than competing methods (e.g., UGround 10M, V2P 9.6M). This is shown in Table 3 with per-domain breakdowns.

- **Stabilized multi-tool RL training that prevents collapse.** The modified GSPO objective with an auxiliary token-level cross-entropy loss (Equation 1–3) demonstrably prevents the format-violation collapse that plagues vanilla GRPO and GSP0. Figure 3 (right panel) shows that while baselines oscillate and degrade after ~300 steps, the proposed method maintains steady improvement throughout.

- **Ablation isolating the benefit of the learned policy over iterative cropping.** Section 5.4 explicitly compares GUI-Spotlight (52.8%) against a training-free repeated single-turn inference baseline (47.6%) and a multi-turn conversational baseline (7.6%), confirming that the gain is not merely from iterative access to the image but from the learned policy.

- **Comprehensive documentation of negative results.** Sections 4.1 and 4.2 systematically catalog 7 RL variants tried and discarded, along with reward design experiments (Figure 4). This transparency about failed attempts is rare and provides practical guidance for future work on tool-augmented grounding.

- **Generality across backbone models.** GUI-Spotlight is evaluated from both UI-TARS-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B initializations. The Qwen variant gains +11.9 points on ScreenSpot-Pro (26.8% → 38.7%) over its raw baseline, demonstrating transfer beyond UI-specialized backbones (Tables 3 and 4).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Factual overclaim on UI-Vision benchmark.** The abstract states that GUI-Spotlight achieves "23.4% on UI-Vision, substantially outperforming comparable 7B baselines." However, Table 4 shows UI-Venus-Ground-7B achieves 26.5% — *higher* than GUI-Spotlight's 23.4%. The text in Section 5.2 similarly states "outperforming other 7B models," which is incorrect. This overclaim undermines credibility and must be corrected. The claim on ScreenSpot-Pro (where GUI-Spotlight genuinely does outperform all 7B models) should be clearly separated.

- **Headline comparisons conflate iterative inference with the learned policy.** Table 3 compares GUI-Spotlight (multi-step iterative inference) against models that produce a single coordinate in one pass. The paper does include an ablation (Section 5.4) showing a repeated single-turn iterative baseline at 47.6% versus 52.8%, which partially addresses this. However, the main results table and abstract present the 52.8% figure without acknowledging this asymmetry or including iterative versions of the baselines. The reader cannot tell how much of the 2.2-point gain over V2P-7B (52.8 vs. 50.6) comes from the multi-step process vs. the learned strategy.

- **Stage 1 SFT collapse is not adequately explained.** Figure 2 shows ScreenSpot-Pro accuracy dropping from 39.3% (base model) to 17.8% after Stage 1 SFT. The paper's explanation is that the model is "under-aligned" — but a training pipeline that first destroys the model's grounding ability and relies on RL to recover is fragile. The paper does not investigate whether a different warm-up (e.g., lower learning rate, more data, skipping SFT entirely, or a smaller loss weight on tool-syntax tokens) could avoid this collapse. While not fatal (RL does recover), this unexplained fragility weakens the claimed robustness of the training pipeline.

### Minor

- **Marginal gain over simple iterative cropping is modest without analysis.** The repeated single-turn baseline (Section 5.4) achieves 47.6% vs. GUI-Spotlight's 52.8%, a 5.2-point absolute gain. However, the paper does not report statistical significance, provide per-case breakdowns of where the learned tool policy succeeds over the simple crop-and-recenter baseline, or analyze tool usage frequency/success rates. Without such analysis, the marginal contribution of the learned multi-tool coordination (beyond the iterative structure) remains unclear.

- **Data efficiency claim omits the 72B teacher cost.** The paper emphasizes that training uses only 18.5K samples, but the Stage 1 SFT trajectories were generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B. The compute and supervision cost of that teacher is not factored into the efficiency narrative. This does not invalidate the claim, but it should be acknowledged as a practical consideration for reproducing the approach.

- **On OSWorld-G, the improvement over the backbone is minimal.** GUI-Spotlight (UI-TARS) improves only 0.8 points over UI-TARS-1.5-7B (61.9% → 62.7%), and several 7B models outperform it (GTA1-7B at 67.7%, UI-Venus-Ground-7B at 58.8%). The paper does not discuss why the method's advantage is less apparent on this benchmark.

### Trivial

- The table in Figure 2 has a stage-numbering inconsistency: Stage 0 is listed with 2561 training samples, but Stage 0 should be the base model with 0 training samples, while Stage 1 (SFT) uses 2561 trajectories.
