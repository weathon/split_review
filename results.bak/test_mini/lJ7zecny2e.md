## Summary

This paper introduces Geo-CoT, a framework for perceptually-grounded chain-of-thought reasoning in remote sensing VLMs. The authors construct Geo-CoT380k (384k structured rationales generated by GPT-4V conditioned on ground-truth annotations), and train RSThinker via two-stage alignment (SFT + GRPO). The model outputs both a final answer and a verifiable reasoning trace with explicit spatial references. Experiments span visual grounding, counting, detection, classification, VQA, and captioning across many benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **Large-scale structured CoT dataset as a community resource**: Geo-CoT380k (384,591 samples) is the first large-scale SFT dataset purpose-built for remote sensing chain-of-thought. The pipeline conditions GPT-4V on verified bounding boxes and captions (Section 3.2), yielding diverse task coverage (Table 1). This provides a concrete substrate for future work on geospatial reasoning.

- **Clean ablation isolating the CoT contribution**: Table 8 systematically decomposes the gain chain: Base → SFT w/o CoT → SFT w/ CoT → SFT w/ CoT + GRPO. The CoT structure itself adds ~6 mIoU on visual grounding, ~0.29 MAE on counting, and ~25 mAP@0.5 on detection over plain task-specific SFT. The KL-regularization analysis (Figure 4) further shows that the KL penalty prevents format collapse during GRPO — a concrete design insight.

- **Impressive zero-shot generalization on out-of-distribution benchmarks**: RSThinker achieves dominant margins on tasks it was not trained on (RSOD counting Acc=95.5 vs next best 51.5; NWPU-VHR Acc=80.0 vs next best 63.5; RRSIS-D @0.5=94.0 vs next best 72.5). These results (Tables 4–5) demonstrate genuine generalization that cannot be explained by in-domain supervision alone.

- **Comprehensive evaluation breadth**: The comparison spans 12+ models (closed-source: Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT-5; open-source: Qwen2.5-VL, MiniGPT-v2; reasoning-specialized: GLM-4.1V-Thinking; RS-specific: GeoChat, VHM, SkySenseGPT, EarthDial) across 5 task families with multiple benchmarks each.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Misleading SOTA comparisons conflate task-specific training with the CoT contribution**: RSThinker is trained on the training splits of several evaluation benchmarks (VRSBench-train-VG, DIOR-RSVG-train, DOTAv2-train, HRRSD-train, etc.) while most baselines — particularly GLM-4.1V-Thinking, which shares the same base architecture — are evaluated off-the-shelf without equivalent in-domain training. From the ablation (Table 8), task-specific SFT w/o CoT alone lifts VG mIoU from 56.26→81.80, while the CoT structure adds a further 81.80→87.70. This means the headline 26-point gap over GLM-4.1V-Thinking on VRSBench-VG is overwhelmingly explained by domain-specific supervised training, not by the Geo-CoT framework. The paper never acknowledges this asymmetry and presents "state-of-the-art" as an unqualified claim. The ablations provide internal validity, but the external comparisons are presented in a way that inflates the perceived contribution of the reasoning architecture.

- **"Faithful reasoning" is asserted but not demonstrated**: The paper's central framing claims RSThinker produces a *faithful* reasoning process, but the evidence supports only *verifiability* (traces can be checked against pixels). The GRPO stage rewards only final answer correctness (Table 3), not the trace's causal role or consistency. The failure case in Figure 7 explicitly shows the model generating a structurally perfect reasoning trace while making an error (misidentifying a dock extension as a ship) — the paper phrases this as a "safety feature" but it actually confirms the trace can be a post-hoc rationalization rather than the causal reasoning process. No intervention experiments (perturbing the trace and checking if the answer changes), no human evaluation of trace correctness, and no analysis of whether traces causally drive outputs are provided. The term "faithful" should be replaced with "verifiable" or the paper should provide direct evidence of causal faithfulness.

### Minor

- **No quality validation of the Geo-CoT380k dataset**: The paper presents Geo-CoT380k as a core contribution but provides zero human evaluation of the generated rationales (correctness, completeness, naturalness), no automated consistency checks (e.g., do bounding boxes actually match objects in the image? are counts consistent with ground truth?), and no analysis of diversity or potential GPT-4V biases. The paper acknowledges stylistic biases in the conclusion but does not quantify them. Given that the SFT stage instills the reasoning structure from this data, its quality is foundational to the approach.

- **No statistical significance or variance reporting**: All results in Tables 4–7 and the ablation (Table 8) are reported as single numbers without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or multiple runs. Given the large number of comparisons, some margins may be within noise.

- **Prediction extraction from baselines not described**: It is unclear how bounding boxes, counts, and detection outputs were obtained from models that may not natively produce structured predictions. Parsing failures or prompt engineering differences could artificially lower baseline scores.

### Trivial

- Figure labels have minor formatting artifacts (e.g., "Claude-some4", "VHIM", "SkSenceGPT" in Table 6) that appear to be PDF extraction issues.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Controlled experiment with finetuned baselines**: Finetuning a strong open-source baseline (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL or GLM-4.1V-Base) on the same task-specific data as RSThinker, with the same compute budget, would cleanly isolate the CoT contribution from domain adaptation. This is the single most impactful experiment the authors could add.

- **Faithfulness evaluation**: A human study where annotators judge whether the reasoning trace correctly justifies the final answer, or intervention experiments (perturbing traces and checking answer sensitivity), would substantiate the faithfulness claim.

- **Geo-CoT380k quality audit**: Human verification of a sample of 200–500 rationales for correctness, object coverage, and diversity would strengthen confidence in the SFT substrate.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting pattern across the reviews is that the paper's strongest evidence — the ablation study showing CoT rationales provide additional gains on top of task-specific SFT — is at odds with its own framing. The paper leads with dramatic SOTA comparisons (25+ point gaps on visual grounding) but the actual CoT-specific gain is ~6 points on VG mIoU and ~2 mAP@0.5 on detection. This suggests the authors are underselling their more modest but genuine contribution (structured reasoning benefits on top of supervised training) while overclaiming through an apples-to-oranges comparison. The KL-regularization analysis (Figure 4) is a genuinely useful design insight that deserves more prominence. The zero-shot results on RSOD and NWPU-VHR are the paper's most impressive finding, showing that the framework's structured reasoning transfers to unseen distributions.

## Suggestions

1. Rewrite the SOTA claims with explicit caveats about training data asymmetry. Headline the internal ablation comparison (SFT w/o CoT vs. SFT w/ CoT) as the primary evidence for the CoT contribution.
2. Replace "faithful reasoning" with "verifiable reasoning" throughout the paper, or add experimental evidence of causal faithfulness (intervention studies or human evaluation of trace correctness).
3. Add confidence intervals / standard deviations to main results (at minimum for the ablation study).
4. Include a human evaluation of 200–500 Geo-CoT380k samples to validate dataset quality.
5. Precisely describe how baseline predictions were extracted (prompt format, parsing logic) for each task family.
