## Summary

This paper introduces DefNTaxS, an automated, training-free framework that uses LLMs to cluster class labels into semantically coherent subcategories and then augments CLIP zero-shot classification prompts with taxonomic context (e.g., "turkey, which has dark wings, a species of farm bird"). Evaluated across seven standard benchmarks, DefNTaxS achieves +5.5% average accuracy over vanilla CLIP and +2.4% over D-CLIP, with a particularly large gain on EuroSAT (+13.0%). The total LLM API cost across all datasets is $0.38.

## Strengths

- **LLM-driven clustering consistently beats k-means (Table 5).** Across all seven benchmarks, the LLM-based subcategory assignment outperforms k-means clustering on CLIP text embeddings, with an average gain of +0.92%. EuroSAT shows the largest benefit (+3.19%). This is concrete evidence that the LLM's semantic understanding provides genuine disambiguation advantage over a simple embedding-distance baseline.

- **Thorough and well-structured ablation studies.** Tables 3, 4, and 5 systematically isolate different components: taxonomic descriptors vs. no descriptors (Table 3), random subcategory labels vs. random descriptors (Table 4), and LLM vs. k-means clustering (Table 5). The ablations are run over 5 iterations with standard errors reported in Table 4, which is more rigorous than many papers in this area. The investigation of whether gains come from semantic content or structural differentiation is a genuine attempt to understand the method.

- **Extremely low computational cost.** Total GPT-4o-mini cost of $0.38 for generating all prompts across all seven benchmarks is a practical strength that the paper correctly highlights. The method requires no model retraining, no training data, and no manual prompt engineering.

- **Competitive performance with a consistent pattern.** DefNTaxS achieves the highest accuracy on 5 of 7 benchmarks, with the average (61.17%) notably ahead of the next-best method (D-CLIP, 58.13%). The consistent improvement across diverse domains (satellite imagery, textures, pets, fine-grained birds) suggests the approach has genuine generality.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **WaffleTaxS ablation (Table 4) undermines the paper's strongest claim about "essential" semantic taxonomy.** The paper repeatedly asserts that taxonomic *semantic content* is "essential" (Abstract, Section 5 intro, Conclusion). However, Table 4 shows that WaffleTaxS — which replaces subcategory labels with *random characters* while preserving the structural differentiation — achieves comparable or better accuracy on ImageNet (+0.28% over DefNTaxS) and Places365 (+0.71%), and is competitive on others. The paper acknowledges this ("differentiation alone has an effect") but does not reconcile it with the central narrative. If random characters work nearly as well on several benchmarks, the claim that taxonomic *semantics* are the key missing ingredient is not supported. The paper would be stronger with a more measured claim: that the subcategory *structure* (even non-semantic) provides substantial benefit, and semantic labels add further gains on some datasets.

- **Main results (Table 1) report single-run point estimates without error bars.** The paper's own 5-run experiments in Table 4 show that DefNTaxS has a standard error of ±0.26 on ImageNet (mean 62.96 across 5 runs), while the Table 1 value is 63.48 — a single run. Without variance information for the main results table, it is impossible to assess whether small margins over baselines (e.g., +0.16 on ImageNet over CGPT-P, +0.08 on DTD over CGPT-P) reflect genuine improvement or run-to-run noise. On Food101 and Places365, where CHiLS outperforms DefNTaxS, error bars would clarify whether these are significant gaps or within noise.

- **Factual error in reporting.** The paper claims that "Table 1 shows DefNTaxS achieving the highest accuracy across six of seven benchmarks." In fact, Table 1 shows DefNTaxS is outperformed by CHiLS on Food101 (83.53 vs. 81.48) and Places365 (40.45 vs. 40.00), making it highest on 5 of 7. This overstatement should be corrected.

### Minor

- **Baseline re-implementation details are ambiguous regarding the LLM used.** Section 4.1 states that descriptors are generated using "a modified version of D-CLIP's generation pipeline... due to the deprecation of OpenAI's GPT-3 API." Section 4.3 says baselines were "recreated using the setup described in 4.1." This suggests all baselines (including D-CLIP) were re-run with GPT-4o-mini under the same descriptor pipeline, which would make the comparisons fair. However, the paper does not state this explicitly. WaffleCLIP+Conc. is specifically noted as using "a high-level semantic concept generated by GPT-3" — if that baseline's concept was generated with GPT-3 while others used GPT-4o-mini, the comparison is confounded. The authors should clarify the exact LLM used for each baseline.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting finding to emerge from the reviews is the tension the paper itself uncovers but does not fully resolve: the WaffleTaxS result (random characters perform competitively) echoes and extends the WaffleCLIP finding from Roth et al. (2023). Together, these suggest that CLIP's zero-shot performance benefits disproportionately from *textual differentiation structure* (having distinct prompts for each class that are easy to tell apart in embedding space), with actual semantic content providing a secondary, dataset-dependent boost. This reframes the paper's contribution: rather than demonstrating that taxonomic *semantics* are essential, the paper shows that any structured, class-grouping prompt template helps, and that LLM-generated semantic labels provide an additional (modest, dataset-specific) signal. This is a more nuanced but still useful finding.

## Suggestions

1. **Correct the factual error** ("six of seven" → "five of seven") and **tone down the language** around "essential" and "paradigm shift." The evidence supports taxonomic context as *helpful* with dataset-dependent benefits, not as strictly essential.
2. **Add error bars to the main results table** (Table 1), at least for DefNTaxS itself, using the 5-run data already collected for Table 4. This would allow readers to assess the significance of margins.
3. **Clarify the baseline re-implementation protocol** — explicitly state whether D-CLIP and other baselines were re-run with GPT-4o-mini using the same descriptor generation pipeline, and if so, include the exact prompt.
4. **Add the WaffleTaxS-vs-DefNTaxS comparison as a dedicated analysis** rather than burying it in Section 6.1.3. Its implications for the paper's central claim deserve prominence and a more thorough discussion.
