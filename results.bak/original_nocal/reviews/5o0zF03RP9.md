## Summary

This paper introduces IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL rewards with a KL-divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and preferred outcome distributions q(o|s). The claimed central novelty is a Bayesian scheme for adapting the incentive weight β online, eliminating manual tuning. Experiments on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey show improvements over vanilla RL (β=0). The paper also draws connections to the Free Energy Principle and dopamine-based reward prediction error.

## Strengths

- **Conceptually clean KL-based incentive framework.** The idea of aligning predicted and preferred outcome distributions via KL divergence is interpretable and provides a principled way to inject preferences into RL. The MiniGrid Doorkey experiment (Figure 2) shows a genuine improvement: β=0.01 achieves 98% success rate vs. 90.5% for β=0, with mean episode length dropping from 75 to 29 steps. This provides some evidence that the KL shaping signal can help in sparse-reward settings.

- **Theoretical guarantee that small β preserves optimality.** Proposition 1 (Section 4.1) formally shows that for sufficiently small β, the optimal policy under standard RL remains optimal under IncentRL. This is a basic continuity argument, but it provides a useful safety property for practitioners considering the method.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Central claimed novelty — Bayesian β adaptation — is never specified.** The paper repeatedly identifies "Bayesian adaptation of β eliminating manual tuning" as its core contribution (Abstract, bullet points, Conclusion). Yet the algorithm description in §5.1 describes only the reward-shaping procedure (predict outcomes, compute KL, shape reward, update policy). No prior distribution, likelihood function, inference method (MAP? MCMC? Variational?), or update rule for β is ever given. The paper says "we tracked the posterior evolution of β using our Bayesian adaptation scheme" (line 234) but never specifies what that scheme is. "Rounds" are mentioned in Figures 3–4 and Table 3 without definition or mapping to training timesteps. Since the Bayesian adaptation of β is the paper's primary claim to novelty over prior intrinsic motivation work, this omission is structural: the main claimed contribution cannot be evaluated, reproduced, or built upon.

2. **Infinite KL divergence in the toy experiment invalidates its results.** The toy MDP (§5.2) sets p(o|s₀,a₁) = {s₁:0.3, s₀:0.7} and q(o|s₀) = {s₁:1.0, s₀:0.0}. The forward KL divergence KL(p‖q) = 0.3·log(0.3/1.0) + 0.7·log(0.7/0.0) is infinite because q assigns zero probability to s₀ while p gives it 0.7 probability. Under the described reward r_shaped = r_ext − β·KL, the shaped reward for the only action leading to the goal would be −∞, completely deterring that action. The paper reports that "all β > 0 improve performance" (line 175), which is impossible under the stated computation. Either the actual computation differs from the description (reverse KL, smoothing, or different distribution support) or the results cannot be trusted. This is the paper's simplest empirical demonstration and it is unsupported as described.

3. **Internal inconsistency in Bayesian adaptation evidence.** The paper's text (line 297) claims the posterior mean "quickly concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)." However, the figure caption (lines 293–295) states the posterior mean "decreases to about 0.02 by Round 4." Table 3 confirms this: Round 4 mean β = 0.0173 (std 0.0182). This is not near 0.1 — it is an order of magnitude smaller and still drifting downward. The figure description and numerical table contradict the prose claim. This inconsistency undermines the empirical case for the Bayesian adaptation working as advertised.

4. **No comparison to existing intrinsic motivation methods.** The paper claims IncentRL "removes the need for manual trade-off tuning, a core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods" (Abstract) and compares only against β=0 (vanilla RL). No comparisons are made to curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al. 2017), RND (Burda et al. 2018), VIME (Houthooft et al. 2016), or other KL-regularized methods (SAC, PPO with KL penalties). Since a wide range of intrinsic motivation methods already improve over vanilla RL on sparse-reward tasks, the reader cannot judge whether IncentRL is competitive with or advances beyond existing approaches. This is particularly important given that the paper's β=0.01 result on MiniGrid (98% vs. 90.5%) uses a very small β, raising the question of whether simpler regularization schemes would achieve similar gains.

### Minor

- **Proposition 1 and 2 are standard continuity/limit arguments.** Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimality) follows from the continuity of J_β(π) in β in a finite MDP. Proposition 2 (β→∞ ignores external reward) is immediate from the definition. These do not constitute a meaningful theoretical contribution.
- **Low statistical power.** MountainCar and MiniGrid results use only 3 seeds. While not disqualifying, this limits the reliability of the reported improvements, especially given the high variance in MountainCar (e.g., β=0.3: 1654.67 ± 908.94).
- **Toy experiment uses ground-truth transition probabilities as p(o|s,a).** In practice, these would need to be learned, but the paper does not discuss how inaccuracies in the learned model would affect results.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the Bayesian β adaptation completely.** Provide the prior, likelihood, update rule (e.g., does a Beta-Bernoulli or Gamma-Poisson conjugate model underpin it?), and how β is actually used in the reward (posterior mean? posterior sample? per-episode? per-step?). Define what a "round" is and how it relates to training timesteps.
2. **Fix the toy experiment.** Either use the reverse KL (KL(q‖p), which is finite for the given distributions), add smoothing (e.g., ϵ=1e-8 to q), or choose q with full support. Report whether the conclusions change.
3. **Resolve the inconsistency in the Bayesian results.** Explain why the posterior mean converges to 0.017 rather than 0.1, and adjust the text to accurately reflect the data. If the effective β for MiniGrid is indeed ≈0.01, correct all references to β≈0.1.
4. **Add at least one strong intrinsic motivation baseline** (e.g., ICM or RND) to demonstrate that IncentRL provides benefits beyond existing methods, not just over vanilla RL.
5. **Increase number of seeds** to at least 5–10 and report confidence intervals for the main experimental results.
