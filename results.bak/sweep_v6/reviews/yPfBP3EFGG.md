## Summary

The paper proposes STNAdam, a stochastic optimizer for "nonconvex + weakly-convex" composite optimization problems. Its key innovation is a two-track iteration framework that maintains two coupled trajectories — an extrapolation track and a regular update track — governed by Nesterov momentum and Adam-style adaptive conditioning. The paper provides convergence analysis under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property and evaluates the method on a low-light image enhancement (LIE) task using the LOL dataset.

## Strengths

- **Novel two-track algorithmic framework.** Algorithm 1 introduces a genuine architectural novelty: two intertwined iteration trajectories that use an extrapolation point \(\bar{x}^{k+1}\) and a regular update \(x^{k+1}\) with different momentum-corrected gradient estimates. This departs from all single-track variants (Adam, NAdam) in a well-defined way, and Figure 1 provides a clear schematic of how the trajectories differ.

- **General convergence analysis under the KL property.** Sections 3 establishes finite-length summability (Lemma 2) and explicit convergence rates (Theorem 2: linear for KL exponent \(\vartheta \le 1/2\), sublinear for \(\vartheta > 1/2\)), covering the challenging "nonconvex + weakly-convex" composite setting. The theory accommodates any variance-reduced gradient estimator (SVRG, SAGA, SARAH) under a unified set of conditions (Lemma 1), which is more general than most Adam-variant analyses.

- **Strong empirical results on low-light image enhancement.** On the LOL dataset (Table 2), STNAdam-SARAH achieves PSNR 22.26, SSIM 0.906, LPIPS 0.050 — substantially outperforming all baselines including SNAdam (17.14, 0.795, 0.098) and Retinex-Net (18.44, 0.821, 0.079). The within-family comparison (SGD → SAdam → SNAdam → STNAdam-SGD → STNAdam-SAGA → STNAdam-SARAH) shows a clear and consistent improvement trend on the same optimization model (14), providing credible evidence that the optimizer design is beneficial.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **ℓ₁/₂ quasi-norm claimed as weakly convex, but it is not.** The paper states that the LIE model (14) can be cast into the form (1) with \(g\) containing \(h\|\nabla L\|_{1/2}^{1/2}\), and asserts in Section 1 that the ℓ₁/₂-norm is an example of a weakly-convex function (line 29). The ℓ₁/₂ quasi-norm \(\sum |x_i|^{1/2}\) is not weakly convex for any finite modulus — near zero its second derivative diverges to \(-\infty\), so adding any quadratic \(\frac{\tau}{2}\|x\|^2\) cannot make it convex. This means the experimental problem may not satisfy the theoretical assumptions under which convergence is proven, creating a fundamental disconnect between the theory and the empirical evaluation. This is the paper's most serious weakness because it undermines the integrity of the claimed contribution.

- **No ablation isolates the two-track mechanism.** The paper's central algorithmic novelty is the two-track structure, yet no experiment removes one track or replaces it with a single-track NAdam-style update while keeping all other algorithmic choices fixed. The comparison with SNAdam does not serve as an ablation because the methods differ in multiple dimensions (variance-reduced estimator integration, parameter scheduling, bias correction). Without an ablation, it is impossible to tell how much of the performance gain comes from the two-track design versus other components of the algorithm.

- **Single-run evaluation; no statistical significance.** All results in Tables 2–3 report a single run for each method. For stochastic algorithms, this is insufficient — the observed improvements could be driven by noise rather than algorithmic merit. Standard deviations over multiple independent runs are needed.

- **Limited experimental scope.** Evaluation is confined to a single task (LIE) on a single dataset (LOL). No experiments on other composite optimization problems (e.g., logistic regression with nonconvex penalties, neural network training, or other sparse recovery tasks) are provided, making it hard to assess generality.

### Minor

- **Almost-sure vs. in-expectation inconsistency.** The abstract claims "the sequence generated by STNAdam almost surely converges to a stationary point … at an explicit rate," but Theorem 1 states convergence "in expectation" and Theorem 2 gives rates in expectation. While Lemma 4 establishes some almost-sure properties (e.g., \(\sum\|\bar{x}^k-\bar{x}^{k-1}\|^2<\infty\) a.s.), the main convergence result is stated in expectation. These two modes of convergence are distinct, and the paper does not reconcile them. This needs clarification.

- **SAdam citation inconsistency.** In the experimental section (line 285), "SAdam" is attributed to Kingma & Ba (2014) — the original Adam paper — while the related work (line 37) correctly attributes SAdam to Wang et al. (2019) and Le-Duc et al. (2024). This raises questions about which baseline was actually run. The paper should clarify what SAdam refers to in the experiments.

- **Convergence theory does not cover STNAdam-SGD.** Lemma 1 defines variance-reduced gradient estimators with conditions including geometric decay (5). The paper explicitly states "SGD does not exhibit variance reduction" (line 128), yet STNAdam-SGD is included in experiments and claimed to converge. The theory in Section 3 therefore does not cover one of the three experimental variants.

- **Parameter intervals depend on unknown global constants.** The intervals (6)–(8) depend on \(L, \tau, V_1, V_2, V_\Upsilon, \rho\), whose values are typically unknown. The paper provides no practical procedure for setting these intervals in a real problem, and Remark 3 merely hand-waves that lower bounds can be "appropriately increased if necessary." This weakens the claim of "removing hand-tuning."

### Trivial

- **Implausible timing units.** The "Time(s)" column in Tables 2–3 shows values like \(2.64\times10^{-5}\) seconds (≈26 microseconds) per image. Even for a single iteration, this is implausibly fast for any algorithm involving gradient computation and proximal operators on full-resolution images. The unit or measurement method needs clarification.

## Removed Points

The paper includes a valid within-family comparison (SGD, SAdam, SNAdam, STNAdam variants — all on the same model (14)) that cleanly separates optimizer effects. The cross-family LIE comparison (NPE, DeHz, LIME, etc.) is an additional practical benchmark. The critic's framing of this as "structural" is overblown; the within-family comparison supports the optimizer claim.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic's focus on the ℓ₁/₂ norm not being weakly convex is a genuinely insightful catch that goes beyond the usual experimental nitpicks. Most Adam-variant papers would not flag this, yet it cuts directly to whether the empirical problem satisfies the paper's own theoretical assumptions. Similarly, the observation that no ablation exists for the two-track mechanism — the paper's signature contribution — is a substantive criticism that reveals a real gap between the claimed innovation and its validation. These two points together shift the assessment from "needs more experiments" to "the core contributions are not convincingly supported."

## Suggestions

1. **Address the ℓ₁/₂ issue directly.** Either provide a justification that the specific form used in (14) is weakly convex (perhaps via a smoothed surrogate), or replace it with a regularizer that provably satisfies weak convexity (e.g., MCP, SCAD, or a smooth approximation).
2. **Add an ablation that removes the second track.** Compare STNAdam-SGD against a variant where the extrapolation update \(\tilde{x}^{k+1}\) is replaced by the regular update \(x^{k+1}\) (or vice versa), keeping all other algorithmic choices identical.
3. **Report means and standard deviations** over at least 5 independent runs for all metrics.
4. **Evaluate on at least one additional optimization problem** (e.g., sparse logistic regression with MCP penalty, or training a small neural network) to demonstrate generality.
5. **Fix the almost-sure vs. in-expectation language** to be consistent throughout the paper, or clarify that the appendix contains the a.s. proof.
6. **Clarify the SAdam baseline**: state explicitly which paper's SAdam was implemented and cite it correctly.
