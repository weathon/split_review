## Summary

The paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework built entirely on SAM. It introduces two components working in synergy: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that iteratively samples prompts from a similarity-derived probability distribution, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS hybrid similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method is evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK, achieving competitive results among SAM-only approaches and particularly strong performance on CARPK (MAE 4.68, beating all listed training-based methods).

## Strengths

- **Novel use of SAM's output tokens for verification is a genuinely interesting and well-motivated idea.** The paper correctly identifies that prior training-free methods rely on image-embedding similarity with inherent uncertainty, while SAM's output tokens integrate semantic and positional information through the decoder's bidirectional attention. This architectural insight (Figure 1) is the paper's strongest conceptual contribution. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) provides qualitative evidence that output tokens separate positive/negative clusters more cleanly than image embeddings.

- **State-of-the-art among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147 (MAE 16.25).** Table 1 shows the method clearly outperforms TFOC (19.95), C-Anything (27.97), OmniCount (18.63), and TFCounter (18.56) — all methods that also use SAM without additional encoder models — while using no training.

- **Strong CARPK results: outperforms all supervised methods listed in Table 1.** The method achieves MAE 4.68 and RMSE 6.13 on CARPK, beating BMNet+ (5.76), SAFECOUNT (5.33), LOCA (9.97), and FamNet (18.19). This is the paper's strongest quantitative result and demonstrates practical viability in a consistent-viewpoint deployment scenario.

- **TS-SS hybrid metric is validated against standard alternatives.** Table 3 shows TS-SS (16.25 MAE) outperforms dot product (19.58), cosine similarity (16.48), and L2 distance (16.53), supporting the argument that output tokens require a metric that captures both angular and magnitude information.

- **Probabilistic prompt generation with iterative expansion reduces redundancy.** Table 4 shows a clear monotonic improvement as iterations and prompts per iteration increase, and the probability update mechanism (setting positive regions to zero, decreasing negative regions) is a sensible approach for coverage efficiency.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Abstract overclaims relative to the paper's own data.** The abstract states the method "achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." Table 1 contradicts this: on FSC-147, training-based methods BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), and LOCA (10.79) all achieve better MAE, as does the training-free TFCAC (12.26). The claim is only supported on CARPK, which is a single-domain dataset. This is not a minor exaggeration — it misrepresents the paper's standing relative to the SOTA. The conclusion correctly uses "competitive performance," which is the accurate framing; the abstract should match it. This weakens trust in the paper's self-assessment.

- **Computational efficiency claim is asserted without evidence.** The paper claims inference is "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC (Section 4.2) and that the method maintains "superior computational efficiency," but provides no runtime data for any competing method. The only runtime reported (1.69s per image on FSC-147) is for the method alone. Given the method runs SAM's decoder for an average of 1,438 prompts per image, the efficiency claim requires direct comparison. Without it, the efficiency argument is unsupported.

- **No statistical variance reported despite a fundamentally stochastic pipeline.** The method involves multinomial prompt sampling, mask-level NMS, and an early-stopping criterion — all stochastic elements. Every table reports a single number per metric with no error bars, standard deviations, or multiple-run statistics. Table 4 shows MAE varies from 16.25 to 29.10 depending on iteration/prompt parameters, so single-run reporting is insufficient. A reader cannot assess whether the reported 16.25 is stable or whether the claimed output-token advantage (16.25 vs. 16.94, Table 5) is reliable.

- **One-shot results are notably weaker, contradicting the "superior" narrative.** Table 2 shows the method achieves MAE 18.57 in the one-shot setting — worse than TFCAC (15.15) and the training-based LOCA (12.53). This setting is the more practical few-exemplar scenario, and the gap to TFCAC is substantial (18.57 vs. 15.15). The paper acknowledges this indirectly but does not adjust its overall narrative accordingly.

### Minor

- **The advantage of output tokens over image embeddings is modest and under-validated.** Table 5 reports MAE 16.25 (output token) vs. 16.94 (image embedding) — a ~4% relative improvement. Without error bars, this could be within noise. The paper does not compare against mask-pooled features (e.g., average-pooled image embeddings inside predicted masks), which would be the most direct ablation. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) is illustrative but not quantitative.

- **Several implementation details are missing or not ablated.** (a) The probability update rule after verification is described solely in prose (Section 3.1) — no equation or pseudocode. (b) The token verification threshold of 300 is stated but not justified or ablated. (c) The NMS IoU threshold is not specified. (d) The early-stopping parameters (IoU 0.95, patience 5) are not ablated. These gaps harm reproducibility.

- **Runtime of 1.69s per image uses ~1,438 prompts — this is not negligible for a "training-free" method.** The paper frames the method as efficient, but 1,438 SAM decoder forward passes per image is nontrivial. A comparison showing how this scales with image complexity would be valuable.

- **Exemplar handling for few-shot counting is underspecified.** For CARPK, the paper says "randomly selected 12 objects from the training set to use as exemplars" but does not state how multiple exemplar tokens are aggregated into a single verification criterion.

### Trivial

- The caption for Figure 5 has swapped column labels in the extracted text (the second column is described as showing image embedding results while the third column shows output token results, but the text says the opposite). This appears to be a parser artifact.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A direct comparison against a baseline that simply thresholds mask areas from a uniform grid (no verification) would help isolate whether the performance gain comes from better prompting or better verification.
- Reporting the method's performance on the "small object" subset of FSC-147 would address the acknowledged limitation about dense small instances.
- An ablation of the temperature parameter τ in Equation 1 would be informative.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation to emerge from this review is that the paper's conceptual contribution — using SAM's own output tokens as a discriminative criterion — is architecturally grounded and clearly motivated, yet its quantitative validation barely clears the bar. The ~4% gap between output tokens and image embeddings is so close to noise that it inadvertently raises a deeper question: if SAM's output tokens genuinely encode a richer representation (semantic + positional) as the paper argues, why is the empirical advantage so small? The PCA visualization suggests qualitative separation, but the quantitative gap does not match the claimed magnitude of benefit. This tension between architectural reasoning and empirical evidence is the paper's most limiting feature.

## Suggestions

1. **Realign the abstract's claims with the actual results.** Replace "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" with a qualified statement (e.g., "competitive with or better than several existing methods"). The CARPK result can be highlighted separately.
2. **Report means and standard deviations over 5+ runs** for the main results and the output-token vs. image-embedding comparison.
3. **Provide runtime comparisons** for TFCAC, TFOC, and C-Anything on the same hardware, including average prompts per image.
4. **Add the missing NMS IoU threshold** and **formalize the probability update rule** as an equation.
5. **Ablate the verification threshold (300) and early-stopping parameters** (IoU 0.95, patience 5) or justify them with validation evidence.
6. **Add a mask-pooled image embedding baseline** to Table 5 to isolate the benefit of output tokens over other SAM-derived representations.
