## Summary

MOCHA describes a planned curation of 10 publicly available multi-subject spatially resolved transcriptomics (SRT) datasets, each purportedly accompanied by pathologist-derived spatial domain annotations, gene expression matrices, spatial coordinates, and co-registered H&E images. The paper catalogs these cohorts (Table 1), shows basic molecular summary statistics (Figure 1), and describes a generic preprocessing pipeline. The motivating gap — the scarcity of multi-sample SRT resources with expert annotations — is real.

## Strengths

- **Cohort catalog (Table 1):** The paper enumerates 10 multi-subject SRT cohorts spanning diverse tissues (breast, colorectal, kidney, lung, brain, olfactory bulb) and platforms (10x Visium, ST), totaling 166 samples across multiple subjects. This provides a useful index for researchers seeking multi-sample SRT datasets, consolidating information that is currently scattered across individual publications.

- **Identifies a genuine gap:** The paper correctly identifies that existing repositories (SORC, Aquila, SODB, STOmicsDB, SpatialDB) lack systematically curated multi-subject datasets with pathologist annotations, a gap that constrains method development for multi-sample integration.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **No verifiable data access mechanism.** Despite claiming in the abstract that "MOCHA is released in formats readily usable with Python and R," the paper provides **no URL, no repository (Zenodo, GitHub, etc.), no DOI, and no license information**. For a dataset resource paper, the inability to access the contributed resource is disqualifying — the reader cannot verify that MOCHA exists, let alone assess its quality, format, or usability. A dataset paper without data access is an untestable description of a plan.

### Major

- **No validation or benchmark experiments.** The paper claims MOCHA is useful "for developing and evaluating multi-sample SRT methods" but presents **zero experiments** that demonstrate this utility — no method is run on the data, no evaluation metric (ARI, NMI, etc.) is reported, and no baseline results are provided. Without even a minimal demonstration (e.g., running BASS or STAGATE on one cohort and reporting agreement with pathologist labels), the reader cannot assess whether the resource works as intended or adds value beyond downloading the original datasets individually.

- **Annotation provenance is unclear.** The selection criteria (Section 2: "our selection criteria required each study to provide... cellular annotations delineated by a pathologist") indicate that annotations are **reused from the original studies**, not newly generated by MOCHA. However, the abstract and introduction phrase this as "expert pathologist annotations" in a way that suggests a new contribution. The paper never clarifies which annotations are newly produced vs. re-aggregated, and no inter-annotator statistics, domain size distributions, or annotation guidelines are provided. For DLPFC, which is known to have layer annotations from Maynard et al. (2021), the reader cannot tell whether MOCHA adds anything annotation-wise.

### Minor

- **Preprocessing section is generic.** Section 3 describes standard steps (library-size normalization, HVG selection, Harmony) that are textbook material and platform-agnostic. The "efficient storage formats for large-scale processing" promised in the abstract are never defined. No file format specification, data schema, or MOCHA-specific protocol is provided.

- **Lacks discussion of limitations.** The paper has no Discussion or Limitations section. Issues such as tissue skew toward cancer, the small size of some cohorts (e.g., KC.TLS has 3 samples from 3 subjects), variable spatial resolution across platforms, and the absence of single-cell resolution data are not acknowledged.

### Trivial

- Figure 2 is illustrated on KC_TLS_10x (Dawo et al., 2023), a publicly available dataset — it shows a standard pipeline, not a MOCHA contribution.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A data card or format specification (e.g., H5AD, AnnData structure) would help users understand what they would download.
