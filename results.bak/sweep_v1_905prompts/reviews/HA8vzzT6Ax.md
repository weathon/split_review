## Summary

This paper studies the interaction between watermarking and speculative sampling in LLM inference. It introduces a quantitative measure of watermark strength (expected KL divergence), characterizes the trade-off between strength and efficiency as a Pareto curve, and proposes Algorithm 1—a pseudorandom draft-token acceptance mechanism that provably achieves maximal watermark strength and maximal sampling efficiency simultaneously for degenerate watermarks (Theorem 4.1). Experiments on ELI5 with Llama and Gemma model pairs show maintained efficiency and improved detectability when using the acceptance variable for detection.

## Strengths

1. **Novel quantitative watermark-strength measure with statistical grounding.** Definition 3.1 defines watermark strength as expected KL divergence between watermarked and original distributions. Theorem 3.1 proves this quantity determines the exponential p-value decay rate of the UMP test, directly linking the measure to sample complexity. Theorem 3.2 shows the maximum is Ent(P), attained iff the watermarked distribution is degenerate. This is a genuine advance over the prior binary notion (Hu & Huang, 2024) that could not capture intermediate strengths.

2. **Formal trade-off characterization as a Pareto curve.** Definition 3.2 casts the trade-off as constrained optimization (Equation 8). This provides a principled framework for comparing watermarking schemes, and the explicit formulation for the linear class (Equation 10) and the visualized curves (Figure 1) concretely show where existing schemes sit relative to the theoretical optimum.

3. **Elegant algorithm with proven optimality.** Algorithm 1 is clearly presented and theoretically justified. Theorem 4.1 proves that replacing the random acceptance coin with a pseudorandom function (line 8) simultaneously achieves unbiasedness, maximal sampling efficiency (1 − TV(Q,P)), and maximal watermark strength (Ent(P)) for degenerate watermarks. This is the first constructive solution that breaks the previously claimed inevitability of the trade-off.

4. **Detection methods that exploit the acceptance variable.** Section 4.2 describes how ζ^R can improve detection for both Gumbel-max (Ars-τ via Equation 11) and SynthID (Bayes-MLP). These are principled extensions enabled by the algorithm.

5. **Clear and honest writing.** The paper does not overclaim: it explicitly states the scope of Theorem 4.1 (degenerate watermarks), acknowledges that SynthID with m=30 is non-degenerate, and discusses limitations in the conclusion (Section 6). The presentation is well-structured and the claims are calibrated to the evidence.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Missing end-to-end comparison against a prior combined baseline.** The paper's central empirical claim is that Algorithm 1 "improves detectability without sacrificing efficiency." The efficiency comparison (AATPS vs unwatermarked Std. SpecSAMPL) is solid. However, the detectability comparison (Ars-T vs Ars-Prior, or Bayes-MLP vs Bayes-Prior) compares detection methods on texts *all generated by Algorithm 1*. While this shows that using ζ^R improves detection for Algorithm 1's outputs, it does not directly compare the *full system* (Algorithm 1 + Ars-T) against the prior state-of-the-art (standard watermarking + speculative sampling with true-random acceptance + Ars-Prior detection). Adding such a baseline would isolate whether the detectability gain comes solely from better detection engineering or also from the pseudorandom generation mechanism itself, and would provide direct end-to-end evidence for the claimed improvement over prior art.

### Minor

2. **Scope mismatch between theory and experiments for SynthID.** Theorem 4.1's guarantee of maximal watermark strength (part c) assumes a degenerate watermark decoder. SynthID with m=30 is non-degenerate (it converges to degeneracy only as m→∞). The paper acknowledges this in Section 6 ("our current work directly applies to unbiased degenerate watermarks") and in Figure 1's caption, but the experimental section (Section 5) does not remind readers of this scope limitation when presenting SynthID results. The SynthID experiments are valuable as empirical heuristics but should be clearly flagged as outside the scope of Theorem 4.1's guarantee.

3. **Low-temperature sampling not justified.** The paper uses temperature 0.5 for Gumbel-max and 0.7 for SynthID "to make the results more pronounced." Lower temperatures sharpen token distributions, which can affect both acceptance rates and watermark detectability in ways that may not generalize to standard temperature (1.0). Results at temperature 1.0, or a discussion of why these specific temperatures were necessary, would strengthen generalizability.

4. **Threshold calibration for Ars-τ is underspecified.** The paper states τ is "calibrated on a held-out validation set by grid-searching" but does not specify the grid range, resolution, or validation set size. This affects reproducibility.

### Trivial

- The detection experiments compare Ars-T vs Ars-Prior on Algorithm 1 outputs. The claim should be read as "our overall approach (Algorithm 1 generation + detection using ζ^R) improves detectability over prior detection methods" — which is supported — rather than "Algorithm 1's generation itself produces more detectable texts" — which would require a different comparison.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Including results at temperature 1.0 and on C4 detectability (currently deferred to Appendix F.2) would strengthen the experimental base.
- A statistical significance test (e.g., bootstrap) for the AATPS comparisons would add rigor, given overlapping confidence intervals.
