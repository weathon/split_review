## Summary

DIPOLE introduces a novel RL algorithm for diffusion policy optimization that decomposes the optimal policy under a "greedified" KL-regularized objective into a pair of dichotomous policies — one maximizing reward, one minimizing it — both trained with bounded sigmoid weights for stability. During inference, actions are generated by linearly combining their scores with a tunable greediness factor, naturally connecting to classifier-free guidance. The method is evaluated on 39 offline RL tasks (ExORL, OGBench), offline-to-online fine-tuning, and a 1B-parameter VLA model for autonomous driving on NAVSIM.

## Strengths

- **Elegant mathematical formulation with practical payoff**: The derivation from the greedified KL-regularized objective (Eq. 5) through the dichotomous decomposition (Eqs. 7–9) to the linear score combination for inference (Eq. 10) is mathematically clean. The key insight — that sigmoid-weighted policies enable bounded, stable training while the optimal policy is recovered through their combination — directly resolves the stability–greediness trade-off that plagues exponential-weighted regression. The connection to classifier-free guidance (line 140) provides a principled interpretation of the greediness factor ω.

- **Comprehensive and convincing empirical validation**: The paper evaluates on 39 offline RL tasks across ExORL (Table 1, 9 tasks) and OGBench (Table 2, 30 tasks across 6 categories), consistently outperforming strong baselines including IQL, ReBRAC, IDQL, IFQL, FQL, and CFGRL. Offline-to-online results (Table 3) show DIPOLE matches or exceeds FQL (the strongest diffusion baseline) while avoiding its direct-value-maximization instability. The results are averaged over 8 seeds with standard deviations reported.

- **Demonstrated scalability to real-world deployment**: The method is applied to a 1B-parameter vision-language-action model (DP-VLA) for autonomous driving, yielding a 6.5-point PDMS improvement on the NAVSIM benchmark (Table 4, 88.3 → 94.8) with qualitative trajectory corrections shown in Figure 2. This goes substantially beyond standard simulated benchmarks and demonstrates the method's practical viability at scale.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The greedified objective (Eq. 5) lacks theoretical justification beyond its derivation.** The paper replaces the standard reference policy μ in the KL divergence with a value-reweighted reference μ·σ(βG)/Z(s) and presents this as a "greedified" variant. While Theorem 1 correctly derives the closed-form solution, no regret bound, policy improvement guarantee, or variational connection to the standard RL objective is provided. The objective is presented as a design choice ("we instead consider…", line 100) rather than as the solution to a principled RL problem. This limits the contribution to that of a clever and effective heuristic rather than a theoretically grounded method. The paper should at minimum explicitly label this as a heuristic and discuss conditions under which it may diverge from principled RL behavior (e.g., when value estimates are noisy, or when the sigmoid weighting concentrates too aggressively).

### Minor

- **Computational overhead of training two diffusion models is not addressed.** For standard RL benchmarks, the paper trains two separate diffusion models (ε⁺ and ε⁻, Eq. 9). No discussion of memory, runtime, or FLOPs overhead relative to single-model baselines is provided. For the VLA model, LoRA modules mitigate this (line 146), but for the main RL experiments this remains unquantified. Given that practical scalability is a central motivation of the paper (stable training at scale), this omission is notable.

- **The humanoidmaze-large-navigate failure case is unacknowledged.** In Table 2, DIPOLE scores 6 ± 2 on this category while IFQL scores 11 ± 2 — a clear and significant gap. The paper neither acknowledges this underperformance nor speculates on its cause (e.g., poor value estimation in sparse-reward maze navigation, exploration difficulty). Understanding this failure would help characterize the method's limitations.

- **The autonomous driving training setup is not fully characterized.** The paper states it uses a "non-reactive pseudo-closed-loop simulation" (line 146) — meaning the environment does not respond to the agent's actions during training. While this is stated transparently, the paper also frames this as following "the offline-to-online RL setting." The distinction between pseudo-closed-loop training on logged data and genuine online RL (with environment feedback) should be made more explicit, as it affects how results should be interpreted relative to methods like DPPO that assume interactive environments.

### Trivial

- Some variance bars in Table 3 are wide (e.g., IQL humanoidmaze-m: 21 ± 13 → 16 ± 8); statistical significance is not reported.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A hyperparameter sensitivity study for β and ω, including extreme values, would guide practitioners on stability boundaries.
- Parameter-sharing strategies (e.g., single model with two output heads, shared backbone) could be explored to reduce the two-model overhead.
- An analysis of how errors in the value/advantage function G(s,a) propagate through the sigmoid weighting and affect policy quality would strengthen understanding of the method's practical requirements.

## Novel Insights

The dichotomous decomposition reveals an interesting structure: the optimal policy under a bounded-weight KL objective naturally factorizes into a ratio of positive and negative policies, and the inference-time score combination mirrors classifier-free guidance exactly. This suggests a broader principle — that many weighted-regression RL methods might be reinterpretable as inference-time combinations of policies trained with complementary weighting schemes, potentially opening a design space for new algorithms that trade off stability against greediness through the choice of weighting function and combination rule.
